# F-d0b5f33599fa

## Purpose

F-d0b5f33599fa describes an ordered sequence of indirect dependency calls, computed-address memory accesses, and fixed-width transformations. Its directly represented effects include publishing the third input twice, clearing selected locations, storing scaled dependency results, and extracting byte-sized portions of input or dependency-produced values.

No application-level purpose is established. Initialization, packed-field construction, and device programming are possible interpretations, not acceptance requirements.

The contract is conditional on execution reaching each operation. The supplied static representation reports invalid instruction data and overlapping global symbols, and its visible path ends at an undecodable boundary. It does not establish a valid executable instruction stream, successful completion, or normal return.

### Widths and notation

The following mathematical labels describe retained data relationships, not additional interface parameters:

## Behavior

### Ordering and dependency conventions

The following phases preserve the represented ordering of direct accesses and dependency calls. Each operation requires all preceding operations on this path to have completed.

For compactness:

- A **four-call group** means four consecutive indirect calls with no explicit arguments shown. Where a result is named, it is the result of the fourth call.
- A **forwarding pair** means a call with no explicit arguments shown, followed by a call receiving that call’s result as its sole explicit argument.

These descriptions do not establish that absent arguments are absent from the actual calling convention. Dependencies may observe additional machine state.

A selector is reread only where a new read is specified. Retained results and selectors follow the static dataflow; actual preservation across calls depends on unresolved machine and calling-convention behavior. No dependency is assumed to preserve globals, avoid aliasing, return normally, or leave memory unchanged.

No explicit conditional branch or loop appears along the represented path.

### Initial publication and selector updates

The first direct effect is a 32-bit store of \(P\) at \(U+\texttt{0x24}\).

Five indirect calls follow. The first receives \(B\); the next four show no explicit arguments. Only afterward is \(P\) written as a 32-bit word at \(B+\texttt{0x24}\).

A 32-bit read at \(B+\texttt{0x74}\) supplies the sole explicit argument to the next call. A four-call group follows; denote its final 32-bit result by \(R\).

The routine reads the selector byte at \(B+1\) and writes the low eight bits of \(Q\) logically shifted right by 22 at the selected address plus `0x11`.

A dependency receives \(4R\). A forwarding pair and then a four-call group follow. The group’s final result supplies the next scaled argument, but does not replace the retained \(R\).

The selector at \(B+1\) is read again, and zero is written as one byte at that newly selected address plus `0x11`. A dependency then receives four times the latest group result. Another four-call group follows.

The two reads at \(B+1\) are independent. Intervening dependencies, or aliasing with earlier writes, can make their selected destinations differ.

### Fourth-input-derived clears

The routine writes a zero byte at \(S+\texttt{0x0f}\). It then reads the selector at \(T+1\) and writes a zero byte at that selector plus `0x11`.

The next dependency receives four explicit arguments. The first three are each the low 32 bits of \(R\) shifted left by 23; the fourth is the low 32 bits of \(R\) shifted left by 25.

A forwarding pair and a four-call group follow. The final result supplies the next four-times argument.

A fresh selector read at \(T+1\) is retained. A zero byte is written at that selector plus `0x11`. A dependency receives four times the latest group result, followed by another four-call group.

The retained selector—not a new read—then determines a zero-word store at selector plus `0x44`. A zero word is subsequently written at absolute address `0x24`. This is the first of four explicit absolute-address clears.

The next dependency receives, in order:

1. Zero.
2. `0x98`.
3. \(T\).
4. `0x98`.

No buffer-fill, copy, allocation, or release semantics are established for this call.

### Result-indexed clears and the remaining absolute clears

Two forwarding pairs occur consecutively, followed by a four-call group. Its final result determines a zero-word destination at four times that result plus `0x44`. The routine then clears absolute address `0x24` a second time.

A dependency receives four times that same result. A four-call group follows, with its final result represented as 64 bits.

The two halves of that result have separate uses:

- Four times the low 32-bit half is passed to the next dependency.
- Twice the high 32-bit half, plus `0x44`, determines a zero-word destination.

The high-half-derived word clear occurs after the low-half argument call. Absolute address `0x24` is then cleared a third time.

The following dependency receives zero, the auxiliary register result, and four times that auxiliary result. The auxiliary value is associated with the preceding scaled-low-half call boundary; its exact production is unresolved.

One call with no explicit arguments follows. A dependency then receives \(128M\), followed by a four-call group.

The group’s final result determines another zero-word destination at four times that result plus `0x44`. Absolute address `0x24` is then cleared a fourth time. Denote the result used for this last indexed clear by \(K\).

The four absolute clears remain four separate observable stores. They cannot be merged merely because each writes zero: intervening dependencies may inspect or modify the location.

This portion contains exactly six direct 32-bit stores: two explicit zero stores and four computed-value stores. None of the four computed values is guaranteed to be nonzero.

Immediately after the fourth absolute clear, a dependency receives \(4K\). A four-call group follows. Its final result is scaled by four and passed to another dependency. Denote that dependency’s result by \(X\).

The first store writes zero at \(4X+\texttt{0x14}\).

The next dependency receives four arguments: \(X\), \(X\), \(X\), and \(128M\). A four-call group follows; denote its final result by \(Y\).

The second store writes zero at \(4Y+\texttt{0x14}\).

A dependency then receives \(4Y\), followed by a four-call group whose final result is \(A\).

The third store writes \(4A\) at \(16A+\texttt{0x14}\). The value \(4A\) is retained for another store.

A dependency receives that retained \(4A\), followed by a four-call group whose final result is \(C\).

The fourth store writes the retained \(4A\) at \(4C+\texttt{0x14}\). Its stored value is therefore not generally derived from its own destination-selecting result.

A dependency receives \(4C\), followed by a four-call group whose final result is \(D\).

The fifth store writes \(4D\) at \(16D+\texttt{0x14}\).

The next dependency receives two arguments, \(4D\) and \(16D\), in that order. Another call produces a result which is passed to the following dependency together with \(16M\). A four-call group follows; denote its final result by \(E\).

The sixth store writes \(128M\) at \(4E+\texttt{0x14}\).

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance examples, not executed tests. All require execution to reach the cited operations and the corresponding accesses to be valid.

1. **Publication order.**  
   For \(P=\texttt{0x12345678}\), the first word store writes that value at \(U+\texttt{0x24}\). Five calls intervene before the store at \(B+\texttt{0x24}\). The first of those calls receives \(B\).

2. **Initial byte extraction.**  
   For \(Q=\texttt{0xffffffff}\), the right-shifted initial byte value is `0x3ff` before truncation. The selector-based store writes one byte, `0xff`.

3. **Input-derived scaling.**  
   For \(Q=8\), \(S=1\), \(M=1\), and \(T=\texttt{0x400}\). The direct zero-byte destination is `0x10`; the selector-read address derived from \(T\) is `0x401`. The masked scaled quantities are \(128M=\texttt{0x80}\) and \(16M=\texttt{0x10}\).

4. **Wrapping address formation.**  
   For \(Q=\texttt{0x200}\), \(S=64\). Under the represented 32-bit interpretation, \(H\) wraps to zero, making the \(H+1\) selector-read address `0x1`.

5. **Reread versus retention.**  
   If the byte at \(B+1\) changes between its two reads, the initial update and subsequent zero-byte store may target different addresses. Conversely, the later zero-word store at selector plus `0x44` uses the selector retained from the second \(T+1\) read, even if intervening calls change that memory.

6. **Six offset-`0x14` stores and retained data.**  
   If \(X=1\), \(Y=2\), \(A=3\), \(C=7\), \(D=5\), \(E=9\), and \(M=0\), the six stores, in order, are:

   | Destination | Word value |
   |---|---:|
   | `0x18` | 0 |
   | `0x1c` | 0 |
   | `0x44` | 12 |
   | `0x30` | 12 |
   | `0x64` | 20 |
   | `0x38` | 0 |

   The fourth store retains 12 from the third store’s computation, rather than storing \(4C=28\). The sixth remains a computed-value store even though its value is zero.

7. **Argument-bearing boundaries.**  
   With \(K=6\), the call immediately after the fourth absolute clear receives 24 before the next four-call group. In the preceding store example, the calls after the second, third, and fourth offset-`0x14` stores receive 8, 12, and 28 respectively, each before its next four-call group.

8. **Packed bytes.**  
   For \(V=\texttt{0x12345678}\), its four-times byte write stores `0xe0`; its masked-and-shifted byte write stores `0x23`. The second value remains derived from \(V\) despite intervening result production.

9. **Split result.**  
   If the 64-bit result has low half 3 and high half 5, the next scaled argument is 12, while the subsequent zero-word destination is `0x4e`. Validity of that potentially unaligned access is not established.

10. **Conditional termination.**  
    A nonreturning early dependency prevents any requirement to perform later stores. Even if every visible call returns, a normal return beyond the terminal boundary is not established.
