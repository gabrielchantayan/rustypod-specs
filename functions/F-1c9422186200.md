### Widths and arithmetic conventions

For compactness, the following are mathematical descriptions, not implementation identifiers:

- \(S_n(v)\): the low 32 bits of \(v\) shifted left by \(n\).
- \(\operatorname{byte}(v)\): the low eight bits of \(v\).
- \(X(v)=((v\mathbin{\&}0x3fffffff)\gg20)\).

Within the conditional 32-bit interpretation, multiplication, addition, and left shifting retain only the low 32 bits when used as word values or address expressions.

That interpretation must not be mistaken for an established language-level guarantee. The representation mixes signed arithmetic, unsigned arithmetic, and pointer conversions. Exact executable overflow, address wrapping, and signed-shift behavior remain **documented_only**.

The extraction \(X(v)\) is ten bits wide. Storing it as a byte retains original bits 20–27; original bits 28–29 are discarded by byte truncation.

Every selector read is an unsigned byte. A destination formed as selector plus `0x11` therefore ranges from `0x11` through `0x110`. A word destination formed as selector plus `0x14` ranges from `0x14` through `0x113`. These are absolute computed addresses in the representation, not established offsets within an owned object.
