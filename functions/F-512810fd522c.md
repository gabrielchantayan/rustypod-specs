# F-512810fd522c

## Purpose

F-512810fd522c prepares a relationship between two objects, captures associated values, and conditionally requests a 64-bit result from F-fbf12e72fd4b. When that request occurs and returns, the operation publishes its least-significant byte. A separate selector controls subsequent record construction.

The operation is not established as an ordinarily returning interface. Its represented paths end either at an interrupt followed by an invalid-instruction boundary, or at a processor operation designated nonreturning. Earlier memory effects are not reversed.

Two record forms are supported by the represented control flow:

- A 12-byte record containing a destination address, previously captured companion data, and the low 32 bits of the original result.
- A 16-byte record containing a destination address, a selector, an indirectly retrieved payload, and the low 32 bits of a result.

A further continuation obtains a replacement result and changes the inputs to 16-byte construction. Under the explicit 32-bit arithmetic model below, its selection condition is unreachable. Its conditional effects remain **documented_only**.

“Object,” “companion,” “publication,” and “record” describe observable data relationships. They do not establish an application protocol, successful delivery, or ownership transfer.

### Widths and arithmetic model

Bytes, halfwords, words, and dependency results are respectively 8, 16, 32, and 64 bits. Addresses and record offsets use 32-bit words and byte units. A result’s low word contains bits 0–31; its high word contains bits 32–63.

Arithmetic conclusions in this contract use these explicit assumptions:

- Word arithmetic and address formation retain the low 32 bits.
- Signed words use two’s-complement interpretation.
- Signed right shifts extend the sign.
- Narrow stores retain only the destination width.
- Unsigned borrow and signed overflow are evaluated for the same 32-bit subtraction.

These assumptions make the numerical analysis definite. They are not independent proof of executable processor semantics. In particular, expressions involving signed overflow or left shifts do not by themselves establish portable language-level behavior.

All computed addresses are absolute addresses. A small signed value is not implicitly an offset within an object.

Each reached access requires suitable storage, permissions, and any architecture-required alignment. Each invocation requires a valid executable target. Further progress requires earlier dependencies to return and, where applicable, an interrupt handler to resume execution.

## Behavior

### Preparation precedes admission

The opening stage invokes F-D01 with the transformed third argument, then supplies its represented 32-bit result to F-D02.

After those dependencies return, the operation obtains the linked-object address from the fourth argument’s +4 word. It records that address at primary object +0x2c and writes the low byte of eight times the scale at linked object +0x1b.

The primary address, interpreted as signed and shifted right by 16 bits, determines a separate metadata base. This base ranges from −32768 through 32767. The operation captures the word at that base +0x54 before storing the primary-object address at the base plus eight times the scale. Only afterward does it capture the companion word at companion source +0x6c.

This ordering defines overlap behavior:

- The principal argument is captured before the indexed metadata store and cannot be retroactively changed by it.
- The companion capture occurs later and can observe that store.
- The earlier link and byte stores can affect subsequent reads.
- The linked-object address is retained after its initial read; the fourth argument’s field is not explicitly reread.

These statements concern direct represented accesses. They do not impose preservation requirements on opaque callees.

Admission depends on whether the metadata base is negative and whether the admission indicator is nonzero. Matching classifications reject; differing classifications admit.

| Metadata base | Indicator | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Nonnegative | Zero | Reject |
| Nonnegative | Nonzero | Admit |
| Negative | Zero | Admit |
| Negative | Nonzero | Reject |

Rejection requests software interrupt 0x10 without invoking F-fbf12e72fd4b. If execution resumes along the represented continuation, it encounters an invalid-instruction boundary. No ordinary return beyond that boundary is established.

Admission does not protect preparation: all preparation accesses and writes occur first.

### Result acquisition and publication

An admitted operation invokes F-fbf12e72fd4b with the captured principal argument. Its represented 64-bit output is the **original result**.

The operation publishes the original result’s low byte at linked object +0x0c. It subsequently reads a 32-bit word from absolute address 0x083fedb4. Thus, overlap between the publication address and that fixed-address word can affect the captured value.

The fixed-address read occurs even when the record selector is zero. Its captured value is relevant only to the conditional replacement continuation.

A zero record selector suppresses record construction, the post-construction dependencies, and the coprocessor operation. It does not suppress preparation, admission, result acquisition, publication, or the fixed-address read. Execution then reaches the terminal operation described below.

### Selection between record forms

For a nonzero record selector, the selection seed is the low 32 bits of 64 times the scale. Every possible seed is therefore a multiple of 64.

Selection performs at least one subtraction of 234. It continues while the signed result is negative and stops at the first nonnegative result. The final subtraction’s signed-overflow, unsigned-borrow, and zero conditions distinguish the represented continuations:

| Final subtraction | Continuation |
|---|---|
| Signed overflow | Original-result 16-byte record |
| No signed overflow; unsigned borrow or zero result | Replacement-result continuation |
| No signed overflow; neither unsigned borrow nor zero result | 12-byte record |

Under the declared arithmetic model, this reduces to the following complete partition:

| Seed | Reachable record form |
|---|---|
| 0x00000100 through 0x7fffffc0, in multiples of 64 | 12-byte record |
| All other multiples of 64 | Original-result 16-byte record |

The replacement condition cannot be selected under this model. Borrow without signed overflow produces a negative result and therefore cannot terminate selection. A terminating zero without overflow would require an initial seed of 234: encountering a positive value of 234 after an earlier subtraction would already have terminated selection. That initial seed is impossible because 234 is not divisible by 64.

Selection is consequently not a remainder calculation. A sufficiently large positive seed terminates after one subtraction. Negative seeds and small nonnegative seeds can require millions of iterations before signed overflow produces a nonnegative result. No iteration limit or cancellation mechanism is represented.

### Twelve-byte record

The 12-byte destination is the linked-object address interpreted as signed and shifted right by 28 bits. Its signed range is −8 through 7. This is an absolute address, not an offset from the linked object.

| Byte offset | Width | Content |
|---|---|---|
| +0 | 32 bits | Destination address |
| +4 | 32 bits | Captured companion word |
| +8 | 32 bits | Original result’s low word |

Fields are written in increasing offset order. Construction uses the already captured companion word rather than reading its source again.

After construction, F-D04 is invoked. If it returns, software interrupt 0x10 is requested. Represented resumption reaches the same invalid-instruction boundary as rejection. This path does not proceed to 16-byte construction or the coprocessor operation.

### Sixteen-byte record

The current result’s high word supplies the 16-byte destination. On paths reachable under the declared arithmetic model, the current result is the original result.

Before any record field is written, the operation acquires an indirect payload:

- An auxiliary address component is obtained by interpreting the destination as signed and shifting it right by three bits.
- The destination’s low halfword is stored at that component +0x24.
- A signed halfword is read at the working metadata base plus that component.
- That halfword is sign-extended to a 32-bit absolute address, from which the payload word is captured.

The preliminary halfword store precedes both payload reads and can affect them through overlap. The final payload address ranges from −32768 through 32767 in signed interpretation. It is not relative to the metadata base.

The record layout is:

| Byte offset | Width | Content |
|---|---|---|
| +0 | 32 bits | Destination address |
| +4 | 32 bits | Working selector |
| +8 | 32 bits | Captured payload |
| +0x0c | 32 bits | Current result’s low word |

Fields are written in increasing offset order. Later field stores cannot change the payload already captured, even if they overlap its source.

F-D05 follows the completed record. If it returns, a coprocessor operation is represented with selector values 9, 7, and 5. Its remaining operands are the working object address, the second opaque processor operand, and the first opaque processor operand, in that order.

On the original-result continuation, the working metadata base, selector, and object address retain their corresponding represented roles from preparation. This is a dataflow statement, not a general guarantee that opaque dependencies preserve machine state.

### Conditional replacement continuation

This continuation is **documented_only** because the declared selection arithmetic excludes it. The following behavior applies only if independent evidence establishes entry.

F-D03 receives the prospective 12-byte destination and the captured companion word interpreted as signed and shifted right by 24 bits. The latter argument ranges from −128 through 127.

F-D03’s target is derived from the original dispatch anchor. Before invocation, the working dispatch anchor changes to 0x083fea7d. F-D03 supplies a replacement 64-bit result.

The previously captured fixed-address word, multiplied by eight with 32-bit truncation, becomes an auxiliary basis. Software interrupt 0x10 occurs before the replacement-specific stores. Conditional on resumption, auxiliary basis +8 first receives the original linked-object address and then receives the replacement result’s high word. Both stores are represented effects; the first is not erased from the contract merely because the second overwrites it.

Next, alternate object +0x30 receives the replacement result’s low halfword. Subsequent 16-byte construction uses:

- The replacement result as the current result.
- Four times the sum of the auxiliary basis and 0x6a as the working metadata base, truncated to 32 bits.
- The replacement-selector basis plus 0x7f as the working selector, truncated to 32 bits.
- The alternate object as the working object.
- 0x083fea7d as the working dispatch anchor.

These substitutions do not establish writes back to caller-owned input storage. The substituted selector is not tested again; zero can therefore be stored as record content on this conditional continuation.

### Terminal behavior

The zero-selector path and the continuation after the coprocessor operation reach a processor operation designated nonreturning, with immediate 0x6d and location operand 0x083feab8.

The representation also attributes a subsequent indirect invocation, F-D06, to a purported result of that operation. Neither a usable target nor an executable continuation is established. F-D06 is **documented_only**, not a promised final callback.

## Side effects

The operation is nontransactional. No rollback, restoration, cleanup, or recovery sequence is represented.

Observable partial effects can include preparation without admission, publication without a record, a preliminary halfword store without completed payload acquisition, and a partially written record. Conditional replacement entry additionally permits the first auxiliary store to occur without its later overwrite.

Ordering does not establish atomicity, synchronization, memory barriers, or visibility guarantees for concurrent observers. Partial-overlap interpretation depends on unresolved byte order and memory behavior.

Opaque dependencies and processor handlers may modify memory, globals, processor state, resources, or ownership. Their behavior is not constrained beyond the explicit relationships documented here. In particular, absence of a direct release does not prove that unknown callees release nothing.

No structured error handling is established for invalid storage, invalid targets, failed dependencies, interrupt handling, or exceptional processor operations.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static expectations, not executed tests. They assume the declared arithmetic model, valid reached accesses and targets, returning dependencies where necessary, and interrupt resumption where specified.

1. **Preparation survives rejection.** A nonnegative metadata base with a zero admission indicator rejects only after preparation. F-fbf12e72fd4b is not invoked; preceding stores are not rolled back.

2. **Admission uses classification, not magnitude.** For a negative metadata base, zero admits. Nonzero indicators such as 0x01 and 0x80 both reject.

3. **Zero selector still publishes.** After admission and a returning principal call, a zero record selector still permits the low-byte store and fixed-address word read. It produces neither record form and reaches the designated nonreturning terminal operation.

4. **Positive seeds need not be reduced repeatedly.** Scale 4 gives seed 0x100 and a first result of 0x16. Scale 16 gives seed 0x400 and a first result of 0x316. Both select 12-byte construction immediately.

5. **A small seed does not select replacement.** Scale 3 gives seed 192 and first result −42. Selection continues until signed overflow yields a nonnegative result, selecting original-result 16-byte construction.

6. **Overflow can terminate immediately.** Scale 0x02000000 gives seed 0x80000000. Subtracting 234 produces 0x7fffff16 with signed overflow, selecting original-result 16-byte construction.

7. **Captured values resist later overlap.** If the indexed metadata store overlaps the principal-argument word, the principal call still receives the earlier capture. An overlapping companion read occurs later and can observe the store.

8. **Payload addresses are sign-extended.** A destination of 0xffffffe0 gives auxiliary component −4. The preliminary store writes halfword 0xffe0 at address 0x20. If the subsequent signed-halfword read yields bit pattern 0x8001, the payload word is read at 0xffff8001.

9. **Conditional replacement retains both stores.** Given independently established entry and interrupt resumption, auxiliary basis +8 receives the original linked address before the replacement high word. A replacement-selector basis of 0xffffff81 yields zero selector content without suppressing construction.
