## Behavior

### Arithmetic model

The following notation separates mathematical descriptions from the represented operations:

- **Unsigned32(a)** means conversion of an integer value to unsigned 32-bit form, reduced modulo $2^{32}$.
- **Signed32(a)** means conversion of an unsigned 32-bit value to signed 32-bit form. Values through 2,147,483,647 convert exactly. Larger values require the implementation-defined conversion rule.
- **Q** is U shifted logically right by 12.
- **T** is U shifted logically right by 24.
- **S** is the signed-typed result of shifting V right by eight.
- **M** is Signed32 of the modulo-$2^{32}$ sum Unsigned32(S) plus Q.
- **X** is Signed32 of the modulo-$2^{32}$ difference Q minus 58.

Q ranges from 0 through 1,048,575. T ranges from 0 through 255, and T equals Q shifted logically right by 12. They are not independently selectable.

For nonnegative V, S is the integer quotient obtained by discarding the lowest eight bits. Negative V requires the target’s signed-right-shift rule; an arithmetic right shift must not be assumed without that condition.

The computation producing M is **unsigned addition followed by signed conversion**, not signed addition. The computation producing X is **unsigned subtraction followed by signed conversion**, not signed subtraction.

In particular:

- When Q is at least 58, X is exactly Q minus 58, between 0 and 1,048,517.
- When Q is below 58, the unsigned subtraction yields a value between 4,294,967,238 and 4,294,967,295. Converting it to X is implementation-defined.
- Under the conventional conversion that maps upper-half unsigned values to signed values by subtracting $2^{32}$, X becomes the mathematical value Q minus 58 even when negative. Examples using negative X explicitly assume that conversion.

Then Q, T, and S are zero. The unsigned sum producing M is zero, so M is exactly zero.

- The first four integer address expressions equal Unsigned32(B).

- The sixth address is Unsigned32(B) plus 57, modulo $2^{32}$.
