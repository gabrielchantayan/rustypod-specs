# F-bf6e093e588c

## Purpose

F-bf6e093e588c describes a serial sequence of indirect dependency invocations with four explicit single-byte stores. Most result-producing invocations are followed by a consumer receiving a four-times-scaled result. Other boundaries forward an unchanged word, duplicate a word into two argument positions, or derive a write address and subsequent arguments from the same result.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. It does not support identifying this behavior as initialization, allocation, serialization, device control, or cleanup.

The represented path ends at an invalid-instruction boundary, not an ordinary return. A control-flow warning also limits confidence in the recovered extent. This contract therefore distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** invocation order, displayed data dependencies, explicit stores, argument multiplicity, and represented termination boundary.
- **Conditional machine interpretation:** arithmetic consequences when words and addresses follow a 32-bit modular execution model.
- **documented_only:** unresolved calling conventions, dependency behavior, memory validity, and actual execution beyond the recovered boundary.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Ordering and invocation vocabulary

The visible operations are serial. A later call or store is reached only after preceding operations complete. No concurrency, retry, rollback, or local recovery sequence is represented.

An **argument-unspecified invocation** is one for which the representation displays no argument expressions. This does not establish a zero-parameter signature or prevent the dependency from consuming ambient machine state.

Two recurring patterns describe the trace:

- A **scaled-result group** contains a result-producing invocation, an immediate consumer receiving that result shifted left by two bits, and three subsequent argument-unspecified invocations.
- A **forwarding boundary** contains three argument-unspecified invocations, a result-producing invocation, a consumer receiving the result unchanged, and three further argument-unspecified invocations. The last forwarding boundary supplies the result twice rather than once.

These patterns group ordered occurrences. They do not imply shared implementations or dependency identities.

### Arithmetic interpretation

Under a conditional 32-bit modular machine model, left shifts retain the low 32 bits:

| Transformation | Numerical scale before truncation |
|---|---:|
| Left shift by two | 4 |
| Left shift by three | 8 |
| Left shift by ten | 1,024 |
| Left shift by fourteen | 16,384 |

No overflow checks are represented. Discarded high bits are not supplied separately to consumers.

The representation mixes signed and unsigned intermediates. The selector-producing result and byte-source word are unsigned; several other shifted values and multiplications are signed. Consequently, modular arithmetic describes a conditional machine interpretation, not a blanket guarantee for every represented signed operation. Negative signed operands, signed overflow, conversion from unsigned to signed, and integer-to-address conversion require further execution-model evidence.

The unsigned byte extraction at the second boundary uses a logical right shift.

### First write boundary

F-d003 produces a 32-bit unsigned **selector result**. Bit 30 determines which value becomes the **selected word**:

- With bit 30 set, the selected word comes from the selector result shifted left by two bits.
- With bit 30 clear, the selected word comes from the incoming signed general-purpose word shifted left by ten bits.

Bit 31 is not the selection condition. The alternate value replaces the result-derived candidate; the two values are not added.

The first store writes the low eight bits of the incoming unsigned word to the address obtained by doubling the selected word. F-d004 is invoked only afterward, receiving the selected word in both displayed argument positions.

The call arguments are therefore not the doubled store address.

Under the modular interpretation, a result-selected address is divisible by eight, and an alternate-selected address is divisible by 2,048. Either may be zero or otherwise invalid. No object ownership, address-range check, or nonzero guarantee follows from these divisibility properties.

### Second write boundary

F-d007 produces an independent **second address word**.

The second store targets the address obtained by shifting that word left by three bits. Its byte value is bits 22 through 29 of the incoming unsigned word: the low eight bits remaining after a logical right shift by 22.

Bits 30 and 31 do not contribute to the stored byte.

After the store, F-d008 receives:

1. The second address word multiplied by four.
2. The second address word shifted left by ten bits.

These arguments are not generally equal. Neither is generally equal to the eight-times-scaled store address. Coincidental equality for particular inputs does not change the transformations.

### Third and fourth write boundaries

F-d011 and F-d015 each produce a separate word.

At each boundary, the direct effect is a one-byte zero store at the address obtained by shifting that boundary’s result left by three bits. The associated consumer, F-d012 or F-d016, follows the store and receives two equal arguments, each obtained by shifting the same result left by fourteen bits.

These operations establish neither a full-word clear nor a buffer initialization.

The two destinations may coincide. They may also coincide with earlier destinations or storage visible to dependencies. No distinctness check is represented.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static-review examples, not executed tests. Unless an example concerns failure, assume preceding dependencies return, the required arithmetic interpretation applies, and the destination is writable.

### Scaled-result consumer

If a scaled-result producer returns `7`, its immediate consumer receives `28`. The group’s three argument-unspecified invocations follow.

This does not permit forwarding `28` to later producers or omitting intervening calls.

### First selection with bit 30 clear

Suppose F-d003 returns zero, the incoming signed general-purpose word is `3`, and the incoming unsigned word is `0x123456AB`.

The selected word is `3,072`. The first store writes `0xAB` at address `6,144`. F-d004 subsequently receives `3,072` twice.

A zero selector result does not force a zero address on this branch.

### First selection with bit 30 set

Under the 32-bit modular interpretation, if F-d003 returns `0x40000001`, the selected word is `4`, and the store address is `8`. F-d004 receives `4` twice.

The incoming general-purpose word does not determine this branch’s selection. Writable storage at address `8` remains a separate precondition.

### Second-byte extraction

If F-d007 returns `5` and the incoming unsigned word is `0xABC00000`, the second store writes `0xAF` at address `40`.

F-d008 then receives `20` and `5,120`, in that order. The stored byte is not the original word’s low byte.

### Independent zero stores

If F-d011 returns `2`, the third store writes zero at address `16`, followed by F-d012 receiving `32,768` twice.

If F-d015 later returns `3`, the fourth store writes zero at address `24`, followed by F-d016 receiving `49,152` twice. The fourth boundary does not reuse the third boundary’s result.

### Duplicated unchanged forwarding

If the producer within F-d017 returns the bit pattern `0xDEADBEEF`, its consumer receives that unchanged 32-bit pattern in both displayed argument positions.

### Terminal invocation count

If the ninth producer in F-d018 returns `7`, its consumer receives `28`. Exactly four argument-unspecified invocations then remain in the represented path: three group-trailing invocations and F-d019.

If all four return, the represented invalid-instruction boundary follows. A trace containing seven such invocations after this consumer would introduce three unsupported calls.

### Interrupted store

If the second store cannot complete, F-d008 and subsequent operations are not required to occur. Restoration of the first store or reversal of preceding dependency effects is not established.
