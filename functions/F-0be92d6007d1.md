# F-0be92d6007d1

## Purpose

F-0be92d6007d1 performs an ordered transformation of addressed storage, followed by dependent address traversal and external interactions. The application-level meaning of that storage is unknown.

Its behavioral contract concerns **which observations supply later effects, and when those observations occur**. Final memory contents alone are insufficient to establish equivalence: overlapping destinations can change later reads, while retained values continue to supply their specified consumers.

The operation comprises initialization, nine rounds of memory effects, and a terminal traversal. The traversal includes publication of a byte whose value has no established preceding definition. Requirements beyond that point describe a conditional continuation, not guaranteed defined execution.

### Storage and arithmetic requirements

A word is four bytes, a halfword two bytes, and a byte one byte. Offsets below are decimal byte offsets unless marked hexadecimal.

Signed-word observations have 32-bit interpretation. Signed-halfword observations preserve their sign when promoted. Character observations occupy one byte, but character signedness is unknown; therefore, a character used in address arithmetic is not necessarily nonnegative.

A **character conversion** is distinct from an explicit low-eight-bit extraction. Out-of-range signed narrowing behavior is unresolved. Signed additions, multiplications, and shifts have no established wrapping guarantee. A later byte conversion does not make an earlier overflowing calculation valid.

Every reached memory access requires suitable mapping, extent, permissions, lifetime, and any applicable alignment. Byte order and pointer representation are not established. Separate names in this contract do not imply disjoint storage.

Receiving or publishing an address establishes no allocation, ownership transfer, retention permission, or release right.

## Behavior

### Observation model

The contract distinguishes retained values from later observations of the same location:

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cursor | Address-bearing value used for recurring annotations |
| Carry | Value transferred to each round’s base-plus-20 location |
| Selector | Signed word used in destination calculations |
| Displacement | Sign-preserved halfword used in address calculations |
| Sample | Retained four-byte observation at root plus 100 |
| Byte selector | Character observation used in subsequent destinations |
| Bridge | Signed word obtained in round 3 |

An **entry** value is the retained value at the beginning of a round. A refresh changes that role only at the specified refresh point. An incidental read from the same address does not refresh it.

A **self-link** publishes an address-bearing value as a word at that value plus 84.

Ordering requirements describe the supplied static behavior. They do not establish volatile accesses, atomicity, synchronization, or a hardware memory-ordering model.

### Initialization guarantees

Argument 2 is published at anchor plus 20 before argument 4 is published at origin plus 17. The initial retained observations then occur in this order:

1. Sample from root plus 100.
2. Selector from origin plus 68.
3. Displacement from origin plus anchor.

Companion is published at root plus 84 before cursor is acquired as a signed word from anchor plus 20. Consequently, cursor need not equal argument 2 when these locations overlap.

Before the first round, the following ordering holds:

- Cursor-plus-13 annotation receives a character conversion of anchor.
- Anchor-plus-displacement annotation receives a halfword conversion of selector.
- Carry is acquired as a signed word from anchor plus 68.
- Cursor receives its self-link.

The halfword annotation therefore precedes, and may affect, initial carry acquisition.

Each round has a base selected by its first environmental observation. A separate observation of that round’s environmental source supplies its payload.

The opening effects have this precedence:

**Base observation → payload observation and publication → entry-carry publication at base plus 20 → opening publication at entry cursor plus 17 → refreshes.**

The two environmental observations need not agree.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Entry selector plus 68 | Entry sample | Selector, displacement |
| 2 | Entry selector plus 68 | Entry cursor shifted left once | Displacement, byte selector |
| 3 | Entry byte selector plus 68 | Entry cursor shifted left once | Displacement, byte selector |

Refresh locations are fixed by role:

| Role | Observation |
|---|---|
| Sample | Four bytes at root plus 100 |
| Selector | Signed word at entry cursor plus 68 |

**Halfword annotation → carry acquisition → current-cursor self-link.**

The annotation destination is closing base plus refreshed displacement. Its input is byte selector in rounds 2–4 and selector in the other closing rounds. Carry comes from closing base plus 68: signed-word content through round 7, and four-byte content without an established signed interpretation in round 8.

Closing base is the round base except where specified below. Round 9 has no closing group.

Refreshed byte selector plus 13 then receives a character conversion of entry carry. Bridge is acquired from round base plus 100 before closing.

**Round 4 — bridge publication.** Between refreshes and closing, the following destinations are affected in order:

### Rounds 7 and 8: shared replacement contract

Both rounds replace cursor from a signed-word observation at refreshed displacement plus 20. Entry cursor is not part of that address.

Following replacement, these effects precede the common closing group, in order:

1. Refreshed selector receives its self-link.
2. Bridge is published at round base plus entry carry.
3. Refreshed selector is published at round base plus 20.
4. A fresh four-byte observation at bridge plus 68 supplies a character conversion at round base plus 13.
5. Replacement cursor plus 13 receives a character conversion of refreshed selector.

The bridge-relative observation does not refresh bridge.

Round 8 additionally has three ordered effects after refreshes but before cursor replacement:

- Entry cursor is published at entry carry plus 84.
- Refreshed sample’s low eight bits are extracted, retained, and published at entry cursor plus 17.
- Round base is published at refreshed selector plus 68.

Round 8 therefore publishes twice at entry cursor plus 17: first from entry sample, then from refreshed sample’s explicit low-eight-bit extraction. The retained extracted byte also supplies round 9’s opening publication.

Round 9 copies round 8’s outgoing four-byte carry content to its own base plus 20. This transfer does not itself dereference that content.

|---|---|

The overwrite can affect factor acquisition through overlap, but does not replace the retained link payload.
