# F-60d8cc88f58d

## Purpose

F-60d8cc88f58d executes a long sequence of indirect calls, transforms values obtained from those calls, performs ordered memory updates, and invokes a final indirect dependency with four arguments.

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated offsets do not establish an object layout, and character-like constants do not establish text generation, serialization, or formatting.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** access widths, value relationships, and ordering represented in the supplied body.
- **Conditional deductions:** consequences of interpreting arithmetic as truncating operations on the represented widths.
- **documented_only:** unresolved details that are not executable requirements.

The memory behavior is order-sensitive. Fixed-location storage and derived addresses may overlap; a later read is not generally interchangeable with an earlier saved value.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Arithmetic and widths

Direct accesses use 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. Represented call results also use 64 bits.

For a 64-bit result, upper-half extraction selects bits 32 through 63. Full-result right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 select different portions; they correspond to the upper half shifted right by two, four, and twelve positions.

Unsigned right shifts are zero-filling where unsigned operands are established. The body uses masks of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits.

Under **conditional truncating 32-bit semantics**, left shifts and scaling retain only the low 32 bits. Intermediate narrowing matters: two successive 28-position left shifts of a 32-bit value yield zero, rather than an arbitrarily wide mathematical result.

Signed shifts, multiplication overflow, and address overflow cannot be assigned portable semantics from this representation alone. Arithmetic-derived address and value relationships below are conditional on the applicable target interpretation.

Byte stores retain the low eight bits; 16-bit stores retain the low sixteen bits. A byte read explicitly represented as unsigned is zero-extended when used as 32-bit data or as an address component.

A terminal signed right shift obtains its count from the low eight bits of a derived address value. Counts may exceed 31. Large-count behavior and right shifting negative operands are **documented_only**.

### Entry to the direct-memory phase

The last call before direct memory effects produces a represented 64-bit result. Its upper half supplies the base for a 32-bit store at offset 0x14. Its lower half, scaled by 256, supplies another base for a 32-bit store at offset 0x54.

Between those stores, and again afterward, a byte is written at offset 9 of a saved shifted base. The first byte comes from the final result’s upper half; the second comes from the saved shifted base itself. Under truncating 32-bit semantics, that base was formed by a 25-position left shift, so the second byte is zero. Both writes remain part of the represented sequence.

The function then clears a word at offset 0x14 of another saved scaled base and reads a byte at offset 5 of that base. It clears a word at offset 0x14 of an index-derived base, publishes the byte-read value as a word at offset 0x34 of the saved scaled base, and reads back the latter base’s offset-0x14 word. Another index-derived clear follows.

The index is obtained from a 32-bit call result by shifting left eight, retaining fifteen bits, and shifting right six. Under truncating semantics it ranges from 0 through 508 in multiples of four. The associated doubled base ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight. These are low-address calculations, not evidence of a bounded array.

### Early fixed-location accesses and linked updates

After duplicate byte writes at offset 0x15 of the doubled index-derived base, the function reads storage associated with absolute location 0x05. **That read precedes the absolute byte write at 0x01.** The byte written at 0x01 is the low byte of the doubled index-derived base.

The value obtained from 0x05 is subsequently used as a base. A byte is written at its offset 0x15; later, its offset-0x54 word is read and saved before being overwritten. The access width of the fixed-location read at 0x05 is not sufficiently established to define a complete storage layout.

For the remaining description, **saved link** means that saved 32-bit offset-0x54 word. It is not assumed to remain equal to the current contents of its source location.

Further linked updates include:

- Word loads at offset 0x74 whose results become new bases.
- Word stores at offsets 0x54 and 0x44 through loaded bases.
- Duplicate self-reference stores at offset 0x24 of the saved link.
- Repeated byte writes at offset 9 of another loaded base.
- Successive word stores at offset 0x14 of the doubled index-derived base.

One nested update reads the same offset-0x74 word twice: one read supplies a destination base, and another supplies the word being stored. The representation establishes two reads, but not their relative evaluation order within that expression. They must not be specified as one cached read.

Within these updates, the absolute **32-bit store at 0x54 receives twice the saved link**. It occurs after an offset-0x14 store of a call-derived scaled value and before further offset-0x14 stores of the saved link.

Later, after two byte writes at derived offset 0x15, the absolute **byte store at 0x15 receives the low byte of the doubled index-derived base**. It does not receive the low byte of the saved link’s fourfold value used by those neighboring derived writes.

These fixed-location writes may affect later derived reads through aliasing.

### Late fixed reads and the byte at 0x67
