# F-5541ba0019da

## Purpose

F-5541ba0019da performs a long sequence of indirect invocations, transforms their results and inherited execution state, and then performs ordered memory updates of several widths. Its final represented action is an unresolved indirect transfer with four arguments.

No higher-level service is established. Recurring offsets and character-like numeric constants do not establish formatting, serialization, initialization, object management, or resource cleanup.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** operations, widths, and ordering directly supported by the supplied representation.
- **[INFERENCE]:** conclusions requiring an additional execution-model assumption.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which this contract supplies no invented guarantee.

The contract is conditional on the static representation accurately describing executable behavior. Ordinary public-call compatibility is not established.

## Interface

### Entry, effective inputs, and result

The represented interface has no explicit parameters and no explicit return value. It nevertheless consumes effective inputs from:

- Inherited control state used to derive the first indirect target.
- Two inherited 32-bit values used in early shifted arguments.
- Primary and secondary result state from indirect invocations.
- Memory reached through computed addresses and loaded address-bearing values.
- Fixed-address byte and word storage.
- A fixed-location 32-bit address-base cell.
- The unresolved final transfer target.

Some secondary results lack independently established producer contracts. Their initialization, validity, and persistence are documented_only.

A return is represented after the final transfer. Whether that target actually returns is unknown. No success flag, error code, output length, or ownership-bearing result is established.

## Behavior

### Indirect invocations and arithmetic

Indirect invocations precede the first explicit data-memory store. Those invocations may themselves have arbitrary memory effects.

The first target is derived from inherited control state plus displacement 0xFFE. Most later targets are formed from fixed code-related anchors plus that displacement. Their represented order matters; repeated shapes do not establish equivalent or interchangeable dependencies.

Call sites contain zero through four explicit arguments. These lists do not independently establish callee signatures. Common argument relationships include:

- A lower result word shifted left by eight bits.
- An unchanged upper result word.
- Shifted copies of lower or upper words.
- Explicit zero arguments.
- Masked result fields.
- Values retained across intervening invocations.

No visible branch interprets a result as success or failure.

The arithmetic distinguishes masking before shifting from shifting before masking. Fields include widths of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits, together with a single-bit extraction. Some 64-bit results are logically shifted right by 34, 36, or 44 bits before narrowing; these are not shifts of the lower word alone.

Under a truncating 32-bit model, successive left shifts can discard every original bit. Locally assigned zero values remain zero through their represented shifts until reassigned. Neither fact establishes preservation of shared state by unknown dependencies.

### Opening access relationships

The opening effects preserve these ordered relationships:

- A word store places the retained shifted value at final upper word plus 0x14.
- A byte store places the low byte of the final upper word at retained shifted value plus 9.
- A word store places the scaled base at 256 times the final lower word plus 0x54.
- The earlier offset-9 byte destination is then overwritten with the low byte of the retained shifted value.

Next, the scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared before its offset-5 byte is read. The small doubled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared before the loaded byte is zero-extended into a word store at scaled base plus 0x34.

The scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is subsequently read. The small doubled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared again, followed by two consecutive byte stores to small doubled base plus 0x15, both supplying the low byte of that word read.

These are access-order requirements, not unconditional final-value claims. Aliasing can affect the reads.

The next fixed-address byte read is at 0x05. Its zero-extended value becomes an address base. After that read, a byte store to fixed address 0x01 supplies the low byte of the small doubled base. Further stores include the small field’s low byte at the fixed-byte-derived base plus 0x15.

### Read-before-replacement and fixed-address writes

The word at the fixed-byte-derived base plus 0x54 is read before being replaced by the earlier word read from scaled base plus 0x14. The retained old field value subsequently drives multiple scaled addresses.

The middle phase also includes:

- Word values used as fresh address bases.
- Byte loads at offset 5 used as address bases.
- Word stores supplying their own address-bearing base.
- Repeated stores to the same destination.
- Byte stores overlapping word locations.

A linked self-value store uses two represented reads from small doubled base plus 0x74: one supplies an address base and another supplies the stored word. Their relative evaluation order within that expression is not established; they must not automatically be treated as one read.

The relevant fixed-address ordering is established:

1. A 32-bit store to fixed address 0x54 supplies twice the old field value.
2. Subsequent stores place the old field value into small doubled base plus 0x14, including a repeated assignment.
3. Two consecutive byte stores to small doubled base plus 0x15 supply the low byte of the four-times base.
4. An **8-bit store to fixed address 0x15** supplies the low byte of the small doubled base.

The fixed-address-0x15 store is distinct from both pairs of offset-0x15 stores. Its destination may alias other reached storage; it is not an unresolved or optional effect.

Later accesses repeatedly use offsets 0x04, 0x05, 0x09, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x14, 0x15, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. Byte stores at offset 0x15 overlap a word beginning at 0x14; byte stores at 0x11 overlap a word beginning at 0x10.

Separate offset-5 byte reads occur with intervening stores. Their values are not guaranteed equal.

### Numeric replacements and address-base acquisition

The later linked value drives three distinct numeric stores:

| Destination | Width | Supplied value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Four times the later linked value | 8 bits | 0x3A |
| Eight times that value | 16 bits | 0x003A |
| Four times that value plus 0x20 | 32 bits | 0x0000003A |

These widths do not establish a string operation.

At adjusted base plus 0x20, the word replacement sequence supplies 0x20, then 0x2F, then a zero-extended byte read from adjusted base plus 4, then 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the first two replacements and before the last replacement.

**After this replacement sequence**, the function reads the fixed-location 32-bit address-base cell, obtaining the publication base. **After that load**, it stores 32-bit numeric 0x20 to fixed address 0x60.

Thus the global-access order is:

**fixed-address-0x54 word write → fixed-address-0x15 byte write → adjusted-base replacement sequence → address-base cell load → fixed-address-0x60 word write.**

Other accesses occur between these events. In particular, the address-base load must not be moved after the fixed-address-0x60 write, and its value must not be assumed unaffected by earlier potentially aliasing writes.

### Repeated four-store groups

After the fixed-address-0x60 store, the repeated portion consists of ordered groups containing:

- A word store to an eight-times-scaled address plus 0x54, supplying four times the later linked value.
- A byte store to publication base plus 0x19, supplying the low byte of the adjusted base.
- A byte store to adjusted base plus 0x11, supplying the low byte of the publication base.
- A word store to publication base plus 0x34, supplying an eight-times-scaled value.

The first group uses eight times the old field value for both its scaled destination and offset-0x34 value. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted value.

The publication base is retained from the earlier load; no reload of its fixed-location cell is represented during these groups.

**[INFERENCE]** The pattern resembles publication of related state. No synchronization, atomic publication protocol, or concurrency guarantee is established.

Repeated writes cannot be collapsed solely because their supplied values repeat. This contract leaves their exact multiplicity documented_only rather than asserting an independently unverified count.

### Final fixed-address provenance and intervening effects

After the repeated portion, a word is read at fixed address 0xA9. A separate word is then read at four times the later linked value plus 0x74.

A byte store to fixed address 0x3A supplies the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30. This is followed by retained word reads from doubled linked base plus 0x14 and adjusted base plus 4.

Before the first write to fixed address 0x67, the function writes:

- The low byte of the adjusted base at doubled linked base plus 0x0D.
- Numeric byte 0x6C at adjusted base plus 8.
- The low byte of the publication base at adjusted base plus 0x0D and plus 9.
- The doubled linked base as a word at its own offset 0x54.

The **initial byte store to fixed address 0x67** supplies the low byte of the retained word read at 0xA9.

Three subsequent reads of fixed address 0x67 have distinct consumers and intervening accesses:

| Read | Consumer and ordering |
|---|---|
| First | Supplies the byte store to fixed address 0x21. A word read from doubled linked base plus 0x44 occurs after the initial 0x67 write and before this 0x67 read. |
| Second | Supplies a byte store to adjusted base plus 9, after the intervening effects described below. |
| Third | Separately supplies another byte store to adjusted base plus 9, after additional intervening effects described below. |

Between the first and second reads, the representation includes, in order: a byte store to adjusted base plus 0x10; a word store to publication base plus 0x54; a shifted-value byte store to doubled linked base plus 1; replacement of publication base plus 0x54 with the adjusted base; a word read at adjusted base plus 0x40; and word stores to publication base plus 0x74, adjusted base plus 0x10, and publication base plus 0x44.

The shifted-value byte store uses the signed word previously read from doubled linked base plus 0x44. Its shift count is the low eight bits of the adjusted base. Counts of 32 or greater are not excluded.

Between the second and third reads of 0x67, the function stores a byte at doubled linked base plus 9, stores the retained 0xA9 word at adjusted base plus 0x44, and reads the word at adjusted base plus 0x54.

These three 0x67 reads are observable requirements. The write to 0x21 does not directly reuse the retained 0xA9 word. Neither later offset-9 store directly reuses the earlier 0x67 read. ## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance checks, not runtime results.

### Width and extension

A byte store supplied 0x123456AB stores byte 0xAB. A loaded byte 0xE1 used in a word-sized unsigned numeric store supplies 0x000000E1, not a sign-extended value.

### Small fixed-address write

Under truncating 32-bit arithmetic, an invocation result of 1 yields small field 4 and small doubled base 8. The later two consecutive stores supplying the four-times base target address 0x1D. The following fixed-address store instead targets **0x15**, with width eight bits and supplied byte 0x08.

### Global-access ordering

A conforming trace places the address-base cell load after the final 0x20 replacement at adjusted base plus 0x20, but before the word write of 0x20 to fixed address 0x60. Earlier writes to fixed addresses 0x54 and 0x15 cannot be deferred past that load.

### Fresh reads of 0x67

Suppose the initial store supplies 0xAB to 0x67. If the intervening word store to adjusted base plus 0x44 covers 0x67 and changes its current byte to 0xCD, the third read supplies 0xCD. An earlier read may have supplied 0xAB. Reusing the original low byte for every consumer would violate the represented read provenance.

With ordinary memory and no interfering or aliasing writes affecting 0x67, all three reads may instead return 0xAB. That equality is conditional, not guaranteed.

### Replacements and failure

Observing only final word 0x20 at adjusted base plus 0x20 does not prove the intervening 0x2F store and byte read occurred.

If an early dependency never returns, the explicit store phase need not be reached. No timeout or recovery result is established.
