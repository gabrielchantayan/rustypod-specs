# F-7233b05bf6e7

## Purpose

F-7233b05bf6e7 invokes a long sequence of unresolved dependencies, transforms machine-word values exposed by those invocations, performs ordered mixed-width memory accesses, and ends with another unresolved invocation.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation shows three explicit inputs, indirect calls, 32-bit field transformations, some 64-bit represented results, byte/halfword/word writes, fixed low-address accesses, and a terminal four-argument invocation.

**Inferred intent:** the masks and shifts suggest packed-value processing. Repeated offset-based writes suggest relationships among addressable records or externally observed storage. Neither inference establishes an application-level purpose.

This contract specifies the visible interaction and memory-access behavior. A complete application-level input/output oracle remains **documented_only** because dependency contracts, execution semantics, and the memory map are unavailable.

## Behavior

### Execution and arithmetic model

The outer representation is straight-line: a dependency-processing region, a direct-memory region, and terminal invocation. No explicit loop or conditional branch appears. Dependencies may nevertheless branch, fail, diverge, or mutate memory.

The following width rules apply to the represented operations:

- A byte is 8 bits, a halfword 16 bits, and a word 32 bits.
- Byte values explicitly used as unsigned integers are zero-extended.
- Byte destinations retain the low eight bits of their supplied values.
- Represented 64-bit results have distinct low and high 32-bit halves.
- Right shifts of an explicitly unsigned quantity are logical.
- Whole-result right shifts by 34, 36, or 44 operate on 64 bits, not on a 32-bit half.

Signed-looking shifts, multiplication, subtraction, and address arithmetic do not by themselves establish portable overflow semantics. Numerical simplifications below are conditional on a 32-bit machine-word model retaining low 32 bits. Actual overflow, signed-right-shift, and address-wrap behavior remain **documented_only**.

Access ordering below describes the supplied representation. It does not establish hardware barriers, volatile semantics, or cross-thread visibility.

### Dependency-mediated processing

F-U001 receives the first three input bit patterns unchanged, followed by the third input shifted left by five.

Later F-U002 invocation sites consume combinations of primary results, high result words, saved earlier values, secondary outputs, and literal zeros. Common transformations include left shifts by eight; separate forwarding of low and high result halves; masking a low result to 24 bits before shifting; and masking high result words to 22 bits before shifting.

Other selections include fields of 1, 11 through 22, or 24 bits; movement of a selected bit to position 16; and sequential shifts that can discard all information under the conditional word model. For example, a word shifted left by 28 and subsequently left by another 28 becomes zero under that model.

These simplifications do not authorize eliminating intervening dependency invocations. Displayed absence of arguments or an unused primary result does not establish absence of effects or hidden inputs.

Saved result-derived values remain available across later invocations in the representation. This is not evidence that memory or globals remain unchanged across those invocations.

### Initial direct-memory effects

The following descriptive labels identify captured values, not recovered objects:

- **Final pair:** the last represented 64-bit result before direct mutation.
- **Tagged value:** an earlier primary result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** another primary result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Small index:** the field obtained after shifting an earlier result left by eight, retaining its low 15 bits, then shifting right by six.
- **Doubled index base:** twice the small index.
- **Saved shifted word:** another retained primary result shifted left by eight.

Under the conditional word model, the small index is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. The doubled index base is consequently a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016.

The initial access order is significant. A word containing the tagged value is written at high-final-word plus 0x14. The byte at tagged-value plus 9 first receives the low byte of the high final word. Between that write and a second write to the same byte, the scaled base is stored at low-final-word times 256 plus 0x54. The second byte write supplies the low byte of the tagged value.

Next, scaled-base plus 0x14 receives a zero word, and a byte is read at scaled-base plus 5. A zero word is written at doubled-index-base plus 0x14 before the captured byte is zero-extended into a word at scaled-base plus 0x34. A fresh word read then captures scaled-base plus 0x14. Another zero word write follows at doubled-index-base plus 0x14.

That fresh read is not replaceable by an assumed zero without proving non-overlap. Intervening stores may overlap its location.

The low byte of the freshly read word is then written twice consecutively at doubled-index-base plus 0x15. Both represented writes are part of the access sequence.

### Fixed-location-5 capture and reuse

After those consecutive byte writes, the byte at fixed address 5 is read and zero-extended into a captured address base. This capture occurs **before** the byte write at fixed address 1, which supplies the low byte of the doubled index base.

The captured address-5 value is reused; the representation does not refresh it after the address-1 write or subsequent writes.

Its dependencies and ordering are:

- The byte at captured-base plus 0x15 receives the low byte of the small index.
- The scaled-base-relative byte at 0x11 receives the low byte of the saved shifted word.
- A word at the previously captured scaled-base-relative word plus 0x54 receives the saved shifted word.
- The scaled-base-relative byte at 0x11 is overwritten with zero.
- Only then is a word read at captured-base plus 0x54.
- That same word location is immediately overwritten with the earlier captured scaled-base-relative word.

The word read before this overwrite is retained for later address calculations and self-relative stores.

Two different preservation rules therefore apply: the address base is the earlier byte captured from fixed address 5, while the word at its offset 0x54 is read at the later position just specified. Even if an intervening store changes fixed address 5, subsequent accesses still use the original captured base. Conversely, intervening changes to captured-base plus 0x54 affect the later word read.

### Four-write sequence and global-base capture

For the following region, define:

- **Retained link:** the word read from captured-address-5-base plus 0x54.
- **Adjusted quantity:** four times the retained link, minus 150.
- **Working base:** four times the adjusted quantity.
- **Publication value:** four times the later word obtained through a byte-based offset-0x74 read.

The word at working-base plus 0x20 receives, in order:

1. 0x20.
2. 0x2f.
3. A zero-extended byte freshly read from working-base plus 4.
4. 0x20 again.

After all four word writes, the unnamed global word is read and captured as the **global base**. The next represented memory effect is the fixed-address word write of 0x20 at address 0x60.

This placement is part of the contract. The global base is neither captured before the four-write sequence nor refreshed after the address-0x60 write. If that fixed write overlaps the global word’s storage, it may change storage without changing the captured base used below.

### Repeated-write region

The captured global base supplies the same base throughout this region. Its global storage is not reread between repetitions.

Each publication group has this ordered relationship:

- A scaled destination at offset 0x54 receives the publication value.
- Global-base plus 0x19 receives the low byte of the working base.

### Finalization and independent address-0x67 reads

After publication, finalization reads a word at fixed address 0xa9 and a word at publication-value plus 0x74. The byte at fixed address 0x3a receives the narrowed value of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30 under the conditional word model.

Define **secondary base** as twice the loaded word used to form the publication value. A word from secondary-base plus 0x14 is saved for eventual terminal forwarding. A word from working-base plus 4 is also captured.

Finalization writes bytes at secondary-base plus 0x0d and working-base plus 8, 0x0d, and 9; the offset-8 byte receives 0x6c. Secondary-base plus 0x54 receives the secondary base itself.

The fixed-address byte at 0x67 then receives the low byte of the previously read fixed-address-0xa9 word.

The subsequent address-0x67 reads are separate observations:

**First read, for fixed address 0x21.** After the address-0x67 write, a word is read at secondary-base plus 0x44. Only afterward is the current byte at address 0x67 read and copied to fixed address 0x21. The copy is a read of current storage, not reuse of the byte originally supplied to address 0x67.

**Second read, for working-base plus 9.** Before this read, the representation performs byte and word stores through the working, global, and secondary bases. These include a byte at working-base plus 0x10; two word writes at global-base plus 0x54 with an intervening shifted-byte store through secondary-base plus 1; a word read at working-base plus 0x40; and word writes at global-base plus 0x74, working-base plus 0x10, and global-base plus 0x44. The current byte at fixed address 0x67 is then independently read for a byte write at working-base plus 9.

**Third read, for another working-base-plus-9 write.** Between the second and third reads, secondary-base plus 9 receives the low byte of the working base, working-base plus 0x44 receives the saved fixed-address-0xa9 word, and a word is read at working-base plus 0x54. Address 0x67 is then read again for another byte write at working-base plus 9.

Any intervening access capable of changing address 0x67 through aliasing or environmental behavior can therefore affect a later read independently. These reads must not be replaced by reuse of the initially published byte or by reuse of one another.

Remaining finalization includes additional byte and word stores through loaded addresses, a halfword write of 0x65 at twice a freshly loaded base, and a word write of 100 at another derived base plus 0x54.

A signed-looking right shift supplies a byte written through secondary-base plus 1. Its count is the low eight bits of the working base. Counts of 32 or greater have no established target-independent result.

F-U003 receives four displayed arguments: literal 0x72; a recently loaded word from offset 0x64; the earlier saved secondary-base-relative offset-0x14 word; and a recently loaded word from offset 0x74. No cleanup or error handling is represented afterward.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Initial forwarding:** with third input 3 and ordinary word-shift behavior, F-U001’s fourth displayed argument is 96. The first three retain their input bit patterns.

2. **Word truncation:** with third-input bit pattern 0x08000000, its shifted form is zero under low-32-bit truncation, not saturation or a wider product.

3. **Index boundary:** an extraction source of 0x7f produces small index 508 and doubled base 1016. A maximum of 1022 would ignore cleared bits introduced by the preceding shift.

4. **Unsigned byte:** a captured byte of 0xff written as an unsigned word supplies 255, not −1.

5. **Address-5 retention:** if fixed address 5 yields 0x20, subsequent captured-base accesses use 0x35 and 0x74 for offsets 0x15 and 0x54. If an intervening operation changes address 5 to 0x40, that does not redirect those accesses. The word at 0x74 is read before being overwritten.

6. **Global capture:** if the post-four-write global read yields base \(G\), a following address-0x60 write overlapping its storage does not redirect publication away from \(G\).

7. **Fresh address-0x67 observations:** if the three described reads yield 0x11, 0x22, and 0x33 respectively, address 0x21 receives 0x11, and the two later working-base-plus-9 writes supply 0x22 and 0x33. No equality among those observations is required.

8. **Intermediate states:** where writes are observable, working-base plus 0x20 receives 0x20, 0x2f, the freshly read unsigned byte, and 0x20. One final store is insufficient.

9. **Partial failure:** if an early direct word write succeeds and the next byte write faults, no rollback is established, and later effects need not occur.
