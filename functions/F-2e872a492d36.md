### Widths and arithmetic

Explicit memory accesses are either 8-bit bytes or 32-bit words. Certain dependency results are represented as 64-bit quantities and separated into low and high 32-bit halves.

Numerical deductions use a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector model**:

- Addition, multiplication, and left shifts retain the low 32 bits.
- Unsigned right shifts insert zeros.
- Byte loads are zero-extended.
- Byte stores retain the low eight bits.
- Address calculations wrap to 32 bits under this model.

The **ten-bit extraction** of a word means masking it with 0x3FFFFFFF and shifting the result right by 20. The extraction ranges from 0 to 1023. Storing it as a byte performs a further truncation to eight bits.

For example, the extraction of **0x3AB00000** is **0x3AB**, whose byte truncation is **0xAB**. The extraction of **0x000003AB** is zero.

This model does not assert portable behavior for signed-language overflow or shifts. Actual address interpretation, endianness, alignment requirements, and the calling-convention meaning of represented wide results remain documented_only.
