### Entry into direct memory access

The final dependency result before direct memory operations is represented as two 32-bit halves. Earlier results supply several address-like quantities:

- **Shifted base:** a dependency result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** a separate represented signed result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Small index:** a result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, then shifted logically right by six.
- **Saved shifted value:** another earlier result shifted left by eight.

These are descriptive labels, not established object identities.

Under truncating 32-bit arithmetic, the small index ranges from 0 through 508 in multiples of four. Twice that index ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight. Neither range establishes valid storage.

The opening writes are ordered: a word at the high final-result half plus 0x14 receives the shifted base; a byte at shifted base plus 9 receives the low byte of that high half; and a word at 256 times the low final-result half plus 0x54 receives the scaled base. The byte at shifted base plus 9 is then overwritten with the low byte of the shifted base.

Next, the function clears the word at scaled base plus 0x14, reads an explicitly unsigned byte at scaled base plus 5, clears the word at twice the small index plus 0x14, and stores the widened byte at scaled base plus 0x34. It subsequently reads the word at scaled base plus 0x14 and repeats the small-index-derived clearing before further byte writes.

No address validation intervenes. These destinations may overlap. In particular, intervening stores can affect the word read after the initial clear.
