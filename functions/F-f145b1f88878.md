### Arithmetic widths

Scalar arithmetic and address-forming quantities are predominantly represented at 32 bits. Under the fixed-width bit-pattern interpretation used here, left shifts and multiplication retain the low 32 result bits. This is not a claim that signed overflow or overflowing address arithmetic has portable language-level semantics.

Paired results are represented as 64 bits and consumed through low and high 32-bit words. An unsigned 64-bit right shift by 34, 36, or 44 extracts the high word shifted logically right by two, four, or twelve, respectively. It does not shift the low word.

Observed operations include:

### Entry into direct-memory effects

For describing the memory phase, the following are semantic labels, not object definitions:

- **Scaled base:** a separate scalar result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Small value:** another result shifted left eight bits, masked with 0x7FFF, then logically shifted right six.

- **Saved field value:** the word later loaded at a zero-extended fixed-address byte plus 0x54.

The small value is a multiple of four from 0 through 508 under the fixed-width interpretation. The mask alone permits 511, but the preceding eight-bit left shift removes the low eight bits.

The final paired result before direct stores supplies two additional words. The opening access order is:

1. Store the shifted-base quantity as a word at the paired high word plus 0x14.
2. Store the high word’s low byte at shifted base +9.
3. Store the scaled-base quantity at the paired low word multiplied by 256, plus 0x54.
4. Overwrite shifted base +9 with the shifted-base quantity’s low byte.
5. Clear scaled base +0x14, then read the unsigned byte at scaled base +5.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static checks, not executed tests.

1. **Input forwarding.** With input 0xDEADBEEF and a valid first target, the first call receives 0xDEADBEEF unchanged.

2. **Paired result.** An early paired result with low word 0x12345678 and high word 0x9ABCDEF0 produces immediate consumer arguments 0x34567800, 0x9ABCDEF0, and zero.

3. **Extraction boundary.** A twenty-bit-mask extraction applied to 0x000FFC00 produces 1023. Left shifts by six and five produce 65472 and 32736, respectively.

4. **Small-value restriction.** The final small value cannot be 511: the preceding left shift restricts it to multiples of four, with maximum 508.

5. **Fixed 0x54 aliasing.** If fixed address 0x54 overlaps small base +0x14, its word store occurs between the shift-derived replacement and the saved-field replacement. Omitting it loses a represented intermediate effect.

6. **Fixed 0x15 ordering.** An observer sees both derived small-base +0x15 stores before the fixed-address 0x15 store. The fixed store is not interchangeable with either derived store.

7. **Global capture.** If the execution-location-associated global overlaps fixed 0x60, its read captures the value before the 0x20 word store, subject to the target’s access semantics.

8. **Final saved-word aliasing.** If the byte destination 0x3A overlaps either subsequently read saved word, those reads occur after that byte write. Treating them as snapshots taken before the write is incompatible with the represented order.

9. **0x67 is not cached.** Suppose the saved 0xA9 low byte is 0x12. If a reached derived write changes 0x67 to 0x34 before the second reload, that reload supplies 0x34 to working base +9. A later change to 0x56 before the third reload supplies 0x56 to the later store.

10. **Partial effects.** If a later address faults, restoration of earlier stores is not required by the evidence. If execution reaches the terminal transfer, its first argument is 0x72, not the preceding stored constant 100.
