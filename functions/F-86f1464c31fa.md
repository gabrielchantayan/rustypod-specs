# F-86f1464c31fa

## Purpose

F-86f1464c31fa performs an ordered series of mixed-width memory updates, captures additional values from memory, and supplies four arguments to an unresolved indirect control transfer. The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose.

The contract describes address relationships, access widths, captured values, overlapping writes, and represented access order. It does not classify the affected regions as objects, arrays, lists, strings, or owned allocations.

Three evidence categories apply:

- **Established static evidence:** behavior explicitly represented in the supplied material.
- **Inferred intent:** possible interpretations, not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior requiring additional evidence.

All described progression is conditional on earlier accesses completing without interruption. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Widths, layouts, and captured values

A byte occupies 8 bits, a halfword 16 bits, and a word 32 bits. Offsets are byte offsets. Byte stores retain the low eight bits. The linked-object byte reads and the byte read used in the transient word sequence are explicitly expanded to unsigned word values.

The following descriptive roles remain stable throughout this contract:

- **Original record:** the third input’s initial address.
- **Linked base:** the word captured from original-record offset 0x04.
- **Index word:** the word read at an address selected by the second linked-object byte capture.
- **Doubled index:** twice the index word.
- **Quadrupled index:** four times the index word.
- **Adjusted record value:** the original third input minus 0x96.
- **Quadrupled base:** four times the adjusted record value.

These names identify captured or computed values, not live references to their source storage. Later changes to that storage do not implicitly refresh a capture.

The representation uses 32-bit quantities, including signed-looking subtraction and multiplication. It does not independently establish machine-level overflow or address-wrap semantics. The quadrupled base is subsequently treated as unsigned, but that does not establish that every preceding arithmetic operation has unsigned modular semantics.

No nonaliasing guarantee is supplied. In particular:

- Original-record offset 0x15 lies inside the word at offset 0x14.
- Quadrupled-base offset 0x11 lies inside the word at offset 0x10.
- Independently named bases may identify overlapping storage.
- Fixed addresses may overlap derived destinations.

### Initial record and linked-region updates

The first store places the primary base’s low byte at original-record offset 0x15. The primary-base region then receives the secondary base’s low byte at offset 0x0D, followed by the full primary base at offset 0x14.

The link value is stored at original-record offset 0x54 before the word read at primary-base offset 0x64. The address captured by that read receives the secondary base’s low byte at offset 0x0D and its own full address value at offset 0x14.

Only afterward is the linked base captured from original-record offset 0x04. Earlier aliased stores can therefore affect this capture.

The tertiary-base region receives the secondary base’s low byte at offset 0x09 and the fourth input as a word at offset 0x44. The first byte capture from linked-base offset 0x05 follows. Before that capture is published as a zero-extended word at secondary-base offset 0x44, the first-input region receives **the tertiary base’s low byte** at offset 0x09.

The original record next receives the fourth input at offset 0x14. The tertiary-base region then receives the linked base’s low byte at offset 0x15 and the original record address as a word at offset 0x44, replacing its earlier payload there.

A second, independent byte read occurs at linked-base offset 0x05. After this capture, the fourth input’s low byte is stored at original-record offset 0x15. The second captured byte, interpreted unsigned, selects the index-word read address by addition of 0x74. Its possible starting addresses are therefore 0x74 through 0x173 inclusive; the read occupies four bytes. No intervening pointer-table lookup is represented.

The remaining early publications occur in this order: the linked base’s low byte at original-record offset 0x15; the link value at original-record offset 0x54; the doubled index’s low byte at first-input offset 0x09; and the complete fourth input at original-record offset 0x14.

That final word store replaces preceding byte modifications within offsets 0x14–0x17. It does not make the earlier accesses unobservable.

The shared base is then captured from a global word. A word value of 0x20 is subsequently stored at fixed address 0x60.

### Late captures and fixed-address publication

After the repeated phase, the represented memory-access order is:

- Capture a word from fixed address 0xA9.
- Capture a word from quadrupled-index offset 0x74.
- Store a byte at fixed address 0x3A.

The fixed-address 0x3A payload is the byte-sized result obtained by scaling the adjusted record value’s low-byte form by eight and adding 0x30. Its retained low eight bits are equivalent to the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted record value plus 0x30.

The doubled-index offset-0x0D byte store occurs **before** the next three quadrupled-base stores. Its payload is the quadrupled base’s low byte. The quadrupled-base region then receives 0x6C at offset 0x08, followed by the shared base’s low byte at offsets 0x0D and 0x09, in that order.

The doubled index is published as a word at its own offset 0x54. Next, the low byte of the fixed-address 0xA9 capture is stored at fixed address 0x67.

### Shift-dependent update and ordered republication

A word is captured from doubled-index offset 0x44. Immediately afterward in the represented memory sequence, fixed address 0x67 is read as a byte and that byte is stored at fixed address 0x21.

Only after the 0x21 store do the following updates occur:

- The doubled-index offset-0x44 capture’s low byte is stored at quadrupled-base offset 0x10.
- Its full word is stored at shared-base offset 0x54.
- A right-shifted byte derived from that capture is stored at doubled-index offset 0x01.
- The quadrupled base is stored as a word at shared-base offset 0x54, replacing the preceding word there.

The shift’s operand is the captured 32-bit doubled-index offset-0x44 value, represented as signed. Its count is the low eight bits of the quadrupled base. **The shift direction is right.** Negative-operand behavior, sign filling, and counts of 32 or greater remain **documented_only**; no count masking or saturation is assumed.

Next, a word is captured from quadrupled-base offset 0x40. Before that capture is stored at quadrupled-base offset 0x10, the earlier quadrupled-base offset-0x04 capture is published at shared-base offset 0x74. The offset-0x40 capture is then stored at quadrupled-base offset 0x10, covering offsets 0x10–0x13, including the earlier byte publication at offset 0x11.

The doubled index is subsequently stored at shared-base offset 0x44.

Two further reads of fixed address 0x67 must remain distinct:

- The first read supplies a byte store at quadrupled-base offset 0x09. This occurs before the quadrupled base’s low byte is stored at doubled-index offset 0x09 and before the fixed-address 0xA9 capture is stored as a word at quadrupled-base offset 0x44.
- A word is then captured from quadrupled-base offset 0x54 as the **terminal base**. Only after that capture does the second 0x67 read supply another byte store at quadrupled-base offset 0x09.

Thus the terminal-base capture separates those two fixed-address reads. Neither read may be replaced by an assumed retained value from the earlier 0x67 store.

After the second fixed-address byte publication, the earlier quadrupled-index offset-0x74 capture’s low byte is stored at terminal-base offset 0x0D. The earlier doubled-index offset-0x14 capture then replaces the word at doubled-index offset 0x54.

**After this offset-0x64 capture, and before the next word store,** the retained low byte from quadrupled-base offset 0x14 is stored at terminal-base offset 0x11. The terminal-base region then receives word value 100 at offset 0x54.

The final-linked-base offset-0x74 word is captured next. A word read from doubled-index offset 0x64 supplies a **destination base**. The remaining stores publish, in order:

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests. Unless stated otherwise, relevant addresses are valid, arithmetic is representable, and unrelated regions do not alias.

### Initial byte payload

If the tertiary base’s low byte is 0xB7, the first store at first-input offset 0x09 writes 0xB7. A later store at that same offset uses the doubled index’s low byte instead.

### Independent byte captures

If the first linked-base offset-0x05 read yields 0xFE, secondary-base offset 0x44 receives word value 0x000000FE. If the second read instead yields 0x02, the index-word read starts at address 0x76. Reusing the first byte for both roles is incorrect.

### Overlapping record writes

If the fourth input is 0x11223344, the last early word store at original-record offset 0x14 writes that complete value. Earlier byte writes at offset 0x15 are not independent surviving fields. Their physical byte position within the word depends on endianness.

### Scaled destinations

For index word 3, the three 0x3A stores start at addresses 12, 24, and 44, with widths 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively.

### Right-shifted byte

If the doubled-index offset-0x44 capture is 0x00001234 and the quadrupled base’s low byte is 4, the shifted byte store writes 0x23 at doubled-index offset 0x01. This example does not resolve negative operands or excessive shift counts.

### Alias-sensitive capture ordering

If shared-base offset 0x74 aliases quadrupled-base offset 0x40, the latter word is captured before the shared-region publication changes that storage. The word subsequently stored at quadrupled-base offset 0x10 uses the earlier capture.

Similarly, if terminal-base offset 0x11 overlaps the final-linked-base offset-0x64 word, the offset-0x64 capture precedes that byte update. Moving the terminal byte update earlier could change a transfer argument.

If an early access faults, later stores and the indirect transfer are not promised. No recovery result is specified.
