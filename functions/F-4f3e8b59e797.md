### Initial direct memory mutation

The following descriptive names identify numeric values, not object types:

- **Saved shifted word:** a preparatory result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** another preparatory result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Small base:** a preparatory result shifted left by 8, masked with 0x7FFF, logically shifted right by 6, and doubled.
- **Preparatory halves:** the low and high words of the final preparatory 64-bit result.

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, the scaled base is a multiple of \(2^{25}\). The small base is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016 inclusive.

The initial access order is:

1. A word store of the saved shifted word at preparatory-high plus 0x14.
2. A byte store of preparatory-high at saved-shifted-word plus 9.
3. A word store of the scaled base at preparatory-low multiplied by 256, plus 0x54.
4. A byte overwrite at saved-shifted-word plus 9 using the saved shifted word.

Next, scaled-base plus 0x14 receives a zero word. The byte at scaled-base plus 5 is captured. Small-base plus 0x14 receives a zero word, and the captured byte is zero-extended into the word at scaled-base plus 0x34.

The function then distinctly reads the word at scaled-base plus 0x14, clears small-base plus 0x14 again, and performs two separate byte stores of the read-back word at small-base plus 0x15.

**The represented intervening stores do not establish a nonzero read-back through ordinary memory aliasing.** Both bases are multiples of eight under the bit-vector interpretation. Their offset-0x14 word locations therefore either coincide, in which case the intervening clear writes zero again, or do not overlap. The store at scaled-base plus 0x34 is 0x20 bytes away from the cleared word.

Consequently, ordinary stable memory yields zero at this read-back. The read remains a distinct represented access: external mutation or device-memory behavior could make its observation significant, but neither is established.
