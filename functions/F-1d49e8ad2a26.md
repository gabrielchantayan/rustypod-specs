# F-1d49e8ad2a26

## Purpose

F-1d49e8ad2a26 describes a visible execution prefix that interleaves indirect dependency calls with byte reads, byte writes, and 32-bit writes. The writes first clear selected locations and later store scaled values or selected bits of dependency results.

The static evidence does not establish an application-level purpose. Initialization, device control, object construction, and serialization are possible interpretations, not contractual behavior. Small numeric addresses and destinations derived from byte values must not be reinterpreted as ordinary object-relative addresses.

This is a **documented_only, conditional contract**. The supplied representation warns of invalid instruction data and terminates at an invalid-instruction boundary. It establishes neither a complete executable function nor successful return.

For the descriptions below:

## Behavior

### Sequencing and dependency boundaries

The following stages describe the order of visible calls and memory accesses, conditional on all earlier actions completing and all dependencies returning.

A **four-call group** means four successive indirect calls, each with no explicit argument expression shown. Where the final result is used, it is the result of the fourth call. This notation does not imply that the four calls share a target.

A call with no explicit arguments shown is not proven to have an argument-free machine interface.

- Repeated reads remain separate observations.
- A prior write is not guaranteed to remain intact across a call.

### 1. Opening calls and hidden-state byte write

The first dependency target is the incoming return-link value plus 4,036. That call receives the second input.

A no-explicit-argument call then produces a 32-bit quantity, which is passed to another dependency. A four-call group follows, with its final result retained.

The procedure reads an unsigned byte at the hidden incoming address plus 1. It writes one byte to the numeric address equal to the observed byte plus 17. The payload is the low eight bits of the hidden unsigned input shifted right by 22.

Thus, the byte-derived destination lies between 17 and 272 inclusive. It is not an offset from the original read address.

The retained dependency result shifted left by two is passed to another dependency. A further four-call group follows; none of that group’s ordinary results is visibly used.

### 2. Input-derived byte clears

The procedure writes a zero byte at fourth input plus 15.

It forms P, reads an unsigned byte at P plus 1, and writes a zero byte at the observed byte’s numeric value plus 17.

A dependency then receives four arguments in this order:

1. First input shifted left by 21.
2. The same shifted value.
3. The same shifted value.
4. First input shifted left by 23.

No validation precedes these signed-looking shifts.

A no-explicit-argument call produces a quantity that is forwarded to another dependency. After a four-call group, the final result is retained.

The procedure reads P plus 1 again, retains this newly observed byte, and clears the byte at that byte’s numeric value plus 17. This read is distinct from the earlier read at P plus 1.

The retained call result shifted left by two is passed to another dependency. After another four-call group, the procedure performs two 32-bit writes in order:

- Zero at the retained byte plus 68.
- Zero at absolute numeric address 36.

The first word destination uses the previously captured byte, not a fresh read after the calls.

A dependency then receives zero, 152, P, and 152, in that order.

### 3. Return-derived clear and split-result clear

A four-call group then yields a result used for:

- A zero 32-bit write at four times that result plus 68.
- A zero 32-bit write at address 36.

The subsequent four-call group ends with the result represented as a 64-bit quantity.

The low 32-bit half of that quantity, shifted left by two, is passed to a dependency. After that call, the procedure clears:

- A 32-bit word at twice the retained high 32-bit half plus 68.

A dependency next receives zero, the additional register result associated with the preceding call, and that additional result shifted left by two. This register result must not be equated with either half of the earlier 64-bit quantity.

### 4. Masked-input processing and further clears

A four-call group supplies the result for another pair of 32-bit clears:

- Zero at address 36.

### 5. Scaled word construction

A four-call group yields a result used to clear a 32-bit word at four times the result plus 20. A dependency receives four times that result.

The next four-call group produces a result used in two distinct roles:

The payload is not recomputed from the newer result.

A dependency receives four times the newer result. A further four-call group produces another result. The procedure writes four times this latest result at sixteen times the same result plus 20.

A dependency then receives two arguments: four times the latest result and sixteen times that result.

### 6. Masked-input word installation

After a four-call group, the procedure writes M multiplied by 128 as a 32-bit word at four times the group’s final result plus 20.

### 7. Two byte writes using Q

A dependency receives that unsigned result shifted left by two.

A no-explicit-argument call then produces a quantity forwarded to another dependency. A four-call group follows, with its final result retained separately.

### 8. Final two byte writes and terminal boundary

A four-call group produces a new unsigned 32-bit result.

The procedure reads and retains a byte at Q plus 1. It writes at the retained byte plus 17, with a payload equal to four times the low byte of the new unsigned result, truncated to eight bits.

A dependency receives the retained byte zero-extended to 32 bits. This argument is the observed byte, not the unsigned result used for the payload.

A no-explicit-argument call produces a quantity forwarded to another dependency. A four-call group follows, with its final result retained.

The last visible byte read is at eight times the new unsigned result plus 1—not at Q plus 1. The procedure writes at this observed byte plus 17. Its payload is the low eight bits of that same unsigned result masked to 30 bits and shifted right by 20.

A dependency receives the latest retained call result shifted left by two. Four further no-explicit-argument calls follow, after which the representation reaches the invalid-instruction boundary.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static review cases, not observed executions.

1. **Numeric byte-derived destination.** If a byte observation is 5, its associated byte write targets address 22. If it is the retained byte used for the first word clear, that word clear targets address 73.

2. **Captured value versus reread.** If the second P-based read yields 7 and a later dependency changes that source byte to 9, the corresponding word clear still targets address 75, conditional on the represented retention.

3. **Distinct Q-based reads.** If the first two Q-based reads yield 2 and 8, their byte writes target addresses 19 and 25. Coalescing the reads would change the contract.

4. **Masking is selective.** For fourth input 4,194,305, M is 1. The masked quantities include 128 and 16, and the final word payload is 128. This input is not globally equivalent to fourth input 1: the direct fourth-input-plus-15 address differs.

5. **Maximum masked quantities.** For M equal to 4,194,303, the seven-bit-shifted quantity is 536,870,784 and the four-bit-shifted quantity is 67,108,848. Neither requires unsigned 32-bit overflow.

6. **Byte truncation.** An unsigned result of 65 gives a left-shift-derived byte payload of 4: multiplying by four produces 260, whose low eight bits are 4.

7. **Split-result asymmetry.** With low half 3 and high half 5, the split-result stage passes 12 to its first dependency and clears a word at address 78.

8. **Retained payload.** If the payload-producing result is 3 and the next destination-producing result is 9, the writes store 12 at address 68 and then the same 12 at address 56.

9. **Final read source differs.** If the newer unsigned result is 65, the final byte-read address is 521. The preceding byte read still uses Q plus 1.

10. **Prefix completion only.** Completing all listed accesses and calls does not establish successful return or any invented return value.
