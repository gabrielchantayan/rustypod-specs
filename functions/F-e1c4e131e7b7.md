# F-e1c4e131e7b7

## Purpose

F-e1c4e131e7b7 transforms three consecutive 32-bit words in place after invoking two dependencies. The first dependency receives the storage address twice. The second supplies a signed 32-bit factor that is acquired once and used for all three replacements.

For a component whose value remains stable during its evaluation, the conditional numerical effect is to retain bits 16 through 47 of the signed 64-bit product of that component and the factor. Bit positions are numbered from zero at the least significant end.

This numerical description is conditional. The represented operations include unsigned-to-signed conversions and a signed left shift. Their permitted behavior depends on values and execution semantics; the contract does not assign an executable result where those operations lack defined behavior.

**Inferred intent:** scaling a three-component quantity by a fixed-point factor with 16 fractional bits. The evidence does not establish component names, units, supported application ranges, or the purpose of the first dependency.

\[
\left\lfloor
\frac{\text{factor}\times\text{signed component}}{2^{16}}
\right\rfloor
\bmod 2^{32}.

The modulo denotes the resulting unsigned 32-bit pattern. It is not a saturation operation or a requirement that the scaled mathematical value fit within signed 32-bit range.

Equivalently, the result retains product bits 16 through 47. For negative products, this interpretation assumes conventional two’s-complement representation and permitted execution of the signed operations.

## Uncertainties

### documented_only: signed arithmetic semantics

The unsigned 32-bit component-to-signed conversion is unresolved for patterns outside the signed destination range.

The upper product contribution also involves converting an unsigned upper portion to a signed 32-bit value before a signed left shift. Conventional interpretation of out-of-range conversions cannot be assumed independently of the execution environment.

Under signed-shift rules requiring a nonnegative operand and a signed-representable shifted result:

- Negative products yield a negative signed upper portion under conventional interpretation, making that left shift impermissible.
- Large positive products can exceed the signed shift’s representability limit even though multiplication itself is exact.
- For a stable component, a nonnegative product strictly below \(2^{47}\) avoids that particular shift limitation.

The last condition is not a complete portability or validity guarantee. Storage validity, component conversion, and actual execution semantics still matter.
