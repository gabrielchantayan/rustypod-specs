# F-351b1810c016

## Behavior

### Indirect-invocation phase

Indirect invocations precede the first explicit data-memory mutation. The first target is formed from incoming control-link state plus displacement 0xFFE. Subsequent targets are formed from embedded control locations using that displacement.

The represented invocation sites occur in a straight-line sequence, without a visible conditional branch or loop. Assuming normal continuation, returned values affect arguments and later addresses, not whether a represented invocation site is reached.

The early invocations include a combined-result producer followed by an invocation receiving:

- The incoming word masked with 0xFFC00000.
- The combined result’s high 32-bit portion.
- Its low portion shifted left by eight.
- Its low portion shifted left by eleven.

Later invocations repeatedly consume shifted primary results, high portions of combined results, saved earlier results, secondary outputs, and explicit zeros. Some invocations have no represented arguments; that does not establish an absence of machine-level inputs.

Visible transformations include fixed left shifts, unsigned right shifts, low-bit masks, and extraction of bits 10–19 by masking to twenty bits and shifting right by ten. Unsigned shifts of combined results by 34, 36, and 44 bits extract the high portion shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 respectively. They are not oversized shifts of a 32-bit operand.

Values explicitly set to zero and subsequently only shifted or masked remain zero in the represented dataflow. This does not imply that intervening dependencies are effect-free. An invocation whose result is unused still contributes an observable event and may affect subsequent state.

The full site-by-site invocation and argument catalogue is not enumerated here; that detailed trace remains **documented_only**.

### Arithmetic boundaries

Unsigned 32-bit left shifts discard bits beyond the word width; unsigned right shifts introduce zeros. Intermediate widths matter: a shift followed by another shift or mask cannot be replaced indiscriminately by unbounded mathematical multiplication.

Signed arithmetic requires separate treatment:

- Signed addition and multiplication have exact mathematical expectations only where their intermediate results are representable.
- A signed left shift is not justified by representability alone. A portable arithmetic interpretation additionally requires a nonnegative left operand, a shift count within the operand width, and an appropriately representable shifted result.
- Negative signed left operands remain unresolved even where the mathematical product is representable.
- Right shifts of negative signed values must not be silently treated as logical shifts.

Where those restrictions are not established, the result is **documented_only**, dependent on unavailable target-instruction semantics. Modulo-\(2^{32}\) behavior must not be assumed for signed overflow.

These restrictions apply to address calculations as well as invocation arguments.

### Opening memory mutations

The memory phase uses several saved invocation-derived quantities: a result multiplied by \(2^{25}\), a doubled extraction from a shifted result, a saved value shifted left by 25, and the high and scaled low portions of the final combined result.

Its opening ordering is significant:

- A word at offset 0x14 from the final high-result base receives the saved shifted value.
- A byte at offset 9 from that saved shifted base receives the low byte of the final high portion.
- A word at offset 0x54 from the low portion scaled by 256 receives the \(2^{25}\)-scaled result.
- The offset-9 byte is overwritten with the low byte of the saved shifted value.

The \(2^{25}\)-scaled base then has its offset-0x14 word cleared. Its offset-5 byte is read before the doubled-extraction base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared. The captured byte is published, zero-extended, as a word at offset 0x34 from the first base.

A later read of the first cleared word supplies another address-bearing value. The doubled-extraction base receives a second word clear and two consecutive offset-0x15 byte writes containing the low byte of that read.

These operations establish access order, not merely final values. Aliasing or device-memory behavior can make the clears and duplicate writes independently observable.

Before those field replacements, fixed address **0x01** receives an 8-bit truncation of the doubled-extraction base. The byte-derived base’s offset-0x15 destination receives the low byte of the undoubled extraction.

An invocation-derived shifted value is temporarily written as a byte at offset 0x11 from the \(2^{25}\)-scaled base. A word write through the previously read cleared-word value follows, at offset 0x54, before the offset-0x11 byte is cleared. Under ordinary stable memory without relevant overlap, that cleared-word value is zero and the destination is address 0x54; no rejection path is represented.

The word read through the fixed-0x05 byte-derived base at offset 0x54 is captured before that field is replaced. Call this captured word the **predecessor value**. This is a descriptive name, not an ownership or structure claim.

- Loads through offset 0x74 supplying additional bases.

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Initial mask:** An incoming word of 0xFFFFFFFF contributes 0xFFC00000; 0x003FFFFF contributes zero.

2. **Combined result:** With high portion 3 and low portion 1, the initial shifted low arguments are 256 and 2048, while the high argument is 3.

3. **Unsigned extraction:** Masking 0xFFFFFFFF to twenty bits and shifting right by ten yields 1023.

4. **Byte extension:** An opening offset-5 byte of 0xFE produces a word value of 254 at the subsequent offset-0x34 publication.

5. **Fixed-byte addressing:** If the fixed 0x05 read yields 0xFE, its offset-0x54 word access is at address 0x152, provided that access is valid.

7. **0x67 rereads:** If an intervening indirect-address store changes byte 0x67 from 0x11 to 0x22 before a later represented read, the later byte publication must use 0x22 under ordinary stable-memory semantics, not a cached 0x11.

8. **Signed shift boundary:** A negative signed left operand does not acquire an exact portable result merely because its mathematical shifted value fits in 32 bits. Such a case remains target-dependent.

9. **Partial execution:** A non-returning early dependency prevents later direct writes; no compensating action is represented.

10. **Final arguments:** Reaching the final transfer establishes first argument 0x72 and third argument equal to the saved restoration word, not a fresh read of its original location.
