# F-1964c44ddc19

## Purpose

F-1964c44ddc19 performs an ordered transformation of interconnected memory locations, derives additional addresses from signed displacements and products, prepares activation storage, and transfers control through external dependencies.

The established contract concerns memory observations, memory modifications, and call boundaries. An application-level purpose, public data structure, ownership protocol, or successful completion condition is not established.

Two reached byte writes have no established input value. Later assignments do not resolve those earlier operands. All behavior beyond either write is conditional on execution continuing.

### Representation and preconditions

A **word** is 32 bits, a **halfword** is 16 bits, and a **byte** is 8 bits. All offsets are byte offsets. Address-bearing integers are represented with 32 bits.

Each routing-stage displacement is read as a signed halfword and promoted to a signed integer: its range is −32768 through 32767. Some accesses use that displacement itself as an address base.

Character storage is eight bits wide, but character signedness is unresolved. A character used as an address therefore may have a negative promoted value. Character conversion and conversion directly to an eight-bit quantity are distinguished below.

Reached accesses require valid addresses, sufficient accessible extent, suitable lifetime and permissions, and applicable alignment. No checks establish these conditions. Address arithmetic, signed products, and left shifts have no established wrapping guarantee. Narrowing the result does not validate an invalid intermediate calculation. Byte order and complete out-of-range conversion rules remain **documented_only**.

## Behavior

### Routing contract

The transformation has fourteen routing stages. These labels describe externally distinguishable portions of the trace; they do not prescribe a loop or implementation structure.

At each stage, the following opening precedence holds:

- The current receiver is stored as a word at its own offset 0x54.
- An external word observation establishes the **initial selection**.
- A separate observation of that stage’s external source supplies a word written at the **opening destination’s** offset 0x44.
- The incoming payload is written at initial-selection offset 0x14.
- The opening byte is written at receiver offset 0x11.

The external observations are separate even though they reference the same represented source within a stage. They need not produce equal values. Source backing storage, overlap, and stability are unknown.

After the opening byte, each stage obtains its signed displacement from the address formed by adding the current receiver and initial selection. Subsequent receiver or selection replacement does not refresh this observation.

Stages 1, 3, 5, 6, and 10–14 also retain a **link** from receiver offset 0x44, before displacement acquisition. Additional observations preceding link acquisition are identified below.

Stages 1–13 finish with a halfword write and then a fresh word read at final-selection offset 0x44. This fresh read becomes the next incoming payload. The first incoming payload is argument 2. Except in stage 4, the closing address combines the retained displacement and final selection.

### Establishing retained state: stages 1–4

**Stage 1.** Between displacement acquisition and closing, three byte destinations are modified in order: selection offset 0x01 receives selection’s character conversion; selection offset 0x0d receives the auxiliary byte; link offset 0x09 receives selection’s character conversion.

**Stage 2.** The displacement-relative word at offset 0x44 is retained before the selection word is read at selection offset 0x14. A word-width observation at argument-1 offset 0x64 subsequently supplies a character-converted byte to receiver offset 0x11, before closing. The source access remains 32 bits despite the byte-sized destination.

**Stage 3.** The displacement-relative word retained in stage 2 becomes the base of a fresh offset-0x64 word observation. This occurs after the opening byte but before link and displacement acquisition.

One direct eight-bit conversion of that observation supplies two additional writes to receiver offset 0x11. Before the first, incoming-payload offset 0x54 receives the receiver as a word. Between the two byte writes, selection is stored at link offset 0x44, followed by observations at displacement offset 0x44 and selection offset 0x14, in that order. The second byte precedes closing. Intervening memory changes do not refresh its retained payload.

**Stage 4.** The displacement-relative word at offset 0x44 establishes the anchor and is recorded at activation coordinate 0x34 before the selection-word observation. A fresh offset-0x64 observation through stage 3’s retained displacement-relative word supplies an additional character-converted receiver byte.

After that byte, anchor offset 0x04 supplies a retained word, then selection offset 0x74 supplies the **companion**. Receiver offset 0x54 receives the retained anchor-offset-0x04 word. The closing halfword uses an address formed from twice the receiver plus selection, rather than displacement plus selection. This doubling is multiplication, not a left shift.

### Receiver and selection transitions: stages 5–11

**Stage 5.** Anchor offset 0x64 is observed after the opening byte and before link acquisition. After displacement acquisition, companion is written at anchor offset 0x54. Initial-selection offset 0x14 then supplies the replacement receiver, whose offset 0x0d receives initial selection’s character conversion. Closing retains the original selection and the link obtained through the outgoing receiver.

**Stage 6.** After link and displacement acquisition, companion is written at anchor offset 0x54. Initial-selection offset 0x14 supplies the replacement receiver, followed by a character-converted selection byte at that receiver’s offset 0x0d. A later word observation at incoming-payload offset 0x14 supplies replacement selection. Anchor is then written at companion offset 0x54. Closing and forwarding use replacement selection.

**Stages 7–9.** Each stage observes a character at displacement plus incoming payload after acquiring displacement. That retained character, with its applicable signed or unsigned promotion, supplies the closing-halfword value and the next stage’s opening-destination base.

### Companion-dependent observations: stages 12–13

Both stages retain a fresh anchor-offset-0x64 word before link and displacement acquisition. The word at displacement offset 0x14 subsequently replaces receiver.

After replacement, these word modifications precede the companion-derived byte observation, in this order:

- Link is written at link offset 0x54.
- Companion is written at the address combining selection and incoming payload.
- Link is written at selection offset 0x14.

Only then is companion offset 0x44 read as a word and character-converted into selection offset 0x0d. Link’s character conversion subsequently reaches replacement-receiver offset 0x0d, before closing.

This ordering matters when any preceding word write overlaps the companion source. The companion-derived byte cannot generally be sampled early.

Stage 13 has an additional interval between displacement acquisition and receiver replacement. Outgoing receiver first reaches incoming-payload offset 0x54 as a word. An unresolved byte then reaches outgoing-receiver offset 0x11. Selection subsequently reaches retained-link offset 0x44 as a word.

That unresolved byte has no established preceding definition. Stage 14’s opening byte, although defined, does not determine it.

### Product-derived addressing: stage 14 and continuation

Stage 14 has no ordinary closing halfword and no outgoing-payload read.

After link and displacement acquisition, two ordered pairs each modify displacement offset 0x54 with the sign-extended displacement, then selection offset 0x11 with receiver’s character conversion. The first byte destination uses raw-byte storage; the second uses character storage. Both values derive from receiver itself, not doubled receiver.

The **first product** is the signed product of displacement and retained link. Endpoint discovery consists of three dependent word observations: first-product offset 0x44, the resulting address’s offset 0x74, then the next resulting address’s offset 0x14.

The discovered **endpoint’s** offset 0x14 is read before receiving receiver shifted left one bit. The retained **old endpoint word** remains the base for a fresh offset-0x14 word observation. Multiplying displacement by this fresh word establishes the **second product**.

A third displacement self-write at offset 0x54 precedes a character-converted old-endpoint-word byte at selection offset 0x11. Second-product offset 0x74 supplies the **product-relative word**, recorded at activation coordinate 0x108. Displacement multiplied by the second product establishes the **evolved product**.

A fourth displacement self-write precedes another unresolved byte at selection offset 0x11. Its value is not established by the later definition of an eight-bit operand.

Conditional on continuation, the remaining observation order is significant:

- Evolved-product offset 0x44 is observed before anchor offset 0x74.
- The product-relative word reaches selection offset 0x64 before the first evolved-product observation supplies a character-converted byte to selection offset 0x09.
- A second, fresh evolved-product-offset-0x44 observation supplies a character-converted byte at evolved-product offset 0x11.
- A fifth displacement self-write precedes the direct eight-bit conversion of product-relative word stored at twice evolved product plus 0x11.

That final doubling is multiplication. The two evolved-product observations cannot generally be merged.

A final word is observed at displacement multiplied by evolved product, plus 0x44. Anchor offset 0x14 subsequently supplies final receiver. Three character-converted byte writes follow in order: retained anchor-offset-0x74 word to product-relative-word offset 0x01; final observed word to final-receiver offset 0x11; twice evolved product to final-receiver offset 0x09.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Reached arithmetic and accesses must satisfy their validity preconditions.

- **Independent external observations:** If a stage’s two external reads differ, the first determines initial selection; the second determines the word written at opening-destination offset 0x44.
- **Read extent:** Argument-1 offset 0x64 is accessed as a full word. Under a conversion retaining the low eight bits, 0x123456ab supplies byte 0xab; byte-sized readability alone is insufficient.
- **Signed displacement:** Halfword pattern 0xffff contributes −1, not 65535, to subsequent displacement arithmetic.
- **Character addressing:** Character pattern 0xff may promote to −1 or 255. The applicable interpretation governs both character-directed addressing and closing conversion.
- **Retained payload:** Altering stage 3’s source after its offset-0x64 observation does not refresh either additional receiver-byte payload.
- **Selection replacement:** Stage 6 places incoming payload at initial selection but closes and forwards through replacement selection.
- **Late companion observation:** If stage 12’s preceding word writes overlap companion offset 0x44, the byte derives from the later word observation, subject to byte order.
- **Endpoint retention:** Overwriting endpoint offset 0x14 does not replace the old endpoint word used as the next observation’s address base.
- **Fresh terminal read:** Intervening writes may distinguish the two evolved-product-offset-0x44 observations; reusing the first is not generally equivalent.
- **Distinct arithmetic:** With receiver 3, the first two terminal receiver-derived bytes convert 3, while the endpoint overwrite receives 6. With anchor 3, the independent activation shifts produce 48 and 768.
- **Undefined-origin operands:** Neither unresolved byte is established as zero, nor as the later-defined byte value.
- **Call boundary:** If F-opaque-01 changes the external global and returns, F-cdec09f5d432 receives the later observation. If F-opaque-01 does not return, subsequent effects are not established as occurring.
