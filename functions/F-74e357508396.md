Early data dependencies include:

- Consecutive left shifts by 25 and 11 produce zero.

- A low-22-bit field shifted left by eight occupies at most 30 bits.

### Initial direct mutations

Both halves of the last paired preparation result affect the first updates. A word store at its high word + 0x14 precedes a byte store at a retained shifted address +9. A word store at 256 times its low word + 0x54 then precedes another byte store to that same byte destination.

The shifted base’s +0x14 word is cleared before its +5 unsigned byte is read. The compact base’s +0x14 word is cleared next, followed by a word store of the widened byte at shifted base +0x34. The shifted base’s +0x14 word is then read back.

Another zero word store at compact base +0x14 follows, then two identical byte stores at compact base +0x15 using the read-back word’s low byte.

**[INFERENCE]** With 32-bit bit-vector shifts, the compact quantity is a multiple of four between 0 and 508; the compact base is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016. These bounds do not imply accessible memory.

The read-back word is not unconditionally zero. Intervening destinations may overlap it, and external memory behavior is unknown.

## Acceptance examples

### Width and extraction

### Compact addressing

For the relevant primary result 0x7F, the bit-vector interpretation gives compact quantity 508 and compact base 1016. Its +0x14 word destination is address 1036. Accessibility remains a separate prerequisite.

### Overlapping constant stores

If the linked quantity is zero and the locations are accessible, the 0x3A byte and halfword stores both begin at address zero, in that order. The 0x3A word store begins at 0x20. A criterion requiring all three destinations to differ is rejected.

### Publication boundary

An access trace places the word load at 0x087FBBBC before the word store of 0x20 at 0x60, then begins publication. Apparent address separation does not justify reversing the first two accesses.

### Fresh bytes and retained words

If memory is stable and intervening writes do not overlap 0x67, the three later byte reads obtain the copied low byte. If a computed write changes 0x67, subsequent reads use the changed value.

If the retained 0xA9 word is 0x123456AB, its byte copy receives 0xAB, while the later full-word store at working base +0x44 receives retained 0x123456AB even if 0xA9 changes afterward.

### Final intervening mutation

Suppose the retained payload word is 0x123456AB and the fresh working-base +0x14 word is 0x765432CD. On reaching the final sequence, linked base +0x11 receives byte 0xAB after that fresh word read and before the working-base +9 byte store.

The final destination base +0x11 later receives 0xCD. Substituting 0xCD for the earlier linked-base +0x11 store, omitting that store, or moving it across potentially overlapping accesses does not preserve the represented behavior.
