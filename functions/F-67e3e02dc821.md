# F-67e3e02dc821

## Purpose

F-67e3e02dc821 performs an ordered sequence of dependency invocations and memory mutations. Established effects include indirect byte stores, indexed 32-bit stores, field reads and writes, and updates to two shared 32-bit storage locations, designated **G-01** and **G-02**.

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated scaled addressing suggests structured-state manipulation, but does not establish parsing, allocation, initialization, cleanup, or service dispatch.

This contract describes the supplied static representation. It does not establish that the represented entry point, function extent, call signatures, or execution path correspond to a valid executable routine. Hidden entry inputs, unresolved secondary results, low-address accesses, and a final call through address zero remain material limitations.

## Behavior

The representation contains one straight-line path, without an explicit branch, loop, validation gate, or early return. Dependencies and invalid accesses may nevertheless prevent further execution.

Several address and value expressions instead use signed 32-bit intermediates. Their overflow and signed-left-shift interpretation is **documented_only**; a portable signed-arithmetic contract cannot be inferred.

The following ordering is significant:

- A selector read precedes its associated store.
- A field-source read precedes its associated destination write.
- A store before an invocation remains observable even if a later store overwrites it.
- Retaining an address does not retain the memory contents at that address.

Calls may change shared storage and subsequently read memory. Their preservation behavior is not established.

After several invocations, the routine reads a selector at the fourth argument logically shifted right by 19, plus 1, and clears the selected byte.

Further early stores use fresh reads of the byte at numeric address 1. Other selectors use secondary results shifted right by 9, plus 1.

Two byte stores use the same retained selector-address expression on opposite sides of invocations, but each rereads the selector byte. A later invocation presents seven arguments: four copies of one result, two extracted values, and a fixed 32-bit value.

Two subsequent reads have **different provenance**:

1. **Earlier wide-result byte read.** The low 32 bits of an earlier represented 64-bit result supply the address of an unsigned byte read after multiplication by two. The widened byte is passed in four argument positions.
2. **Subsequent wide-result word read.** A later represented 64-bit result supplies a different low 32-bit value. That later value supplies both the word-read base and the displacement derived by shifting it right by 19 and then left by 31.

For the second read, the displacement is either zero or hexadecimal 80000000 at unsigned 32-bit width; it depends only on bit 19 of the later low half. Address addition follows the represented 32-bit arithmetic. The word-read base must not be substituted with the earlier byte-read base.

### Field mutations and repeated slot population

At offset 13 from the retained base-like value, the routine first writes the low byte of twice that value and later writes 4. The first write remains independently observable.

### Retained numeric-address-1 selector and G-02 assignment

5. Further dependencies are invoked.

### Second post-reset G-01 snapshot and its two byte values

Much later, after further field copies, indexed stores, and a wide-result-driven indexed store, the routine independently reads G-01 again.

This **second snapshot** is retained across dependency invocations and another indexed word store. Only afterward does the routine read a 32-bit word at the retained snapshot plus 20.

The loaded word is retained and supplies two distinct byte-store values:

- Its low eight bits are written through a selector read at an address formed from the previously retained full offset-116 word shifted right by 23, plus 1.
- After intervening calls, its value shifted right by 22 is truncated to eight bits and written through another selector.

The second selector address uses the low half of a newer wide result, masked to thirty bits, shifted right by 21, plus 1. Thus both the stored-value provenance and selector-address provenance differ between these stores.

Neither the second G-01 snapshot nor the word loaded through it is derived from the first snapshot’s necessarily-zero twice-shifted value.

### Final mutations and attempted return

Following further invocations, a secondary result supplies an address for an unconditional byte clear.

G-02 is then overwritten with a 32-bit composition: the final one-byte dependency result supplies bits 0–7, and a separately referenced three-byte shared value supplies bits 8–31. The shared-storage overlap warning prevents assuming that source is independent of other shared accesses. In particular, the preceding indirect clear may alias storage relevant to this composition.

A 16-bit zero is stored at the newer wide result’s low half masked to thirty bits and shifted right by 7. This address is not necessarily aligned.

Finally, the routine attempts an invocation through numeric address zero with three explicit zero arguments. It returns only if that invocation returns.
