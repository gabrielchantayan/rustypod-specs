# F-27e6faa36563

## Purpose

F-27e6faa36563 specifies a linear, machine-state-dependent sequence of indirect calls, two identified direct dependency calls, fourteen coprocessor transfers, and a final 16-bit memory store. The supplied representation ends at an invalid-instruction termination marker rather than an ordinary return.

No application-level purpose is established. Describing the sequence as initialization, decoding, cryptography, allocation, or device configuration would be speculative.

This contract describes the represented path, conditional on the supplied material correctly identifying instructions, call boundaries, and dataflow. The warning about invalid instruction data limits that condition.

## Interface

### Explicit inputs

The represented interface has three opaque 32-bit inputs. The first indirect invocation receives these words unchanged, in their original order, followed by a fourth word derived from entry machine state.

No interpretation as pointers, lengths, flags, handles, or signed numbers is established. No validation precedes their forwarding.

Reaching the final store requires every preceding operation to permit continued execution. This is a prerequisite, not a guarantee.

### Widths and arithmetic

Unless otherwise stated, a **word** is a 32-bit bit pattern. A represented 64-bit result is divided into low and high 32-bit words. This does not establish a source-level 64-bit return convention; paired register outputs are also possible.

Left-shift deductions retain only the low 32 bits. These are machine-word dataflow deductions, not assertions about the definedness of signed arithmetic in an implementation language. Extraction from a represented 64-bit result uses unsigned right shifts.

There are three represented occurrences of signed right shift:

- A right shift by 5 in phase 6, whose operand can have its sign bit set.
- Two occurrences of right shift by 11 in the terminal address expression.

The phase-6 operation is the only **potentially sign-extending** right shift. Each terminal signed shift operates on the same nonnegative intermediate, bounded by `0x007FFFFF`. Consequently, both terminal shifts equal unsigned division by 2,048; neither can introduce sign bits. The subsequent right shift by 2 in the address calculation is unsigned.

“Call without represented arguments” means that no explicit arguments are supplied in the representation. It does not exclude implicit register, stack, or global inputs.

### Outputs and completion

No ordinary return value or normal-return path is shown.

If execution reaches the final write, the function attempts to store the 16-bit numeric value `0x00CD` at a calculated low numeric address. The next represented action is invalid-instruction termination. Successful storage and the precise termination mechanism are not guaranteed.

No error code, recovery branch, retry, rollback, or cleanup path is visible.

### 9. Final arithmetic, forwarding, and store

Two calls without represented arguments precede T12 and T13, both zero-payload transfers. A local mask calculation between those transfers introduces no additional call or transfer.

A call receiving zero produces a word. T14 then transfers the phase-8 shifted-by-4 word shifted left by another 8 bits.

- The same word shifted left by another 12 bits.
- The latest call result shifted left by 9 bits.

A call without represented arguments produces a 64-bit result. A following call receives its low word, high word, and low word shifted left by 1 bit.

The phase-8 shifted-by-4 word has zero low two bits. A mask-and-shift value derived from those bits is therefore zero. A call receives a further-shifted version of this zero and produces a word; two calls successively forward its result.

One call without represented arguments follows. Another produces a 64-bit result. Its low word, high word, and high word shifted left by 11 bits are passed to a transformation call producing the **final relevant 64-bit result**.

An unsigned right shift of this result by 41 produces a 32-bit intermediate containing original bits 41–63. Its range is 0 through `0x007FFFFF`.

Four indirect calls occur afterward:

1. A call receiving the final relevant result’s low word.
2. A call without represented arguments, producing a word.
3. A call receiving that word and zero.
4. A call without represented arguments.

The destination expression contains two signed right shifts by 11 of the retained intermediate. Because the intermediate is nonnegative and at most `0x007FFFFF`, both shifts produce the same unsigned 12-bit value.

Let **Q** denote this value: original bits 52–63 of the final relevant 64-bit result. The address is:

**Q + floor(Q / 4)**

The division by 4 corresponds to the unsigned right shift by 2 applied to one occurrence of the shifted intermediate. The addition cannot overflow a 32-bit word: its maximum is 5,118.

The function attempts a 16-bit store of `0x00CD` at that address, then reaches the invalid-instruction termination marker.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Input forwarding.** If the first indirect call is reached with a valid target, its first three arguments equal the three explicit input words in order.

2. **Entry-bit truncation.** Under 32-bit shifts, changing only incoming general-purpose bits 5–31 leaves the total-left-shift-by-27 argument unchanged. Changes within bits 5–13 can still affect the initial shifted-by-18 argument.

3. **Masked low word.** A first paired-result low word of `0xFFFFFFFF` produces masked argument `0x003FFFF0`. A low word of `0x00040000` produces zero.

4. **Additional register forwarding.** If F-0e02e4c13579 exposes additional register word `0x12345678`, the immediately following indirect call receives `(0, 0x12345678, 0)`. No particular primary return value is required.

5. **Potential sign extension.** Under arithmetic right-shift semantics, the phase-6 left-by-5/right-by-5 sequence maps `0x04000000` to `0xFC000000`, not to the original value.

6. **Ordered duplicate payload.** If T6 and T7 are reached, both receive the same payload. T6 precedes T7, and their operand pairs remain distinct.

7. **Terminal signed-shift bounds.** A terminal intermediate of `0x007FFFFF` produces `0x00000FFF` from each signed right shift by 11. Its sign bit is clear; neither occurrence sign-extends. The resulting destination is 5,118.

8. **Terminal address boundaries.** If the store is reached, Q values 0, 3, 4, and 4,095 select addresses 0, 3, 5, and 5,118 respectively.

9. **Terminal-result independence.** Holding bits 52–63 fixed while varying only bits 0–51 leaves the explicit address calculation unchanged, including when bits 41–51 change. This does not constrain dependency effects involving those bits.

10. **Faulting destination.** If the selected address is inaccessible or violates alignment requirements, the evidence does not require the store to complete or terminal invalid-instruction handling to occur afterward.
