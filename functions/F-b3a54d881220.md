# F-b3a54d881220

## Purpose

F-b3a54d881220 transforms one 32-bit input word into a 64-bit result assembled from two 32-bit words.

Under a sign-extending interpretation of the signed right shift, the result is the IEEE 754 binary64 encoding of the input interpreted as a two’s-complement signed 32-bit integer. This interpretation is exact: every value in that input range is representable in binary64 without rounding.

The supplied representation establishes the bit manipulation and return-word composition. It does not establish that the external calling convention returns a floating-point value rather than a 64-bit integer or another eight-byte carrier. Consumers must distinguish the returned bit pattern from its interpretation as a floating-point number.

## Interface

**Input**

One unsigned 32-bit word, with values from 0 through 4,294,967,295.

Although the input is declared unsigned, bit 31 controls sign handling. Consequently, the apparent numeric interpretation is:

- Inputs from `0x00000000` through `0x7FFFFFFF`: nonnegative integers.
- Inputs from `0x80000000` through `0xFFFFFFFF`: negative integers represented in two’s complement.

In particular, `0xFFFFFFFF` is treated as −1, not as the positive integer 4,294,967,295.

**Output**

One 64-bit result. For the nonzero path, it is composed of:

- A calculated 32-bit word in result bits 63 through 32.

The zero-result path returns all 64 bits clear.

## Behavior

### Sign and magnitude selection

The original input’s bit 31 is retained as a sign marker. Its only possible values are zero and `0x80000000`.

When that bit is clear, the input word is used unchanged as the magnitude.

When that bit is set, the magnitude is obtained by unsigned negation at 32-bit width. Equivalently, the magnitude is the additive inverse of the input modulo \(2^{32}\).

This width matters at the negative endpoint:

- `0x80000000` produces magnitude `0x80000000`.
- `0xFFFFFFFF` produces magnitude `0x00000001`.

The endpoint does not require a positive signed 32-bit intermediate. Its magnitude remains representable as an unsigned 32-bit word.

### Normalization

The magnitude is counted for leading zero bits and shifted left by that count. The shifted result is retained at 32-bit width and interpreted as signed for the later right shift.

For every nonzero magnitude:

- The leading-zero count is between 0 and 31, inclusive.
- The normalized word has bit 31 set.
- Normalization discards no significant magnitude bits.
- The normalized word is nonzero.

The function tests the normalized word for zero. If it is zero, the function returns the 64-bit zero word without constructing the two output halves.

For nonzero inputs, that branch is not taken under ordinary 32-bit leading-zero-count semantics.

For zero input, the apparent intended result is zero. However, the count and left shift precede the test. A fully portable zero-input guarantee therefore depends on the semantics assigned to counting zero and shifting by the resulting count.

### Nonzero result construction

Let:

- \(s\) be the retained sign marker.
- \(m\) be the unsigned magnitude.
- \(z\) be the leading-zero count of \(m\).
- \(n\) be the normalized 32-bit word.

The low output word is the low 32 bits of \(n\) shifted left by 21 positions. Thus, the low 11 bits of \(n\) occupy output bits 31 through 21, and output bits 20 through 0 are clear.

The high output word combines:

- The retained sign marker.
- A fixed contribution of `0x40000000`.
- A further contribution of `0x01F00000`.
- A subtraction of `0x00100000` for each leading zero.
- The result of shifting the signed normalized word right by 11 positions.

All contributions to that high word are combined at 32-bit width. The high and low words are then concatenated, with the high word occupying the most significant half of the 64-bit result.

The supplied expression contains two leading-zero-count occurrences: one participates in normalization, and the other participates in high-word construction. No change to the magnitude is visible between them.

| Result bits | Meaning |

| 63 | Original input bit 31 |
| 62–52 | Binary64 biased exponent |
| 51–0 | Binary64 fraction |

No rounding step is needed. The magnitude contains at most 32 significant bits, while binary64 provides 53 bits of significand precision. The construction preserves those significant bits exactly.

There is no distinct negative-zero input encoding in the two’s-complement interpretation. Zero has its sign marker clear.
