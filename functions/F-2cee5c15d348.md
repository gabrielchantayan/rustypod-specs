# F-2cee5c15d348

## Purpose

F-2cee5c15d348 performs a fixed sequence of indirect memory accesses, partially prepares a stack-backed area, calls two dependencies, and attempts a terminal control transfer. No application-level purpose is established.

This contract specifies access widths, value provenance, ordering, and call-visible state. It does not establish that any input permits the entire sequence to execute with defined behavior. Invalid addresses, invalid signed arithmetic, values consumed before definition, or dependency behavior can prevent later effects.

The sixteen **selection epochs** below identify successive uses of sixteen global data sources. They are descriptive boundaries, not an exposed iteration interface.

### Results and execution requirements

No scalar return value is established. Observable results consist of memory effects, calls, and attempted terminal control behavior.

Offsets denote bytes. Byte, halfword, and word accesses have widths of 8, 16, and 32 bits. Narrow stores retain the low bits of the converted value. A word read remains word-width even when only its low byte is subsequently stored.

Address-forming halfwords are signed and widen to 32 bits. Plain-byte signedness is unresolved. A byte used in address arithmetic or converted to a halfword can therefore require either sign extension or zero extension.

Valid addresses, sufficient accessible storage, applicable alignment, initialized operands, and valid arithmetic are execution preconditions. The function does not validate them. ## Behavior

### Memory roles and ordering model

The **active address** initially equals its entry-state value. It changes only at the replacement boundaries specified below.

Each epoch has:

- An **acquired selection** from its corresponding global source.
- An **effective selection**, normally identical to the acquired selection.
- An **incoming transfer**, supplied by the preceding epoch’s closing word read.
- A **payload**, whose width and origin depend on the epoch.

The effective selection differs from the acquired selection only in epochs 8, 12, and 13. Earlier effects continue to use their specified acquired value.

Samples retained across later accesses do not change retroactively. Fresh reads must remain distinguishable from retained samples, even when their addresses coincide. Indirect addresses, globals, and stack-backed fields are not established to be disjoint. Ordering requirements therefore apply even to apparently redundant stores.

The **recurring-address field** is a word at stack-backed offset 0x34, first defined during epoch 6. The **late-word field** is a word at offset 0x108, defined during final arithmetic. These are mutable storage, not unconditional immutable snapshots.

### Opening boundary

Before selection processing, the initial active address’s offset 0x11 receives the first argument’s low byte, followed by the auxiliary address’s offset 0x09 receiving the opening source address’s low byte.

A halfword store then places the auxiliary address’s low 16 bits at the sum of the third argument and opening source address. The following word read at opening-source offset 0x44 establishes the first incoming transfer. A word store of the initial active address at its own offset 0x54 completes this boundary.

These effects precede the first selection acquisition; they do not establish that even this opening boundary is safely executable.

### Recurring selection protocol

Every epoch acquires a selection before a companion word store. The companion store separately references the same global source. The representation does not guarantee that this second reference yields the acquired value.

The companion destination is auxiliary offset 0x44 in epoch 1. Thereafter it is the preceding payload value plus 0x44. For byte payloads, this means the widened byte value, not the address from which that byte was read.

Within each epoch, the companion store precedes a word store of the incoming transfer at acquired-selection offset 0x14. That transfer store precedes a prefix byte store at incoming-active offset 0x11.

Epochs 1–15 also have a signed-halfword sample at incoming active address plus acquired selection. Their halfword mutation stores the payload, narrowed to 16 bits, at sampled-halfword value plus effective selection. Epoch 6 is the exception: its mutation destination is twice the incoming active address plus acquired selection.

Each of epochs 1–15 closes with a fresh word read at effective-selection offset 0x44, then a word store of the outgoing active address at its own offset 0x54. The read supplies the next incoming transfer. Both accesses precede the next selection acquisition.

Next, acquired-selection offset 0x09 receives the active-address byte, and payload-value offset 0x0d receives the acquired-selection byte. These effects precede halfword mutation.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Width preservation:** A required word read yielding low byte 0xab supplies byte 0xab. A byte-only read is not an equivalent implementation.
2. **Signed halfword:** A sampled halfword pattern 0xffff gives a normal mutation destination one byte below the effective selection, provided the arithmetic and address are valid. Epoch 6 instead uses its exceptional destination.
3. **Byte promotion:** A payload pattern 0x80 contributes either −128 or 128 according to unresolved plain-byte signedness. Its halfword mutation can consequently store 0xff80 or 0x0080.
4. **Retention:** Changing first-argument offset 0x64 after epoch 2’s sample does not change epoch 3’s retained prefix value.
5. **Distinct effects:** Epoch 5 requires both additional byte stores even when their destinations and values coincide.
6. **Replacement:** Epoch 8’s outgoing-active offset-0x0d store uses the acquired selection. Its halfword mutation and closing read use the effective replacement.
7. **Aliasing:** Epoch 11’s closing read equals the tenth-epoch word previously stored there only if subsequent overlapping effects or interference have not changed it.
8. **Arithmetic provenance:** The product-derived address uses the intermediate product, not the late word. Container mutation does not replace the retained saved word.
9. **Partial preparation:** With recurring value 0x00001234, valid arithmetic, and no interference, the shift-derived groups receive 0x00012340 and 0x00123400. Other fields are not implicitly zeroed.
10. **Call boundary:** If the first dependency does not return, the post-call field assignments and second call are not reached.
11. **Undefined-before-use values:** Neither byte’s later definition supplies a valid value retroactively for its earlier store.
