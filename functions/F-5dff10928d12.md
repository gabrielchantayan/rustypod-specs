# F-5dff10928d12

Its application-level purpose is unknown. The established behavior consists of extracting fields from input words and call results, selecting byte-store destinations through byte reads, and passing transformed values to dependencies.

## Behavior

### Selection rules and ordering

A **primary selector store** reads the primary selector afresh, adds seventeen to its unsigned value, and stores the specified byte at the resulting address.

A **derived selector store** follows the same selection rule but reads its selector from an address derived from a retained dependency result. Every derived selector store writes zero.

Each selector read precedes its associated store. Neither selector values nor selected destinations may be reused across separate occurrences merely because their address expressions are unchanged. Intervening calls and stores can change memory.

All sequencing below is conditional on preceding operations completing normally. F-D01 through F-D37 are opaque dependency-role identifiers. Separate identifiers distinguish call roles or targets in this contract; they do not establish different implementations.

### Initial input handling

The first observable operation is a primary selector store of the first input’s low byte. F-D01 is then called with three listed arguments, each equal to the full first input.

F-D02 supplies a 32-bit result that becomes the sole listed argument to F-D03. F-D04, F-D05, and F-D06 follow, in that order, with no listed arguments. F-D07 then supplies another 32-bit result.

Before that result is passed onward, a second primary selector store writes bits twenty-two through twenty-nine of the first input. The extraction uses an unsigned right shift by twenty-two followed by byte truncation; input bits thirty and thirty-one do not survive the store.

F-D08 receives the F-D07 result shifted left by two, subject to the signed-arithmetic qualifications below. F-D09, F-D10, and F-D11 follow in order. F-D12 then supplies the retained **seed word**.

Failure of the initial store prevents all represented calls from being reached. Failure of the second primary selector store prevents F-D08 and subsequent operations from being reached.

### Fourth-input store and seed processing

The seed word is shifted left by two as an unsigned 32-bit operation. Its low byte is stored at the address obtained by logically shifting the fourth input right by seven and adding twenty-eight.

This destination ranges from twenty-eight through 33,554,459. Its calculation does not overflow a 32-bit unsigned word.

The shifted seed is also retained in a signed 32-bit intermediate for later derived-selector addressing. No later dependency result replaces this retained value.

After the fourth-input-derived store, a fresh primary selector store writes zero. F-D13 then receives the fourth input logically shifted right by seven. F-D14 follows with no listed arguments.

F-D15 receives seed bits six through twenty-nine, positioned in bits zero through twenty-three. Equivalently, the seed’s high two bits are discarded before the unsigned right shift by six. The argument ranges from zero through 16,777,215.

F-D16, F-D17, and F-D18 follow in order. F-D19 then supplies a result. Another fresh primary selector store writes zero before F-D20 receives the F-D19 result shifted left by two. F-D21, F-D22, and F-D23 follow. F-D24 supplies the **field-result word**.

### Derived selector and loop entry

The initial **field state** consists of field-result bits twenty-five through twenty-nine, positioned in the low five bits. Its range is zero through thirty-one.

The derived selector address is formed by multiplying the retained shifted seed by 1,024 and adding one. This operation uses a signed intermediate in the representation.

Under a 32-bit wrapping interpretation, the address is the seed word shifted left by twelve, reduced to 32 bits, plus one. Such addresses are congruent to one modulo 4,096. The same congruence holds where the represented signed calculation is defined without overflow.

A derived selector store writes zero before F-D25 is called. F-D25 receives twice the initial field state: an even value from zero through sixty-two. F-D26 then supplies the initial **carried word**, after which the loop begins.

The derived selector address remains based on the original seed throughout the loop. Its selector byte is nevertheless reread on every occurrence.

### Repeating cycle

The loop-head dependency receives the current carried word as its first listed argument and the field state logically shifted right by eight as its second.

The first iteration uses F-D27 as the loop-head dependency. Its carried word is the F-D26 result. Because the initial field state is at most thirty-one, its second argument is always zero.

After the loop-head call, F-D28, F-D29, and F-D30 execute in that order. F-D31 then supplies a result. A fresh derived selector store writes zero before F-D32 receives that result shifted left by two.

F-D33, F-D34, and F-D35 follow in order. F-D36 supplies the iteration-result word. The next carried word is formed by shifting that result left by two; the next field state is formed by shifting the same result left by twenty-six.

There is no intervening exit test. Every later iteration uses F-D37, rather than F-D27, as its loop-head dependency. This distinction follows from the changed value used in the target expression when the cycle repeats.

Under 32-bit wrapping shift semantics:

- The next carried word retains the iteration result’s low thirty bits, shifted left by two.
- The next field state retains its low six bits, shifted into positions twenty-six through thirty-one.
- The next loop-head second argument places those six bits in positions eighteen through twenty-three.
- That second argument ranges from zero through 16,515,072, in multiples of 262,144.

### Arithmetic qualifications

Unsigned extraction and range calculations above follow directly from 32-bit widths.

Other operations require qualification. Results shifted for F-D08, F-D20, and F-D32 pass through signed intermediates. The iteration-result shifts are also represented as signed operations before their results are retained. Negative operands or signed overflow can prevent a portable source-language interpretation.

Likewise, assigning the unsigned shifted seed to a signed intermediate can require an implementation-dependent interpretation when its high bit is set. The subsequent multiplication for derived-selector addressing may overflow signed range.

Consequently, wrapping equivalences are conditional machine-level interpretations, not unconditional guarantees of portable signed arithmetic.

### Aliasing constraints

No general non-aliasing guarantee is available. However, the derived-address arithmetic establishes a specific exclusion under the defined or wrapping interpretations described above:

- Every selector-selected destination lies between seventeen and 272.
- The derived selector address is congruent to one modulo 4,096.
- No address from seventeen through 272 has that congruence.

Therefore, neither a primary nor a derived selector-selected store can overwrite the derived selector byte under those interpretations. In particular, a derived selector store cannot select its own selector address.

Other overlaps remain possible:

- A primary selector store may overwrite the primary selector itself.
- Primary and derived selected destinations may coincide.
- The fourth-input-derived store may overwrite either selector or another destination, if the numerical addresses coincide.
- Dependencies may modify any memory used by later operations.

Repeated zero stores remain observable and cannot be collapsed based only on equal stored values. A selector read may encounter memory changed by an earlier store or call.

These are conditional static deductions, not executed tests. They assume successful accesses, normally returning dependencies, and the arithmetic interpretation stated for each example.

### Primary-selector self-modification

Suppose the primary selector resides at numerical address twenty and initially contains three. Let the first input be zero.

The initial selector read selects address twenty, and the initial store writes zero there. If intervening dependencies do not change the selector, the second primary selector read obtains zero and selects address seventeen.

This is a feasible read-before-write example: the first store changes a later selection, not its own already determined destination.

### Later loop state
