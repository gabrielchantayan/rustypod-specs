## Behavior

### Destination formation

Under the recovered 32-bit interpretation, the destination is derived entirely from the third argument.

The argument first undergoes a signed right shift by eleven bit positions. That result contributes directly to the destination calculation. A second contribution is obtained by treating the same shifted result as an unsigned 32-bit quantity and shifting it right by two positions. The destination is the sum of those two contributions.

The unsigned contribution determines the final addition’s unsigned 32-bit interpretation under the recovered arithmetic rules. The numerical result is therefore reduced modulo 4,294,967,296 rather than checked for signed overflow.

There is no separate base address. No explicit argument or global value is added as a buffer base. The resulting integer is converted directly into the address used for the write.

The destination’s 16-bit element width does not introduce an additional factor of two into this address calculation. The evidence describes conversion of the calculated integer into a destination address, not movement by a calculated number of 16-bit elements from a base.

### Nonnegative and negative arguments

For a nonnegative third argument, the first shift discards its lowest eleven bits. The second contribution is one quarter of that shifted value, rounded down. All nonnegative arguments within the same aligned group of 2,048 consecutive integers therefore produce the same numerical destination.

Negative arguments require a separate qualification. If the first shift preserves the sign, its result remains negative. Reinterpreting that result as unsigned makes the second contribution a large nonnegative number. The final address must not be calculated by simply applying the nonnegative interpretation to a negative quotient.

The supplied representation alone does not independently establish the original target’s signed-shift semantics. Numerical examples involving negative inputs are conditional on sign preservation.

### Stored value

The otherwise unexplained signed 16-bit quantity is increased by 170 before storage.

Under the conventional recovered widths, the addition occurs at a width sufficient to represent every possible sum. A signed 16-bit input ranges from −32,768 through 32,767; adding 170 gives a range from −32,598 through 32,937. Every value in that range fits in a signed 32-bit integer. The addition itself therefore does not require signed 32-bit overflow.

The destination remains 16 bits wide. It does not become a wider write when the sum exceeds the positive range of a signed 16-bit value.

Under ordinary low-sixteen-bit storage, the written pattern is the sum reduced modulo 65,536. That bit-pattern interpretation is conditional: the evidence does not independently resolve every signed narrowing rule or establish the original machine instruction’s exact semantics.
