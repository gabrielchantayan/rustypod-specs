# F-653308149a67

## Purpose

The static evidence establishes ordered indirect calls, byte accesses, and 32-bit accesses. It does not establish an application-level purpose, recognizable object model, or valid execution environment.

The function combines explicit arguments, incoming machine state, memory contents, primary call results, and secondary register outputs. Some destinations are absolute low addresses, including 0x0D and 0x11. The supplied representation ends at a bad-instruction boundary rather than an established normal return.

This contract describes that visible behavior without assigning allocation, serialization, device-control, or ownership semantics. All examples are conditional static consequences. No runtime tests have been performed.

### Widths and notation

Bytes are 8 bits; words and the address-bearing arithmetic shown here are 32 bits. Three call results are represented as 64-bit pairs, each split into low and high 32-bit halves.

“Low byte” means truncation to eight bits. Byte reads used in addresses are zero-extended. Right shifts of unsigned quantities are logical. All offsets are byte offsets.

Scaled and left-shifted values below describe low-32-bit bit patterns. This does not resolve the machine semantics of expressions displayed using signed types, including overflow and signed left shifts. Endianness, alignment requirements, and address wrapping remain unestablished.

Dependency IDs identify individual displayed call sites, not recovered functions. A range such as **F-D01.02–F-D01.05** means four calls, in increasing order. “No explicit inputs” means none are displayed; it does not mean the call has no implicit inputs.

## Behavior

### Ordering contract

The visible body is linear, with no displayed conditional branch, loop, validation, retry, or recovery.

Each phase below continues immediately from the preceding phase. Call ranges preserve every visible call boundary. Results described as retained are already captured values, not permission to repeat their originating read or call. Calls may modify memory between accesses, including accesses to identical addresses.

### 1. Entry write and initial calls

Read a byte at incoming address-bearing state plus 1. Store the incoming byte at that byte’s value plus 0x11.

**F-D01.01** receives three copies of argument 2; its target derives from incoming link state. **F-D01.02–F-D01.05** have no explicit inputs. Retain the word returned by F-D01.05.

Next, read a byte at the incoming address-bearing state logically shifted right by two, plus 1. Retain this byte as the **initial selector**, and write zero at initial selector plus 0x11.

The two initial byte reads are separate ordered events. Their expressions differ, but their evaluated addresses can coincide. In particular, incoming address-bearing state zero makes both addresses 1. The intervening five calls prevent substituting the first read for the second.

**F-D01.06** receives the retained word shifted left by two. **F-D01.07–F-D01.10** have no explicit inputs; retain the last result as the **initial call word**.

### 2. Argument-derived and selector-derived words

1. A word at argument 4 plus 4, retained as the **argument word**.
2. A word at initial selector plus 0x74, retained as the **initial linked word**.

Write the low byte of the initial linked word at initial selector plus 1.

**F-D02.01** receives, in order: initial linked word, argument 4 shifted left by 19, that shifted argument again, and initial call word shifted left by two.

**F-D02.02** has no explicit inputs and supplies a retained word. The argument word logically shifted right by 22 forms the **extracted field**, ranging from 0 through 1023.

**F-D02.03** receives the retained result of F-D02.02 and initial linked word logically shifted right by two. **F-D02.04–F-D02.07** have no explicit inputs. Four times the last result becomes the **first record base**.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional examples, not executed tests.

1. **Coincident initial reads.** With incoming address-bearing state zero, both initial read addresses are 1. If that location initially contains 2 but contains 9 after F-D01.01–F-D01.05, the first store targets 0x13 and the later clear targets 0x1A. Capturing only one byte is incorrect.

2. **Unsigned byte selection.** A selecting byte of 0xFF yields destination 0x110 after adding 0x11, not an address derived from −1.

3. **Extracted-field boundary.** Argument word 0xFFC00000 yields extracted field 1023. Under the stated 32-bit interpretation, F-D03.08’s duplicated field inputs are 0x3FE00000, while the field-derived word store contains 0xFF800000.

4. **Call after field store.** If the field destination base is 0x100, the field word is stored at 0x114 before F-D03.13 receives 0x100. That call is required even if its result is unused.

5. **Fresh selector after calls.** If block base plus 1 supplies 3 initially and 9 later, the small-index-byte store targets 0x14 and the later clear targets 0x1A.

6. **Word versus byte.** Payload 0x12345678 produces payload byte 0xCF, but the indexed full-word store in phase 11 contains 0x02468ACF.

7. **Final four-input call.** If final token is 1 and F-D12.08 returns 3, F-D12.09 receives 0x20, 0x20, 4, and 12, after the first final-scale-derived clear. The second clear follows four additional calls and a fresh selector read.

8. **Interruption.** A non-returning dependency prevents every later operation from being reached through the displayed sequence; no recovery is specified.
