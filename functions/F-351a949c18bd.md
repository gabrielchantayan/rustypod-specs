# F-351a949c18bd

## Purpose

F-351a949c18bd performs an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect calls, transforms values obtained from those calls, mutates memory through fixed and derived addresses, and makes a final unresolved indirect transfer.

No application-level purpose is established. Numeric constants resembling characters do not establish text formatting, and recurring offsets do not establish an object type, collection, or protocol.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** operations and data dependencies present in the supplied representation.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations consistent with those operations but not established by them.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which this contract supplies no executable acceptance rule.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Inputs and outputs

The represented interface has no explicit parameters and no declared return value. No success flag, error code, output buffer, or output length is exposed.

Effective inputs nevertheless include:

- Incoming continuation-related machine state determining the first indirect destination.
- An incoming register-carried value consumed by early shifts.

## Behavior

### Indirect-call prefix

The largest initial region is straight-line dependency execution: no governing conditional branch or loop is displayed.

The first destination depends on incoming continuation-related state plus displacement 0xffe. Subsequent destinations predominantly depend on embedded continuation locations plus that displacement. These expressions do not establish dependency identities.

Displayed argument counts vary. Some calls have no displayed arguments; others receive up to four displayed words. Absence of displayed arguments does not exclude implicit machine-state inputs. An unused result does not make a call effect-free or removable.

Data passed between calls includes:

- Result words shifted left eight bits.
- High words forwarded unchanged alongside transformed low words.
- Fields positioned by six or seven bits.
- Larger shifts reaching the upper part of a 32-bit word.
- Masked fields, unsigned right shifts, and explicit zero arguments.
- Apparent secondary result words whose interface is not recovered.

Masks include widths of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. These are not interchangeable. Masking a 24-bit field before shifting it six positions differs from shifting an unrestricted 32-bit word.

Several represented values are explicitly zeroed and later shifted or masked. Their derived values remain zero within the represented dataflow. This does not constrain intervening dependency effects.

If a dependency faults or does not return, later calls and direct memory effects need not occur. No recovery mechanism is displayed.

### Direct-memory entry

For the following descriptions, descriptive names denote retained numeric values, not source identifiers or inferred objects:

- **Retained positioned base:** an earlier result positioned at bit 25.
- **Second positioned base:** another result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Small index:** four times a seven-bit field, ranging from 0 through 508.
- **Doubled index:** twice the small index, ranging from 0 through 1016.
- **Saved shifted word:** an earlier result shifted left eight bits.

The small-index extraction retains bits 8 through 14 of an already shifted 32-bit word and shifts the selected value right six places. Its range follows under the represented bit-vector interpretation.

After the final paired result in the prefix, the opening effects are ordered as follows: a word at the final high word plus 0x14 receives the retained positioned base; a byte at the retained positioned base plus 9 receives the low byte of the final high word; and a word at 256 times the final low word plus 0x54 receives the second positioned base. The earlier offset-9 byte is then overwritten with the low byte of the retained positioned base—zero under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic.

The word at the second positioned base plus 0x14 is cleared. An unsigned byte at that base plus 5 is read; the doubled-index word at offset 0x14 is cleared before that byte is widened and stored at the second positioned base plus 0x34.

The cleared word at the second positioned base plus 0x14 is reread. The doubled-index word at offset 0x14 is cleared again, followed by two byte stores at doubled index plus 0x15 using the low byte of the reread word.

The reread is not universally replaceable with zero. An intervening access may alias the cleared storage, and device-backed memory may have different read semantics.

### Early fixed-address accesses and linked updates

Immediately after those duplicate byte stores, an unsigned byte is read at absolute address **0x05**. Its widened value is retained as a base. A byte at absolute address **0x01** then receives the low byte of the doubled index, before the first access through the widened base.

The ensuing byte at widened base plus 0x15 receives the low byte of the small index. Next, the second positioned base’s offset-0x11 byte receives the low byte of the saved shifted word; a word at offset 0x54 from the earlier reread word receives that shifted word; and the offset-0x11 byte is overwritten with zero.

Only then is the word at widened base plus 0x54 read and saved. This **saved offset-0x54 word** is retained across replacement of its source location with the earlier reread word.

Further updates store the small index at doubled index plus 0x34 and follow the second positioned base’s offset-0x74 word. The resulting linked base receives word and byte updates at offsets 0x54 and 9. Two self-referential stores at saved offset-0x54 word plus 0x24 are interleaved with those linked-base stores.

A separate nested update reads the doubled-index offset-0x74 link twice: once to obtain a destination base and once to obtain the value stored at that destination’s offset 0x54. The representation does not establish an immutable shared snapshot or resolve the relative evaluation order of these two reads.

The absolute-address word and byte writes have distinct positions:

- After the doubled-index offset-0x14 word receives a shifted earlier result, the word at absolute **0x54** receives twice the saved offset-0x54 word.
- The doubled-index offset-0x14 word then receives the saved offset-0x54 word twice.
- Two byte stores at doubled index plus 0x15 receive the low byte of four times that saved word.
- The byte at absolute **0x15** then receives the low byte of the doubled index.
- Subsequent derived-address updates include a word at four times the saved word plus 0x54 receiving the saved shifted word, and a word at doubled index plus 0x34 receiving four times the saved word.

Thus, the absolute 0x54 store writes a doubled **value** to a fixed address; it is not a store through an address equal to twice the saved word.

All these accesses may alias derived-address storage. In particular, the byte loaded at 0x05 is retained before the write at 0x01; later accesses use that retained numeric base rather than rereading 0x05.

### Further linked and overlapping state

Subsequent effects use fourfold and eightfold scalings of the saved offset-0x54 word, doubled offset-0x74 links, self-referential word stores, and low-byte copies of address-bearing values.

An unsigned byte read at offset 5 from a scaled base becomes the direct base for a word read at offset 0x74. No intervening pointer-table lookup is shown.

A linked word at offset 0x64 is followed and receives byte and self-referential word updates. Another word at offset 4 supplies a base whose offset-5 byte is read on separate occasions. These reads must not be merged: intervening writes can alter their sources through aliasing.

The later offset-5 read feeds another offset-0x74 word read. Call the resulting word the **late link**. Twice this word becomes the **late doubled base** used in the final region.

### Constants and publication-base timing

The value 0x3a is written as:

- A byte at four times the late link.
- A halfword at eight times the late link.
- A word at four times the late link plus 0x20.

These are numeric writes, not established string operations.

The fourfold saved-word value is reduced by 150, producing the **adjusted value**. Four times the adjusted value is the **scaled base**.

The word at scaled base plus 0x20 receives, in order, 0x20, 0x2f, a widened unsigned byte read from scaled base plus 4, and 0x20 again.

**After that final offset-0x20 store, the publication-base global word is read and retained. Only afterward is the word at absolute address 0x60 assigned 0x20.**

Consequently, a write at 0x60 cannot retroactively change the retained publication base, even if it overlaps the global’s storage. Conversely, the preceding derived-address writes may affect the global before its read.

The publication region repeatedly performs the same four-access pattern: an offset-0x54 word store through an eightfold-scaled address, an offset-0x19 byte store through the publication base, an offset-0x11 byte store through the scaled base, and an offset-0x34 word store through the publication base.

The first group uses eight times the saved offset-0x54 word for its first destination base and final stored word. Later groups use eight times the adjusted value. The offset-0x54 payload is four times the late link. The byte payloads are the low bytes of the scaled base and publication base respectively.

The publication base is retained, not reloaded between repetitions. No cursor advancement or collection traversal is established. Exact repetition-count acceptance is **documented_only** in this contract; collapsing the finite repeated region to one final assignment is not authorized.

### Final fixed-address and saved-value dependencies

After publication, a word at absolute **0xa9** is loaded and saved. This precedes both the word read at four times the late link plus 0x74 and the byte write at absolute **0x3a**.

The byte written at 0x3a is the byte-truncated result of **eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30**. It is not merely 0x30 or a copy of the saved global byte.

The function then saves a word at late doubled base plus 0x14 and a word at scaled base plus 4. Before publishing the saved global byte, intervening writes update the late doubled base’s offsets 0x0d and 0x54 and the scaled base’s offsets 8, 0x0d, and 9. The offset-8 byte receives 0x6c; the latter two bytes receive the publication base’s low byte.

The saved low byte from the 0xa9 word is then written to absolute **0x67**. After a word read at late doubled base plus 0x44, the **then-current byte at 0x67** is read and stored at absolute **0x21**.

Further derived-address writes precede two additional, distinct reads of 0x67:

- The first supplies a byte store at scaled base plus 9 after updates involving scaled-base offset 0x10 and publication-base offsets 0x44, 0x54, and 0x74.
- Between the two reads, a byte is written at late doubled base plus 9, the saved full 0xa9 word is stored at scaled base plus 0x44, and the word at scaled base plus 0x54 is loaded.
- The second read supplies another byte store at scaled base plus 9.

These are three fresh reads of 0x67: one for 0x21 and one for each of the later offset-9 stores. They are not unconditional republications of the original saved low byte. Intervening derived writes may alias 0x67.

The final region also includes a signed-looking right shift of the loaded offset-0x44 word, with a count equal to the scaled base’s low eight bits. Its low byte is stored at late doubled base plus 1. Counts of 32 or more and negative-value right-shift semantics remain unresolved.

A byte at scaled base plus 0x10 is later overlapped by a word store at that offset. Saved words remain earlier-load snapshots even when their source storage changes.

- Its offset-0x64 word is loaded and retained.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Opening overwrite:** If both offset-9 stores complete, the final high word’s low byte is written before zero. A trace exposing individual stores must retain both events.
- **Unsigned widening:** An offset-5 byte of 0xff contributes word value 255, not a sign-extended negative value.
- **Index boundary:** A seven-bit field of 127 yields small index 508 and doubled index 1016. This establishes no permission to access those addresses.
- **Early fixed-address ordering:** If 0x05 yields 0x80, the widened base remains 128 after the subsequent 0x01 store. The first access through that base follows the 0x01 store.
- **Fixed 0x54 payload:** If the saved offset-0x54 word is 7, and arithmetic and accesses are valid, absolute 0x54 receives word value 14 between the specified doubled-index stores.
- **Publication snapshot:** If the publication global yields one base and the subsequent 0x60 store changes its storage, publication still uses the retained base.
- **0x3a truncation:** If the adjusted value is 35 and the region is reached, the byte at 0x3a receives 0x48: the low byte of 328.
- **Fresh 0x67 reads:** If aliasing changes 0x67 between its successive reads, 0x21 and the two scaled-base offset-9 stores use their respective observed bytes, not necessarily the original saved byte.
- **Partial failure:** A nonreturning dependency prevents any requirement for later effects. A later fault does not roll back earlier completed writes.
- **Final arguments:** Reaching the final transfer supplies the specified saved words, not arbitrary current contents of their former source locations.
