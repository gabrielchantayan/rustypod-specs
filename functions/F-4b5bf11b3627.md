### Bit-field transformations

The prefix repeatedly shifts, masks, and extracts portions of call-produced values. These transformations constrain the represented argument values without establishing their application meaning.

Notable field widths include:

- 24-bit fields selected by 0xFFFFFF.
- 22-bit fields selected by 0x3FFFFF.

- 19-bit fields selected by 0x7FFFF.
- 17-bit fields selected by 0x1FFFF.

Unsigned right shifts discard low bits and introduce zeros at the high end. Extracting a 64-bit result after shifting it right by 34, 36, or 44 bits is distinct from shifting its low 32-bit portion; these operations select portions of the high word.

Intermediate truncation matters. For example, under a 32-bit word interpretation, shifting a value left by eight bits and then right by eight bits retains only its original low 24 bits. It does not restore the original word.

Some values are explicitly set to zero and later shifted repeatedly without being reassigned. Their represented derived arguments remain zero. Likewise, consecutive left shifts whose total displacement exceeds the word width can eliminate every original bit under a bit-vector interpretation. These consequences do not establish that calls receiving such values are effect-free.
