# F-5af3549848b1

## Purpose

F-5af3549848b1 performs an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect calls, transforms dependency-provided values, mutates memory through computed and fixed addresses, and invokes an unresolved terminal target.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation has three 32-bit input slots, no explicit return value, mixed-width memory accesses, repeated stores, and four arguments to the terminal interaction. No explicit validation branch, recoverable error result, or rollback mechanism is represented.

**Inferred intent:** some mutations resemble record-link manipulation, and some constants resemble character values. Neither resemblance establishes a structure definition, text encoding, protocol, or application purpose.

This contract distinguishes represented access history from conditional memory-value conclusions. Unresolved execution details are marked **documented_only**.

## Behavior

### Widths and interpretation

A byte, halfword, and word occupy 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Offsets are byte offsets. Descriptive names identify dataflow roles, not separate allocations or established record types.

The access history matters independently of the final memory image. Different computed bases may alias fixed addresses or other computed regions. Captured values remain captured; explicit later reads obtain values at their own positions in the history.

This does not establish atomicity, barriers, or concurrency guarantees. Nor does possible aliasing imply that every pair of accesses can overlap: regions at fixed, sufficiently separated offsets from the same unchanged base remain distinct under ordinary address arithmetic.

### Indirect-call phase

A long straight-line sequence of indirect calls precedes direct mutation. The first target depends on incoming control-link state. Later targets are constructed from embedded control locations using a recurring displacement of 0xFFE.

Calls with ignored results remain observable interactions. They may affect memory, globals, external state, ownership, or control flow. Similar-looking sites must not be merged or assumed to invoke one common dependency.

Visible argument construction includes:

- Extraction of low and high words from represented 64-bit results.
- Unsigned whole-container right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 bits.

The second input’s 11-bit shift is used before the third input’s first 3-bit-shifted argument. An additional 10-bit shift of the already-shifted third input gives an effective displacement of 13 bits, conditional on intermediate-width semantics.

A represented 64-bit result is treated here only as a bit container. Its halves are not established as pointers, ownership-bearing objects, or a status/value pair.

There is no represented conditional exit from this phase, but any dependency may prevent subsequent execution by faulting or not returning.

### Arithmetic boundaries and descriptive quantities

Arithmetic must not be silently widened where doing so changes argument bits or addresses. Under 32-bit modular bit-vector semantics, left shifts discard bits above bit 31. A 25-bit left shift followed by another 7-bit left shift therefore produces zero.

The representation also contains signed shifts and signed multiplication. It does not independently establish portable overflow or shift behavior. Such execution semantics remain **documented_only**.

Unsigned extractions support these bounds:

- Low 24 bits shifted right 8: 0–65,535.
- Low 20 bits shifted right 10: 0–1,023.
- Low 19 bits shifted right 10: 0–511.
- Unsigned 64-bit value shifted right 44: its uppermost 20 bits.

The same offset-0x09 byte destination then receives the opening shifted value’s low byte.

The next access history is explicit:

2. Capture its byte at offset 0x05.
3. Clear the compact doubled base’s word at offset 0x14.
4. Store the captured byte, zero-extended to a word, at the opening scaled base’s offset 0x34.
5. Load the opening scaled base’s word at offset 0x14.

The explicit load and stores nevertheless remain part of the represented history. Memory-mapped behavior or unresolved execution semantics may make them independently observable. That uncertainty is not an overlap between the opening base’s offsets 0x14 and 0x34.
