Unsigned right shifts are logical. Byte stores retain only eight bits:

- A four-times-result byte payload exposes the source’s low six bits shifted left by two; its bottom two bits are zero.
- A masked-and-right-shifted byte payload exposes source bits 20 through 27.

For example, a context quotient of `0x2000` produces a zero unsigned product. The signed conversion and subsequent address interpretation are not fully established.

The final selector-source expression, eight times the unsigned F-d084 result plus one, also uses represented unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. F-d072’s unsigned left shift likewise retains a 32-bit result.

These unsigned rules do not establish universal modular behavior for signed expressions. The fourth input’s left shift, signed dependency-result shifts and products, signed address additions, and the signed use of F-d026’s high half require separate caution. Overflow behavior, negative-value handling, pointer conversion, effective address width, and hardware access semantics remain unresolved.
