The established behavior is limited to that represented execution prefix. The evidence does not establish a complete executable routine, a normal calling convention, or the actual processor response after the final represented store.

## Side effects

There is no visible dynamic allocation, release, resource acquisition, ownership transfer, or explicit dependency invocation before the recovery boundary. The third argument is used as an assigned value; no dereference of that argument is shown.

The global destinations may overlap other storage. The supplied material specifically warns of overlapping global symbols, but does not identify their addresses or extents. Consequently:

- The two global writes cannot be assumed independent.
- Neither global destination can be assumed disjoint from the calculated destination.
- A later write may replace some or all of an earlier write's effects if their storage overlaps.

These are ordinary consequences of the represented access order, not evidence that such overlap occurs in a particular execution.
