### Arithmetic boundaries

Calculations must not be silently widened. Under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, addition, multiplication, and left shift retain only the resulting 32 bits.

Relevant consequences include:

- A word shifted left eight loses its original high eight bits.
- A 24-bit masked value shifted left six occupies at most 30 bits.
- A 22-bit masked value shifted left eight occupies at most 30 bits.
- A 19-bit masked value shifted left two occupies at most 21 bits.
- Two successive 28-bit left shifts eliminate all original bits under this interpretation.

Some expressions are represented as signed arithmetic. Signed overflow, left shifts of negative values, and integer-to-address conversions are not resolved into portable mathematical guarantees.

Unsigned right shifts are distinct from the late signed variable-count right shift. That count is restricted to eight bits, not necessarily to the valid shift range of a 32-bit word. Behavior for out-of-range counts remains **documented_only**.

### Opening direct-memory effects

Near the end of the dependency phase, captured results supply:

- A base formed by a 25-bit left shift.
- Another base formed by multiplication by 2²⁵.
- A small offset formed by left-shifting a result eight bits, retaining 15 low bits, and right-shifting six.
- A final paired result whose high word becomes an address and whose low word is multiplied by 256.

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, the small offset is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Its doubled value is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016. Neither bound establishes access safety.

The opening stores have an alias-sensitive order:

- A word at the final high-word address plus 0x14 is written before a byte at the shifted base plus 9.
- A word at the scaled final low-word address plus 0x54 follows.

The multiplied base’s word at offset 0x14 is cleared before its byte at offset 5 is captured. The doubled-small-offset base’s word at offset 0x14 is then cleared. The captured byte is zero-extended and stored at the multiplied base plus 0x34 before that base’s word at offset 0x14 is read again.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

### Fixed-storage conversion is not necessarily byte extension

If authoritative type information established an unsigned 8-bit source value of 0xFE at 0x05, its unsigned 32-bit conversion would be 0x000000FE.

If authoritative information instead established a signed 8-bit source value of −2, conversion to unsigned 32 bits would yield 0xFFFFFFFE. A wider source could also contribute bits above bit 7.

The supplied body selects none of these source interpretations. An implementation that always uses only the byte at 0x05 would therefore assume behavior not established here.

### Word separation and bounded offset

For a paired result with low word 0x12345678 and high word 0x9ABCDEF0, a site forwarding the shifted low word and unchanged high word supplies 0x34567800 and 0x9ABCDEF0 under 32-bit bit-vector semantics.

If the small-offset input’s low seven bits are 0x7F, the offset is 508 and its doubled value is 1016. Higher bits do not survive that calculation.

### Saved address and late capture order

If the global-address capture yields a particular address, subsequent repeated stores continue using that saved address after the word write at 0x60.

If the adjusted value’s low byte is 0x1F, the byte written at 0x3A is 0x28 under bit-vector semantics. The 0xA9 and fourfold-selected-base offset-0x74 captures precede that write; the other two boundary captures follow it.

### Fresh reads, extension, and failure

If an overlapping store changes 0x67 from 0xAA to 0xBB before the first late read, the corresponding computed-base offset-9 store uses 0xBB. If another intervening store changes it to 0xCC, the second uses 0xCC.

An explicitly captured unsigned byte 0xFE copied into a word yields 254. This example does not apply to the unresolved original access at 0x05.

Repeated observable writes cannot be reduced to a final-content check. An inaccessible destination establishes neither successful completion nor rollback.
