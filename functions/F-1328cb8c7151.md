# F-1328cb8c7151

## Purpose

F-1328cb8c7151 invokes a long, ordered sequence of unresolved dependencies, transforms their outputs, and subsequently performs mixed-width memory accesses through computed addresses and fixed shared-storage addresses. It ends with an unresolved indirect control transfer.

No application-level purpose is established. The access patterns do not establish an initializer, formatter, collection operation, or protocol implementation.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented operations, widths, value dependencies, and ordering.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations that are not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior or execution semantics.

All later behavior is conditional on earlier invocations and accesses completing. Successful execution and normal return are not guaranteed.

## Behavior

### Ordered dependency phase

The first indirect invocation derives its target from incoming control context. Later invocations predominantly derive targets from embedded control-location values plus displacement `0xffe`. This pattern does not resolve their identities or establish that they share a signature.

Visible invocations have zero through four represented word arguments. Transformations include:

- Eight-bit left shifts of ordinary results or low result words.
- Forwarding high result words unchanged or shifted.
- Shifts by six and seven bits, later shifts through sixteen bits, and larger shifts up to 31 bits.
- Low-bit masks, including widths of 11, 13, 14, 17–22, and 24 bits.
- Logical right shifts of unsigned values.
- Reuse of captured values across intervening invocations.
- Explicit zero arguments, including shifted zero-valued intermediates.

These are fixed-width transformations, not unbounded mathematical scaling.

Invocation order is significant. Invocations with no arguments or unused ordinary results are not established to be side-effect-free. No visible branch selects among invocation groups, and no visible error-code check causes an early return. Dependencies may nevertheless fail to return or prevent subsequent execution.

### Initial computed-memory effects

Several late dependency outputs establish address-bearing values:

- A **shift-derived base**, formed by shifting a low result word left by 25 bits.
- A **scaled-result base**, formed by multiplying another result by `0x02000000`.
- A **small index**, formed by shifting a result left by eight bits, retaining the low 15 bits, then shifting logically right by six.
- A **doubled-index base**, twice the small index.
- Low and high words of the last captured 64-bit result.

Under truncating 32-bit arithmetic, the small index is a multiple of four from 0 through 508; the doubled-index base is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

The first direct stores occur in this order:

1. A word at the final result’s high-word base plus `0x14` receives the shift-derived base.
2. A byte at the shift-derived base plus `9` receives the low byte of that high word.
3. A word at 256 times the final result’s low word, plus `0x54`, receives the scaled-result base.
4. The earlier offset-9 byte receives the low byte of the shift-derived base.

Both byte stores are part of the trace, even if their values coincide.

Next, the word at the scaled-result base plus `0x14` is cleared, followed by a byte capture at that base plus `5`. A clear at doubled-index base plus `0x14` precedes storing the captured byte, zero-extended, at scaled-result base plus `0x34`. The cleared scaled-result word is then read back. After another doubled-index word clear, the low byte of that read-back value is written twice at doubled-index base plus `0x15`.

Intervening stores can alias cleared storage. The read-back value is therefore not unconditionally zero.

### Early fixed shared-storage effects

The following direct shared effects occur within the computed-memory phase, not as a separate final commit:

| Fixed address | Width | Value and ordering |
|---|---:|---|
| `1` | 8 bits | Receives the low byte of the doubled-index base, immediately after storage at address `5` is read and converted to unsigned 32 bits. |
| `0x54` | 32 bits | Receives twice a captured word described below, before the following doubled-index word replacements. |
| `0x15` | 8 bits | Receives the low byte of the doubled-index base, after two intervening computed offset-`0x15` byte writes. |

The converted value from address `5` becomes a computed base. After the store to address `1`, that base plus `0x15` receives the low byte of the small index. Further computed writes precede a word capture at that base plus `0x54`; the location is then overwritten with the earlier read-back value.

Call the captured, pre-overwrite word the **propagation seed**. It remains a captured numeric value even if its original storage changes.

Subsequent accesses include offset-`0x74` word reads used to obtain further bases, repeated seed stores at seed plus `0x24`, repeated byte stores, and word replacements at doubled-index base plus `0x14`.

The fixed word store at address `0x54` receives twice the propagation seed. It follows an earlier doubled-index offset-`0x14` store derived from a dependency result and precedes two stores of the propagation seed to that same computed word destination.

Four times the seed supplies two subsequent byte writes at doubled-index base plus `0x15`. Only after both writes does fixed byte storage at `0x15` receive the low byte of the doubled-index base.

These fixed addresses may alias computed accesses. Their ordering cannot be reduced to generic “shared-state mutation.”

### Scaled propagation and constant stores

The propagation seed contributes values scaled by two, four, and eight, used both numerically and as addresses. Word accesses recur at offsets `0x14`, `0x24`, `0x34`, `0x44`, `0x54`, `0x64`, and `0x74`; byte accesses include offsets `5`, `9`, `0x0d`, `0x11`, and `0x15`.

An explicit unsigned byte read at offset `5` is used numerically as a base for a subsequent offset-`0x74` word read. It is not a full-width pointer capture.

Nested expressions include repeated reads of an offset-`0x74` location. Their represented read dependencies must be preserved; a total evaluation order within such expressions is not established.

For the later region, use these descriptive quantities:

- The **adjusted scaled value** is four times the propagation seed minus decimal 150.
- The **adjusted base** is four times that adjusted scaled value.
- The **indirect seed** is a word obtained through the later byte-derived offset-`0x74` read.
- The **doubled indirect base** is twice the indirect seed.
- The **quadrupled indirect base** is four times the indirect seed.

These descriptions do not establish pointer validity. Overflow qualifications still apply.

The constant `0x3a` is written as a byte at the quadrupled indirect base, as a halfword at eight times the indirect seed, and as a word at quadrupled indirect base plus `0x20`, in that order.

At adjusted base plus `0x20`, four word writes occur in order: `0x20`, `0x2f`, a zero-extended byte freshly read from adjusted base plus `4`, and `0x20` again.

### Captured shared base and repeated writes

After those four word writes, a shared word is captured as the **repetition base**. Its storage address need not be exposed to describe its role. **After this capture and before the repeated computed stores, fixed word storage at address `0x60` receives `0x20`.**

The first repeated-write group uses eight times the propagation seed. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted scaled value. Each group preserves this access order:

- A word at the corresponding scaled destination plus `0x54` receives the quadrupled indirect base.
- A byte at repetition base plus `0x19` receives the low byte of the adjusted base.
- A byte at adjusted base plus `0x11` receives the low byte of the repetition base.
- A word at repetition base plus `0x34` receives the corresponding scaled destination.

The repetition base and these byte values are captured values, not fresh shared reads in each group. The material represents explicit straight-line repetition, not a data-dependent loop. Exact group multiplicity remains **documented_only** in this contract; collapsing the region to its final state is not asserted equivalent.

### Final captures and shared-byte production

After the repeated region, the following ordering is established:

- The 32-bit shared word at fixed address `0xa9` is captured first.
- A word at quadrupled indirect base plus `0x74` is captured next.
- Fixed byte storage at `0x3a` is updated from eight times the adjusted scaled value plus `0x30`, retaining the byte result.
- Only afterward are words captured at doubled indirect base plus `0x14` and adjusted base plus `4`.

Thus the `0xa9` capture precedes the indirect offset-`0x74` read. The store to `0x3a` precedes both later captures and may affect them through aliasing. The byte at `0x3a` is computed from the adjusted scaled value, not copied from either preceding word capture.

The function then writes the low byte of the adjusted base at doubled indirect base plus `0x0d`. It writes `0x6c` at adjusted base plus `8`, and the low byte of the repetition base at adjusted base plus `0x0d` and plus `9`. A word at doubled indirect base plus `0x54` receives that base itself.

Only after these effects does fixed byte storage at `0x67` receive the low byte of the captured `0xa9` word.

### Fresh reads of shared byte storage

Storage at `0x67` remains mutable shared storage. Its later uses are not forwarding of an immutable captured byte:

- After the store to `0x67`, a word is read at doubled indirect base plus `0x44`.
- Storage at `0x67` is then read for a byte store to fixed address `0x21`.
- Later, storage at `0x67` is read again for a byte store at adjusted base plus `9`.
- Still later, storage at `0x67` is read once more for another byte store at that same computed offset-9 destination.

Computed stores separate these reads and may alias `0x67`.

Between the `0x21` update and the first later shared-byte read, effects include a byte store at adjusted base plus `0x10`, two word writes at repetition base plus `0x54`, and a byte store at doubled indirect base plus `1`. That byte is derived from the captured offset-`0x44` signed word shifted right by the low byte of the adjusted base.

A word captured from adjusted base plus `0x40` is subsequently written at adjusted base plus `0x10`, potentially overwriting the earlier byte store. The earlier adjusted-base offset-4 capture is written at repetition base plus `0x74`; repetition base plus `0x44` receives the doubled indirect base.

Between the two computed offset-9 writes sourced from fresh `0x67` reads, the function writes a byte at doubled indirect base plus `9`, writes the captured `0xa9` word at adjusted base plus `0x44`, and captures a word at adjusted base plus `0x54`.

### Remaining propagation and final transfer

The captured adjusted-base offset-`0x54` word becomes another base. Its offset `0x0d` receives the low byte of the earlier quadrupled-indirect offset-`0x74` capture. The earlier doubled-indirect offset-`0x14` capture is restored to doubled indirect base plus `0x54`.

A new word is captured at adjusted base plus `0x14`. Further effects include:

- A byte at doubled indirect base plus `0x11` from the earlier offset-`0x74` capture.
- A byte at adjusted base plus `9` from the captured adjusted-base offset-`0x54` word.
- Replacement of adjusted base plus `0x44` with that captured word.

A word read at doubled indirect base plus `4` supplies another base. A halfword at twice that base receives `0x65`; its offset-`0x64` word is then captured. The preceding offset-`0x54`-derived base receives a byte at offset `0x11` from the new adjusted-base offset-`0x14` capture, followed by a word value of decimal `100` at offset `0x54`.

A word at the newest base plus `0x74` and a word at doubled indirect base plus `0x64` are captured. The final stores include a byte at doubled indirect base plus `9`, a word at the latter captured base plus `0x54`, and a word through twice the captured offset-`0x64` value.

The final transfer has four represented arguments:

1. `0x72`.
2. The captured offset-`0x64` value from the base obtained through doubled indirect base plus `4`.
3. The earlier doubled-indirect offset-`0x14` capture.
4. The latest offset-`0x74` capture.

No transfer result is consumed. Normal return is conditional on the unresolved target returning and control continuing as represented.

## Side effects

Byte, halfword, and word writes affect computed memory and fixed shared storage. Explicit fixed-address stores include `1`, `0x15`, `0x21`, `0x3a`, `0x54`, `0x60`, and `0x67`; address `0xa9` supplies a captured word.

Overlapping accesses can affect subsequent reads. Captured words retain their captured numeric values; fresh reads, particularly those at `0x67`, observe the then-current storage. Identical repeated writes remain distinct represented events.

No validation error result, rollback, cleanup path, exception handler, or atomic commit is visible. Invalid targets, inaccessible memory, alignment faults, or nonreturning dependencies may stop progress after earlier effects. Those effects are not visibly restored.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static deductions, not runtime observations.

1. **Truncating forwarding.** Under 32-bit truncating shifts, forwarding `0x12345678` after an eight-bit left shift contributes `0x34567800`.

2. **Small index.** Under the same model, producer value `1` yields small index `4` and doubled-index base `8`. Accessible object storage is not implied.

3. **Address-5 conversion.** If reading storage at address `5` converts to unsigned 32-bit value `0x1234`, that value supplies the subsequent computed base. Acceptance must not infer the original read width from the conversion. The byte store to address `1` follows that capture and precedes the computed offset-`0x15` store.

4. **Fixed-store ordering.** With all relevant accesses reachable, the fixed word store at `0x54` precedes the later fixed byte store at `0x15`. The repetition-base capture precedes the fixed word store of `0x20` at `0x60`, which precedes the repeated computed-store region.

5. **Adjusted-value byte.** Assuming truncating arithmetic, adjusted scaled value `5` produces byte `0x58` at fixed address `0x3a`. That store occurs after the `0xa9` and indirect offset-`0x74` captures but before the following offset-`0x14` and offset-4 captures.

6. **Shared-byte rereads.** If `0x67` receives `0xab` and no intervening access changes it, its three later reads supply `0xab`. If an intervening computed store overlaps `0x67`, later reads must instead reflect the resulting storage. Reusing the initially stored byte is not generally equivalent.

7. **Repeated word destination.** Without interference, adjusted base plus `0x20` ends at `0x20`; its trace still includes `0x2f` and the byte-derived word between the two `0x20` writes.

8. **Partial completion.** A dependency that does not return prevents later operations. A fault after fixed storage has changed does not imply restoration. Reaching the final transfer does not guarantee normal return.
