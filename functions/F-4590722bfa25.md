# F-4590722bfa25

## Purpose

F-4590722bfa25 publishes address-related state before admitting or rejecting an invocation. An admitted invocation obtains a 64-bit transformation result and, when construction is enabled, writes one of two record formats.

Publication is not rolled back on rejection. Admission does not guarantee construction, and construction does not establish successful delivery or an ordinary return.

“Publication,” “record,” “payload,” and “completion” describe observable roles only. Application-level meaning, persistence, and ownership implications are not established.

## Behavior

### Initial call, publication, and admission

The initial target is selected by clearing the initial-target seed’s lowest two bits and adding 0x820 using unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. F-000000000001 receives the first argument. Subsequent local behavior requires that call to return.

Publication has the following access order:

1. Capture the original address from the fourth argument’s storage at offset 0x04.
2. Store the original address as a word at the current publication anchor plus 0x2c.
3. Store a character conversion of eight times the publication working quantity at the original address plus 0x1b.
4. Retain a publication base obtained by signed-right-shifting a subsequent publication-anchor observation by 16 positions.
5. Capture the transformation-input word at the retained base plus 0x54.
6. Store a publication-anchor observation at the retained base plus eight times the **same publication working quantity used in step 3**.
7. Retain the signed secondary word at the secondary-source address plus 0x6c.

Thus, the two multiplications by eight use an identical working quantity. Publication stores may affect later reads from overlapping memory, but they do not establish a change to that register-like quantity.

By contrast, the publication anchor is not one immutable capture throughout this sequence. Earlier writes can affect its subsequently observed stack storage. The anchor stored by the indexed publication write therefore need not equal the anchor used to derive the retained base. Relative evaluation order between the contextual operands of that indexed write is not established.

Admission uses the retained publication base and the indication, interpreted as zero versus nonzero:

| Retained publication base | Zero indication | Nonzero indication |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | Admit | Reject |
| Nonnegative | Reject | Admit |

Rejection requests software interrupt 0x10. If that interaction returns, an invalid-instruction endpoint follows. This route makes no local transformation call and constructs no record. Completed publication effects remain.

### Admitted preliminary effects

An admitted invocation passes the captured transformation-input word to F-fbf12e72fd4b and receives a represented 64-bit result. Its low 32 bits are explicitly retained for possible three-word construction.

After that dependency returns, the following order is established:

1. Form the 32-bit selection by shifting the post-call working quantity left by six positions.
2. Store a character conversion of the complete transformation result at the original address plus 0x0c.
3. Capture the signed replacement seed from address 0x083fedb4.
4. Examine construction control.

The post-call working quantity may differ from the publication working quantity; preservation across F-fbf12e72fd4b is not established.

Zero construction control bypasses record construction, completion dependencies, and the coprocessor interaction. It does not bypass selection formation, the admitted character write, or replacement-seed capture. It proceeds to final exceptional dispatch.

Enabled construction also derives a compact address by signed-right-shifting the original address by 28 positions.

### Route selection

Under conventional two’s-complement interpretation of the represented 32-bit subtraction-overflow decisions, classification has this partition:

| Initial selection bit pattern | Construction route |
|---|---|
| 0 through 233 | Ordinary four-word |
| 234 | Replacement four-word |
| 235 through 0x7fffffff | Three-word |
| 0x80000000 through 0xffffffff | Ordinary four-word |

This classification is not a remainder test. Its unsigned reduction can require many iterations for small selections or selections with the high bit set. No useful latency guarantee is established.

Defined preceding signed selection formation permits only nonnegative multiples of 64, no greater than 0x7fffffc0. Consequently:

- Post-call working quantities zero through three select ordinary four-word construction.
- Post-call working quantities four through 33,554,431 select three-word construction.
- Selection 234 and high-bit-set selections cannot arise through defined preceding selection formation.

Replacement behavior is therefore conditional. Its presence in the classifier does not establish that a completely defined invocation can reach it.

### Three-word construction

The compact address is an absolute destination address, not an offset within another object.

| Offset | Width | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 0x00 | 32 bits | Compact address |
| 0x04 | 32 bits | Retained secondary word |
| 0x08 | 32 bits | Explicitly extracted low word of the transformation result |

The writes occur in ascending offset order. This route performs no preparation-halfword write, payload lookup, or fourth-field write.

After the fields, F-000000000003 is invoked. If it returns, software interrupt 0x10 is requested. A returning interrupt reaches the invalid-instruction endpoint.

No continuation from this route into four-word construction, the coprocessor interaction, or final exceptional dispatch is established.

### Ordinary four-word construction

Ordinary construction uses the original transformation result as its active 64-bit value and the retained publication base as its payload base.

The unsigned upper word of the active value is converted to a signed 32-bit destination address. Preparation and payload acquisition precede the record fields:

1. Write a signed-halfword conversion of the active value’s unsigned upper word at the destination signed-right-shifted by three positions, plus 0x24.
2. Read a signed halfword at the payload base plus the destination signed-right-shifted by three positions.
3. Sign-extend that halfword to a 32-bit address representation and retain the word at that address as the payload.

The preparation address does not include the payload base. The lookup halfword supplies the complete payload address, not an offset relative to the payload base.

The record is:

| Offset | Width | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 0x00 | 32 bits | Destination address |
| 0x04 | 32 bits | Construction control observed after the first field write |
| 0x08 | 32 bits | Retained payload |
| 0x0c | 32 bits | Signed-word conversion of the complete active 64-bit value |

Fields are written in ascending offset order. An overlapping first-field write can affect the control observed for the second field. The third field uses the already acquired payload.

The fourth field is not specified as the explicit low-word extraction used by three-word construction. Its out-of-range conversion behavior depends on the environment’s conversion rules.

### Conditional replacement construction

Replacement first invokes F-000000000002 with the compact address and the retained secondary word signed-right-shifted by 24 positions. Its represented 64-bit result becomes the active value.

The previously captured replacement seed is multiplied by eight to obtain the replacement location. Software interrupt 0x10 is then requested. Subsequent replacement effects require that interrupt to return.

The following effects occur in order:

1. Write the original address at the replacement location plus 0x08.
2. Overwrite that same word with the unsigned upper word of the replacement result.
3. Derive the replacement payload base as four times the sum of the replacement location and 0x6a.
4. Store a signed-halfword conversion of the complete replacement result at a replacement-address observation plus 0x30.
5. Assign construction control the value of a subsequent replacement-adjustment observation plus 127.
6. Retain a later replacement-address observation for the coprocessor interaction.

There is no guarantee that another observer sees the first of the two replacement-location writes. The replacement seed is not reread.

The two replacement-address observations need not agree: the intervening halfword write or control assignment may overlap contextual stack storage. Likewise, the halfword write can affect the adjustment read afterward.

Common four-word preparation, payload acquisition, and field writes then use the replacement active value and replacement payload base. Construction control is not checked again. Assigning zero does not cancel construction.

### Completion and final exceptional dispatch

Ordinary four-word construction invokes F-000000000004 after its fields. Replacement four-word construction invokes F-000000000006 instead.

If the applicable completion returns, a coprocessor interaction uses selectors 9, 7, and 5. Its address-role operand is:

- The retained original address for ordinary construction.
- The later retained replacement address for replacement construction.

The two opaque auxiliary operands are supplied after completion returns. Their relative observation order and preservation across completion are unresolved.

Final exceptional dispatch is reached when construction is disabled, or after the four-word coprocessor interaction if execution continues. It uses selector 0x6d and associated value 0x083feab8.

The representation marks this dispatch as nonreturning while also representing invocation through its supplied callable result. If that callable continuation occurs, it is designated F-000000000005, with no explicit arguments established. Ordinary dispatch return, successful target delivery, and behavior after a returning target remain unresolved.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes valid reached storage, applicable arithmetic and conversion rules, and returning interactions wherever continuation is described.

1. **Unsigned target wraparound.** An initial-target seed of 0xfffffff0 selects address representation 0x00000810 after alignment-bit clearing and addition.

2. **One publication working quantity.** With publication working quantity three, the publication character conversion uses 24, and the indexed word write uses retained publication base plus 24. Intervening publication writes do not justify substituting a different working quantity for the indexed write.

3. **Stack observations can differ.** If the publication character write overlaps publication-anchor storage, the subsequently retained base can reflect the changed anchor. This does not change the register-like publication working quantity.

4. **Publication precedes rejection.** A nonnegative retained publication base with zero indication rejects only after publication and secondary-word capture. It makes no local transformation call. A negative retained base with the same indication admits.

5. **Captured input is not refreshed.** If indexed publication changes the transformation-input source, F-fbf12e72fd4b still receives the earlier captured word. If the indexed write changes the secondary-word source, the later secondary capture can observe that change.

6. **Dependency preservation is not assumed.** If F-fbf12e72fd4b changes the working quantity from its publication value to four, subsequent defined selection formation produces 256. Publication still used its earlier, internally consistent quantity.

7. **Disabled construction retains preliminary effects.** Zero construction control still follows selection formation, the character write at original address plus 0x0c, and replacement-seed capture. It bypasses records, completion dependencies, and the coprocessor interaction, but reaches final exceptional dispatch.

8. **Defined route boundary.** Post-call working quantities zero, three, and four produce selections zero, 192, and 256. With construction enabled, the first two select ordinary four-word construction; the third selects three-word construction.

9. **Classifier behavior is not reachability.** Conditional classifier inputs 234, 235, and 0x80000000 select replacement, three-word, and ordinary four-word construction respectively. The first and third cannot result from defined preceding selection formation.

10. **Compact destination is absolute.** With the applicable signed-shift interpretation, original address 0x40000000 gives compact address four. Three-word fields occupy addresses four, eight, and twelve if those accesses are valid.

11. **Preparation differs from lookup.** Destination 0x800 and payload base 0x3000 give preparation address 0x124 and lookup address 0x3100. A lookup halfword of 0x1800 identifies payload address 0x1800, not 0x4800.

12. **Replacement retains ordering.** Changing address 0x083fedb4 after capture does not change the replacement location. Following a returning replacement interrupt, its word at offset 0x08 receives the original address before the replacement result’s upper word.

13. **Replacement-assigned zero does not cancel construction.** Replacement adjustment −127 assigns zero to construction control. Four-word construction and F-000000000006 still follow. The control field is zero if no intervening overlap changes the observed control.

14. **Terminal routes differ.** Returning three-word completion leads to interrupt 0x10 and potentially the invalid-instruction endpoint. Returning four-word completion leads to the coprocessor interaction and, if execution continues, final exceptional dispatch.
