# F-2b1e1009dc44

## Purpose

F-2b1e1009dc44 accepts one opaque 32-bit value, performs an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect invocations and integer transformations, modifies computed-address storage and named globals, and reaches a final four-argument indirect handoff.

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated offsets, self-valued stores, and character-sized constants do not establish text processing, object initialization, list maintenance, or resource release.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented accesses, conversions, value relationships, and ordering.
- **[INFERENCE]:** possible intent that adds no acceptance requirement.

All effects are conditional on execution reaching them. The supplied representation does not establish successful execution of the complete path in any particular environment.

## Behavior

### Indirect invocation phase

The first target is derived by adding 0xFFE to incoming control-link state. Later targets are similarly derived from embedded locations. Dependency identities and calling conventions are unresolved.

The explicit input is forwarded before the first directly represented data-memory mutation. This does not promise unchanged memory beforehand: dependencies may modify memory and globals.

No explicit conditional branch or loop appears in this phase. Subject to normal returns, invocations occur in represented order. Calls remain significant when no explicit arguments appear or their primary results are unused.

Argument relationships include:

- Thirty-two-bit results shifted left by eight.
- Shifted low words paired with unchanged high words.
- Shifted low words paired with separately shifted high words.
- Explicit zeros and repeated copies of saved values.
- Results retained across intervening invocations.
- Secondary result-channel values consumed later.

An unchanged high word is not interchangeable with a shifted high word. Reusing a captured result is not equivalent to invoking a dependency again. Individual invocation-site contracts are not exhaustively catalogued here and remain documented_only.

### Integer interpretation and extraction

Transformations include masks, logical shifts, multiplication by powers of two, and subtraction of 0x96. Extraction masks retain widths including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits.

Logical right shifts of 64-bit results by 34, 36, and 44 retain at most 30, 28, and 20 bits respectively. They are not unchanged-high-word extraction.

Under a **32-bit wrapping bit-vector interpretation**:

- Left shift by eight followed by logical right shift by eight retains the original low 24 bits.
- Two successive left shifts by 28 produce zero.
- Two successive left shifts by 14 retain only the original low four bits, positioned at bits 28–31.
- A 19-bit-masked value shifted left by two is at most 0x001FFFFC; subsequent logical right shift by 23 yields zero.
- That zero conclusion does not hold for an arbitrary 24-bit-masked value.
- A word shifted left by 16 has a zero low halfword.

The represented zero-producing extraction uses the 19-bit bound.

Some expressions use signed interpretations. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, character-sized conversions, and actual machine shift semantics remain documented_only where not otherwise established. Conditional bit-vector conclusions do not resolve those execution semantics.

One dependency result is shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, and logically shifted right by six. This contract calls the result the **extracted value**. Under wrapping interpretation, it is four times the dependency result’s low seven bits:

| Quantity | Conditional possibilities |
|---|---|
| Extracted value | Multiples of four from 0 through 508. |
| Doubled extracted value | Multiples of eight from 0 through 1016. |
| Extracted value’s low byte | A multiple of four. |
| Doubled value’s low byte | A multiple of eight. |

These bounds constrain retained bits, not address validity or words later read through those addresses.

### Opening mutations and early global accesses

The last invocation before direct mutation supplies low and high result words. Earlier results supply other bases and payloads.

The opening computed word store through the high result word at offset 0x14 precedes a byte store through an earlier shifted-result base at offset 0x09. A word store through the low result word scaled by 0x100, at offset 0x54, intervenes before a second byte store to that same byte destination.

A computed offset-0x14 word is cleared before an explicit unsigned-byte read at offset 0x05 through that base. The byte is zero-extended for a computed word store at offset 0x34. A separate offset-0x14 word through the doubled extracted value is cleared twice, with an intervening word read through the other base. Two consecutive computed byte stores then target offset 0x15 through the doubled extracted value.

The intervening word read cannot automatically be replaced with zero: intervening writes may alias its source.

Next, the global associated with 0x05 is read and converted to unsigned 32-bit form. After that capture:

- The global associated with 0x01 is assigned an explicitly eight-bit-converted doubled extracted value.
- A computed byte destination at global-derived-base offset 0x15 receives the extracted value’s low byte.
- Global-derived-base offset 0x54 is subsequently read and replaced using explicit word accesses.

The first effect has an eight-bit **assignment payload**, not an established eight-bit global destination.

The publication base is captured once, not at entry and not separately for each group. Each publication group orders:

2. A byte store at publication-base offset 0x19 carrying the scratch base’s low byte.

### Non-byte arithmetic at the 0x3A-associated global
