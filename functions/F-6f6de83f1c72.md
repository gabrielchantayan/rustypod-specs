# F-6f6de83f1c72

No application-level purpose is established. The evidence does not identify a string operation, collection, object constructor, serialization format, or hardware protocol. Repeated writes and link-following accesses are behavioral facts, not evidence of a particular data structure.

## Interface

## Behavior

### Dependency interaction and arithmetic model

The dependency phase is straight-line in the supplied representation. It contains ordered indirect calls with zero to four represented arguments; no retry, loop, or data-dependent target selection is represented. Most targets are position-associated addresses displaced by 4,094 bytes.

Arguments combine constants, dependency results, secondary register outputs, masks, shifts, and extracted fields. A call whose result is unused remains behaviorally relevant: its effects and termination behavior are unknown.

For combined results, the lower component is bits 0–31 and the upper component is bits 32–63. Logical shifts of a combined result by 34, 36, or 44 consequently correspond to logical shifts of its upper component by 2, 4, or 12. This decomposition establishes no memory endianness.

Where a conditional example uses **32-bit bit-vector arithmetic**, truncation is modulo \(2^{32}\). That model does not establish the executable meaning of signed overflow, signed left shifts, integer-to-address conversion, or address wrapping.

### Initial destinations and the zero invariant

The memory phase retains several dependency-derived quantities:

- A **shifted token** formed from a result’s lower word shifted left by 25.
- A **shifted destination** formed from another word multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- A **saved token** formed from another word shifted left by 8.
- A **small field** formed by shifting a word left by 8, keeping its low 15 bits, and logically shifting right by 6. The **small destination** is twice that field.
- The final combined dependency result, whose upper and lower components supply additional destinations.

Under the bit-vector model, the small field is four times the dependency word’s low seven bits. Its range is therefore 0–508 in multiples of four; the small destination is 0–1,016 in multiples of eight. These are arithmetic ranges, not validity guarantees.

The opening memory effects associate the shifted token with the final result’s upper-component destination at offset 20, and the shifted destination with 256 times the final result’s lower component at offset 84. Both are word writes. Two byte writes target shifted-token offset 9: the earlier uses the final upper component’s low byte, and the later uses the shifted token’s low byte. The latter is zero under bit-vector arithmetic.

A word at shifted-destination offset 20 is cleared and subsequently reread. Between that clear and capture, the represented memory operations are a byte read at shifted-destination offset 5, a word clear at small-destination offset 20, and a zero-extended word write of the byte at shifted-destination offset 52.

**[INFERENCE]: Under ordinary coherent byte-addressed memory, defined address arithmetic, no side-effecting accesses, and no external modification, the captured cleared word is zero.** Ordinary overlap with the small-destination clear cannot introduce nonzero bits. The offset-52 write is disjoint from the offset-20 word. No dependency call intervenes.

The small-destination word is cleared again, and two subsequent byte writes at its offset 21 use the captured cleared word’s low byte. Both therefore write zero under those assumptions. Nonzero capture requires behavior outside those assumptions, not merely ordinary overlap.

The value at absolute address 5 is converted to unsigned 32 bits and retained as an address basis. It is not established as an eight-bit value. Its offset-84 word is captured as the **anchor** before being overwritten with the captured cleared word.

The ordering around this capture matters:

- Address 5 is read before the small destination is narrowed to eight bits and assigned at address 1.
- A byte write at address-5-capture offset 21 uses the small field’s low byte.
- Before the anchor read, shifted-destination offset 17 receives the saved token’s low byte and then zero. Between those byte writes, a word write places the saved token at captured-cleared-word offset 84.
- Small-destination offset 52 receives the small field after the anchor capture and overwrite.

Under the zero invariant, the saved-token word write begins at absolute address 84. The anchor is nevertheless not necessarily zero: it is read before its own clearing overwrite, and prior writes can overlap its source.

Subsequent mutations use offset-116 links through the shifted and small destinations. The shifted-destination link is captured; the small-destination self-valued link expression contains two separate reads. A single universally cached small-destination link is not established, nor is an evaluation order between independent operand reads within that expression.

This phase includes two self-valued word writes at anchor offset 36, two byte writes at captured-shifted-link offset 9 using the anchor’s low byte, and offset-84 writes through links. ## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

- **Opening forwarding:** Arbitrary explicit 32-bit input patterns reach F-dep-001 unchanged. Under bit-vector arithmetic, an implicit value of 1 produces 4,194,304 in each remaining opening position; 1,024 produces zero.
- **Byte-to-word conversion:** An explicit offset-5 byte observation of 255 supplies word value 255 to the corresponding shifted-destination offset-52 write.
- **Overlapping clears:** Under the ordinary-memory assumptions, partial or complete overlap between the two offset-20 clears still yields captured word zero. The two following small-destination offset-21 byte writes therefore receive zero.
- **Small-field boundary:** Under bit-vector arithmetic, dependency words with low seven bits 0 and 127 produce small destinations 0 and 1,016, respectively. Neither result implies an accessible address.
- **Unknown global width:** If declarations independently establish byte-sized unsigned storage at address 5, value 7 produces calculated addresses 28 and 91 for its offset-21 byte write and offset-84 word access. Without those declarations, no 0–255 range is promised.

- **Retained destination:** A global-destination capture of 4,096 continues to determine repeated-region destinations even if storage at 0x087fbbbc subsequently changes.
- **Final provenance:** Assuming valid, nonoverlapping accesses and representable arithmetic, earlier and later offset-100 values of 4,096 and 8,192 yield a word containing 4,096 at address 8,276 and a word containing 8,192 at address 8,192. Final argument 2 remains 4,096.
