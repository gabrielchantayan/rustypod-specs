# F-6f25363c06e5

## Purpose

F-6f25363c06e5 coordinates destination-directed interactions with a bounded relative-position request and selects one of two completion operations. Its normal result is a 32-bit value equal to either 0 or 1, identifying the completion path taken. Neither value is established as application-level success or failure.

The bounds policy is **displacement rejection, not position clamping**. An initial candidate determines whether the requested displacement remains effective. If the candidate violates either signed bound, the effective displacement becomes zero. Later operations calculate their position arguments from fresh base-position reads; those positions are not checked against the bounds again.

“State,” “destination,” “base position,” and “auxiliary value” are structural descriptions. Their application-level meanings are unknown. The relative-position interpretation is inferred from the arithmetic and comparisons, not from an established domain model.

| Destination | Opaque 32-bit value | Repeatedly passed to dependencies and initially copied into a local cell. |

The destination is not directly dereferenced by this function. Whether it represents an address, handle, or another kind of value is **documented_only**.

The function creates two distinct, addressable, 32-bit local cells. The destination-initialized cell is populated first, followed by the auxiliary-initialized cell, before any dependency call. These are value copies. They do not establish a deep copy, ownership transfer, or extension of any referenced resource’s lifetime.

There is no direct modification of the caller’s argument storage. Replacing the effective displacement with zero changes the function’s internal value, not the caller’s displacement argument.

## Behavior

### Effects before the bounds decision

Destination interaction is unconditional with respect to bounds acceptance. Before the first base or bound read, F-8c34796da81c receives the auxiliary-initialized cell’s address and two zero arguments. Its 32-bit result supplies the second argument of a call to F-5399f0fd65ec, whose first argument is the original destination. F-e0bf74703fb9 subsequently receives the same cell’s address.

These calls can have observable effects even when the requested displacement is later rejected. Their effects are not characterized as assignment, destruction, clearing, or release. In particular, they may affect the state that the bounds decision will subsequently read.

### Bounds policy

The candidate used for the decision is the initial base-position read plus the requested displacement.

The candidate is rejected when it is greater than the signed upper bound or less than the signed lower bound. Equality at either endpoint is accepted by that comparison. The upper-bound comparison is evaluated first; an upper-bound rejection prevents the lower-bound read for this decision.

An accepted candidate preserves the requested displacement. A rejected candidate selects zero as the effective displacement. The function does not clamp the candidate to an endpoint.

There is no independent validation of bound ordering, no requirement enforced here that the base already lies within the interval, and no validation of the zero-displacement fallback. With stable inverted bounds and representable arithmetic, every candidate is rejected.

The effective displacement remains the same for both subsequent position computations. The original candidate is not reused as a fixed position.

### Position-dependent destination preparation

F-4c3eb0b87e54 receives four arguments, in this order:

1. The address of the auxiliary-initialized cell.
2. The state’s interior address at offset `0x01C`.
3. A fresh base-position read plus the effective displacement.
4. The opaque 32-bit field at offset `0x8D0`.

The position may differ from the initial candidate because the base is read again. No bounds read or comparison accompanies this new position.

After that dependency returns, F-5399f0fd65ec receives the original destination and the auxiliary cell’s address. F-e0bf74703fb9 then receives that address.

The second argument supplied to F-5399f0fd65ec here is therefore an address, whereas its initial second argument was the result of F-8c34796da81c. The required interpretation and compatibility of those argument forms belong to the dependency contract and are unknown.

The function does not directly reinitialize the auxiliary cell between its earlier use and F-4c3eb0b87e54. Its contents at each later interaction depend on earlier dependencies; preservation or destruction must not be presumed.

### Completion selection

Selection occurs after position-dependent preparation and its associated destination and local-cell interactions.

F-be88abc7b50c receives the original destination. A zero result selects the zero-return completion path immediately: neither a second call to that dependency nor a call to F-c1687db9b7ed occurs.

A nonzero first result requires a second call to F-be88abc7b50c with the same destination value. The second result is ignored. F-c1687db9b7ed is then called without explicit arguments. Its zero result selects the zero-return path; any nonzero result selects the one-return path.

The second F-be88abc7b50c call remains required even though its result is unused. Its side effects may affect F-c1687db9b7ed or later completion behavior. Neither tested result needs to equal 1; the distinction is zero versus nonzero.

### Completion outcomes

On the **one-return path**, F-edf86093a19f receives the state reference and a position calculated from another fresh base-position read plus the effective displacement. After that dependency returns normally, the function returns 1. The final destination interaction described below does not occur.

On the **zero-return path**, F-52cae007644e receives the state reference and a position calculated from another fresh base-position read plus the effective displacement. After it returns, a final destination interaction uses the destination-initialized local cell: F-8c34796da81c receives that cell’s address and two zeros; its result supplies the second argument to F-5399f0fd65ec, with the original destination as the first argument; F-e0bf74703fb9 then receives the cell’s address. Normal completion of these interactions is followed by return of 0.

Exactly one of the two completion dependencies is reached during normally progressing execution. Its position is not revalidated, even if dependencies have changed the base or bounds.

## Side effects

### Direct and delegated effects

The function directly initializes its two local cells and may replace its internal effective displacement with zero. No direct state-field write or direct destination dereference is established.

Externally meaningful state and destination changes are delegated. The absence of a direct write does not imply that state, destination-associated storage, globals, or aliased memory remain unchanged.

The original destination value is reused throughout the invocation. Reuse of that value does not establish stability of an object or resource it might identify.

### Observable ordering and reads

The initial destination interaction and associated local-cell interaction finish before the bounds decision reads the base or bounds. The initial base read precedes the upper-bound comparison, and the lower-bound read is conditional on that comparison not rejecting the candidate.

Bounds selection finishes before position-dependent preparation. Preparation and its following destination and local-cell interactions finish before the first completion-gate call. When reached, the second gate call precedes F-c1687db9b7ed. The selected completion follows the gate. Only the zero-return path performs the final destination interaction, after F-52cae007644e.

Normal execution contains three distinct base reads: one for bounds selection, one for preparation, and one for the selected completion. There is no atomic snapshot of state.

The supplied representation does not establish an order between independent argument-expression reads within a single call. In particular, the relative order of the fresh base read and the opaque-field read for F-4c3eb0b87e54 is unresolved. Concurrent or asynchronous mutation cannot be assigned stronger ordering or consistency guarantees from this evidence.

### Ownership, lifetime, and abnormal exits

The local cells provide addressable storage during the invocation. Passing their addresses does not establish permission to retain or use them after that storage ceases to exist. Actual retention behavior, escape restrictions, ownership transfer, allocation, reference counting, and release semantics remain unknown.

No recovery handler, rollback, or compensating action is visible. If a dependency does not return normally, subsequent interactions and the final result are not guaranteed. The function does not visibly interpret dependency failures as a separate error outcome.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional consequences of the static contract, **not runtime test results**. Unless an example states otherwise, assume valid inputs, normally returning dependencies, stable relevant state fields, and representable arithmetic.

### Inclusive bounds

For base 20, lower bound 10, upper bound 30, and displacement 5, the displacement remains 5. Preparation and completion receive position 25.

Displacements −10 and 10 also remain effective, producing positions 10 and 30 respectively. Endpoint equality does not cause rejection.

### Rejection without clamping

With the same base and bounds, displacement 11 produces candidate 31 and is replaced by zero. Later position arguments are 20, not 30. Displacement −11 likewise produces later positions of 20, not 10.

The initial destination and local-cell interactions still occur before either rejection.

### Unchecked fallback

For base 40, bounds 10 through 30, and displacement 5, the candidate is rejected. Later position arguments are nevertheless 40. The fallback position does not undergo a second bounds check.

### Inverted interval and conditional reads

For stable lower bound 30 and upper bound 10, every representable candidate is rejected. A candidate above 10 is rejected without reading the lower-bound field for the decision. A candidate at or below 10 reaches the lower-bound comparison and fails it.

### Zero first gate result

A zero first result from F-be88abc7b50c requires the zero-return path. There is no second F-be88abc7b50c call and no F-c1687db9b7ed call. F-52cae007644e precedes the final destination interaction, and normal completion returns 0.

### Discarded second gate result

If the first F-be88abc7b50c result is nonzero, the second is zero, and F-c1687db9b7ed returns nonzero, the one-return path is selected. F-edf86093a19f is called and normal completion returns 1. The second gate result does not override this choice.

If F-c1687db9b7ed instead returns zero, the zero-return path and its final destination interaction are required.

### Base changes between stages

Suppose base 20 and displacement 5 pass the bounds decision, and the base remains 20 until preparation. F-4c3eb0b87e54 receives position 25. If a later dependency changes the base to 100 before completion’s position is evaluated, the selected completion dependency receives 105. No repeated bounds decision prevents that value.

### Signed arithmetic boundary

Base 2,147,483,647 plus displacement 1 is outside the representable signed 32-bit range. This contract does not prescribe a wrapped candidate, completion path, or normal return for that case.
