## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not observed executions.

1. **Loss of seed high bits.**  
   If the seed is 134,217,728, its 32-bit left shift by 5 produces zero. The third argument at order 2 and the second argument at order 10 are therefore zero under the stated arithmetic model.

2. **Arithmetic right-shift distinction.**  
   If the seed is 67,108,864, shifting left 5 produces the bit pattern with only bit 31 set. Arithmetic right shift by 5 yields signed −67,108,864 at order 10, not the original positive seed. A reconstruction that simply forwards the seed would differ.

3. **Configuration narrowing.**  
   If the configuration source is 1, order 16 receives 134,217,728 as its third argument. Transfer 5 and both arguments of order 26 nevertheless receive zero. Order 31’s second argument is also zero.

4. **Working-result shifts.**  
   If the working result is 1 and the auxiliary result is 2, order 31 receives 2, zero, 16, zero. Transfer 10 receives 4096. Order 35 receives 4096, 65,536, and 1024.

5. **Terminal address boundaries.**  
   If bits 52–63 of the terminal result are zero, the final store targets address zero. If those bits are all set, it targets address 5118. Both stores remain conditional on all intervening operations completing and the address being writable.

6. **Noncontiguous address mapping.**  
   Terminal-result upper fields of 3 and 4 produce addresses 3 and 5, respectively. The address mapping is not an unbroken sequence of byte addresses.

7. **Earlier failure.**
