### Arithmetic interpretation

Wide results are separated numerically into low and high 32-bit portions. This does not establish their in-memory byte order.

Transformations include left shifts, unsigned right shifts, masks, multiplication, addition, and subtraction. Retaining 20 bits and shifting right by ten selects bits 10 through 19. Unsigned right shifts of 64-bit containers by 34, 36, and 44 bits also occur. A 44-bit shift selects the upper 20 bits of a 64-bit quantity; it is not an oversized 32-bit shift.

**Conditional 32-bit modular interpretation:** Arithmetic results retain their low 32 bits. Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and left shifts wrap accordingly.

Consecutive left shifts agree under intermediate truncation and final-only truncation when only the final low 32 bits are considered. In particular, shifting a 32-bit value left by 25 and then by seven produces zero after final 32-bit truncation.

That equivalence does not permit changing an intermediate argument observed by a dependency. It also does not extend to arbitrary mixtures of left and right shifts. One represented path retains a low result word shifted left by eight in an unsigned 32-bit quantity, then later shifts that quantity right by eight. The result preserves only the original low 24 bits.

Signed-looking expressions require separate caution. ## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Entry transformation.** An incoming fourth-argument value of one produces 0x80 in both represented positions of the first call under 32-bit shift semantics.

2. **Consecutive left shifts.** Any 32-bit value shifted left by 25 and then seven yields zero after final 32-bit truncation. This does not remove an independently observed intermediate argument.

3. **Discarded high bits.** For 0x01000000, unsigned 32-bit left shift by eight yields zero; subsequent right shift by eight remains zero. Preserving unlimited-precision high bits across both shifts would incorrectly recover 0x01000000.

4. **Small-index boundary.** An all-ones input to the small-index transformation produces 508. Twice that value plus 0x14 is 0x40C, without any allocated base.

5. **Unsigned byte expansion.** A byte value of 0xE1 fetched at scaled base plus five becomes the word 0x000000E1, not a sign-extended word.

6. **Global-derived addressing.** If the symbolic global converts to 0x1234, its offset-0x15 and offset-0x54 accesses use 0x1249 and 0x1288. Truncating the base to 0x34 is unsupported.

7. **Association coincidence.** Retained association value 50 produces adjusted association value 50 and adjusted base 200. The first and later scaled destinations both equal 400, but their write events remain distinct.

8. **Association difference.** Retained association value 51 produces adjusted association value 54 and adjusted base 216. The first scaled destination is 408; later destinations are 432.

9. **Constrained variable counts.** Retained association values six, seven, eight, and five produce shift counts eight, 24, 40, and 248 respectively. Count 40 is possible and exceeds 31. Counts zero, four, 16, and 252 are incompatible with the adjusted-base formula.

10. **Unresolved shift result.** For retained association value 50, the count is 200. No resulting byte is prescribed without the missing oversized-shift semantics; silently replacing 200 with eight is unsupported.

11. **Overlapping stores.** A word clear followed by two byte writes at its second byte comprises three stores. Numerical interpretation of the resulting word depends on byte order.

12. **Intermediate constants.** With completing, noninterfering accesses, the adjusted offset-0x20 sequence ends at 0x20. Earlier 0x2F and byte-derived stores remain part of the behavior.

13. **Interrupted execution.** Rejection of a low-address access may prevent completion. No handled failure or reversal of earlier effects is established.

14. **Final transfer.** If reached, the transfer receives 0x72, the saved offset-0x64 word, the earlier saved offset-0x14 word, and the later offset-0x74 word, in that order.
