### 2. Arithmetic constraints

The representation uses word-sized operations, unsigned masks, explicit narrowing, and unsigned 64-bit extraction. It also contains signed-looking shifts and arithmetic whose exact machine interpretation is not independently established.

Useful constraints include:

- A twenty-bit mask followed by a logical right shift of ten yields at most 1023.
- A twenty-four-bit mask followed by a logical right shift of twenty yields at most 15.
- A nineteen-bit mask followed by a left shift of two yields at most 0x1FFFFC. A subsequent logical right shift of twenty-three or twenty-four therefore yields zero.
- Explicitly initialized zero values remain zero through the shown shifts until reassigned.

Thus a word shifted left twenty-five bits and then left seven bits becomes zero, rather than an unbounded integer multiplied by \(2^{32}\). A word shifted left sixteen bits has a zero low halfword under that same interpretation.
