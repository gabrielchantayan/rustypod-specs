### Rounded counts and product bit length

The function processes state field `0x36` before state field `0x34`.

For each field, the representation contains two unsigned 16-bit reads:

- The first supplies a value shifted right by four, producing an unsigned 32-bit count.
- The second is compared with 16 times that count.
- If they differ, the count is incremented.

For a stable field, this computes its ceiling after division by 16. Zero remains zero. A value of `65535` produces `4096`.

If the two reads disagree, the result is determined by the first read’s quotient and the second read’s equality comparison; it is not necessarily the ceiling of either observed value. Even then, each count remains between `0` and `4096`.

The two unsigned counts are multiplied. Their maximum product is `16,777,216`, so the product is representable in both unsigned and signed 32-bit arithmetic. It is then processed as a signed, nonnegative 32-bit value:

- Start the bit count at zero.
- Repeatedly shift the product right by one until zero, increasing the bit count once per shift.

The result is zero for a zero product and the ordinary binary bit length otherwise. Its maximum is `25`.
