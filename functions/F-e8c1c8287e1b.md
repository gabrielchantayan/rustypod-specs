# F-e8c1c8287e1b

## Behavior

### Ordering and recurring access forms

The displayed body contains no branches or loops. It presents one straight-line sequence, conditional on preceding accesses succeeding and calls returning.

Calls are observable boundaries. Direct accesses must retain their displayed order relative to calls. In particular, a read cannot be moved across a dependency call on an assumption that memory or globals remain unchanged.

Two recurring forms are:

- **Scaled-slot publication:** a 32-bit write at `0x14` plus a scaled index. Scales include 4, 16, and 256.
- **Byte-selected write:** read an unsigned selector byte, then write a byte at its numeric value plus `0x11`.

A byte-selected destination lies between `0x11` and `0x110`. The selector’s own address need not lie there. Repeated selector accesses are fresh reads except where retention is expressly described below.

### Initial publications and extractions

Before any dependency call, the function stores the input word at four times the input word plus `0x14`.

It then reads an unsigned word from the entry-context base plus `0x14`. The first call receives the input word and that read shifted right by 19.

Following calls, a returned index selects another four-scaled slot receiving the input word. A later returned index produces a fourfold intermediate, stored at sixteen times that index plus `0x14`. A fresh word at the intermediate plus `0x14`, shifted right by 19, is passed onward with the intermediate and zero. Further calls precede publication of the retained intermediate into another four-scaled slot.

A subsequent unsigned result supplies distinct derived values:

- Its left shift by two, truncated for a selected-byte store.
- Its low 30 bits shifted right by 20, truncated for another selected-byte store.
- Its low 30 bits shifted right by 17.
- Its low 30 bits shifted right by 7.

The first two stores separately read the entry-context selector, with calls between them. The latter two extractions become dependency arguments; substituting the original full-width result changes the interface.

### Early selector clearing and data reads

The next portions interleave calls with selected zero-byte writes. Selector addresses derive from retained results, extractions, secondary results, and absolute address `1`.

Aggregate results provide low halves retained for address arithmetic and high halves passed independently to later calls. Some calls repeat a value in several argument positions; those repetitions remain observable.

Distinct direct data reads include:

- A byte at twice a retained unsigned word.
- A word at a retained unsigned word plus that word shifted right by 19 and then left by 31.
- A byte at a secondary-result base plus `0x0e`.
- A byte at a retained base plus a secondary result.
- A halfword at an extracted word plus a previously read unsigned byte.

Calls and selector writes separate these reads. Several read values subsequently occupy all four argument positions of a call.

Under 32-bit modular arithmetic, the left-shift-by-31 address contribution is either zero or `0x80000000`, determined by bit 19 of the retained word. It is not an unbounded offset.

One call receives the same stack-relative address four times. The accessible extent, initialization, possible modifications, and escape lifetime of that region are not established.

### Linked state and offset `0x24`

The link becomes a retained base. After calls, a returned unsigned word shifted right by 20 is stored at that base plus `0x24`. A fresh word at the base plus `0x74` is passed to the following dependency.

After further calls, the byte at the retained base plus `1` selects a computed-byte write. Its data comes from an earlier unsigned result masked to 30 bits and shifted right by 20, then truncated to eight bits. Further calls precede another read of that selector and a selected zero-byte write.

The computed byte need not be nonzero. Separately, the unsigned right-shift-by-20 extraction is at most `0xfff`, so shifting that extraction right by 21 produces zero.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. Numeric examples assume accessible storage and arithmetic without the unresolved overflow cases.

1. **Initial order:** Input `3` first stores the 32-bit value `3` at `0x20`, before any call. Input zero instead selects `0x14`; no guard suppresses it.

2. **Unsigned selector and truncation:** Selector `0xff` selects `0x110`. A selected-byte store whose data is `0x104` writes `0x04`, not a word.

3. **Fresh ordinary selector:** If intervening calls change an ordinarily reread selector from `2` to `5`, successive selected destinations are `0x13` and `0x16`.

4. **Computed linked byte:** An extraction of `0x100` becomes stored byte zero. A nonzero extraction does not imply a nonzero byte.

5. **Pre-reset computed read:** If its returned index is `3`, the store writes `12` at `0x44`. The fresh word read is at `0x20`, not `0x44`. If that word is `0xffe00000`, the next call receives sole argument `0x7ff`.

6. **First post-reset computed read:** For index `4`, the store writes `16` at `0x54`; the fresh read is at `0x24`. If the read yields `0x80600000`, the next call’s arguments are `16`, `16`, and `0x403`.

7. **Second post-reset computed read:** For index `5`, the store writes `20` at `0x64`; the fresh read is at `0x28`. A read of `0xffffffff` produces arguments `0`, `0`, `0`, and `0x7ff`, because masking precedes the right shift.

8. **Call-relative freshness:** If calls before any of those three occurrences modify its eventual read location, the occurrence uses the word present when its direct read is reached. A replacement cannot derive that word from the sixteen-scaled store.

9. **Reset is not persistence:** A dependency change after the explicit shared reset is visible to a later direct read of that location.

10. **Retained late selector:** A late snapshot of `2` clears `0x13`. Even if calls change absolute address `1` to `5`, the post-publication word read remains at `0x76`, and its low byte is written at address `3`. If the fresh word is `0x123456ab`, that byte is `0xab`.

11. **Final replacement and completion:** Final result `0xab` supplies the publication’s low byte; the upper bits come from the separate three-byte source. An uncallable address zero cannot be treated as successful completion.
