# F-0555e661d802

## Purpose

F-0555e661d802 performs an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect invocations, transforms returned bit patterns, mutates memory through computed addresses, and finishes with an unresolved indirect transfer carrying four arguments.

No application-level purpose is established. Character-like constants do not establish text output. Address relationships do not establish allocation, object construction, linked-list manipulation, or serialization.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** accesses, widths, argument relationships, captured values, and ordering directly represented in the supplied material.
- **[INFERENCE]:** deductions conditional on an explicitly stated arithmetic or memory model.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior or trace details not fully specified here. These are not executable acceptance requirements.

The represented function boundary and incoming-state assumptions are not independently validated. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Indirect invocations and arithmetic

The opening phase is a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. The first target depends on incoming return-link state plus 0xFFE. Later targets derive from embedded code-position values with the same displacement.

The first invocation receives two identical arguments: the incoming word shifted left by ten bits. Later invocations consume primary results, secondary results, and separate words of paired results.

Invocation boundaries remain observable even where no explicit result is consumed. Calls with no represented arguments are not no-ops. Duplicate arguments, explicit zero arguments, retained earlier results, and fresh results are distinct interface facts.

No loop or conditional branch is represented in this phase. Each successive operation is reached only if prior operations permit continuation. Dependency-internal behavior is unknown. The complete site-by-site invocation trace is not enumerated here and remains documented_only.

Transformations include left shifts, low-24-bit masks, extraction of bits 10 through 19, narrower masks, and logical right shifts of high words.

**[INFERENCE]:** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, shifts, multiplication, subtraction, and address calculations retain their low 32 bits. Intermediate truncation matters: two successive left shifts by 28 bits yield zero, not an unbounded shifted integer.

Signed-looking expressions do not establish portable signed-overflow behavior. A late right shift uses a signed-looking word and a count obtained from a low byte. Negative-operand behavior and counts outside 0 through 31 remain documented_only; no implicit count masking is specified.

### Initial fields, low-address effects, and saved-link capture

After those four stores, shifted-base offset 0x14 is cleared as a word. A byte is read from shifted-base offset 5; doubled-index offset 0x14 is cleared as a word; and the zero-extended byte is stored as a word at shifted-base offset 0x34.

The next word read from shifted-base offset 0x14 establishes the **earlier captured address**. It must not automatically be replaced with zero: intervening accesses may overlap its storage.

Another word clear at doubled-index offset 0x14 precedes two consecutive byte stores at doubled-index offset 0x15. Both receive the earlier captured address’s low byte.

A byte read from absolute address 0x05 establishes the **byte-derived base** before absolute address 0x01 receives the doubled index’s low byte. Byte-derived-base offset 0x15 then receives the small index’s low byte.

Immediately after that offset-0x15 store, the following access order is required:

| Order | Access | Width | Value or result |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Store at shifted-base offset 0x11 | 8 bits | Eight-bit-shifted word, truncated |
| 2 | Store at earlier-captured-address offset 0x54 | 32 bits | Eight-bit-shifted word |
| 3 | Store at shifted-base offset 0x11 | 8 bits | Zero |
| 4 | Read at byte-derived-base offset 0x54 | 32 bits | Capture the saved link |
| 5 | Store at byte-derived-base offset 0x54 | 32 bits | Earlier captured address |

The saved link is therefore captured **after both eight-bit-shifted-word stores and the zero-byte overwrite**, not before them. If the computed word-store destination overlaps byte-derived-base offset 0x54, that store can affect the saved link. The zero-byte overwrite can also affect the capture if its destination overlaps the read. The replacement uses the retained earlier captured address, not a new read.

Doubled-index offset 0x34 subsequently receives the small index as a word. A word followed at shifted-base offset 0x74 supplies another destination. Its offset 0x54 first receives the shifted-base source shifted left by 26 bits and later receives the eight-bit-shifted word. An additional offset-0x74-based access writes a followed word through its own offset 0x54.

Saved-link offset 0x24 receives repeated self-value word stores, interleaved with byte stores of the saved link’s low byte through another destination’s offset 9. Doubled-index offset 0x14 receives the shifted-base source shifted left by 26 bits and repeated saved-link word values. Absolute address 0x54 receives twice the saved link as a word.

A second pair of consecutive byte stores at doubled-index offset 0x15 receives the low byte of four times the saved link. **After this pair, absolute address 0x15 receives the doubled index’s low byte.** The following memory store writes the eight-bit-shifted word at offset 0x54 from four times the saved link.

The absolute-address-0x15 store is distinct from the computed offset-0x15 stores, even when their destinations coincide.
