## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

The first indirect target is calculated from incoming link context plus 0xffe. Subsequent targets use embedded anchors plus the same displacement. Separate call events must not be treated as equivalent dependencies merely because their target calculations have similar forms.

The represented call sequence has no visible loop or conditional branch. If every preceding call returns normally, later calls occur in represented order. Calls lacking represented arguments may still consume implicit state. Ignored results do not imply absent side effects.

Results are forwarded through shifts, masks, duplicated argument positions, and retained values that survive intervening calls. Established transformation families include:

- Primary results shifted left eight positions.
- Upper result halves forwarded unchanged or shifted independently.
- Low-field extraction using masks of several widths, including 24, 22, 20, 19, and 17 bits.
- Whole-container unsigned shifts by 34, 36, and 44 positions.
- Repeated shifts of values previously set to zero.
- Left shifts of 32-bit quantities by distances up to 31.

**[INFERENCE: 32-bit bitvector model]** Arithmetic and left shifts retain their low 32 bits. Intermediate truncation is significant: consecutive shifts must not be replaced by unrestricted integer arithmetic. This interpretation does not establish signed-language overflow behavior.

No dependency may be assumed to preserve globals, own memory, release memory, terminate, or return normally.

### Initial memory mutations and first reload

The initial access sequence establishes the following ordering:

- A word containing the shifted base is stored at the final upper half plus 0x14. A byte containing that upper half’s low byte is then stored at the shifted base plus 9.
- The scaled base is stored as a word at 256 times the final lower half plus 0x54. The byte at the shifted base plus 9 is then overwritten with the shifted base’s own low byte.
- The word at the scaled base plus 0x14 is cleared. A byte is read at that base plus 5.
- The word at the doubled seed plus 0x14 is cleared. The captured byte is widened unsigned and stored as a word at the scaled base plus 0x34.
- The word at the scaled base plus 0x14 is reloaded. The word at the doubled seed plus 0x14 is cleared again.

**[INFERENCE: ordinary coherent, non-device memory, ordinary address mapping, and no external mutation]** That first reload returns zero. The widened-byte store at offset 0x34 does not overlap the cleared word at offset 0x14 of the same base. Any overlap from the other intervening clear writes only zero. Writes occurring before the first clear do not justify a nonzero reload.

This conclusion is not extended to device-backed memory, read side effects, unusual mappings, or concurrent mutation.

The reloaded word’s low byte is subsequently written twice at the doubled seed plus 0x15. Both writes remain represented events.

### Fixed-location accesses and address-bearing mutations

The fixed-location value at 0x5 is read before the byte store at fixed address 0x1. Its converted value becomes a computed base. The byte stored at 0x1 is the doubled seed’s low byte.

The operation then publishes the bounded seed’s low byte at the fixed-read-derived base plus 0x15. At the scaled base plus 0x11, it writes the retained word’s low byte, performs a word store through the earlier reload plus 0x54, and then overwrites the offset-0x11 byte with zero.

Under the ordinary-memory assumptions above, the store through the zero reload is a word store at fixed address 0x54 containing the retained word. It is distinct from the later unconditional fixed-address update at 0x54.

A word is captured from the fixed-read-derived base plus 0x54 before that location is overwritten with the earlier reload. Call this captured word the **saved address value**. Later arithmetic uses its doubled, quadrupled, and eightfold forms.

The intervening mutation region includes:

- Word loads through offsets 0x74, followed by stores through the loaded values plus 0x54.

After two byte stores at the doubled seed plus 0x15, fixed address 0x15 receives the doubled seed’s low byte.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static-review examples, not executed tests.

- **Truncation:** A byte store supplied 0x123456ab writes 0xab, not a word.
- **Unsigned widening:** A captured byte of 0xfe becomes word value 254.
- **Seed boundary:** Under the bitvector model, contributing low seven bits of 0x7f produce bounded seed 508 and doubled seed 1016.
- **First cleared-word reload:** Under ordinary coherent memory, ordinary mapping, and no external mutation, the initial scaled-base-plus-0x14 reload returns zero despite the intervening offset-0x34 store.
- **Fixed-read ordering:** The fixed 0x5 read is completed before the byte write at 0x1; its captured value is not recomputed afterward.
- **Publication boundary:** A publication group starts with its offset-0x54 word store, not either byte publication. The initial group uses the saved-address scale; later groups use the adjusted scale.
- **Intervening capture mutation:** If the adjusted base is 2, the byte written at 0x3a is 0x40. A subsequently captured word overlapping 0x3a must reflect that write under coherent memory; earlier captures must not be refreshed.
- **Fresh fixed-byte reads:** If a computed store changes 0x67 after publication to 0x21, a later publication-base-plus-9 store uses the later byte read, not necessarily the originally saved low byte.
- **Halfword width:** The late 0x0065 store writes 16 bits. Byte order depends on endianness.
- **Dependency nonreturn:** If an early dependency does not return, later mutations and the terminal invocation need not occur.
- **Terminal preservation:** The third terminal argument is the previously captured offset-0x14 word, not a fresh read from that location.
