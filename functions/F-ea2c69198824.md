# F-ea2c69198824

## Purpose

F-ea2c69198824 performs a straight-line sequence of dependency calls and state mutations. Its established behavior includes computed word publications, selector-directed byte writes, bit-field extraction, record-like accesses, two named global references, and a terminal invocation of address zero.

No reliable application-level purpose is established. “Publication,” “selector,” and “record-like” describe observable access patterns, not a protocol, ownership model, or validated data structure.

This contract is conditional on execution reaching each operation, the accessed storage supporting that operation, and preceding dependencies returning. Successful completion is not guaranteed.

### Initial word and byte publications

The first local effect stores the incoming payload as a word at four times the second explicit argument plus 0x14, before any dependency invocation.

A word is then read at offset 0x14 from an incoming implicit base. Its logical right shift by nineteen is passed to F-unknown-001, together with the first explicit argument in the first and fourth positions and zero in the third.

After intervening calls, a dependency result selects another scale-four-plus-0x14 word destination. The original incoming payload is stored there; the scaled destination component is then forwarded.

A later unsigned word result supplies:

- A thirty-two-bit left shift by two.
- Its low thirty bits shifted right by seventeen.
- Its low thirty bits shifted right by twenty.
- Its low thirty bits shifted right by seven.

A byte read at offset one from another incoming base selects a destination by adding 0x11. The low byte of the left-shifted value is stored there.

After further calls, the selector is read again. The second publication stores the low byte of the low-thirty-bit field shifted right by twenty. The selector must not be cached across these publications.

Each such selector-directed destination lies between 0x11 and 0x110 inclusive. This arithmetic bound does not establish valid memory.

### Wide results and selector-directed clears

Extracted fields and call results are forwarded through additional dependencies, including a call with four equal leading arguments and retained fields in later positions. A control-address-derived value also appears as an argument.

A sixty-four-bit result supplies independently retained low and high words. The high word is forwarded. The low word supplies later addresses and fields.

The earlier low-thirty-bit field shifted right by seven selects a byte read at field plus one. That byte selects a zero-byte store at selector plus 0x11. This read-and-clear operation occurs twice, with calls between occurrences.

The wide result’s low word subsequently supplies:

- Its high nibble as an argument.
- A selector-read address of one plus its logical right shift by nine.
- An unsigned byte read at twice the low word.
- That loaded byte in four equal argument positions.

Further selector-directed clears follow, including another read at one plus the low word shifted right by nine and a separate selector read at absolute address one.

A value already shifted left by two is later shifted left by thirty-one. Under thirty-two-bit bit-vector semantics, that result is zero. An unsigned loaded byte shifted left by thirty-one instead contributes either zero or 0x80000000, according to its least significant bit.

### Differently sized reads and stack exposure

Later results supply a word-read address consisting of a retained low word plus its shift-right-by-nineteen field shifted left by thirty-one. The added unsigned contribution is either zero or 0x80000000, not a sign-extended offset.

Other reads include:

- One byte at offset 0x0e from a secondary result.
- One unsigned byte at a retained base plus a secondary result.
- One halfword at a retained shift-right-by-nineteen field plus that loaded unsigned byte.

Several loaded values are forwarded in four equal argument positions. Explicit unsigned byte loads produce values from zero through 255. For storage represented only by width, additional signed interpretation is not established.

F-unknown-002 receives the same stack address in four positions. The exposed region’s size, initialization, intended contents, and permitted retention are unknown.

A following wide result is accompanied by another selector-directed clear. Its selector-read address is one plus a thirty-two-bit multiplication of a retained field by 0x80000000; therefore that address is either one or 0x80000001.

The halfword is eventually forwarded. Another wide result supplies a retained low-word base and a high word selecting a scale-four-plus-0x14 word publication.

### Record-like publications and field updates

A retained shift-right-by-nineteen field is published to two dependency-selected word destinations.

At offset 0x0d from a retained base, a byte store writes the low byte of twice that base. Calls intervene before another store writes four to the same offset.

Two surrounding word reads use different address calculations:

- Four times a dependency-produced unsigned word plus 0x44.
- That word’s low thirty bits shifted right by twenty, plus 0x44.

The second address is not multiplied by four.

A later word publication uses a scale-256-plus-0x14 destination and stores a scale-four value. Another publication stores a field formed by retaining twenty-six bits and shifting right by thirteen.

A recurring pattern stores four times a dependency result at sixteen times that result plus 0x14. Separate calls select scale-four-plus-0x14 destinations for retained values. These destination scales are not interchangeable.

One scale-sixteen record receives a zero word at offset 0x24, followed by a word read at offset 0x74. That read supplies a new retained base.

After calls, a dependency result shifted right by twenty is stored at offset 0x24 from the new base. A word at offset 0x74 from that base is then read and forwarded.

Separate selector reads at base plus one first direct a masked-field byte publication and later a zero-byte publication. Calls separate them.

The retained shift-right-by-twenty value is also shifted right by twenty-one for arguments. Since it is at most twelve bits wide, those arguments are zero.

### Late traversal and first G-clear snapshot

Late traversal combines computed publications with word reads at offsets 0x14, 0x34, and 0x74.

A scaled dependency result selects a byte clear at an address increased by nine. Later, a retained base receives its own low byte at offset nine. A word at offset 0x34 from that base is read as a dependency argument.

A secondary result selects a scale-four-plus-0x14 word publication. Immediately afterward, a word is read at offset 0x14 from that secondary result itself. After calls and another publication, its shift-right-by-twenty-two field selects another word read at field plus 0x14.

**First G-clear snapshot:** after a computed word publication of a retained result shifted left by two, G-clear is read into a thirty-two-bit retained snapshot, before the next dependency call.

After intervening calls:

- The retained traversal word is shifted right by twenty-two and published through a secondary-result-selected word slot.
- The first G-clear snapshot is shifted right by twenty-three. Its value, between zero and 511, is passed as an argument alongside the shifted traversal word.
- That shifted snapshot is itself shifted right by twenty-three, producing zero.
- The zero is retained for subsequent argument, publication, and selector-address uses.

A later computed word publication stores this zero. The retained zero also makes a subsequent selector read occur at absolute address one. These uses derive from the first snapshot, not a reload of G-clear.

### Captured absolute-one byte and first G-publish publication

A later byte read at absolute address one is a separate observation. Its unsigned result is retained as a base between zero and 255, and immediately selects a zero-byte store at captured base plus 0x11.

The captured base survives intervening calls. In particular, F-unknown-003 receives two equal scale-four arguments; F-unknown-004 follows and produces a thirty-two-bit result.

That result is assigned to G-publish **before** F-unknown-005 is called with the result. F-unknown-006 then executes. Only after those calls does the routine read a word at captured base plus 0x74 and store its low byte at captured base plus one.

Neither the G-publish assignment nor intervening calls cause a selector reload in this local data flow. Even if absolute address one changes, both addresses in this word-to-byte transfer use the captured base. The word contents themselves are read after the calls and may reflect their effects.

Later dependency-derived scale-four bases receive similar offset-0x74 word reads and offset-one byte stores. One pair repeats at the same retained base with calls between repetitions; the word must be read again.

Another such word is retained in full, copied by low byte to offset one, and later published through both scale-sixteen and scale-four word destinations.

### Second G-clear snapshot and final publications

A later word publication uses a retained scale-four value with its low two bits cleared, plus 0x14. Under the thirty-two-bit shift model, those bits are already zero.

After additional calls and a zero-word publication, a wide result supplies a high-word-selected scale-four destination and a low word shifted left by two as payload.

**Second G-clear snapshot:** immediately after that computed word publication, G-clear is read again as a thirty-two-bit value. This is independent of the first snapshot.

The second snapshot is retained across intervening calls. A later dependency result selects another scale-four-plus-0x14 publication of the same shifted payload. Immediately after that publication, and before the next call, a word is read at **second snapshot plus 0x14**.

The second snapshot is not shifted before this addressing use. The address must not instead use zero, the first snapshot, or a fresh G-clear read.

The newly loaded word is retained across another dependency sequence. Its low byte is then published through a selector derived from an earlier retained word shifted right by twenty-three. Later, its shift-right-by-twenty-two field supplies another byte publication through a selector derived from a newer wide result.

The terminal local sequence is:

1. Store zero through a secondary-result byte pointer.
2. Assign G-publish a composed thirty-two-bit value: a dependency-produced low byte and a represented twenty-four-bit quantity originating at absolute address nine.
3. Clear a halfword at the address formed by retaining thirty bits of a retained result and shifting right by seven.
4. Invoke F-unknown-007, whose represented target is address zero, with three zero arguments.
5. Return only if that invocation returns.
