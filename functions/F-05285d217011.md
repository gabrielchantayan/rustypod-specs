### Transition to direct memory mutation

The final dependency results feed several distinct address-generating roles:

- A **primary base**, derived by multiplying a returned word by \(2^{25}\).
- A **packed base**, derived from the low half of an earlier result shifted left by 25.
- A **compact index**, derived from another returned word by shifting left by 8, masking with `0x00007fff`, and shifting right by 6.
- A **compact base**, equal to twice that compact index.
- A **shifted word**, derived from an earlier returned word shifted left by 8.
- The low and high halves of the last displayed 64-bit dependency result.

These are descriptive roles, not evidence of separate allocations.

Under the bit-vector interpretation:

- The primary and packed bases are multiples of `0x02000000`, including zero.
- The compact index is a multiple of four in the inclusive range 0–508.
- The compact base is a multiple of eight in the inclusive range 0–1016.
- The packed base’s low byte is zero.

The first direct updates include a word written at the final high-half value plus `0x14`, byte writes at packed-base offset `0x09`, and a word written at an address derived from the final low half multiplied by 256 plus `0x54`.

The packed-base byte is first assigned the low byte of the final high half and then overwritten with the low byte of the packed value. The latter is zero under the interpretation above. The first write remains part of the displayed access order.

The function then clears a primary-base word at offset `0x14`, reads a byte at primary-base offset `0x05`, clears a compact-base word at offset `0x14`, and writes the zero-extended byte at primary-base offset `0x34`.

Subsequent operations reuse loaded words as addresses. Therefore a value that was ordinary stored data can become a later destination base. The function does not validate that transition.

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Input forwarding.** If the explicit input is `0x89abcdef` and execution reaches F-U001, its displayed input is the same 32-bit pattern. No byte extraction or sign-based validation precedes that forwarding.

2. **Masked extraction.** For a word of `0xff123456`, masking to 24 bits and shifting right by eight produces `0x1234`, not `0xff1234`.

3. **Compact-base boundary.** If the compact-index source has lowest seven bits equal to `0x7f`, the index is 508 and the compact base is 1016. Upper source bits do not affect these values.

4. **Truncating packed-base store.** If packed-base construction follows 32-bit shift semantics, its low byte is zero. The later byte store using that low byte writes zero, regardless of the earlier byte written at the same displayed destination.

5. **Intermediate observations.** If a destination exposes each write, the sequence `0x20`, `0x2f`, an extracted byte widened to a word, and `0x20` must remain distinguishable from a single final store.

6. **Aliased fields.** If a byte destination overlaps a subsequently loaded word, that load reflects the intervening byte write according to the platform’s byte order. An earlier cached word is not an acceptable substitute.

7. **Invalid address.** If a computed destination is inaccessible, no graceful error return or rollback is required by this evidence. Earlier effects may remain.

8. **Final dispatch.** If execution reaches F-U003, its first displayed argument is `0x72`; it is not the original input. The other arguments preserve their respective load-time values.
