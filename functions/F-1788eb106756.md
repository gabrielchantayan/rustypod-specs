The next byte read uses the initial address word logically shifted right two bits, plus `0x01`. Its zero-extended value becomes the **small base**. Zero is written at small base plus `0x11`, followed by an exchange beginning with the preceding retained result shifted left two bits.

After that exchange, execution captures the **input field** from first input plus `0x04`, then captures the **loaded address** from small base plus `0x74`. The low byte of the loaded address is written at small base plus `0x01`.

The initial address word and the small base are distinct captured quantities. The second byte read is not a reread of the first byte location.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional arithmetic and ordering examples, not executed tests.

1. **Initial absolute destination.** If the first byte read is `0xFF` and the incoming byte is `0xA6`, the first direct store writes `0xA6` at `0x110`, before any indirect call.

2. **Distinct addressing expressions.** With initial address word `0x1000`, the initial byte read uses `0x1001`; the small-base read uses `0x0401`. They are not interchangeable.

3. **Extracted-field boundary.** With captured input field `0xFFFFFFFF`, extracted field is `0x3FF`. Under 32-bit wrapping, its masked, repeated argument is `0x3FE00000`; its later shifted store payload is `0xFF800000`.

4. **Capture versus refresh.** A dependency changing the input field’s backing memory after capture does not cause another field read. A dependency changing a selector location between explicitly repeated byte reads may change the later destination.

5. **Mask timing.** With raw index `0xC0000003`, saved scale is `0x0000000C` and its pre-mask destination is `0x44` under wrapping arithmetic. Masked index subsequently becomes `3`; quarter index becomes zero.

6. **Three compound results.** Reaching the final-index phase requires traversing all three represented compound-result call sites in their stated order. Their halves must remain associated with the correct result, not substituted for one another.

7. **Selector boundary.** Saved selector `0xFF` gives double selector `0x1FE`. The selector-relative late-byte destination is `0x20B`, and the word read supplying late address uses `0x272`.

8. **Repeated writes.** Both stores at eightfold slot plus `0x34` remain observable even if they contain equal values. Both saved-scale byte writes at sixteenfold slot plus `0x0D` likewise remain distinct.

9. **Low-address overwrite.** The explicit store of saved selector at `0x0D` precedes the explicit store of `0xC0` there. This does not guarantee the final memory value: dependencies may also write that address.

10. **Access failure.** If a required access cannot complete, no returned error, recovery action, later write, or normal completion is specified.
