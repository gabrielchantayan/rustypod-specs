## Behavior

### Address derivation

The function forms a first argument by adding 48 bytes to the first input and calls F-917e96f61d51 with that argument.

After that call returns, it subtracts 24 bytes from the returned integer/address value. This adjusted value becomes the argument to a second call to the same dependency.

After the second call returns, it subtracts another 24 bytes from that second result. The resulting address is the destination of the subsequent write and the address eventually returned.

These are byte displacements, not counts of 32-bit elements. In particular, the final subtraction identifies a location 24 bytes before the second dependency result.

The two dependency calls cannot generally be collapsed into a single adjustment of the original input. Their results and side effects are unknown, and the second call receives a value derived from the first call’s result.
