## Behavior

### Widths, layout, and sequencing conventions

Selectors are unsigned eight-bit loads. Byte stores retain the low eight bits of their supplied values. Word loads and stores are 32 bits. Four dependency results are represented as 64 bits and consumed through low and high 32-bit halves.

All address offsets below are byte offsets. A **selector store** consists of a fresh byte read at the stated selector address, followed by a byte write at absolute address:

**17 + the zero-extended selector.**

Its mathematical destination range is 17–272. The selector’s source address is not added to that destination.

A **four-call suffix** means four consecutive indirect calls with no displayed explicit arguments. Where a result is named, it comes from the fourth call. Calls with no displayed arguments are not established to have zero machine-level inputs.

Descriptive value names below identify captured dataflow values, not storage layouts or dependency identities. A repeated value means reuse of the represented captured value, not a new read of its earlier destination.

Operations are ordered as described, including calls between memory accesses. No dependency is assumed pure or memory-preserving.

Shifts and products involving 32-bit values are stated as represented. Numerical claims about discarded high bits, address wraparound, or signed-looking expressions are conditional on a 32-bit wrapping interpretation. Pointer formation, alignment, and signed arithmetic semantics remain unresolved.
