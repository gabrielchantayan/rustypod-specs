Those observations precede both initial stores: the inherited byte goes to entry-anchor offset 29, and the widened byte observation goes to a halfword at the entry anchor plus the signed halfword value.

### First and second stages: byte-derived addressing

The first two stages retain the entry cursor.

After first-stage installation, cursor offset 17 receives the low byte of twice the cursor. A signed halfword at the cursor plus the first selection supplies an address component; the next byte is read at that component plus the incoming carried word.

Two separate payload observations are important:

- Primary-input offset 100 is read and copied as a word to offset 84 of the signed-halfword-derived base.
- First-selection offset 100 is read and retained as the **saved payload** for later sum-addressed stores.

Between those observations, offset 13 of the newly byte-derived base receives the incoming carried word’s low byte. After the saved payload is obtained, the widened byte observation is stored as a halfword at the signed halfword plus the first selection.

First-selection offset 68 is then read as the next carried word. The cursor’s offset-84 self-reference is written before second-stage selection.

After second-stage installation, cursor offset 17 again receives the doubled cursor’s low byte. A fresh primary-input offset-100 observation precedes the signed-halfword read at the cursor plus the second selection and the byte read at that halfword plus the incoming carried word.

The second-stage payload stores have this order:

| Destination | Payload |
|---|---|
| Primary-input offset 1 | Second selection’s low byte |

| Second-selection offset 68 | Saved payload |

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Signed halfword:** With initial halfword bits `0xffff`, the initial byte address uses −1 plus the secondary input. The initial halfword destination uses −1 plus the entry anchor, provided those operations and addresses are usable.

2. **Byte widening:** An initial byte of `0xff` produces halfword bits `0xffff` under signed-character widening or `0x00ff` under unsigned-character widening. Its later address contribution differs accordingly.

3. **Selection timing:** If an earlier mutation overlaps a later external selection location, that later selection is not required to retain the location’s entry contents.

4. **Independent retained payload:** A later primary-input offset-100 change may affect cursor-byte writes. It does not replace the separately retained first-selection offset-100 payload used in the fifth- and sixth-stage sum-addressed stores.

5. **Replacement history:** Without interfering aliasing, fifth-selection offset 20 receives the installation carry and later the observed cursor offset-68 base. Both writes are required.

6. **Fresh observation:** If seventh-selection offset 9 overlaps third-product offset 68, the subsequent third-product offset-17 byte uses the fresh word observation, not the earlier retained word.

7. **Repeated stores:** Equal resulting values do not eliminate either occurrence of the initial duplicated final-phase self-reference/byte-store pair.

8. **Call boundary:** Offset 264 is prepared before F-indirect. The explicit zero store to offset 256 occurs only after that dependency returns; no earlier explicit zero initialization is established.

9. **Non-returning dependency:** If F-indirect never returns, none of the five post-call stack stores, second call, or terminal continuation is reached through this path. If only the second dependency fails to return, only the terminal continuation is excluded.

10. **Late failure:** An unusable product-derived address can prevent completion after earlier mutations. No represented recovery restores those mutations.
