# F-9aea0b2311c3

## Purpose

F-9aea0b2311c3 computes the difference between two signed integer products. It multiplies the first input by the fourth input, multiplies the third input by the second input, and subtracts the latter product from the former.

For signed 32-bit inputs \(a,b,c,d\), in interface order, its mathematical result is:

\[
R = ad-cb
\]

The output is a 64-bit value containing the two’s-complement representation of \(R\). Every permitted input combination produces a mathematical result representable as a signed 64-bit integer; the calculation does not require saturation or loss of significant bits.

This expression is also the determinant of a two-by-two arrangement with rows \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\). That interpretation is inferred mathematical intent, not evidence that the function operates on matrices, coordinates, or any particular application data.

## Behavior

### Arithmetic meaning

Both products use the full signed 32-bit input values, extended to signed 64-bit values before multiplication. Their full 64-bit products participate in the result.

The product pairing is significant:

- The positive term pairs the first and fourth inputs.
- The subtracted term pairs the third and second inputs.

The function does not instead subtract the product of the third and fourth inputs from the product of the first and second inputs.

No 32-bit product truncation is allowed before forming the final mathematical result. Products outside the signed 32-bit range remain meaningful.

### Low-half subtraction and borrow

The returned low half is the difference between the low halves of the two products, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).

When the first product’s unsigned low half is smaller than the second product’s unsigned low half, subtraction requires a borrow from the high half. That borrow reduces the high-half difference by one.

The low-half comparison is unsigned. It is not a comparison between the signs or signed magnitudes of the complete products.

The high halves represent the upper bits of the complete signed products. Together with the borrow adjustment, they preserve the full signed subtraction. Discarding the borrow would produce incorrect results whenever low-half subtraction wraps.
