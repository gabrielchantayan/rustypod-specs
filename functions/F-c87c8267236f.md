### Call-sequence terminology

A **five-call chain** means an initial call with the stated explicit arguments followed by four calls without explicit arguments. When a chain’s ordinary result is used, it is the fifth call’s result.

A **nine-call forwarding sequence** means:

1. An initial call with the stated arguments.
2. A call without explicit arguments whose result is captured.
3. A call receiving that captured result.
4. Another call without explicit arguments whose result is captured.
5. A call receiving the second captured result, with any additional argument stated below.
6. Four further calls without explicit arguments.

The ninth call supplies the sequence’s final ordinary result where used.

These terms describe ordering and explicit argument flow. They do not identify shared callees, complete signatures, or preservation guarantees.

## Behavior

A five-call chain follows. Its initial call receives the first argument, the first argument again, the third argument, and the first argument again, in that order. Its initial target depends on entry linkage state. The final returned index selects another word slot, which receives the original first argument shifted right by seven.

A second five-call chain begins with four times that returned index. Its result becomes the **initial byte sample**.

The routine reads an unsigned byte at the first argument shifted right by eight, plus one. It writes the low byte of the initial byte sample shifted left by two to the byte-selected destination.

A five-call chain begins with that shifted sample. Afterward, the routine freshly reads the same lookup address and writes the low byte of the initial byte sample masked to 30 bits and shifted right by 20 to the newly selected destination.

### 3. First word sample
