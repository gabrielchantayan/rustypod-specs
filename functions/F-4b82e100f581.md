## Behavior

### Source acquisition and record layout

The routine first reads source word 1 into record word 1, then source word 2 into record word 2, and then source word 3 into record word 3.

These are three separate acquisitions in the stated order. The evidence does not establish an atomic snapshot of the sources. If some external agent can change them during execution, the resulting record need not represent their values at one common instant. Whether such concurrent modification is valid in the execution environment is unknown.

The routine subsequently operates on the local record, not directly on the original source locations. It does not explicitly write any transformed value back to those locations.

### Word transformation

Each record word undergoes an unsigned, logical right shift by one bit. Bits move toward the least significant position, bit 0 is discarded, and the newly exposed most significant bit becomes zero.

The processing order is:

1. Record word 3.
2. Record word 2.
3. Record word 1.

This order follows from the backward traversal shown in the supplied representation. It does not change the resulting values when the three local words are independent.

After all three shifts, record word 3 is masked to retain only its least significant 24 bits. The mask does not apply to record words 1 or 2.

If the acquired unsigned values are \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\), the record immediately before dependency processing contains:

- Word 1: \(\lfloor A/2 \rfloor\).
- Word 2: \(\lfloor B/2 \rfloor\).
- Word 3: \(\lfloor C/2 \rfloor \bmod 2^{24}\).

The shifts are independent word operations. They are not one right shift of a combined 96-bit quantity. No bit carries between neighboring words.

For word 3, original input bits 1 through 24 become output bits 0 through 23. Original bit 0 and original bits 25 through 31 do not affect its transformed value. Its upper eight output bits are zero before the dependencies are invoked.

The arithmetic requires no signed interpretation, sign extension, rounding beyond truncation inherent in the logical shift, or saturation. The supplied operations do not introduce arithmetic overflow.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static expectations, not executed tests. They assume valid storage, normal dependency returns, and no dependency behavior that invalidates the stated observation point.

### Independent unsigned shifts

If the acquired source words are:

- Word 1: `0x80000001`.
- Word 2: `0xFFFFFFFF`.
- Word 3: `0xFFFFFFFF`.

Then, immediately before dependency processing, the record must contain:

- Word 1: `0x40000000`.
- Word 2: `0x7FFFFFFF`.
- Word 3: `0x00FFFFFF`.

This distinguishes logical from arithmetic right shift and confirms that only the third word receives the 24-bit mask.

### No cross-word carry

If the acquired source words are 1, 1, and 1, the prepared record contains zero in all three positions.

The discarded low bit of one word does not become a high bit in another word.

### Third-word mask boundary

If source word 3 is `0x01000000`, its prepared value is `0x00800000`: input bit 24 survives.

If source word 3 is instead `0x02000000`, its prepared value is zero: input bit 25 shifts into bit 24 and is then removed by the mask.

These outcomes apply regardless of source words 1 and 2.

### Record positions

If the acquired source words are 2, 4, and 6, the second dependency receives a record whose words at offsets 0, 4, and 8 are respectively 1, 2, and 3, provided the first dependency has not indirectly altered the record.

This establishes field placement, not byte order within each word.

No success interpretation is attached to `0xA5`.
