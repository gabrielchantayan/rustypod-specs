# F-d1f11f163b00

## Purpose

Those effects include byte overwrites, two 16-bit reads, assignment to a distinguished global destination, and a 32-bit overwrite through a previously obtained pointer. The represented sequence ends by requesting a callable destination from a terminal facility and invoking it.

**Established static evidence:** the represented interface supplies no explicit arguments or declared result. The visible behavior has no data-dependent branch, loop, validation response, retry, or rollback.

**Inferred intent:** no application-level purpose is established. In particular, the evidence does not justify describing this operation as initialization, reset, allocation, buffer clearing, or device control.

This contract describes operations only when execution reaches them. A dependency that does not return, an invalid transfer destination, or a failed memory access can prevent all subsequent effects.

## Behavior

### Observation and ordering contract

The following phase order is established:

- Early overwrites reuse the incoming byte.
- Six subsequent overwrites explicitly write zero bytes.
- Later overwrites use address-derived or retained payloads.
- The first 16-bit read occurs during the retained-payload phase.
- The distinguished global is assigned the retained low-byte payload.
- Further retained-payload overwrites precede the independent bit-slice overwrite and the reduced-payload phase.
- The second 16-bit read occurs during the reduced-payload phase.
- A later byte overwrite is immediately followed by the retained-pointer 32-bit overwrite.
- Further interactions and byte overwrites precede terminal dispatch.

This is not a transaction. Dependencies interspersed throughout these phases may inspect or modify the same memory, including the distinguished global.

Invocation order remains significant when a primary result is discarded or when two invocations have identical displayed argument shapes. The evidence provides no basis for merging such interactions, treating them as pure computations, or assuming that they preserve implicit state.

### Payload identity

Four payload origins must remain distinct.

**Address-derived byte.** One overwrite carries the low eight bits of the eightfold-scaled quantity that determines its own destination. Its consumer receives two equal arguments at 16,384-fold scale and a third carrying the eightfold-scaled quantity.

**Retained low byte.** A later unsigned 32-bit dependency result supplies the low eight bits of its eightfold-scaled value. This byte is retained across many interactions and reused at destinations derived from other values.

Its first destination uses sixteenfold scaling of that unsigned result; the associated equal arguments use a scale of 32,768. Later uses include the following relationships:

| Destination relationship | Associated displayed arguments |
|---|---|
| Source scaled by 65,536 | Two equal quantities scaled by \(2^{27}\) |
| Source scaled by 8192 | At several boundaries, two equal quantities scaled by \(2^{24}\) |
| Source scaled by 4096 | Two equal quantities scaled by \(2^{23}\) |
| Source scaled by eight | Commonly, two equal quantities scaled by \(2^{14}\) |
| Later eightfold destination | At one boundary, two equal fourfold quantities |

The destination source is not always the newest primary result. Some sources are retained across intervening calls or derive from fixed image-associated state.

**High-order byte and reduced payload.** A separate retained arithmetic quantity supplies bits 24 through 31, interpreted unsigned. Its origin includes scaling an earlier retained quantity by 32,768; that earlier quantity had already been scaled by 16,384 at an incoming-byte boundary.

The reduced payload consists of the upper three bits of this high-order byte. Its numeric range is zero through seven. It is not a Boolean flag and must not be normalized to zero or one.

**Independent bit-slice byte.** Another overwrite derives from the earlier unsigned result, independently of the retained low byte. The represented extraction admits a ten-bit intermediate from original bit positions 19 through 28, but the destination is only eight bits wide. The stored byte therefore carries original bit positions 19 through 26 under the represented truncation.

These arithmetic descriptions remain subject to the signed-operation limitations below.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional obligations, not executed tests or claims that a complete execution is feasible.

1. **Incoming payload remains independent of the address.** At an ordinary incoming-byte boundary, a selected result of 512 and payload 0xA6 require a one-byte overwrite at address 4096 before the associated consumer receives 8,388,608 in both displayed positions. This example excludes exceptional boundaries.

2. **Unsigned truncation is width-bounded.** An unsigned source of 0x21 supplies retained payload 0x08. Its initial destination is 0x210, and the associated equal arguments are 0x108000. The overwrite affects one byte.

3. **Wide-result layouts are not interchangeable.** For low half 0x11223344 and high half 0x55667788, the early three-position consumer repeats the low half. The two later three-position consumers repeat the high half. The two-position consumer receives low then high.

4. **Four-argument provenance matters.** With selected result three and incoming 32-bit quantity five, the four-position consumer receives 49,152 in positions one, two, and four, but 5120 in position three. If the relevant secondary result is seven, the following eightfold consumer receives 56.

5. **First read uses the current location.** With selected result three, the retained-byte overwrite targets 24,576 and the subsequent read targets 12,344. If the read yields 0xBEEF, its consumer receives base 12,288 and quantity 0xBEEF.

6. **Intermediate width does not widen a store.** If original unsigned-source bits 19 through 28 are all set, the bit-slice intermediate is 1023, but the stored byte is 0xFF.

7. **Reduced payload is numeric.** Conditional on a retained high-order byte of 0xE0, the reduced payload is seven. Applicable overwrites carry 0x07, not 0x01. This does not assert that unresolved signed arithmetic necessarily produces that byte.

8. **Later mutation remains observable.** If a dependency changes the second read location before the read boundary, the consumer receives the newly read quantity. Similarly, later modification of the distinguished global does not contradict its earlier direct assignment.

9. **Overlapping writes preserve order, not the first value.** If the retained-pointer word destination overlaps the immediately preceding byte destination, the byte write must occur first. Its payload need not survive the word write.

10. **Unresolved arithmetic stays unresolved.** No concrete destination is required for the width-equal-shift boundary without establishing its arithmetic semantics.

11. **Interrupted execution is a prefix.** If an early dependency never returns, later zero writes, reads, word overwrite, and terminal dispatch are not required to occur.
