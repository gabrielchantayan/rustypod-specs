# F-92456c455b47

The established behavior is this preparation, call sequence, and final byte write. A device-control or registration purpose is plausible from the descriptive name associated with the static-data argument, but is not established by the supplied evidence. In particular, the evidence does not establish that the final byte is a success flag, handle, device identifier, or error code.

## Behavior

### 1. Capture the seven source words

Before calling any dependency, the function initializes the payload by reading seven global 32-bit values.

The visible read-and-copy order is slot zero through slot six. These are separate reads, not an established atomic snapshot. If another execution context can modify the source storage during this sequence, the resulting payload can contain values observed at different times. No synchronization is shown.

Once these reads finish, the local transformation operates on the captured words. There is no subsequent visible reread of the seven source locations.

### 2. Shift every payload word

The function processes the seven initialized words in descending address order:

1. Slot six.
2. Slot five.
3. Slot four.
4. Slot three.
5. Slot two.
6. Slot one.
7. Slot zero.

Each word is read, shifted right by one bit as a 32-bit unsigned value, and written back to the same slot.

Consequences of this operation include:

- The original least-significant bit is discarded.
- The original most-significant bit does not cause sign extension.
- Zero remains zero.
- An odd value is rounded downward when interpreted as division by two.
- No word carries bits into an adjacent word.
- The seven words are not treated as one larger integer.

The operation covers exactly the seven initialized words. The adjacent position used to begin the descending traversal is not itself one of the shifted words.

### 3. Narrow a subfield of the final word

After all seven shifts finish, the function performs an additional operation on the three-byte portion associated with slot six.

That three-byte value is narrowed to 16 bits and then assigned back into a three-byte field. As a value transformation, this:

- Preserves bits 0 through 15 of that field.
- Clears bits 16 through 23 of that field.

The separate fourth byte of slot six is not assigned by this narrowing operation. It retains the value produced by the preceding 32-bit shift.

This is not a visible assignment of a 16-bit result to the entire 32-bit slot. In particular, the evidence does not support a contract that the whole last word is reduced to its low 16 bits.

Under the conventional interpretation that the three-byte field is the low 24 bits of the word, the final slot-six value equals its shifted value with word bits 16 through 23 cleared; bits 24 through 31 remain as produced by the shift. Mapping those bit positions to increasing byte addresses requires a target-layout interpretation not independently supplied here.

| Initial value | Value after transformation |

### Slot-six subfield preservation

Suppose the three-byte field, immediately after its containing word is shifted, has value `0xABCDEF`. The subsequent narrowing operation makes that field `0x00CDEF`.

The separate fourth byte remains as produced by the shift.

Under the low-24-bit field interpretation, an initial slot-six word of `0xFFFFFFFF` first becomes `0x7FFFFFFF` and then becomes `0x7F00FFFF`. Replacing that whole word with `0x0000FFFF` would incorrectly clear the separate fourth byte.
