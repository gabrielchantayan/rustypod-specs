### Widths and arithmetic

The representation distinguishes 8-, 16-, 32-, and 64-bit quantities.

Under a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector interpretation**, left shifts discard high bits and multiplication retains the low 32 bits. Consequently:

- Left shifts by 8, 25, 26, and 31 retain contributions from only the original low 24, 7, 6, and 1 bits, respectively.
- Successive left shifts totaling at least 32 produce zero if no intervening operation replaces the value.
- Multiplication by 2²⁵ clears the low 25 result bits.
- Byte and halfword stores retain the low 8 and 16 operand bits.

These deductions do not resolve the machine meaning of represented signed overflow or signed left shifts.

Unsigned right shifts zero-fill. Right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 act on explicitly unsigned 64-bit quantities before narrowing. They correspond to shifting the high 32-bit half right by 2, 4, and 12, not to oversized 32-bit shifts.

Masks include 24-, 22-, 21-, 20-, 19-, 18-, 17-, 15-, 14-, 13-, 12-, and 11-bit selections. Selection before shifting or narrowing is significant.

One final signed right shift takes its count from the low eight bits of a computed base. Counts of 32 or greater are possible. Count normalization and negative-operand behavior remain **documented_only**.
