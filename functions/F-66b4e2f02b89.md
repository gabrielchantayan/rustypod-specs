# F-66b4e2f02b89

## Purpose

F-66b4e2f02b89 coordinates a sequence of dependency calls using a private descriptor, an intermediate object, backing storage, and a separate context region.

Its established responsibilities are to:

- Capture four words from a caller-supplied descriptor after an initial context operation.
- Submit that private descriptor to a dependency.
- Calculate a 32-bit size from a caller-supplied field and the current fourth private descriptor word.
- Supply local storage and that size to an intermediate-object preparation dependency.
- Read two referenced program-data values and pass their values into the first of two ordered main operations.
- Invoke a final dependency on the original context region.

The domain meaning of these operations is not established. Terms such as “descriptor,” “context,” “intermediate object,” and “backing storage” distinguish their visible roles; they do not establish object types, initialization protocols, or ownership rules.

The function has no explicit return value. Any externally visible result arises through dependency effects or mutations reachable through supplied arguments.

### Size transformation and widths

Let:

- \(a\) be the signed 32-bit field read at argument 2 plus offset 8.
- \(b\) be the signed 32-bit interpretation of the fourth private descriptor word after F-c6d95d05936a returns.
- \(x\) be the signed 32-bit intermediate formed by multiplying \(a\) and \(b\), then adding seven.

The subsequent expression uses signed and unsigned 32-bit operations:

- A signed right shift of \(x\) by 31 bits contributes to the correction term.
- That result is interpreted as unsigned 32-bit data and shifted right by 29 bits.
- The correction is added to \(x\), and the sum is interpreted as signed 32-bit data and shifted right by three bits.
- One is added using unsigned 32-bit arithmetic.
- A 32-bit mask, `0xFFFFFFFE`, clears the least significant bit.

Under sign-propagating signed-right-shift semantics, the correction is zero for nonnegative \(x\) and seven for negative \(x\). The corrected shift implements division of \(x\) by eight with truncation toward zero.

For a nonnegative product whose multiplication and addition of seven are representable as signed 32-bit values, the result is equivalent to rounding the product upward to a whole number of bytes and then rounding that byte count upward to an even number. This is an arithmetic interpretation, not proof that the product represents bits or that the result represents a stride.

The final mask guarantees an even 32-bit bit pattern where execution of the expression is defined. It does not guarantee a small or usable capacity.

Negative inputs are not rejected. Signed overflow is not checked. The evidence does not settle whether out-of-range signed arithmetic wraps or has some other execution consequence. Accordingly, the nonoverflowing interpretation must not be extended to arbitrary mathematical products.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional expectations derived from static evidence, not runtime test results.

### Capture follows the initial dependency

If F-b7b0d6305d98 changes the original descriptor’s fourth word through reachable state and returns normally, the later capture reads the changed word.

### Negative products are not clamped

For a product of −16, adding seven gives −9. Truncating division by eight gives −1; the unsigned addition and final mask produce zero.

For a product of −24, adding seven gives −17. The corresponding quotient is −2, and the final size is `0xFFFFFFFE`.

These expectations assume the stated arithmetic interpretation. Acceptance must not replace negative inputs with rejection or a general clamp to zero.
