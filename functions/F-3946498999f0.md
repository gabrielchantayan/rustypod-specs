# F-3946498999f0

## Purpose

F-3946498999f0 computes a 64-bit arithmetic result from a two-word multiplicand, a one-word multiplier, and a shift input.

Under the interpretation conditions below, its multiplication stage retains the lowest 64 product bits **before** applying the selected shift behavior. Ordinary shift inputs from 1 through 63 then produce an arithmetic right shift of that retained pattern, subject to the signed-shift qualifications specified in **Behavior**. Discarded multiplication bits cannot contribute to the subsequent shift.

Although the multiplier’s interface is unsigned, the retained-product calculation gives it an effective signed 32-bit interpretation. The multiplicand comprises an unsigned low word and a signed high word. Interpreting the complete multiplicand as signed or unsigned does not change the retained product bits.

A fixed-point scaling purpose is **inferred**, not established. The supplied function does not establish application-level units, a caller-supported shift range, saturation requirements, or a rounding policy independent of the represented arithmetic operations.

This contract is based on static evidence only. No runtime tests have been performed. Results that depend on unresolved signed conversions or shift semantics are conditional, not unconditional execution guarantees.

## Behavior

### Multiplication and retention

Under the interpretation conditions, the multiplication stage produces the pattern

\[
P=(X M)\bmod 2^{64}.
\]

Let \(P_{\mathrm{signed}}\) denote the signed, two’s-complement interpretation of that retained pattern.

The low product word is the lowest 32 bits of the unsigned product \(L U\). That unsigned product fits exactly in 64 bits. The high product word combines its upper 32 bits with word-width contributions from the multiplicand’s high word and the multiplier’s sign.

The multiplier’s sign contribution is material. When its top bit is set, the high-word arithmetic corrects the unsigned multiplication so that the retained result corresponds to multiplication by \(M\), not by \(U\).

This distinction can also be expressed as

\[
M=
\begin{cases}
U,&U<2^{31},\\
U-2^{32},&U\ge2^{31}.
\end{cases}
\]

The correction changes the high product word modulo \(2^{32}\); it does not change the low product word.

The sign used by subsequent arithmetic shifting is bit 63 of **the retained pattern**. It is not necessarily the sign of an untruncated mathematical product.

Interpreting the complete multiplicand as signed instead of unsigned changes its mathematical value by either zero or \(2^{64}\). Consequently, it cannot change the lowest 64 product bits.

There is no full-precision product retained for later shifting, no saturation, and no overflow indicator.

### Full-width regime selection

The entire unsigned 32-bit shift input participates in choosing between two regimes. Selection depends on the signed interpretation of the word-width difference between the input and 32.

Under bit-preserving conversion, the domains are:

| Shift-input interval, inclusive | Selected regime |
|---|---|
| `0x00000000`–`0x0000001F` | Cross-word |
| `0x00000020`–`0x8000001F` | Upper-word-only |
| `0x80000020`–`0xFFFFFFFF` | Cross-word |

This is not equivalent to an unsigned comparison with 32.

Within the selected regime, variable shift counts are reduced to their lowest eight bits. Let

\[
r=s\bmod256.
\]

Full-width selection and eight-bit count reduction are distinct operations. Neither unrestricted modulo-64 normalization nor unrestricted modulo-256 normalization describes the function as a whole.

### Cross-word regime

In this regime, the result combines:

- A signed right shift of the retained product’s high word for the upper result word.
- A logical right shift of the retained product’s low word.
- A left-shifted contribution from the signed high word, combined into the lower result word.

The right-shift count is \(r\). The left-shift count is the least nonnegative residue of \(32-r\) modulo 256.

Both counts are below 32 precisely when \(1\le r\le31\). For those residues, the conventional result is the 64-bit pattern of

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{P_{\mathrm{signed}}}{2^r}\right\rfloor,
\]

provided the applicable signed-shift conditions hold.

This is an arithmetic right shift: negative fractions round toward negative infinity, not toward zero.

#### Signed-left-shift qualification

A bounded count alone does not establish the conventional cross-word result. The transferred high-word contribution is formed using a **signed** left operand.

For a selected residue \(r\) from 1 through 31, let \(H\) be the unsigned value of the retained product’s high word. The left shift has a nonnegative, signed-32-bit-representable result precisely when

\[
0\le H<2^{r-1}.
\]

A negative signed high word fails this condition. A positive high word can also fail: at residue 1, shifting high-word value 1 left by 31 reaches the signed sign-bit position.

When the condition fails, the conventional arithmetic-right-shift result is conditional on word-sized bit-pattern left shifting. Wrapping, trapping, or another concrete execution outcome is not established by the supplied representation.

At residue zero, the left-shift count is 32. Other residues outside 1–31 also select at least one oversized count. Their execution results are **documented_only** rather than resolved by this contract.

A zero operand does not make an oversized shift count valid.

### Upper-word-only regime

In this regime, the original low product word does not affect the return.

The upper result word is the sign extension of the retained product’s high word. The lower result word is obtained by a signed right shift of that high word.

The variable count is the least nonnegative residue of \(r-32\) modulo 256. It is below 32 precisely when \(32\le r\le63\).

For those residues, sign-propagating right shifts produce the same 64-bit pattern as

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{P_{\mathrm{signed}}}{2^r}\right\rfloor.
\]

No signed left shift is involved in this regime.

Other residues select counts above 31. Their execution semantics remain **documented_only**; no additional processor-level count masking is established.

### Ordinary shift inputs and boundaries

For ordinary inputs \(1\le s\le63\), the conventional interpretation is an arithmetic right shift of the retained product:

| Shift range | Qualification |
|---|---|
| 1–31 | Requires sign-propagating right shifts and the signed-left-shift condition or additional bit-pattern semantics |
| 32–63 | Requires sign-propagating right shifts; no signed left shift occurs |

At shift 32, the lower result word equals the retained product’s high-word pattern, and the upper result word is its sign extension.

At shift 63, the return is zero for a nonnegative retained product or all ones for a negative retained product.

Shift zero is not established as an identity operation: its selected cross-word calculation includes a left-shift count of 32.

Noncanonical inputs can select bounded counts. For example, input 288 selects the upper-word-only regime with variable count zero, matching the conditional result for shift 32. That example does not establish general periodicity.
