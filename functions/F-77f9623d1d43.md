## Behavior

### Widths, arithmetic, and layouts

A byte is eight bits, a halfword is 16 bits, and a word is 32 bits. Numerically consumed byte reads are unsigned and widen to values from 0 through 255. Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Halfword and word stores are separate-width effects.

Arithmetic conclusions involving overflow use a conditional **32-bit bit-vector interpretation**: address arithmetic wraps modulo 2³², and left shifts discard bits above bit 31. This interpretation neither establishes portable signed-language behavior nor validates the resulting addresses.

Some indirect results are represented as unsigned 64-bit quantities. Their low and high halves are separate 32-bit values. Whole-result right shifts by 34, 36, or 44 correspond to reducing the high half by another 2, 4, or 12 bits; they are not low-half shifts.

Masks apply before subsequent shifts where represented. Intermediate truncation matters when successive left shifts are combined.

Offsets identify access locations, not a recovered structure type. Reused offsets across different bases do not establish a common object layout. Endianness, alignment requirements, and ordinary versus device-memory behavior remain unresolved.

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

- **Unsigned widening:** an offset-five byte of 0xFE supplies word value 0x000000FE.
- **Ordinary zero readback:** after the high-scale-base-plus-0x14 zero store, suppose accesses succeed, memory is conventional, and no external mutation occurs. The later readback is zero even if the intervening low-base zero word overlaps it. The widened-byte word at the same high-scale base plus 0x34 cannot overlap it.
- **Nonordinary exception:** a nonzero readback requires an unresolved influence such as external mutation or nonordinary access semantics. Neither intervening explicit store provides a nonzero overlapping payload.
- **Fixed-word scaling:** saved link 0x12345678 produces word payload 0x2468ACF0 at numeric address 0x54 under bit-vector arithmetic.
- **Computed fixed byte:** adjusted quantity zero produces byte 0x30 at fixed address 0x3A.
- **Fresh propagation:** retained fixed word 0x12345678 initially supplies byte 0x78 to 0x67. If a computed store later changes 0x67 to 0xAB before a fresh propagation read, that consumer uses 0xAB while the retained word remains 0x12345678.
- **Ordered stores:** final-memory equality does not validate replacing the four active-base-plus-0x20 stores with their last value.
- **Incomplete execution:** a non-returning dependency or failed access can stop subsequent effects; no rollback is specified.
