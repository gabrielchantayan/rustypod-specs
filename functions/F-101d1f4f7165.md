### Initial direct-memory effects

The direct region consumes a final paired result and retained call-derived values. Its initial ordering is:

- A word destination at the final high-half value plus 0x14 receives a retained value.
- A byte destination at another retained base plus 9 receives the low byte of that high half.
- A word at the final low-half value scaled by 256, plus 0x54, receives a separately retained scaled value.
- The earlier offset-9 byte destination is overwritten from the retained value used by the first word store.

A different call-derived base then has its offset-0x14 word cleared. Its offset-5 byte is captured before another computed offset-0x14 word is cleared. The captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at the first base plus 0x34.

The first cleared word is subsequently reloaded. A second clearing store to the other word precedes two identical byte stores at that other base plus 0x15, using the reloaded word’s low byte.

That reload is not unconditionally zero: intervening computed accesses may overlap its storage.

For the following descriptions, the **small derived base** is twice an unsigned value obtained by masking an earlier result shifted left by eight with 0x7FFF, then shifting right by six. Under the bit-vector interpretation, the masked value is at most 0x1FC and the small derived base is at most 0x3F8. These bounds do not establish valid storage.

### Early fixed-location dependencies

After those duplicate offset-0x15 byte writes, a byte is read from fixed address **0x05** and promoted to an unsigned 32-bit value. Call this the **captured low-memory base**. The byte at fixed address **0x01** is then written with the small derived base’s low byte.

The captured low-memory base remains the address source even if the write at 0x01, or a later computed store, changes fixed location 0x05.

The ensuing dependency chain includes:

- A byte write at the captured low-memory base plus 0x15, sourced from the unsigned value before its doubling into the small derived base.
- A byte write at the earlier cleared base plus 0x11.
- A word write at the previously reloaded word, interpreted as an address, plus 0x54.
- A clearing byte write at that earlier offset-0x11 destination.
- A word read at the captured low-memory base plus 0x54.
- A word overwrite at that same captured-base offset, using the previously reloaded word.

The word read before this overwrite is the **captured link value**. It remains the source for subsequent scaling and address calculations; it is not a later reread of the overwritten field.

Further pointer-derived stores and duplicate self-address-like stores occur before the fixed word at **0x54** receives twice the captured link value. This fixed write lies between successive writes to the small derived base plus 0x14. It must not be treated as unrelated global bookkeeping.

After two writes of the captured link value to that computed offset-0x14 word, the small derived base plus 0x15 receives the low byte of four times the captured link value twice. Fixed byte **0x15** is then written with the small derived base’s low byte.

These are distinct fixed and computed accesses. A computed destination may coincide with 0x01, 0x05, 0x15, or any byte occupied by the fixed word at 0x54.

### Constants and repeated-store region

A later byte-derived offset-0x74 word capture supplies a **scaled source** equal to four times that captured word and a **terminal base** equal to twice that word.

The adjusted base plus 0x20 receives, in order, word values 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from the adjusted base plus 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read follows the first two stores.

### Shift width and paired results

For a paired result 0x1234567800000001, forwarding its low half shifted by eight and its high half unchanged produces 0x00000100 and 0x12345678 respectively.

### Fixed read versus captured base

If fixed byte 0x05 yields 0x80, subsequent captured-base accesses use addresses 0x95 and 0xD4 for offsets 0x15 and 0x54. A later change to fixed byte 0x05 does not change that captured base.

The word at 0xD4 is read before its overwrite. Later scaling uses the captured pre-overwrite word.

### Fixed write aliasing a terminal load

If fixed byte 0x3A lies within the terminal-offset-0x14 word, that word capture occurs after the byte write. Reading the word before writing 0x3A is not equivalent. The affected byte’s numeric contribution depends on endianness.

### Fresh reads of 0x67

If the write to 0x67 installs 0xA5, but an intervening computed store changes it to 0x7C before a later offset-9 copy, that copy must use 0x7C. Reusing 0xA5 would lose the later memory dependency.

### Promotion and access widths

The three constant-0x3A stores occupy one, two, and four bytes. They are not three byte stores or a terminated string.

### Final captures survive later stores
