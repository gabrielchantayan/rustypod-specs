### Outputs, widths, and layout

No normal return value or designated output buffer is established. Outputs are explicit memory effects and dependency interactions.

Bytes are 8 bits; words are 32 bits. Selector reads are unsigned byte reads. Byte writes retain only the low eight bits of their values. Two results are represented as 64-bit quantities whose low and high 32-bit halves are consumed separately.

The following arithmetic convention describes the represented bit patterns: scaling, address addition, and left shifts retain their low 32 bits. Logical right shifts insert zeros. This is not a claim that overflowing signed expressions have portable-language semantics or that the recovered operations accurately describe every machine instruction.

Two field operations recur:

- **Half-field:** retain the low 30 bits, then logically shift right by one.
- **Upper-field:** retain the low 30 bits, then logically shift right by twenty.

An upper-field result can contain ten bits; a subsequent byte write retains only original bits 20–27.

Offsets such as `0x14`, `0x24`, `0x44`, and `0x74` identify access locations, not established structure members. Endianness, alignment constraints, and fault behavior are unknown.
