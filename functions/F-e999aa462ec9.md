# F-e999aa462ec9

## Purpose

F-e999aa462ec9 adjusts one caller-designated signed integer by a contribution derived from two supplied values. A selector requests a half-difference adjustment, a full-difference adjustment, or inactivity.

**Established static behavior:** the difference is directional: the second supplied operand is subtracted from the first. Selector 1 adds the integer half of that complete difference to the existing target value. Selector 2 adds the complete difference. Every other selector leaves the target unaccessed.

The operation is an adjustment, not a replacement. The target’s previous value contributes to the result in both active modes.

**Inferred intent:** the two active modes represent different adjustment strengths. The evidence does not identify an application domain, measurement unit, coordinate system, or reason for selecting either strength.

This contract describes ordinary completion only where the required storage accesses and arithmetic are valid. Invalid storage and unsupported arithmetic have no established successful outcome.

## Behavior

### Result relationship

For an active invocation, consider the mathematical difference between the first and second operands.

- Selector 1 contributes the integer quotient obtained by dividing that complete difference by two.
- Selector 2 contributes the complete difference.

The successful final target is its initial value plus the selected contribution.

All selectors other than 1 and 2 are inactive. This includes zero, negative values, and positive values greater than two. Inactive behavior neither reads nor assigns the target and does not evaluate the operand difference.

There is no evidenced absolute-value conversion, clamping, saturation, sign normalization, range restriction, or replacement of the target with the contribution alone.

### Integer halving

Half strength applies after the complete difference has been formed. It is not the difference between separately halved operands.

For example, operands 12 and 7 have a difference of 5. Their half-strength contribution is 2. Separately halving those operands would not satisfy the required relationship.

Positive odd differences lose the fractional half: a difference of 5 contributes 2, and a difference of 1 contributes zero. Even differences divide exactly, including negative even differences.

The supplied material does not independently identify the execution environment’s rule for a negative signed quotient with a fractional part. Under truncation toward zero, a difference of −5 contributes −2. If another signed-division rule applies, its result governs instead. Exact negative-odd outcomes remain **documented_only** until that rule is established.

No remainder or fractional carry is exposed or retained. Separate invocations do not accumulate discarded halves in any evidenced internal state.

There is no evidenced intermediate assignment to the target. In particular, the difference and the selected contribution are not separately published through it.

The evidence does not establish whether the target read occurs before or after evaluation of the difference. The result relationship does not depend on choosing either order because both operands are already supplied scalar values.

These statements concern logical accesses. They do not guarantee a particular number of machine instructions, physical reads, bus transactions, or observable accesses to device memory. Nor do they establish an indivisible read-modify-write operation.

The complete difference and the final sum impose separate arithmetic requirements.

Both active modes form the complete signed difference. Half strength does not establish a widened intermediate range. Consequently, a mathematical half-difference that would fit the target type is insufficient when the complete difference itself is not supported.

After a supported difference is obtained, adding the selected contribution to the initial target must also be supported. A valid subtraction does not guarantee a valid final sum.

The evidence contains no overflow check, saturation rule, diagnostic, recovery action, or specified wrapping behavior. Under an execution model in which signed overflow invalidates ordinary execution, an overflowing difference or final addition has no required ordinary result. Neither preservation of the old target nor a wrapped result is guaranteed.

Inactive selections avoid both arithmetic operations within this function. This does not excuse invalid arithmetic performed by the caller while preparing argument values.
