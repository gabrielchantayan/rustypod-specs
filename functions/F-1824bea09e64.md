Here, **byte**, **halfword**, and **word** mean 8, 16, and 32 bits. Offsets are byte offsets. Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Explicit unsigned byte reads promoted to words yield values from zero through 255.

The opening clear-and-promotion sequence has significant interleaving:

- The scaled-base offset-0x14 word is cleared, then its offset-0x05 byte is captured.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static requirements, not executed tests.

1. **Input truncation.** Under truncating 32-bit shifts, fourth inputs 0x00000001 and 0x00000011 both produce 0x10000000 at the twenty-eight-bit-shift argument position. Third inputs 0x00000001 and 0x10000001 both produce 0x00000010 at their four-bit-shift position. Whole-execution equivalence does not follow.

2. **Unsigned and wide extraction.** Masking 0xFFFFFFFF to twenty bits and logically shifting right by ten yields 0x3FF. A wide result with high word 0x80000000, logically shifted right by thirty-four, yields 0x20000000.

3. **Captured byte promotion.** If the opening scaled-base offset-0x05 read captures 0xFE, the associated offset-0x34 word store receives 0x000000FE even if the intervening small-base clear changes the byte’s source storage.

4. **Readback ordering.** A zero readback requires memory and aliasing conditions that preserve the cleared word through its read. Moving both clears before the byte capture does not preserve the established ordering.

5. **Fresh fixed-byte reads.** If 0x67 receives 0xAB from the captured 0xA9 word but an intervening computed store changes it to 0xCD, a subsequent fresh read consumes 0xCD. The captured full word remains available for its separate word store.

6. **Distinct final destinations.** Given A = 0x1000 and B = 0x3000, with supported arithmetic and writable destinations, the final word stores place 0x1000 at 0x3054, then 0x3000 at 0x2000. They do not specify a store of 0x3000 at 0x1054.

7. **Coincident final destinations.** Given A = 0x1000 and B = 0x1FAC, B plus 0x54 and twice A both equal 0x2000. With ordinary supported memory, two distinct word stores occur there: first 0x1000, then 0x1FAC. The second terminal argument remains the captured A, 0x1000, not the final memory content.

8. **Capture-source overlap.** If the first final destination overlaps the original storage from which A or B was read, the second store still uses captured A for its destination calculation and captured B for its value, subject to ordinary captured-value semantics.

9. **Terminal invocation.** Reaching F-U003 establishes arguments 0x72, A, the earlier linked-base offset-0x14 capture, and the later new-base offset-0x74 capture. It establishes neither a success status nor guaranteed return.
