# F-e43478b171a1

## Purpose

F-e43478b171a1 performs fifteen explicitly represented memory stores and then transfers control indirectly with one explicitly represented 32-bit argument.

Its destinations and payloads depend on two explicit inputs, additional entry state, and external storage read at specific points during execution. No application-level purpose, object model, initialization protocol, or ownership operation is established.

The principal behavioral constraint is **ordered access to potentially overlapping storage**. External values are not all captured at entry. Earlier writes can affect later reads, and separate reads of the same location must not be treated as one observation without additional justification.

This is a static behavioral contract. It does not establish that the represented accesses or transfer are executable on a particular target.

## Behavior

### Arithmetic and width rules

Byte stores retain the low 8 bits of their payloads. Halfword stores retain the low 16 bits. Word stores carry 32 bits.

The following distinctions matter:

- The third input is squared using represented signed 32-bit multiplication.
- The offset is also squared using represented signed 32-bit multiplication.
- Doubling, negative doubling, and several address-contribution additions involve signed integer expressions.
- The early adjustment first adds 70 to a signed value, then adds an unsigned comparison result, and finally assigns the result to a signed 32-bit value.
- The square adjustment first adds 65 to the signed input square, then adds an unsigned equality result, and finally assigns the result to a signed 32-bit value.
- The final adjustment first adds 78 to the first signed late arithmetic reading, then adds an unsigned comparison result. The resulting adjustment is unsigned 32-bit.
- The transfer argument combines two unsigned 32-bit quantities using bitwise OR.

Unsigned arithmetic does not retroactively define an overflowing signed intermediate. In particular, the signed additions preceding the comparison-result additions remain significant.

Where an unsigned 32-bit addition is established, its result is reduced modulo \(2^{32}\). Conversion of an out-of-range unsigned result to signed 32-bit storage is not resolved by the supplied representation. Signed overflow, pointer ordering, and conversion of calculated integers to usable addresses likewise remain unresolved.

The negative-adjustment byte store retains the low eight bits of the negated byte-converted adjustment. Within the represented eight-bit storage model, this has the same stored bit pattern as the low eight bits of the adjustment’s negation. It is not an unrestricted signed payload.

### Initial observations and first store

The function reads the anchor and retains it. It then reads the initial displacement.

**Store 1:** a halfword is written at the original third input plus the initial displacement. Its payload is the low 16 bits of the original third input.

Only after this store does the function read:

1. The early shared arithmetic value.
2. The byte payload.

Consequently, overlap between the first destination and either external location can change the value subsequently observed.

**Store 2:** the anchor’s low 16 bits are written at the retained anchor plus 68.

The third input is then squared. Subsequent references to the **input square** mean this result; they do not replace the original input’s role in Store 1.

The **early adjustment** is the early shared arithmetic reading plus 70, with one additional increment when the indirect destination compares less than or equal to the retained anchor. This comparison is not the later unsigned-integer comparison. Its address-ordering semantics are unresolved.

Two byte stores follow:

| Store | Destination | Payload |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Twice the early shared arithmetic reading | Low byte of the retained byte payload |
| 4 | Negative twice the early adjustment | Low byte of the negated byte-converted early adjustment |

The early shared reading remains distinct from the later reading of the same external location.

### Seed capture and square-dependent stores

After Store 4, the function reads the comparison seed and then the offset.

**Store 5:** the anchor’s low 16 bits are written again at the retained anchor plus 68.

This is a separate represented store. An intervening overlapping write can make it consequential even though its nominal destination and payload match Store 2.

The **square adjustment** is the input square plus 65, with one additional increment if the early adjustment equals zero.

**Store 6:** a byte is written at the input square plus the retained, unsquared offset. Its payload is the input square’s low byte.

The record base is read only after Store 6. Thus, overlap between that byte destination and the record-base storage can affect the observed record base.

The offset is then squared, producing the **offset square**. Four stores follow in this order:

| Store | Destination | Width | Payload |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 7 | Square adjustment plus offset square | 16 bits | Low 16 bits of the offset square |
| 8 | Retained record base plus square adjustment | 32 bits | Square adjustment’s 32-bit representation |
| 9 | Input square plus offset square | 16 bits | Retained anchor’s low 16 bits |
| 10 | Retained anchor plus offset square | 16 bits | Low 16 bits of the offset square |

These expressions do not establish a record, array, allocation extent, or valid address range. Their destinations may overlap one another, previous destinations, or external locations that remain unread.

### Late observations

After Store 10, the function reads, in order:

1. The wide payload.
2. The shared arithmetic value again.
3. The late arithmetic value for the first time.

All three observations are retained.

A second read of the late arithmetic location then supplies the destination contribution for the next store.

**Store 11:** the comparison seed’s low byte is written at twice the **second** late arithmetic reading.

The first and second late arithmetic readings are separate observations. The first remains the input to the final adjustment, Store 14’s destination, and Store 15’s destination and payload.

**Store 12:** the halfword value 78 is written at twice the **late shared arithmetic reading**. This is neither the early shared reading nor either late arithmetic reading.

The final base is then read for the first time and retained.

### Final adjustment and final stores

The **final adjustment** is the first late arithmetic reading plus 78, with one additional increment when the unsigned 32-bit extra entry operand is less than or equal to the retained comparison seed.

Equality therefore selects the incremented result. Both operands of this comparison are unsigned 32-bit quantities.

The final base is read a second time.

**Store 13:** the final adjustment’s low byte is written at the second final-base reading plus the final adjustment. The represented unsigned 32-bit addition wraps modulo \(2^{32}\); whether the resulting address is usable remains a separate target requirement.

The second final-base reading does not replace the first reading retained for the transfer argument.

Two stores complete the explicit memory effects:

| Store | Destination | Width | Payload |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 14 | Twice the first late arithmetic reading | 32 bits | Retained wide payload |
| 15 | Fourth input plus the first late arithmetic reading | 16 bits | Low 16 bits of the first late arithmetic reading |

### Indirect transfer

After all fifteen stores, control transfers to the entry-supplied indirect destination.

The one explicitly represented argument is the 32-bit bitwise OR of:

- The unsigned final adjustment.
- The **first** final-base reading.

Addition is not an equivalent operation.

The supplied representation expresses an invocation followed by return, but does not establish the actual machine transfer convention. An ordinary call, tail transfer, or other indirect-transfer mechanism cannot be selected from this evidence alone.

There are no further explicit body memory accesses after the transfer. The destination’s own accesses, return behavior, and effects remain unknown.

### Unsigned comparison and equality

| Extra entry operand | Unsigned comparison | Final adjustment |
|---:|---|---:|
| 5 | True | 99 |
| 6 | False | 98 |
