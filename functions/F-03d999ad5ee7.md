# F-03d999ad5ee7

## Purpose

F-03d999ad5ee7 computes a two-word product and constructs a shifted 64-bit return value from the retained product words.

Under the fixed-width arithmetic interpretation described below, the multiplication is between a signed 64-bit multiplicand and a signed 32-bit factor. Only the low 64 product bits are retained. For shift selectors from 1 through 63, the return value is the arithmetic right shift of that retained product.

Truncation occurs **before** shifting. Multiplying at unlimited precision, shifting, and then retaining 64 bits is not equivalent in general.

The selector is not simply a conventional 64-bit shift distance. Branch selection uses a signed interpretation of a wrapping 32-bit subtraction, while variable shift counts retain eight bits. Some selectors outside 1 through 63 therefore have determinate behavior under the stated model; others require unavailable semantics for word shifts of 32 or more.

This is a static behavioral contract. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Arguments

The function accepts four scalar arguments in this order:

| Position | Contract name | Representation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low multiplicand word | Unsigned 32-bit value |
| 2 | High multiplicand word | Signed 32-bit value |
| 3 | Factor word | Unsigned 32-bit argument, interpreted through its sign bit during multiplication |
| 4 | Selector | Unsigned 32-bit value |

These are descriptive contract names, not implementation identifiers.

Define:

- \(A\): the unsigned low multiplicand word.
- \(B\): the signed high multiplicand word.
- \(C\): the unsigned factor word.
- \(N\): the unsigned selector.
- \(S\): the signed 32-bit interpretation of the factor word.

Thus:

\[
S=
\begin{cases}
C,&C<2^{31},\\
C-2^{32},&C\ge2^{31}.
\end{cases}
\]

The multiplicand’s signed 64-bit interpretation is:

\[
X=A+2^{32}B.
\]

There are no pointer arguments, buffers, lengths, handles, or ownership-transfer parameters.

### Return value and layout

The return is a 64-bit scalar formed by concatenating two 32-bit words. If their unsigned bit-pattern values are \(R_{\mathrm{high}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{low}}\), the returned pattern is:

\[
R=2^{32}R_{\mathrm{high}}+R_{\mathrm{low}}.
\]

The supplied representation establishes the return width, but not a declared signed return type. Signed interpretations used in this contract explain the arithmetic; they do not change the returned bits.

Concatenation specifies numeric word significance. It does not establish byte order, an in-memory structure, alignment, register placement, or a serialized format.

### Arithmetic model and preconditions

The following interpretation is used for determinate arithmetic claims:

- Words contain 32 bits.
- Unsigned 32-by-32 multiplication is widened to 64 bits before extracting its halves.
- Signed interpretations use two’s complement.
- Right shifting a signed high word is arithmetic and extends its sign.
- High-word arithmetic retains its low 32 bits.
- A left shift described as a word operation retains the resulting 32-bit pattern when its count is within range.

This is a fixed-width interpretation of the supplied arithmetic representation, not a claim of portability under an unspecified implementation language.

No input validation or caller precondition is expressed by the function. Selectors 1 through 63 form a sufficient domain for avoiding word-shift counts of 32 or greater. This does not establish that callers are restricted to that domain.

Cases requiring unavailable shift semantics are identified as `documented_only`; they are not assigned guessed results.

## Behavior

### Product construction

The function derives the two retained product words before constructing the return.

First consider the exact unsigned product:

\[
U=AC.
\]

Because both operands are 32-bit unsigned values, this product fits in 64 unsigned bits. Define its halves:

\[
L=U\bmod2^{32},
\qquad
Q=\left\lfloor U/2^{32}\right\rfloor.
\]

The retained low product word is \(L\). The retained high product word has unsigned bit pattern:

\[
H=
\left(
Q+CB+
\begin{cases}
0,&C<2^{31},\\
-A,&C\ge2^{31}
\end{cases}
\right)\bmod2^{32}.
\]

There are three contributions to this high word:

1. The upper half of the unsigned multiplication of the low multiplicand word by the factor word.
2. The factor’s contribution through the high multiplicand word.
3. A sign correction controlled by the factor’s top bit.

The correction means that treating the factor as purely unsigned would change the behavior.

Under the fixed-width model, the concatenated product satisfies:

\[
2^{32}H+L=(XS)\bmod2^{64}.
\]

This is a statement about retained bits, not about whether the mathematical signed product fits in 64 bits. There is no overflow indication.

For subsequent descriptions, let:

- \(h\) be the signed 32-bit interpretation of \(H\);
- \(P\) be the signed 64-bit interpretation of the concatenation \(H:L\).

### Branch selection

The branch decision uses the signed 32-bit interpretation of the selector minus 32, with subtraction wrapping at 32 bits.

The lower-selector construction is selected when:

\[
\operatorname{signed32}\bigl((N-32)\bmod2^{32}\bigr)<0.
\]

Its exact unsigned selector ranges are:

- `0x00000000` through `0x0000001F`;
- `0x80000020` through `0xFFFFFFFF`.

The other-selector construction is selected for:

- `0x00000020` through `0x8000001F`.

The branch is therefore not equivalent to an unsigned comparison against 32. In particular, a selector’s high bit alone does not determine the branch.

### Lower-selector construction

This construction uses two effective counts:

\[
k=N\bmod256,
\qquad
j=(32-N)\bmod256.
\]

It constructs:

- The high return word by arithmetic-right-shifting \(h\) by \(k\).
- The low return word by bitwise OR of the logical right shift of \(L\) by \(k\) and the 32-bit left shift of the high product word by \(j\).

For \(1\le N\le31\), the effective counts are \(N\) and \(32-N\), both between 1 and 31. The high-word contribution supplies the bits crossing into the low word, while the arithmetic right shift extends the product’s sign into the high return word.

Consequently, in this interval:

\[
R=\operatorname{bits64}\left(\left\lfloor P/2^N\right\rfloor\right),
\]

where \(\operatorname{bits64}\) means the corresponding 64-bit two’s-complement pattern. For negative products, rounding is downward, not toward zero.

At selector zero, the construction includes a left shift by 32. Although an unchanged product is a plausible intended result, that result is not established by the supplied shift semantics. It must not be assumed by treating a shift of 32 as either a shift of zero or a zero-producing operation.

The same construction can be reached by large selectors. Their behavior depends on the branch decision and both eight-bit counts, not on a global reduction of the selector.

### Other-selector construction

This construction uses:

\[
k=(N-32)\bmod256.
\]

It constructs:

- The high return word by arithmetic-right-shifting \(h\) by 31.
- The low return word by arithmetic-right-shifting \(h\) by \(k\).

The high return word is therefore zero when \(h\) is nonnegative and `0xFFFFFFFF` when \(h\) is negative.

The low product word does not contribute to this return construction.

For \(32\le N\le63\), the effective count is \(N-32\), between 0 and 31. The result again satisfies:

\[
R=\operatorname{bits64}\left(\left\lfloor P/2^N\right\rfloor\right).
\]

At selector 32, the product high word becomes the result low word, with its sign extended into the result high word. At selector 63, all 64 returned bits equal the sign bit of the retained product.

### Selectors outside 1 through 63

There is no expressed rejection, clamping, or universal reduction modulo 64.

Some larger selectors yield in-range counts. Selector 288, for example, enters the other-selector construction with effective count zero and therefore uses the same return construction as selector 32.

Other selectors leave part of the result unresolved. Selector 64 enters the other-selector construction with effective count 32. Its high return word remains the sign extension of \(h\); its low return word requires unavailable full-word shift semantics.

Two large selectors illustrate why branch selection must be evaluated separately from the low count bits:

- For `0x80000001`, the wrapping subtraction yields `0x7FFFFFE1`. Its signed interpretation is nonnegative, so the **other-selector construction** applies. The effective low-word arithmetic-right-shift count is 225. The high return word is the sign extension of \(h\), but the complete result is `documented_only`. Equivalence to selector 1 is unsupported.
- For `0x80000101`, the subtraction yields `0x800000E1`, whose signed interpretation is negative. The **lower-selector construction** applies, with effective counts 1 and 31. Under the stated arithmetic model, it produces the same result as selector 1.
