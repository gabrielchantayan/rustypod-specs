# F-c0985d8666ae

## Purpose

F-c0985d8666ae compares two descriptors using their stored element counts first and their unsigned array words second.

For stable inputs and defined operations, its ordering rule is:

- Different counts: return the signed difference, left count minus right count.
- Equal positive counts: compare corresponding unsigned words, giving higher indices precedence over lower indices. Return +1 or −1 according to the highest-index unequal pair.
- Equal positive counts with identical included words: return zero.
- Equal nonpositive counts: return zero, provided the required count decrement is representable.

The result is **not uniformly normalized**. Count-based results retain the magnitude of the difference; word-based unequal results have magnitude one.

**Inferred intent:** the arrays may represent nonnegative multiword quantities whose lower-index words are less significant. This interpretation is not a guarantee of mathematical-number comparison. Stored lengths take precedence even when additional words are zero, and no representation normalization is established.

## Behavior

### Count precedence

The count is the primary ordering key. When stable counts differ, array contents do not participate in the decision.

The difference is returned directly rather than reduced to its sign. For example, counts five and two produce three. Reversing those counts produces minus three.

Consequences include:

- More stored words compare greater when the count subtraction is defined, regardless of their values.
- Extra zero words are significant through the count.
- Invalid or inaccessible array storage does not affect a count-only execution if the required descriptor count accesses remain valid.
- An obvious mathematical ordering does not remove the requirement for representable subtraction.

For example, under a signed 32-bit interpretation, the difference between 2,147,483,647 and −1 is not representable. The function does not establish a comparison result for that case.

The count accesses do not form a snapshot of both descriptors. The left count is retained after its initial read. The right count participates in the initial decision and is accessed again when an unequal-count result is formed. Stable storage makes these uses consistent. Changing or read-sensitive storage has no established comparison contract.

### Word precedence

When counts are equal and positive, the array comparison covers indices from count minus one down to zero.

The highest index at which corresponding words differ determines the result:

- A larger unsigned left word produces +1.
- A smaller unsigned left word produces −1.
- Lower indices cannot override that decision and are not subsequently examined.

When every included pair matches, the result is zero.

Word comparison is unsigned. Under the 32-bit interpretation, a word valued 2,147,483,648 compares greater than a word valued 2,147,483,647. The highest bit does not make an array word negative.

The unequal-word result is not the arithmetic difference between the words. Even widely separated values produce only +1 or −1.

This is an ordering of decoded words, not a bytewise comparison. The environment determines how bytes at a computed address become an unsigned word.

Descriptor identity and equal array addresses do not provide a visible shortcut. A positive-count comparison of a descriptor with itself still requires valid address calculations and the reached word reads.

### Zero and negative counts

Equal zero counts produce zero without reading either array-address field.

Equal negative counts also produce zero without reading array-address fields, but only after a required decrement of the retained count. Therefore, representability of that decrement matters even though no word is examined.

Under the signed 32-bit interpretation:

- Equal counts of −2 produce zero.
- Equal counts of −2,147,483,647 produce zero.
- Equal counts of −2,147,483,648 have no established result because the decrement falls outside the signed range.

Acceptance of other negative equal counts is an observed static control-flow property, not evidence that negative counts are intended valid representations.
