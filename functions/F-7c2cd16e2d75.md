# F-7c2cd16e2d75

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Character-like constants, pointer-like values, and repeated stores do not establish text generation, object construction, or a particular data structure.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven prefix

The prefix contains an extensive, ordered sequence of indirect calls. Most targets are formed from position-related values using a common displacement of 0xffe; the initial target additionally depends on incoming state.

Calls with no represented arguments, and calls whose primary results are unused, remain observable interactions. They may affect storage, control flow, or secondary machine-result values consumed later.

Result transformations include:

- Left shifts, frequently by eight bits.
- Separate consumption of low and high components of paired results.
- Masks selecting fields of several widths.
- Logical right shifts of explicitly unsigned values.
- Repetition of a transformed value in multiple argument positions.
- Explicit zero arguments and shifts of values already set to zero.

These operations establish numerical relationships, not semantic types.

[INFERENCE] Under truncating 32-bit arithmetic, an eight-bit left shift followed by an eight-bit logical right shift retains only the original low 24 bits. Successive left shifts can discard every original bit when their combined displacement reaches or exceeds 32; they are not unbounded multiplication. Signed operations and architecture-specific execution remain qualified under **Uncertainties**.

### Initial mutation and the fixed-byte dependency

The first mutations are ordered: a word store at the final pair’s high component plus 0x14 receives the positioned value; a byte store at the positioned value plus nine receives the low byte of that high component; a word store at the final pair’s low component multiplied by 256 plus 0x54 receives the scaled base; then the byte at the positioned value plus nine is overwritten with the positioned value’s low byte.

The following region combines zero stores, byte loads, and word loads:

- The word at the scaled base plus 0x14 receives zero before the byte at the scaled base plus five is read.
- A zero store at the doubled selector plus 0x14 precedes storing that loaded byte, zero-extended, at the scaled base plus 0x34.
- The word at the scaled base plus 0x14 is then loaded and retained.
- Another zero store at the doubled selector plus 0x14 precedes two byte stores at the doubled selector plus 0x15, both using the retained word’s low byte.

The loaded word is not necessarily zero: intervening stores can overlap its storage.

**Only after those accesses**, the function reads an unsigned byte at fixed address **0x05**. That read precedes the byte write at fixed address **0x01**, which receives the doubled selector’s low byte.

The byte read from 0x05 becomes a numerical address base. Subsequent accesses include a byte store at that base plus 0x15 and a word read followed by a word overwrite at that base plus 0x54. The word read there supplies an important retained value used in later scaling and addressing.

Neither the byte read nor its address-forming role may be replaced by an entry-time snapshot. Earlier computed stores may overlap 0x05.

### Interdependent computed updates

The next region uses retained words, byte-derived bases, and word-loaded bases in mixed-width accesses. It includes:

- A retained-word byte store at the scaled base plus 0x11, subsequently overwritten with zero.
- Word stores at computed bases plus 0x54.
- Word loads through offsets 0x64 and 0x74.
- Self-referential stores that place a numerical base into storage associated with that base.
- Repeated byte stores at offsets nine and 0x15.
- Fixed word storage at 0x54 and fixed byte storage at 0x15.
- Further byte loads at computed offset five whose unsigned values form bases for word reads at offset 0x74.

A self-referential store does not establish a linked structure or ownership transfer. Repeated reads at the same nominal offset remain distinct reads; intervening overlapping writes may change their results.

For describing the later phases:

- The **link word** is the retained word read from the byte-derived base plus 0x54.
- The **adjusted value** is four times the link word, less 0x96.
- The **working base** is four times the adjusted value.
- The **traversal word** is a later word obtained through a byte-derived base plus 0x74.
- The **twice-traversal base** and **four-times-traversal base** are twice and four times that traversal word.

These arithmetic relationships retain the represented 32-bit qualifications.

### Constant stores and the fixed-word dependency

The constant 0x3a is stored using three distinct widths: a byte at the four-times-traversal base, a halfword at eight times the traversal word, and a word at the four-times-traversal base plus 0x20.

At the working base plus 0x20, the word-write sequence is:

1. 0x20.
2. 0x2f.
3. The zero-extended byte read from the working base plus four at that point.
4. 0x20 again.

The intervening byte read follows the first two stores. Checking only the final word value loses established ordering and potential alias effects.

After this sequence, the function reads a **32-bit word at fixed address 0x087fbbbc**. Its result is the **repetition base**, retained for the repeated-update region and subsequent mutations.

This read occurs **before** the fixed word store of 0x20 at **0x60**, and before the repeated updates begin. Earlier computed stores may overlap 0x087fbbbc; its value is therefore not an entry-time constant. Conversely, later modification of that storage does not itself replace the already retained repetition base.

### Repeated-update region

Each represented update group preserves this relative ordering:

- A computed word destination at offset 0x54 receives the four-times-traversal base.
- The byte at the repetition base plus 0x19 receives the working base’s low byte.
- The byte at the working base plus 0x11 receives the repetition base’s low byte.
- The word at the repetition base plus 0x34 receives a scaled address-forming value.

The first group uses eight times the link word for its first destination base and its final stored value. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted value instead.

The stores use retained numerical values, not fresh reads from 0x087fbbbc. They remain separate events even if aliasing makes destinations coincide or stored values repeat.

Exact repeated-group multiplicity is documented_only in this contract; no runtime trace or independently validated instruction-level count is available. This does not authorize collapsing the region into a single final-state update.

### Final fixed-address ordering and saved values

After the repeated region, the established ordering boundary is:

1. Read the word at fixed address **0xa9**, retaining it as the **saved fixed word**.
2. Read the word at the four-times-traversal base plus **0x74**, retaining it as the **saved traversal field**.
3. Write the byte at fixed address **0x3a**, using eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30, narrowed to eight bits.
4. Read and save the word at the twice-traversal base plus **0x14**.
5. Read and save the word at the working base plus **0x04**.

The two saved computed words are read **after** the 0x3a write. If that write overlaps either read, the read uses the resulting storage state. The saved traversal field, however, was already read before the 0x3a write.

Further mutations write bytes at the twice-traversal base plus 0x0d and at the working base plus eight, 0x0d, and nine. The offset-eight byte receives 0x6c. A word at the twice-traversal base plus 0x54 receives that base itself.

The function then writes the low byte of the saved fixed word to fixed address **0x67**. Crucially:

- A computed word read at the twice-traversal base plus **0x44** occurs next.
- Only afterward is the byte at **0x67 read** for the write to fixed address **0x21**.

The write to 0x21 therefore consumes a memory read from 0x67, not merely the saved fixed word’s low byte. The intervening computed read remains ordered, including where reads have externally visible effects.

### Later reloads and final argument acquisition

The computed word read at offset 0x44 supplies a byte store at the working base plus 0x10 and a word store at the repetition base plus 0x54. It also supplies a shifted byte at the twice-traversal base plus one. The shift count is the working base’s low eight bits; the represented right shift acts on a signed word.

The repetition base plus 0x54 is subsequently overwritten with the working base. A word read at the working base plus 0x40 supplies a word write at the working base plus 0x10, replacing the earlier byte-level update. Between that read and write, the saved offset-0x04 word is stored at the repetition base plus 0x74.

After another computed word store, **0x67 is read again** for a byte store at the working base plus nine. Following further mutations—including storing the saved fixed word at the working base plus 0x44—the function reads the word at the working base plus 0x54. It then **reads 0x67 a third time** for another byte store at the working base plus nine.

These three reads of 0x67 are distinct. Computed writes between them can overlap 0x67, so their results need not equal one another or the originally copied byte.

The remaining region uses the newly loaded word as another address base, restores the saved offset-0x14 word to the twice-traversal base plus 0x54, and reads a word at the working base plus 0x14 for later byte narrowing. The saved traversal field supplies low-byte stores at computed offsets 0x0d and 0x11.

A later word read at the twice-traversal base plus four supplies the **terminal base**. The final mutations include:

- A halfword store of 0x65 at twice the terminal base.
- A word read at the terminal base plus 0x64, retained as the second final argument.
- Byte and word stores through the other loaded base, including a word value of decimal 100.
- A word read at the terminal base plus 0x74, retained as the fourth final argument.
- A later word read at the twice-traversal base plus 0x64, followed by final byte and word writes using these retained values.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

### Width-sensitive transformation

Assuming truncating 32-bit shifts, 0x12345678 shifted left by eight becomes 0x34567800. A subsequent logical right shift by eight yields 0x00345678. Arbitrary-precision preservation would not match this interpretation.

### Fixed byte changed before consumption

Assume earlier computed writes change address 0x05 from 0x12 to 0x34 and all relevant accesses complete. The later unsigned-byte read supplies numerical base 0x34 for the dependent accesses. Using the entry value 0x12 would violate the established dependency. The read must precede the write to 0x01.

### Retained repetition base

Assume storage at 0x087fbbbc contains one word on entry but a different word after the preceding computed stores. The later word is retained as the repetition base before the 0x60 write. Changing that storage afterward does not trigger an unrepresented reload.

### Save boundary around 0x3a

Assume the byte write at 0x3a overlaps one of the subsequent saved computed words. That word must be read after the write. The saved traversal field must instead reflect its earlier read, before the write.

### Distinct 0x67 reloads

Assume the saved fixed word initially contributes low byte 0x11 to 0x67, and an intervening computed store changes 0x67 to 0x22 before a later reload. The later dependent byte store receives 0x22. Reusing 0x11 for every occurrence is not equivalent.

### Final saved argument

Assume later stores change the original offset-0x14 storage after its value was saved. F-U03 still receives the saved value in its third position, not a fresh read.

### Unsupported address

If a computed access is unsupported, graceful rejection, a status return, cleanup, and rollback are not required by the available evidence.
