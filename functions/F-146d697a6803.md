# F-146d697a6803

## Purpose

F-146d697a6803 multiplies a sequence of unsigned 32-bit words by one unsigned 32-bit scalar. It writes the low-order words of the result to caller-supplied storage and returns the final carry as a pointer-shaped value.

The arithmetic proceeds from the first word toward higher-indexed words. Under the intended arithmetic interpretation, the first word is the least significant limb of a base-$2^{32}$ integer.

For a positive word count, disjoint buffers, and a stable source, the relationship is:

$$
D + C\,2^{32n} = S\,m
$$

where:

- $n$ is the word count.
- $m$ is the unsigned scalar.
- $S$ is the integer represented by the source words in increasing significance.
- $D$ is the integer represented by the destination words in increasing significance.
- $C$ is the numeric value encoded in the return value.

This relationship also holds for exact in-place operation. With other overlapping buffers, writes can change source words before they are consumed; the sequential behavior below takes precedence over a product of the original source contents.

The multiword multiplication interpretation is inferred intent. The supplied static representation directly establishes the wordwise arithmetic, carry propagation, access order, and early exits, subject to the target-width qualifications in **Uncertainties**.

## Behavior

### Nonpositive counts

If the signed count is less than one, the function returns zero in its pointer-shaped return representation.

On this path:

- No source word is read.
- No destination word is written.
- The scalar does not affect the result.
- There is no attempt to validate either buffer address.

Negative counts are not converted into large unsigned lengths.

### Positive counts

Processing begins with a numeric carry of zero. Each word contributes one destination word and a carry for the following word.

Let $B=2^{32}$. For source word $x_i$ as observed when position $i$ is processed, incoming carry $c_i$, and multiplier $m$, the intended per-word contract is:

$$
t_i = x_i m + c_i
$$

$$
d_i = t_i \bmod B
$$

$$
c_{i+1} = \left\lfloor t_i/B \right\rfloor
$$

with $c_0=0$.

The function writes $d_i$ to destination position $i$ before proceeding to position $i+1$. After the final destination write, it returns the numeric value $c_n$ through the pointer-shaped return representation.

No extra carry word is written beyond the requested destination range. A caller needing a full result must separately account for the returned carry.

### Arithmetic widths and overflow

The supplied representation constructs each word product using 16-bit halves:

- The lower half of a word is its value masked to 16 bits.
- The upper half is obtained by an unsigned shift of 16 bits.
- Each half lies between zero and $2^{16}-1$.
- Each individual half-by-half product fits in an unsigned 32-bit word.
- Sums of cross-products can exceed 32 bits and require explicit carry correction.

The arithmetic handles three distinct contributions to the high result word:

1. Overflow of the cross-product sum, compensated at the appropriate 16-bit position.
2. Overflow when assembling the low product word.
3. Overflow when adding the incoming carry to that low word.

The overflow checks are strict unsigned comparisons. Equality alone does not create a carry.

Under the intended fixed-width machine interpretation, intermediate low-word results wrap modulo $2^{32}$, while the corrections reconstruct the high word. The mathematical temporary $t_i$ fits within 64 unsigned bits: both operands and the incoming carry are at most $2^{32}-1$.

The supplied representation also uses signed integer conversions and pointer-shaped intermediates. Consequently, this contract does not assert that recompiling that representation under arbitrary language implementations would preserve the arithmetic. The numeric contract depends on the intended target’s widths, conversions, wrapping arithmetic, and address comparison behavior.

### Processing groups and termination

The visible control flow handles up to four consecutive words per loop group. It exits immediately after the final requested word:

- A remainder of one ends after the first word.
- A remainder of two ends after the second word.
- A remainder of three ends after the third word.
- A remainder of four ends after the fourth word.
- Larger remaining counts continue with the next four-word region.

This grouping does not introduce padding reads or writes in the supplied representation. A count not divisible by four still processes exactly the requested number of words.

For a positive signed count, subtracting four occurs only after four available positions have been processed. The visible count arithmetic therefore does not require a negative remainder.

### Observable access order

Word positions are processed in increasing index order. For each position:

1. The current source word supplies its low and high halves.
2. The arithmetic combines that word with the multiplier and incoming carry.
3. The destination word for that position is written.
4. Only then does processing advance to the next source position.

The supplied representation expresses the low-half and high-half extractions as separate source accesses. It does not establish whether execution performs two physical loads or reuses one load. The mathematical recurrence assumes both extractions observe the same word value.

No later source position is visibly read before the current destination position is written. This matters for overlapping buffers.

### Aliasing and overlapping buffers

Exact in-place use is supported by the visible order: the current source word is consumed before its corresponding destination word is overwritten, and that write does not alter later source positions.

For distinct, nonoverlapping regions, each destination word derives from the corresponding original source word.

Other overlaps are order-sensitive:

- A destination located earlier than the source can overwrite already-consumed source positions.
- A destination located later than the source can overwrite source positions that have not yet been consumed.
- Such forward overlap does not have snapshot semantics.
- The function performs no overlap detection and uses no temporary copy.

These statements describe sequential accesses to ordinary storage. They do not establish synchronization or atomicity guarantees for concurrent access.

## Side effects

There is no visible destination read and no accumulation into preexisting destination contents. However, destination contents may also be source contents when the regions overlap.
