### Ordinary conversion: rounding

For representations that pass the lower boundary:

- Low-word bit 28 is the guard bit.
- Low-word bits 0–27 are the remainder below the guard bit.
- Low-word bit 29 is the least significant retained bit.

Rounding increments the retained representation exactly when the guard bit is set and either the remainder is nonzero or bit 29 is set.

Thus an exact halfway value increments only when its retained low bit is odd. Where the floating-point interpretation applies, this is round to nearest, ties to even.

The increment adds `0x20000000` to the low word with 32-bit wraparound. The adjusted quantity also increases by one exactly when the **pre-increment** low word is greater than `0xdfffffff` under an unsigned comparison. This propagates a rounding carry across the low/high word boundary.

If the rounding condition is false, neither quantity changes.

### Ordinary conversion: upper boundary and packing

After rounding, the adjusted quantity is compared as signed against `0x0fefffff`.

If it is greater, the result is the sign word combined with `0x7f800000`.

Otherwise, the result combines:

- The sign word.
- The adjusted quantity shifted left by three bits.
- The possibly rounded low word shifted right logically by 29 bits.

Only three bits from the low word contribute to the packed result.

The overflow check occurs after rounding, so a rounding carry can change a finite output into the signed infinity pattern.
