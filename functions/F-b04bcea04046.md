# F-b04bcea04046

## Purpose

F-b04bcea04046 is a global-state-directed dispatcher. Its available operations acquire a resource word, invoke a second resource-related dependency, request 40 bytes of storage, calculate attribute-dependent words, initialize selected record fields, publish a record address, and invoke cleanup-shaped dependencies.

**Established static evidence:** The supplied representation determines the operation selected by each selector, direct memory-access ordering, dependency arguments, retained state, arithmetic relationships, and identities of shared global locations. It does not provide the global contents needed to establish an execution path.

**Inferred intent:** The function resembles construction of a resource-bearing record containing two integer attributes and a 24-byte payload.

That interpretation does not establish a constructor lifecycle. Acquisition need not precede allocation; publication need not precede return; cleanup need not be balanced; termination is not established.

| First attribute | Signed 32 bits | Used in arithmetic and the four-byte record store at offset 4. |

The return value is an unsigned 32-bit word. Its retained value initially equals zero. Selector 3 returns that value without additional direct cleanup.

The saved arguments and their working copies are distinct. Working attributes and payload source initially equal zero; the working output location initially equals null. Selector 12 activates the saved arguments by transferring them into working storage.

There is no universal validation phase. Publication requires writable record storage and a writable output location. Payload-access requirements depend on F-unknown-01. A null check in another selector does not establish that publication with an invalid pointer is unreachable.

### Entry and dispatch

The first global read obtains G-cached; its value is retained as \(K\). The initial-state read obtains G-seed before dispatch begins.

Entry initializes the resource slot, secondary-output slot, working inputs, record pointer, retained secondary-operation result, return-result word, and two retained arithmetic words to zero or null. It also saves the four supplied arguments.

Each dispatch iteration:

- Reads G-fallback.
- Reads G-adjust and forms the selector from the current state plus that adjustment.
- Performs the selected operation.

The G-fallback read occurs even for selector 3, which returns, and for operations that do not use that fallback.

Selectors 0–16 have explicit behavior. Selector 0 and every selector outside that range perform the secondary resource operation; there is no direct unsupported-selector error.

For arithmetic-predicate branches, predicate zero selects the fallback candidate and predicate one selects the primary candidate. Selectors 0/default and 9 replace the iteration-entry fallback with their own transition samples. Allocation and output validation use separate Boolean selection rules described below.

### Arithmetic model

Inputs and several intermediates are represented as signed 32-bit quantities; other words are unsigned 32-bit quantities. Bit 31 is the most significant bit. Unsigned extraction of that bit yields zero or one.

The formulas below describe a **conditional modulo-\(2^{32}\) word model**. Addition, subtraction, and multiplication in those formulas retain their low 32 bits; complement and bitwise operations act on 32-bit patterns. This model does not resolve signed-overflow rules in the supplied representation, including state arithmetic and signed-looking multiplications.

Let \(x\) and \(y\) denote the working first and second attributes. Let \(H\) and \(J\) denote the two retained arithmetic words. These are specification notation.

### Attribute arithmetic

**Selector 1** updates \(H\) to \(Ky\). It reads G-coefficient to form \(J=yA+2\), then reads that same location again to form the return-result word:

\[
R=(H-1)\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}(yB+1).
\]

Here \(A\) and \(B\) are separate samples of G-coefficient. Their equality is not established. Bit 31 of \(R\) is the predicate. The primary candidate comes from G-next-1; the fallback remains the iteration-entry G-fallback sample.

**Selector 6** first reads G-coefficient and multiplies that sample by \(x\), producing a word \(t\). It then reads G-retained-mask, obtaining \(M\), and retains:

\[
H=(t+65537)\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}M,\qquad J=t+65536.
\]

Its result expression contains five further global reads: two occurrences of G-arithmetic-repeat, one G-arithmetic-subtract read, one G-arithmetic-multiply read, and one G-arithmetic-mask read. No evaluation order among those operands is established.

An independent word-model characterization uses samples \(A,B,D,E,N\), respectively. Define:

\[
V=t+65537,\qquad
Q=(t-D)+((xE+1)\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}N).
\]

The result is:

\[
R=
\left[A-B-((t-1)\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}\mathop{\mathrm{NOT}}B)\right]
+
\left[(Q\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}\mathop{\mathrm{NOT}}V)
      \mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}H\right].
\]

The two samples \(A\) and \(B\) come from the same location but remain independent observations. This characterization does not require them to agree.

Bit 31 of \(R\) supplies the predicate. The primary candidate is read from G-next-6; the fallback remains the iteration-entry G-fallback sample.

**Selector 4** does not refresh \(H\) or \(J\). It sets:

\[
R=(H-2)\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}J.
\]

Bit 31 supplies the predicate. The primary candidate comes from G-next-4; the fallback is the iteration-entry G-fallback sample.

Thus selector 4 uses whichever values selectors 1 or 6 most recently installed. Before either executes, both retained words are zero. Selector 12 does not refresh them.

None of these operations independently establishes an ordinary range, minimum, maximum, or validity check.

## Side effects

Direct publication writes target record offsets 4, 36, and 0, in that order. The payload dependency follows those stores; the four-byte caller-output write follows its normal return.

Entry does not directly clear caller output. No direct store initializes record bytes 8–11.

Final field contents require noninterference assumptions. F-unknown-01 may affect accessible memory, and the output location is not established to be disjoint from the record. An aliasing publication write can overwrite an earlier field.

Addresses of the resource slot, secondary-output slot, and payload destination escape to dependencies. Their writes, retained aliases, and later access are unresolved. Dependencies must not be assumed to preserve globals or caller-accessible memory.

No direct global assignment appears. Distinct global reads remain part of the represented ordering even when their values are unused or a branch selects another candidate.

Ownership is unresolved. Allocation-shaped calls, address publication, and cleanup-shaped calls do not establish ownership transfer, release, invalidation, or exactly-once cleanup. No local ownership flag prevents repeated cleanup or cleanup after publication.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not observed executions. They assume the stated selector is reached and any relevant arithmetic is defined or interpreted under the conditional word model.

### Allocation and validation

### Shared locations across selectors

If G-next-shared remains 100, selectors 5, 11, 14, and 16 each obtain 100 from their next-state read. Their other effects differ. These are next states, not necessarily the next selectors: the following iteration applies G-adjust.

If a dependency changes G-next-shared to 200, a later read by any of those four selectors observes that same location’s then-current contents. Four independent per-selector locations would not preserve the established identity.

The corresponding shared-location requirement applies to selectors 7 and 15 through G-next-cleanup-status, and to noncached coefficient reads through G-coefficient.

### Publication and aliasing

Given prior argument transfer, valid record storage, valid output storage, and normal dependency return, publication performs the three field stores before the payload call and publishes the address afterward.

With \(K=1\) and representable products, the attribute stores equal the transferred attributes. Their final contents additionally require that dependency effects and the output write do not overwrite them.

### Retained arithmetic and independent samples

If selector 4 runs before selectors 1 and 6, its retained words are zero. Its word-model result is \(0xFFFFFFFE\), giving predicate one.

After selector 6, selector 4 uses selector 6’s retained words even if selector 12 subsequently transfers different attributes.

For selector 6, holding every other sample fixed while increasing \(A\) by one increases the result by one modulo \(2^{32}\). This does not authorize replacing \(A\) and \(B\) with one sample.

### Cleanup and incomplete initialization

If publication is followed by another allocation and then selector 7, the cleanup-shaped dependency receives the replacement pointer, not necessarily the published pointer.

If F-unknown-01 writes exactly its 24-byte destination and no other access modifies bytes 8–11, those four bytes retain their prior storage contents. Complete zero-initialization is not required.

A zero return without publication does not establish construction success. An existing output sentinel remains unchanged only if dependencies and aliases also leave it unchanged.
