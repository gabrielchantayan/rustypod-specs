### Arithmetic interpretation

Let **H** denote the final pattern’s high 32-bit word and **L** its low 32-bit word. Where signed comparisons are described, H has the signed 32-bit interpretation represented by the supplied material.

The selector is obtained by an arithmetic right shift of signed H by 20 positions. Under that interpretation, its range is −2048 through 2047.

The candidate combines:

- H shifted left by 11 positions.
- L logically shifted right by 21 positions.

These components are combined by bitwise OR. If the selector is nonzero, the candidate’s most significant bit is then forced to one.

**The candidate construction is not an unconditional portable signed-integer operation.** Its left-shift operand is signed in the supplied representation. A negative operand, or a positive operand whose shifted result exceeds the signed 32-bit range, prevents assigning portable semantics to that expression.

Accordingly, numerical candidate results below assume **32-bit bitvector semantics**: left shifts retain only the low 32 bits, and bitwise operations act on those retained bits. Machine-level confirmation of this interpretation is unresolved. The later assignment to an unsigned value does not itself resolve the preceding signed-shift issue.

The arithmetic-right-shift description also follows the represented signed interpretation; it is not a claim that every language implementation treats negative signed shifts identically.
