### Initial memory mutations

The last split dependency result contributes addresses to the first mutations. The initial five stores have this relative order:

1. A word at the high result half plus 0x14 receives a retained prefix value.
2. A retained shift-by-25 base at offset 9 receives the high half’s low byte.
3. A word at 256 times the low result half plus 0x54 receives a separately retained scaled prefix value.
4. The same offset-9 byte destination receives the retained prefix value’s low byte.
5. A word at the separately retained scaled prefix base plus 0x14 is cleared.

**[INFERENCE]:** the fourth store writes zero under 32-bit arithmetic because its source was shifted left twenty-five. It remains distinct from the preceding store to that byte.

Next, an offset-5 byte is read from the scaled prefix base. Extracted-base offset 0x14 is cleared; the byte is zero-extended into a word at scaled-prefix-base offset 0x34; scaled-prefix-base offset 0x14 is read; and extracted-base offset 0x14 is cleared again. The loaded word’s low byte is then written twice consecutively at extracted-base offset 0x15.

**[INFERENCE]:** in stable ordinary memory, the scaled-prefix-base offset-0x14 read observes the earlier zero. The intervening clear cannot introduce nonzero bytes, and the offset-0x34 store does not overlap that same-base word under ordinary address interpretation. Side-effecting memory, unusual address semantics, or external modification can invalidate this consequence.

### Absolute byte input and seed capture

After the two offset-0x15 byte stores, an 8-bit input is read from **absolute address 0x05**. This is not an offset-5 read through a derived base. Its zero-extended value is retained as the **byte-selected base**.

Before seed capture, the represented effects occur in this order:

- Absolute 0x01 receives the extracted base’s low byte.
- Byte-selected-base offset 0x15 receives the extracted quantity’s low byte.
- Scaled-prefix-base offset 0x11 receives a retained prefix byte.
- A word through the earlier offset-0x14 capture plus 0x54 receives a retained prefix word.
- Scaled-prefix-base offset 0x11 is cleared.
- Byte-selected-base offset 0x54 is read as a word.
- That word location is overwritten with the earlier offset-0x14 capture.

The word obtained by the offset-0x54 read is the **seed word**. Its value is determined at that read, after all preceding aliasing effects.

Before address-wrapping considerations, byte-selected-base offset 0x15 ranges from 0x15 through 0x114. Its offset-0x54 word begins between 0x54 and 0x153. Input zero selects 0x15 and 0x54 without a special branch.

### Seed-linked effects and fixed-address stores

Extracted-base offset 0x34 receives the extracted quantity. A word from scaled-prefix-base offset 0x74 supplies another address source.

This region includes a compound self-referential effect: reads at extracted-base offset 0x74 supply a destination base and a value stored at that base plus 0x54. The internal order of those separate reads is not established. They need not agree for side-effecting storage.

The seed word is stored twice at its own offset 0x24, with other stores between them. A derived offset-9 byte receives the seed’s low byte twice.

The following relative order is established:

1. Extracted-base offset 0x14 receives a retained prefix-derived value.
2. **Absolute 0x54 receives a 32-bit word equal to the seed shifted left one.**
3. Extracted-base offset 0x14 receives the seed twice, as two separate word stores.
4. Extracted-base offset 0x15 receives the low byte of four times the seed twice.
5. **Absolute 0x15 receives the extracted base’s low byte.**

**[INFERENCE]:** under 32-bit truncation, absolute 0x54 receives the low 32 bits of twice the seed. The derived byte stores retain the low eight bits of four times the seed.

The byte stores can alter the previously written seed word because offset 0x15 lies within the word at offset 0x14. If the extracted base is zero, the final absolute 0x15 store overwrites the same byte after both derived byte stores. Fixed-address effects are not independent of derived effects.

### Terminal captures and fresh global reads

The terminal region first captures a word from absolute 0xA9 as the **global snapshot**, then a word from scaled-link-base offset 0x74 as the **link snapshot**. Absolute 0x3A next receives the low byte of eight times the adjusted seed plus 0x30.

The function then captures words from doubled-link-base offset 0x14 and working-base offset 4. The first capture becomes the third final argument; the second is retained for a later external-base store.

Further effects copy captured base bytes to doubled-link-base offset 0x0D and working-base offsets 0x0D and 9. Working-base offset 8 receives 0x6C. Doubled-link-base offset 0x54 receives the doubled-link base itself.

The global snapshot’s low byte is stored at absolute 0x67. A fresh word is then read from doubled-link-base offset 0x44. Only afterward is absolute 0x67 freshly read as a byte and copied to absolute 0x21.

The fresh offset-0x44 word supplies:

- Its low byte to working-base offset 0x10.
- Its full word to external-base offset 0x54.
- A shifted byte to doubled-link-base offset 1.

The shift is represented as a signed right shift by the working base’s low eight bits. Negative operands and counts beyond the operand width remain unresolved. External-base offset 0x54 is subsequently overwritten with the working base.

**The next three effects have this exact interleaving:**

1. Read and retain the 32-bit word at working-base offset 0x40.
2. Store the previously captured working-base offset-4 word at external-base offset 0x74.
3. Store the retained offset-0x40 word at working-base offset 0x10.

Thus the offset-0x40 capture precedes the external-base offset-0x74 store, while the working-base offset-0x10 replacement follows that store. Neither store may be exchanged with the other on an assumption of disjoint storage. If the external-base destination aliases the offset-0x40 source, the final offset-0x10 write still uses the earlier capture.

External-base offset 0x44 then receives the doubled-link base.

Two later stores to working-base offset 9 each independently read absolute 0x67. Between those reads, doubled-link-base offset 9 receives the captured working-base byte, working-base offset 0x44 receives the global snapshot, and working-base offset 0x54 is read as a word.

Those intervening writes may alias 0x67. The two reads must not be replaced with the snapshot’s low byte, the byte copied to 0x21, or each other.

Doubled-link-base offset 0x11 receives the link snapshot’s low byte. Working-base offsets 9 and 0x44 receive, respectively, the terminal-derived base’s low byte and full word.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static deductions, not executed tests.

1. **Masked extraction:** masking 0xAB123456 with 0xFFFFFF and shifting right eight yields 0x1234.

2. **Bounded extraction:** under the 32-bit model, extracted quantity 508 is possible; 511 is not. Its doubled base is 1,016.

3. **Successive shifts:** shifting 0x00000011 left fourteen twice gives 0x10000000 under 32-bit truncation.

4. **Byte-selected input:** an absolute 0x05 read returning 0x80 selects byte destination 0x95 and word-load address 0xD4. The word is read only when reached, after intervening mutations. Input zero instead selects 0x15 and 0x54 without rejection.

5. **Seed ordering:** for seed 0x12345678 and extracted base 8, the relevant sequence writes word 0x2468ACF0 at absolute 0x54, writes the seed twice at 0x1C, writes byte 0xE0 twice at 0x1D, then writes byte 0x08 at absolute 0x15. Endianness determines the significance of the overlapping 0x1D byte.

6. **Absolute alias:** with extracted base zero, both fourfold-seed byte stores target 0x15. The following absolute store writes zero there in stable ordinary memory.

7. **Ordered overlapping destinations:** suppose working-base offset 0x10 and external-base offset 0x74 designate the same word, the retained working-base offset-4 capture is 0x11223344, and the fresh offset-0x40 capture is 0xAABBCCDD. The external-base store first writes 0x11223344; the subsequent working-base store writes 0xAABBCCDD. Immediately after those two stores, the latter value wins, assuming ordinary valid memory and no external modification.

8. **Capture before overwrite:** if external-base offset 0x74 aliases working-base offset 0x40, the offset-0x40 read still precedes that store. Its captured value, not the replacement value, supplies the later offset-0x10 write.

9. **Fresh global bytes:** if 0x67 initially receives 0xAB but an intervening alias changes it to 0xCD, a later fresh read uses 0xCD. The two later working-base offset-9 copies may independently observe different bytes.

10. **Retained final argument:** changing doubled-link-base offset 0x14 after its capture does not change the third final argument. Completion remains conditional on F-D003.
