## Behavior

### 1. Initial calls and first two word stores

F-D01, F-D02, and F-D03 execute in order. F-D01’s target is calculated from the incoming link-related value plus \(0xFFE\).

F-D03 supplies the **first index**. The entry then stores the incoming register-associated 32-bit value at that index’s word slot. F-D04 follows with one explicit argument: four times the first index.

F-D05, F-D06, F-D07, and F-D08 execute next. F-D08 supplies the **second index**. The entry calculates four times that index, stores that value at the second index’s wide slot, and invokes F-D09 with the same scaled value.

The first store precedes F-D04; the second store follows F-D08 and precedes F-D09. Intervening dependencies may modify memory. No direct readback of the first store is represented.

### 2. Repeated scaled value and captured word

F-D10 through F-D13 execute in order. F-D13 supplies the **third index**.

The entry stores the retained value, four times the second index, at the third index’s word slot. F-D14 receives four times the third index.

F-D15 through F-D18 follow. F-D18 supplies the **fourth index**. The entry then:

1. Stores four times the fourth index at that index’s wide slot.
2. Reads an unsigned 32-bit word from that index’s word slot.

The loaded value is the **captured word**. F-D19 executes afterward with four times the fourth index.

The store and load have different address scaling. They are not generally a write and read of the same location. For a fourth index of one, the store address is \(0x24\), while the read address is \(0x18\).

The captured value is read before F-D19. Later memory changes do not change that already captured value in the represented data flow.

### 3. Shifted captured word and three-argument call

F-D20 through F-D23 execute in order. F-D23 supplies the **fifth index**.

The entry logically shifts the captured word right by 12 bits and stores the resulting 32-bit value at the fifth index’s word slot. F-D24 receives four times the fifth index.

F-D25 through F-D28 follow. F-D28 supplies the **sixth index**. The entry stores four times the sixth index at that index’s wide slot.

F-D29 then receives three explicit arguments, in order:

1. The low six bits of four times the fifth index, shifted left by 14 bits.
2. Four times the sixth index.
3. The fifth index shifted left by 30 bits.

The first argument uses an unsigned 32-bit intermediate. Under a wrapping 32-bit interpretation of the preceding multiplication, it ranges from zero through \(0xF0000\), in multiples of \(0x10000\). The spacing follows because four times an integer has two low zero bits.

Under wrapping 32-bit shift semantics, the third argument places the fifth index’s low two bits in the top two bit positions. This interpretation is conditional: the represented shift has a signed operand, and defined signed-shift behavior is not established.

The three arguments remain separate inputs to F-D29. No operation combining them into a single packed result is represented.

### 4. Retained scaled value and first byte store

F-D30 through F-D33 execute in order. F-D33 supplies the **seventh index**.

The entry stores the retained value, four times the sixth index, at the seventh index’s word slot. F-D34 receives four times the seventh index.

F-D35 through F-D38 follow. F-D38 supplies the unsigned 32-bit **byte-source word**.

The entry then reads an unsigned byte from the selector address calculated using the retained fifth index. It uses that byte’s numeric value as an address base and stores one byte at that base plus \(0x11\).

The stored byte is the low eight bits of the byte-source word shifted left by two bits. Equivalently, it contains the byte-source word’s low six bits shifted left by two positions; its two low bits are zero.

The selector is an 8-bit numeric value, not a fetched pointer. The second-level store address therefore lies in the inclusive numeric range \(0x11\) through \(0x110\). Accessibility of that range is not established.

F-D39 follows the byte store. Its target is its selected code-related base plus **\(0xFEC\)**, not \(0xFFE\). It receives:

1. The byte-source word shifted left by two bits.
2. The same value again.
3. The byte-source word shifted left by four bits.

The source shifts are unsigned 32-bit operations. The first shifted value is represented through a signed intermediate before forwarding; its signed numeric interpretation, where relevant to the callee interface, is not independently established.

### 5. Forwarded result and second byte store

F-D40 produces an opaque 32-bit result. F-D41 receives that result as its one explicit argument. No interpretation, ownership transfer, or memory-management effect follows merely from this forwarding.

F-D42 through F-D45 execute in order. F-D45 supplies the **eighth index**.

The entry performs a fresh unsigned byte read from the selector address, again calculated using the retained fifth index. It does not reuse the first selector byte.

Using this newly read byte as a numeric address base, the entry stores one byte at that base plus \(0x11\). Its value is obtained by retaining the byte-source word’s low 30 bits, logically shifting right by 20 bits, and retaining the low eight bits for the store.

The effective byte is therefore bits 20 through 27 of the byte-source word. Bits 28 and 29 survive the mask and shift but not the final byte truncation.

The two selector reads may produce different values. Dependencies between them may change memory, and a direct store may alias the selector address where the arithmetic and memory mapping permit it.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional arithmetic and ordering examples, not executed tests.

### Target construction

If the incoming link-related value is \(0x1000\), and the relevant arithmetic is representable without overflow, F-D01’s calculated target is \(0x1FFE\).

For F-D39, if its selected base is \(B\), its target is \(B + 0xFEC\). Substituting \(B + 0xFFE\) changes the target by \(0x12\) and does not match the representation. These examples do not establish target validity.

### Distinct store and load addresses

For a fourth index of one, the entry stores \(4\) at \(0x24\), then reads the captured word from \(0x18\).

For a fourth index of zero, both addresses are \(0x14\). With ordinary memory and no intervening interference, the read observes the zero just stored.

### Logical extraction

For a captured word of \(0xFEDCBA98\), the later shifted-word store contains \(0x000FEDCB\). Sign extension would not match the represented unsigned right shift.

### Three-argument boundary

For a fifth index of three and sixth index of two, under wrapping 32-bit arithmetic, F-D29 receives:

- \(0x30000\).
- \(8\).
- \(0xC0000000\).

The third value specifically requires the stated wrapping interpretation; it is not evidence of defined signed-language shift behavior.

### Independent selector reads and byte truncation

If the first selector read yields \(0x20\), the first byte store targets \(0x31\). If the second read yields \(0x40\), the second store targets \(0x51\), and F-D51 receives \(0x40\) as its second argument.

For a byte-source word of \(0xABCDEF12\), the first byte store writes \(0x48\), and the second writes \(0xBC\). These values do not imply equal destinations.

### Interrupted execution

If F-D24 does not return, F-D25 and all later operations are not reached. The shifted-word store precedes F-D24; no rollback operation is represented.
