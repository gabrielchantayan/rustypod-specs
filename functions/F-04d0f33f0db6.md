### Opening calculated-memory effects

Three retained quantities govern the opening region:

- the **shifted base**, a preparation result shifted left by 25;
- the **cleared base**, another preparation result multiplied by 2²⁵;
- the **selector**, formed by shifting a preparation result left by eight, retaining its low fifteen bits, and shifting right by six.

Under truncating 32-bit semantics, the selector is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Twice the selector, called the **selector base**, ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

The opening effects first store the shifted base as a word at offset 0x14 from the high half of the final combined preparation result. The low byte of that high half is stored at shifted-base offset 9. A word at offset 0x54 from the low half multiplied by 256 receives the cleared base. The shifted-base offset-9 byte is then overwritten with the shifted base’s low byte.

Next, cleared-base offset 0x14 is cleared as a word. A byte is captured from cleared-base offset 5; selector-base offset 0x14 is cleared as a word; and the captured byte is zero-extended into a word at cleared-base offset 0x34. The cleared-base offset-0x14 word is then read and retained.

**Under ordinary byte-addressed writable-memory semantics, without external mutation, this readback is zero.** The intervening selector-derived word store also writes zero, so overlap cannot introduce nonzero bytes. The widening store is 0x20 bytes beyond the cleared word and does not overlap it under ordinary address arithmetic.

Selector-base offset 0x14 is cleared again. Two successive byte stores at selector-base offset 0x15 receive the retained readback’s low byte, therefore zero under those conditions.

Device-backed memory, external mutation, and nonordinary mappings remain separate uncertainties.

### Global-derived addressing and chained updates

After the duplicate byte stores, the address-5 global is read and converted to unsigned 32 bits. That converted value is retained as the **global-derived base**. The address-1 global is then assigned the selector base narrowed to eight bits.

A byte at global-derived-base offset 0x15 receives the selector’s low byte. Cleared-base offset 0x11 receives a retained preparation value’s low byte, followed later by a zero-byte overwrite. Between those byte stores, a word at readback offset 0x54 receives that retained preparation value. Under the ordinary-memory zero-readback conclusion, this is an explicitly typed 32-bit store at absolute address 0x54, distinct from the later named-global assignment at that address.

The word at global-derived-base offset 0x54 is captured before being overwritten with the readback. Call the captured value the **seed word**. Subsequent calculations use that retained value, not a reload of its overwritten source.

The chained region contains repeated word stores of the seed at seed offset 0x24; repeated byte stores through another loaded base; replacement and repetition of selector-base offset-0x14 words; and the named address-0x54 assignment of twice the seed. The latter assignment’s storage width remains unresolved.

Four times the seed becomes a retained calculated base. Its offset 0x54 receives preparation-derived and seed-scaled values at distinct points. Selector-base offset 0x34 receives four times the seed. Another selector-base offset-0x74 read supplies a doubled base used for subsequent updates.
