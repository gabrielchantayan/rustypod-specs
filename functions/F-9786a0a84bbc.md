# F-9786a0a84bbc

## Purpose

F-9786a0a84bbc performs an ordered series of indirect calls and direct memory updates. Its visible effects include transferring 32-bit values, copying selected low bytes, clearing byte and halfword locations, updating a global word twice, and finally calling address zero.

The application-level purpose is not established. Scaling values by four and repeatedly accessing offsets 1, 17, 20, and 116 do not establish a collection, allocator, object format, or hardware interface.

The contract describes the supplied representation conditionally on execution reaching each operation. Dependencies have unknown effects and return behavior. The routine therefore cannot be treated as an atomic transformation of its three explicit arguments.

All evidence is static. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Ordering convention

The visible body has no explicit conditional branch or loop. The following phases form one ordered path, subject to dependencies returning and accesses completing.

A **four-call sequence** means four separately observable dependency invocations. The first three have no explicitly consumed primary result; the fourth supplies the stated result. None may be omitted merely because its primary result is unused.

A **producer-consumer pair** means a dependency produces a word and the immediately following dependency receives that word. Both invocations remain observable.

### 1. Entry and first word transfer

The first dependency, F-D01, receives the second argument. Its target is the entry control-address quantity plus 3896.

Next, one dependency produces a word and another receives it. **Four additional calls then occur**, with the fourth producing a result used for the first computed store. Thus seven calls precede that store: the entry call, the producer, its consumer, and four further calls.

The routine then, in order:

1. Stores the third argument logically shifted right by 23 at the fourth call’s result scaled by four, plus 20.
2. Reads a word at `(H-A logically shifted right by 22) + 20`, retaining it.
3. Calls a dependency with the scaled result, without the added 20.

The stored value ranges from 0 through 511. The H-A-derived read address ranges from 20 through 1043 and is not necessarily word-aligned.

### 2. Initial byte-derived updates

Four further calls occur. The routine then derives:

- **R:** H-B logically shifted right by 22.
- **S:** the retained word from phase 1 logically shifted right by 22.

Both range from 0 through 1023.

It reads the byte at H-B plus 1, then writes low8(S) at that byte’s value plus 17.

A dependency receives the same unexplained secondary 32-bit quantity in its first three displayed positions, followed by R. One further call occurs. The next dependency receives R logically shifted right by 23, which is necessarily zero under the displayed widths.

After a four-call sequence producing another result, the routine reads a byte at scale4(S) plus 1 and clears the byte at the resulting value plus 17. A dependency then receives the newly produced result scaled by four.

### 3. Sign-selected updates and retention of T

A four-call sequence produces a result whose scaled value is supplied twice to the next dependency. A producer-consumer pair follows; its produced word is also retained for a later argument. One more call occurs.

The routine selects a byte address by interpreting H-B as signed, shifting it arithmetically right by 31, negating that result, and adding one. Under that interpretation:

- A clear sign bit selects address 1.
- A set sign bit selects address 2.

It reads the selected byte and clears the byte at that value plus 17.

An unexplained secondary word is retained as **T** and passed three times to a dependency. Another dependency produces a word; the next receives that word and the retained producer result from earlier in this phase logically shifted right by 23.

After a four-call sequence, the sign-selected byte is read again. This new byte value is retained as **B**, and the byte at B plus 17 is cleared. B ranges from 0 through 255.

The two selector reads are distinct. Intervening calls can change the selected memory, so equal results are not required.

A dependency receives the latest four-call result scaled by four.

### 4. First global write and repeated byte refresh

A four-call sequence produces a result, and a dependency receives its scaled value twice. Another dependency produces a word retained as **P**.

The routine writes P to G-control, passes P to a dependency, and makes another call. It then reads the word at B plus 116 and writes that word’s low byte at B plus 1.

A dependency receives left11(T). A following dependency produces a pair result. The next receives, in order, that pair’s low half, its high half, and P logically shifted right by 23.

A four-call sequence produces a result whose scaled value is retained as **C**. The routine reads word(C + 116), writes its low byte at C + 1, and calls a dependency with C.

**Exactly four additional calls follow the call receiving C.** The fourth produces **J**. The routine retains **K = scale4(J)**, rereads word(C + 116), and again writes its low byte at C + 1.

The second refresh must use the second read. The intervening calls can change memory.

A dependency then receives, in order:

- left12(T);
- K;
- K again;
- left18(J).

### 5. Retaining and copying a full word

Two producer-consumer pairs occur. A four-call sequence then produces a result used as a scaled base.

The routine reads a word at that base plus 116 and retains the complete word as **V**. It writes low8(V) at the same base plus 1, then passes the base to a dependency.

After another four-call sequence, V is stored at scale16 of the new result plus 20.

A dependency receives the value formed by logically shifting K right by 2 and then shifting that result left by 16. The right shift applies to the already scaled, 32-bit K; it does not recover bits discarded while forming K.

Two producer-consumer pairs and another four-call sequence follow. V is stored again, now at scale4 of the latest result plus 20. A dependency receives that scaled result.

Both stores sourced from V transfer all 32 bits, not merely the byte previously written beside its original read location.

### 6. Further word transfers and capture of G-base

A four-call sequence produces a result whose scaled value is retained as **L**. The routine stores L at `(K bitwise-AND 0xfffffffc) + 20`.

Under the represented 32-bit scaling, K already has two zero low bits, so this destination is K plus 20.

A dependency receives L twice. Two producer-consumer pairs and a four-call sequence follow. The routine clears the word at scale4 of the new result plus 20, then passes that scaled result to a dependency.

Another four-call sequence produces a pair. Its low half scaled by four is retained as **M**. M is stored at scale4 of the pair’s high half plus 20.

The routine next reads G-base and captures its word as an address base. A dependency then receives M, the pair’s high half, M, and M, in that order.

### 7. Captured-base read and final pair retention

A four-call sequence produces a result. M is stored at scale4 of that result plus 20.

The routine then reads a word at the previously captured G-base value plus 20, retaining it as **W**. This is not a fresh read of G-base. Calls between capture and use may change the global or the selected memory.

A dependency receives the latest scaled result. A further four-call sequence produces a pair. Its low half is retained as **Q**; its high half is retained for the next dependency’s repeated arguments.

### 8. Final byte-derived updates

The routine reads the byte at `(V logically shifted right by 23) + 1`, then writes low8(W) at that byte’s value plus 17.

A dependency receives the latest pair’s high half three times, followed by scale4(Q).

A producer-consumer pair and a four-call sequence follow. The routine then reads a byte at `((Q bitwise-AND 0x3fffffff) logically shifted right by 21) + 1`. It writes low8(W logically shifted right by 22) at the resulting byte value plus 17.

Both selector-read addresses in this phase range from 1 through 512. Their derived write addresses range from 17 through 272.

A dependency receives the latest scaled result. A four-call sequence produces another result, whose scaled value is supplied twice to a dependency. One additional call, a producer-consumer pair, and a final dependency producing an 8-bit result follow.

### 9. Terminal effects

If execution reaches this segment, the observable order is:

1. A byte is cleared through the unexplained pointer available after the final dependency.
2. G-upper supplies the three-byte portion used to compose a word whose low byte is the final dependency’s 8-bit result.
3. That composed word overwrites G-control.
4. A 16-bit zero is stored at `(Q bitwise-AND 0x3fffffff) logically shifted right by 7`.
5. F-D03 calls address zero with three displayed zero arguments.
6. The routine returns only if F-D03 returns.

The composition places G-upper in bits 8–31 and the final byte result in bits 0–7. This specifies numerical composition, not memory byte order or the physical transaction width used to obtain G-upper.

The halfword destination ranges from 0 through 8,388,607 and can be odd.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes execution reaches the relevant operation and required accesses complete.

1. **Entry call ordering.** Before the first word store, the observable sequence contains seven calls. After the dependency consuming the preceding word, four further calls must occur; the fourth supplies the store’s address-selection result.

2. **Third-argument extraction.** A third argument of 0x00800000 produces a stored word of 1. A third argument of 0xffffffff produces 511.

3. **Byte narrowing.** If S is 1023 and the byte at H-B plus 1 is 7, the first byte-derived write stores 255 at address 24.

4. **Zero argument.** Every 32-bit H-B produces R at most 1023. The subsequent argument formed by shifting R right by 23 is zero.

5. **Signed selection and rereading.** Under arithmetic signed right shift, H-B of 0x7fffffff selects address 1, while 0x80000000 selects address 2. If the selected byte changes between reads, each clear uses its own read result.

6. **C-to-J ordering.** After the call receiving C, exactly four further calls occur before J is available and the repeated C-based refresh takes place. The call receiving C is not one of those four.

7. **Repeated refresh.** If word(C + 116) changes from 0x123456ab to 0x876543cd between reads, the corresponding writes at C + 1 use 0xab and 0xcd.

8. **Full-word retention.** If V is 0x123456ab, its adjacent byte write uses 0xab, while both later V-sourced word stores use 0x123456ab.

9. **Scaling truncation.** Under 32-bit truncation, J of 0x40000001 produces K of 4. The later K-based right-then-left shift produces 0x00010000.

10. **Captured global base.** If G-base supplies 0x1000 when captured and later changes, the W read still addresses 0x1014. Its value is whatever that location supplies when read.

11. **Terminal composition and partial completion.** If G-upper supplies 0x123456 and the final byte is 0xab, G-control receives 0x123456ab. The terminal byte clear precedes that composition; the halfword clear follows it. Failure or nonreturn at F-D03 does not imply that these stores were undone.
