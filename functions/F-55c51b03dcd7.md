### 2. Entry into direct memory mutation

The final paired result supplies low and high words used in the initial stores. Earlier interactions supply a shifted-by-25 base, a retained shifted-by-eight value, another shifted-by-25 quantity, and a small index.

The index is formed by shifting a word left by eight, retaining its low 15 bits, and shifting right by six. Under the fixed-width interpretation, it is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Twice that index is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

The opening memory effects preserve the following order:

- A retained shifted quantity is stored as a word at offset 0x14 from the final high word.
- The low byte of that high word is stored at offset 9 from the other shifted-by-25 quantity.
- The shifted-by-25 base is stored as a word at offset 0x54 from 256 times the final low word.
- The byte at offset 9 is overwritten with the low byte of the retained shifted quantity.

The intermediate byte store is part of the represented trace, even when a later store replaces it.

Next, the function clears the word at offset 0x14 from the shifted base, reads an unsigned byte at offset 5, clears the index-derived word at offset 0x14, and stores the widened byte at offset 0x34 from the shifted base. Only then does it read back the shifted base’s word at offset 0x14.

That read-back is a real memory dependency. Replacing it with a retained zero is not justified: an intervening store may overlap it, and external or device-memory behavior is unresolved.

## Acceptance examples

### Width preservation

### Fixed-location observation before 0x60

If the fixed-location load returns one base and the subsequent store to 0x60 changes storage relevant to that load, later addressing still uses the already loaded base. Moving the load after the store is not accepted.

### Observation after the 0x3A write

If the word range at retained-doubled-base offset 0x14 includes absolute address 0x3A, its read occurs after the byte write there. Acceptance uses the word actually read, with the platform’s byte order, not an earlier snapshot.

### Independent observations of 0x67

If the initial store writes 0xAB to 0x67 but the subsequent read returns 0xCD, the byte stored to 0x21 is 0xCD.

If the two later reads of 0x67 return 0x12 and 0x34 because intervening operations alter that address, their respective stores to adjusted-base offset 9 use 0x12 and 0x34. Reusing either the initial 0xAB or the first later result for both stores is not accepted.

### Intermediate writes
