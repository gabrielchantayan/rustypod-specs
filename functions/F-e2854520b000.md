# F-e2854520b000

## Purpose

F-e2854520b000 performs an ordered sequence of memory reads and mutations, followed by an unresolved indirect invocation. Its direct effects include link-sized word stores, byte and halfword stores, repeated writes, read-and-write-back operations, and accesses at addresses formed by addition, subtraction, or doubling.

No application-level purpose is established. In particular, link-like accesses do not establish a conventional object layout, list operation, initialization procedure, or ownership protocol.

This contract describes the supplied static representation. It does not establish successful execution, a recovered entry ABI, or reliable reconstruction of the final control transfer.

### Memory and arithmetic conventions

A **byte**, **halfword**, and **word** mean 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Address-valued words are represented as 32 bits. Every displacement below is measured in bytes and written in hexadecimal unless explicitly stated otherwise. A location described as an address’s “base” has displacement zero.

“Low byte” and “low halfword” mean truncation to the least significant 8 or 16 bits. Byte order determines how wider accesses overlap neighboring bytes.

All named addresses and retained words below denote captures at the stated point. A fresh read is not interchangeable with an earlier capture. Names do not imply disjoint storage, valid objects, ownership, or allocation boundaries.

The early doubled-address byte read explicitly uses unsigned 32-bit multiplication. Terminal byte-derived additions also use the represented unsigned 32-bit value. Those operations have modulo-\(2^{32}\) unsigned arithmetic. Other address expressions include represented signed arithmetic; defined wrapping behavior for overflowing signed expressions is not established.

## Behavior

### Access discipline

The represented body contains no conditional branch, loop, validation, or callable dependency before its final invocation. Subject to accesses and arithmetic remaining executable, it follows the sequence below.

All reads and writes remain significant, including overwritten stores and stores of a value just read from the same location. Aliasing can make any preceding overlapping write affect a subsequent read. The sequence is therefore not reducible to a final memory image.

Each paragraph below is chronological; actions within it are also ordered. Captures persist until their stated uses, regardless of later changes to their source storage.

### 1. Opening links and shifted byte

Root is read through the second explicit argument. Entry A’s `0x14` word is then captured as **Opening link**. Root’s `0x54` receives Entry A; Opening link’s `0x54` receives Opening link itself; Root’s `0x44` receives Entry B; Entry A’s `0x09` receives Root’s low byte.

Reading Root’s `0x64` obtains **Opening parent**. Reading Opening parent’s `0x14` obtains the unsigned address **Shift source**. Shift source’s `0x09` receives Opening link’s low byte, then Opening parent’s low byte. Early external word is read only after these stores.

Entry B’s `0x14` is captured as **Adjustment base**; adding `0x20` forms **Adjusted address**. Reading Shift source’s `0x64` supplies a destination whose `0x14` receives Root. Entry B’s `0x11` then receives Adjusted address’s low byte.

Two consecutive reads of Root’s `0x44` produce **First opening capture** and **Second opening capture**. A character is read at twice Shift source, promoted to an integer, and right-shifted by Shift source’s low eight bits. Its truncated result is **Shifted byte**. Character signedness, negative right-shift behavior, and oversized shift counts remain unresolved.

Second opening capture’s `0x08` receives Shifted byte; Root’s `0x74` receives First opening capture; Root’s `0x11` receives Shifted byte. Entry B’s `0x54` receives Adjusted address and then Early external word. Entry B’s `0x19` receives Adjusted address’s low byte. Second opening capture’s `0x44` receives Root, and Adjustment base’s `0x74` receives Adjusted address.

Second opening capture’s `0x14` is read as **Opening destination**. Root’s `0x11` receives Early external word’s low byte. A fresh Root `0x14` read supplies the low byte written to Opening destination’s `0x15`.

### 2. First region rewrite

Another Root `0x14` read establishes **First region**. Root’s `0x64` is captured as **Earlier root link**.

First region’s `0x10` receives word `0x73`; Root’s `0x44` receives Root; First region’s `0x14` receives byte `0x73`; Root’s `0x54` receives Early external word. Root’s `0x74` is captured as **Earlier root tail**. Root’s `0x09` receives Root’s low byte, and First region’s `0x54` receives Earlier root tail.

First region’s `0x10` receives word `0x6E`; Root’s `0x44` again receives Root; First region’s `0x20` receives word `0x6E`. A fresh Root `0x64` read produces **Later root link**. Its low byte is written to First region’s `0x09`.

First region’s `0x74` supplies **Region-linked address**, whose `0x54` is captured as **Saved linked word**. First region’s `0x1C` receives byte `0x79`; Root’s `0x11` receives Later root link’s low byte; Root’s `0x09` receives Earlier root link’s low byte; First region’s `0x1C` receives byte `0x68`; Root’s `0x11` again receives Later root link’s low byte. Region-linked address’s `0x11` receives Root’s low byte.

First region’s `0x50` is captured as **Region payload**. Root’s `0x44` receives Earlier root link; Root’s `0x64` and then `0x34` receive Later root link. First region’s `0x11` receives Later root link’s low byte. A fresh Root `0x74` read produces **Later root tail**.

First region’s `0x10` receives Region payload, Region-linked address’s `0x54` receives Saved linked word, and First region’s base receives Region payload’s low byte. First region’s `0x09` receives Root’s low byte.

A fresh Root `0x14` read establishes **Principal target**, retained throughout the remaining body. Region-linked address’s `0x11` receives First region’s low byte; First region’s `0x44` receives Principal target; First region’s base again receives Region payload’s low byte.

Region-linked address’s `0x74` and then `0x14` are captured as **Subtraction anchor** and **Hub**. First region’s `0x54` receives its own address. Only then is Hub’s `0x04` read to obtain **First numeric target**.

### 3. First numeric group and propagation

First numeric target’s `0x11` receives Later root tail’s low byte, immediately followed by byte 100 at its `0x10`. Principal target’s `0x54` receives word 100; First numeric target’s `0x60` receives word 100.

Principal target’s old `0x74` is captured, that field receives Hub, and the captured old destination’s `0x30` receives word 100.

Hub’s `0x14` supplies **First propagated address**, whose `0x09` receives Later root tail’s low byte. Principal target’s `0x54` receives Principal target. The address Subtraction anchor minus `0x20` receives a word equal to Subtraction anchor minus `0x74`.

First propagated address’s `0x14` supplies **Propagation child**. Hub’s `0x11` receives Earlier root tail’s low byte. A fresh Hub `0x14` read yields **Captured hub link**, whose low byte is written to Propagation child’s `0x15`. Another Hub `0x14` read establishes **Working address**.

Principal target’s `0x64` is read as **First principal link** and immediately written back. Working address’s `0x0C` receives byte `0x73`; Principal target’s `0x15` receives First principal link’s low byte; Hub’s `0x34` receives Captured hub link.

Working address’s `0x74` is captured as **Earlier working tail**. Working address’s `0x54` receives itself; Hub’s `0x0D` receives Principal target’s low byte; Hub’s `0x54` receives Captured hub link; Hub’s `0x14` receives Working address.

### 4. Second and third numeric groups

Principal target’s `0x64` is freshly captured as **Second principal link**. Working address’s `0x10` receives byte `0x74`; Principal target’s `0x54` receives word `0x74`; Working address’s `0x10` receives word `0x74`.

Working address’s `0x74` is read as **Later working tail**. Hub’s `0x54` receives Hub; Working address’s `0x30` receives word `0x74`; Principal target’s `0x01` receives First principal link’s low byte. The address represented by First principal link receives Second principal link’s low byte at `0x09`.

Principal target’s `0x74` receives Second principal link, and its `0x11` receives byte `0x74`. Working address’s `0x60` is captured as **Working payload**. Principal target’s `0x11` receives First principal link’s low byte, and its `0x34` receives Second principal link. Working address’s `0x10` receives Working payload’s low byte.

A fresh Principal target `0x14` read establishes **Third numeric target**. Hub’s `0x11` receives Earlier working tail’s low byte; Principal target’s `0x44` receives Hub; Principal target’s `0x11` receives Second principal link’s low byte. Principal target’s `0x74` is captured as **Pre-third tail**.

Principal target’s `0x0D` receives Hub’s low byte. Third numeric target’s `0x10` receives word `0x66`; Principal target’s `0x44` again receives Hub; Third numeric target’s `0x10` receives byte `0x66`. Principal target’s `0x54` and Third numeric target’s `0x60` receive word `0x66`.

Principal target’s `0x74` is freshly captured as **Third-group destination**, then replaced with Hub. Third-group destination’s `0x40` receives word `0x66`; Principal target’s `0x0D` receives Pre-third tail’s low byte.

Third-group destination’s `0x44` is captured as **Third-group payload**. Principal target’s `0x54` receives itself; Later working tail’s `0x54` receives Later working tail itself. Third-group destination’s `0x14` supplies a destination. Hub’s `0x11` receives Third-group payload’s low byte, and that destination’s `0x15` receives the low byte of a fresh Hub `0x14` read.

### 5. Later propagation and Anchor Q

Another Hub `0x14` read establishes **Later reached address**. Principal target’s `0x64` is captured as **Before-store link**. Later reached address’s `0x20` then receives word `0x73`. Only afterward is Principal target’s `0x64` captured again as **After-store link**. Later reached address’s `0x09` receives After-store link’s low byte.

Later reached address’s `0x74` supplies **Propagation bridge**. Its `0x0D` receives Principal target’s low byte; its `0x54` is read and written back; its `0x09` receives byte `0x74`. Principal target’s `0x44` receives Before-store link, then Propagation bridge’s `0x34` receives Principal target.

Propagation bridge’s `0x04` supplies **Anchor carrier**. Its `0x24` receives After-store link. Principal target’s `0x19` receives Before-store link’s low byte. Anchor carrier’s `0x74` is captured as **Anchor Q**, and its `0x04` as **Replacement principal link**.

Principal target’s `0x74` supplies **Traversal parent**. Reading Traversal parent’s `0x14` supplies a destination whose `0x54` receives Anchor Q. Traversal parent’s `0x04` is captured as **Captured numeric destination**.

Principal target’s `0x0D` receives After-store link’s low byte twice consecutively. Principal target’s `0x64` receives Replacement principal link. Immediately afterward, Captured numeric destination’s `0x30` receives word `0x74`.

Only after that store is Traversal parent’s `0x04` reread. Reading the newly reached address’s `0x74` establishes **Current traversal**. Thus the earlier `0x30` store can affect this traversal through aliasing.

A fresh Principal target `0x64` read supplies **Retained low byte**, written to Principal target’s `0x1D`.

Principal target’s `0x44` is read and followed through `0x14` to capture **Replacement region**. Current traversal’s `0x34` then receives Anchor Q. Only after that store does Principal target’s `0x14` receive the previously captured Replacement region.

### 6. Final region and numeric target

Principal target’s `0x54` is captured as **Saved principal word**. Replacement region’s `0x09` receives Principal target’s low byte.

Following Current traversal’s `0x04` and then `0x74` establishes **Final propagation address**. Its `0x15` receives Anchor Q’s low byte. Principal target’s `0x54` receives itself; its `0x11` receives Current traversal’s low byte; Final propagation address’s `0x11` receives Saved principal word’s low byte.

Final propagation address’s `0x64` is captured as **Final propagated word**. Principal target’s `0x09` receives Retained low byte. A fresh Current traversal `0x04` read establishes **Final region**.

Final region’s `0x09` receives Principal target’s low byte. Its `0x50` is captured as **Final region payload**. Its `0x11` receives Final propagated word’s low byte, then its `0x09` again receives Principal target’s low byte.

Principal target’s `0x14` receives Final propagated word; its `0x0D` receives Final region’s low byte. Final region’s base receives Final region payload’s low byte.

The following order is significant:

1. Current traversal’s `0x74` is captured as **Anchor K**.
2. Current traversal’s `0x14` is captured as **Final numeric parent**.
3. Final region’s `0x54` receives Final region itself.
4. Final numeric parent’s `0x04` is read to establish **Final numeric target**.

Final numeric target’s `0x10` receives byte 100; Principal target’s `0x54` receives word 100; Final numeric target’s `0x40` receives word 100.

Principal target’s `0x0D` receives Final propagated word’s low byte. Final numeric parent’s `0x09` receives Final numeric target’s low byte. Principal target’s `0x24` receives Final propagated word. Final numeric parent’s `0x11` receives Principal target’s low byte.

### 7. Terminal accesses and invocation

The terminal sequence is strictly ordered:

1. Read and capture Terminal external A.
2. Read Principal target’s `0x74` as **Terminal link**.
3. Write Terminal link’s low halfword at Terminal link plus Principal target.
4. Write Terminal external A’s low halfword at twice Final numeric target.
5. Read a character at Anchor K plus Principal target and convert it to unsigned 32 bits as **Terminal byte value**.
6. Write Terminal link’s low halfword at Terminal external A plus Terminal link.
7. Again write Terminal link’s low halfword at Terminal link plus Principal target.
8. Write word `0x45` at Terminal byte value plus `0x45`.
9. Read and capture Terminal external B.
10. Read and capture Terminal payload.
11. Read and capture Principal target’s `0x44` as **Invocation link**.
12. Write byte `0x44` at Terminal byte value plus `0x44`.
13. Write Terminal payload as a word at twice Terminal external B.
14. Write Terminal external B’s low halfword at Anchor Q plus Terminal external B.
15. Invoke F-indirect-01.

The represented invocation arguments, in order, are Terminal byte value bitwise ORed with `0x44`, captured Terminal external B, and Invocation link.

If the terminal character is signed and negative, its conversion to unsigned 32 bits is not restricted to 0–255.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Opening self-link:** If accessible, Opening link’s initial `0x54` store contains Opening link itself, not Entry A.

2. **Retained-byte mutation:** First numeric target’s `0x11` receives Later root tail’s low byte immediately before its byte-100 `0x10` store. Omitting the former changes the access sequence.

3. **Capture straddling a store:** If Later reached address’s `0x20` overlaps Principal target’s `0x64`, Before-store link reflects memory before the word-`0x73` store; After-store link reflects memory afterward. They cannot be merged into one capture.

4. **Mutation before retraversal:** Captured numeric destination’s word-`0x74` store at `0x30` occurs after Principal target’s `0x64` replacement and before Traversal parent’s `0x04` reread. If those locations overlap, the reread may follow a changed link.

5. **Replacement capture survives aliasing:** Replacement region is captured before Current traversal’s Anchor Q store at `0x34`. Principal target’s `0x14` receives that capture afterward, even if the Anchor Q store changes the replacement’s source field.

6. **Final self-reference boundary:** Anchor K and Final numeric parent are captured before Final region’s `0x54` self-reference. Final numeric parent’s `0x04` is read afterward. An overlapping self-reference can affect the final target read, but cannot retroactively change either capture.

7. **Terminal capture timing:** Terminal external A is captured before Terminal link and all terminal halfword stores. The word-`0x45` store precedes both late external reads; those reads precede Invocation link’s capture and the byte-`0x44` store.

8. **Captured payload and argument:** If later terminal stores overlap Terminal payload or Principal target’s `0x44`, the doubled-address store and third invocation argument still use their earlier captures.

9. **Widths:** Supplying `0x123456AB` to a byte store writes `0xAB`; supplying it to a halfword store writes `0x56AB`. Byte placement depends on target byte order.

10. **Signed terminal character:** Under signed-character semantics, character −1 converts to `0xFFFFFFFF`; OR with `0x44` leaves that value unchanged. Accessibility of derived addresses is not guaranteed.
