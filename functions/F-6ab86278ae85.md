## Behavior

The initial phase is an extended straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. Targets use an incoming continuation-related value or embedded address-like values with a recurring displacement of 0xFFE. This does not establish a conventional function table.

Invocation order matters independently of displayed result use. Calls with no displayed arguments may consume implicit state; discarded results do not imply absent side effects.

Recurring argument relationships include:

- A primary word shifted left by 8.
- A shifted low result accompanied by an unshifted or transformed high result.
- The same transformed value in multiple argument positions.
- Explicit zeros and values derived from retained zeros.
- Masked fields subsequently shifted or forwarded.

One early invocation receives an incoming value shifted left by 22 and a dependency result shifted left by 8. Another receives a shifted low result, its high portion, and two copies of a second incoming value shifted left by 22.

Observed masks include widths of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 8, and 1 bit. Their positions matter:

- A 20-bit mask followed by a logical right shift of 10 selects 10 bits.
- A 24-bit mask before a left shift excludes the original upper eight bits.
- Right shifts of a combined 64-bit result by 34, 36, or 44 consume its high portion, not merely its low word.
- Under the conditional bit-vector model, successive left shifts totaling at least 32 can erase the original value.
- Left shifting and then logically right shifting need not restore the input.

No dependency result is visibly checked for success. A dependency that does not return prevents later displayed operations from being reached.

### Opening computed and fixed-state updates

The opening establishes the following observable ordering:

- A word at final-pair-high offset 0x14 receives the packed base. The byte at packed-base offset 9 then receives the low byte of final-pair-high.
- A word at 256 times final-pair-low plus 0x54 receives the shifted base. The same packed-base byte is then overwritten with the packed base’s low byte.
- The word at shifted-base offset 0x14 is cleared. A byte is read at shifted-base offset 5. The word at small-base offset 0x14 is cleared, and the widened byte is stored at shifted-base offset 0x34.
- The word at shifted-base offset 0x14 is read and retained. Another clear of small-base offset 0x14 follows, then two separate identical byte stores at small-base offset 0x15 publish that retained word’s low byte.

The later read of shifted-base offset 0x14 is not guaranteed to return zero: intervening stores may overlap it.

The fixed byte at **0x05 is read next, before the byte write at 0x01**. The read is zero-extended and retained as a base; the write at 0x01 publishes the small base’s low byte. Updating 0x01 does not cause the previously captured byte from 0x05 to be re-read.

Through that captured byte-base, the function publishes the extracted field’s low byte at offset 0x15. It writes the retained payload’s low byte at shifted-base offset 0x11, stores the payload through the earlier retained word at offset 0x54, and then clears the shifted-base byte at 0x11.

A word read at captured-byte-base offset 0x54 supplies the **linked seed**. That field is then replaced with the earlier retained word. Further linked and self-derived stores follow, including repeated stores and byte publications through values read at offsets 0x74.

Two later fixed writes have explicit positions within these computed updates:

- **0x54 receives a 32-bit value equal to twice the linked seed.** This occurs after a word publication at small-base offset 0x14 derived from the shifted-base predecessor, and before that computed field is overwritten with the linked seed.
- After further repeated computed stores at small-base offsets 0x14 and 0x15, **0x15 receives the small base’s low byte**.

These fixed destinations are ordinary participants in the possible aliasing relationships. They are not implicitly isolated globals.

### Linked updates and constant publication

It reads a byte at quadrupled-seed offset 5 and uses the zero-extended byte as a base for a word read at offset 0x74. The second such byte read supplies another word read at byte-base offset 0x74. Call that resulting word the **terminal link**. Twice the terminal link becomes the **closing linked base**; four times the terminal link becomes the **scaled terminal base**.

Distinct stores publish 0x3A as:

- A halfword at eight times the terminal link.

**Only after these preceding computed stores is the word at fixed address 0x087FBBBC read to obtain the publication base.** This load cannot be hoisted to entry without additional non-aliasing evidence. The fixed word at **0x60 then receives 0x20**, before the repeated publication region begins.

**First fixed-byte publication and read.** Fixed byte **0x67 now receives the low byte of the retained word from 0xA9**. A word is then read at closing-linked-base offset 0x44. After that read, **0x67 is read afresh to supply the byte write at 0x21**.

The captured computed word supplies a byte at publication-address offset 0x10 and a word at publication-base offset 0x54. ## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not runtime tests.

- **Truncation:** under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, 0x01000000 shifted left by 8 contributes zero, not an unbounded 0x100000000.
- **Small-base boundary:** an input result of 0x7F produces extracted field 508 and small base 1016; 0x80 produces zero for both.
- **Unsigned byte widening:** a byte value of 0xFE stored as a widened word contributes 0x000000FE.
- **Early fixed-state order:** the byte captured from 0x05 precedes the write at 0x01. Even if the environment couples these locations, subsequent addressing uses the captured byte, not an invented post-write reload.
- **Delayed publication-base load:** if preceding computed stores change memory at 0x087FBBBC, the later load must observe that state according to the target memory model.
- **Closing read order:** the word read at 0xA9 and computed read at scaled-terminal-base offset 0x74 precede the byte write at 0x3A. The write at 0x67 occurs only after the intervening computed captures and stores.
- **Independent 0x67 reads:** if an intervening computed store overlaps 0x67, a later read can differ from the originally published byte. Each computed byte publication must use its corresponding read event.
- **Retained final argument:** changing closing-linked-base offset 0x14 after its capture does not itself change the saved linked word passed as argument three.
- **Observable repetition:** where intermediate writes are observable, collapsing the 0x20, 0x2F, byte-derived, 0x20 sequence to one write is unacceptable.
- **Non-return:** a dependency that does not return does not require subsequent memory effects or the final transfer.
