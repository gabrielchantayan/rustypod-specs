### Arithmetic widths and boundaries

Both readings and the candidate are unsigned 32-bit quantities. Their ordering is therefore between 0 and 4,294,967,295; a high bit does not make them negative.

The signed adjustment participates in two signed multiplication expressions: multiplication by negative two for the threshold, and multiplication by positive two for the replacement candidate. The latter is evaluated only when the first comparison succeeds.

Where a signed multiplication is representable, its subsequent combination with an unsigned reading uses unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. That addition is modulo 4,294,967,296. In particular:

- A negative adjustment does not imply a signed comparison.
- Subtracting a positive doubled adjustment from a small second reading can wrap the threshold to a large unsigned value.
- Adding a negative doubled adjustment to a small first reading can wrap the candidate to a large unsigned value.
- The final upper-bound comparison occurs after any such unsigned wrap.
- There is no visible lower-bound clamp, rejection of negative adjustments, or rejection of extreme readings.

Both signed products are representable for adjustments from −1,073,741,823 through 1,073,741,823, inclusive. This is a sufficient arithmetic precondition for every branch of the visible calculation.

Outside that interval, signed multiplication overflow becomes relevant. The supplied recovered representation does not establish whether the actual executable wraps these products, whether all bit patterns are intended inputs, or whether a caller-side range restriction applies. Extreme-input behavior is therefore **documented_only**, rather than assigned an unconditional two’s-complement wraparound contract.

The selection rule must not be replaced by an unrestricted mathematical “minimum of the limit and the advanced value.” That simplification loses the threshold calculation’s width, wraparound, and strict-comparison behavior.
