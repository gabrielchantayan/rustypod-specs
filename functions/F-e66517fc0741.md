### Address derivation

- \(a\) be the unsigned 32-bit sum
  \[
  a = \left(u + \left\lfloor u/4 \right\rfloor\right) \bmod 2^{32}.
  \]

The value \(a\) supplies the destination address, subject to the target’s integer-to-pointer conversion.

The division by four in this description expresses the logical right shift of the unsigned value by two positions. It must not be substituted with signed division of \(s\), especially for negative inputs.

The addition combines a signed intermediate with an explicitly unsigned intermediate. Under the 32-bit interpretation, the unsigned operand causes the addition to use unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. The final address is therefore not generally the signed quantity \(s+\lfloor s/4\rfloor\).

The third argument contributes through two occurrences of the initial right shift in the recovered expression. Both operate on the same by-value argument. No memory read is shown as part of either occurrence.

### Signed right-shift qualification

For nonnegative third arguments, the initial shift has the ordinary result of discarding the low 11 bits.

For negative third arguments, the result depends on the signed right-shift semantics. An arithmetic right shift is a plausible interpretation, but it is not established by independent architecture evidence supplied here.

For negative \(s\), conversion to unsigned form retains its 32-bit bit pattern. Consequently, negative third arguments can produce large positive address values rather than negative addresses.
