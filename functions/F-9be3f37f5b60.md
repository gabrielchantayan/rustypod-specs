# F-9be3f37f5b60

## Purpose

F-9be3f37f5b60 invokes an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect dependencies, transforms returned and ambient machine values, and performs byte, halfword, and word accesses through computed addresses and fixed storage. Its last represented invocation supplies four values to an unresolved target.

Application-level purpose is **documented_only**. The supplied representation does not establish parsing, initialization, formatting, object management, or device control. Character-sized constants do not establish a text-output interface.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented calls, value relationships, access widths, and access ordering.
- **Inferred interpretation:** machine-level behavior conditional on architecture and calling-convention assumptions.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which no executable acceptance claim is established.

All obligations are conditional on the supplied representation accurately describing execution and on execution reaching the relevant operation.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven activity

The initial region is an ordered, straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. The first target is derived from incoming control context plus displacement 0xFFE. Subsequent represented targets use embedded code-location values with the same displacement.

No local conditional branch, retry, or error-exit decision is visible. Similar argument shapes do not establish identical targets or interchangeable effects.

The observable value transformations include:

- Shifting returned 32-bit quantities left by eight before another invocation.
- Separately supplying a composite result’s shifted low word and high word.
- Supplying additional shifts of either word, earlier retained results, incoming context, or secondary register values.
- Repeating the same computed argument in multiple positions.
- Passing explicit zeros and values derived from explicitly zeroed quantities.
- Masking low-word values to 24 bits before further extraction or shifting.
- Applying 22-, 21-, 20-, 19-, 18-, and 17-bit masks, along with narrower field masks.
- Retaining values across intervening calls.

An unsigned 64-bit right shift by 34, 36, or 44 extracts the high word shifted logically right by 2, 4, or 12 respectively. These operations do not extract shifted low-word fields.

Masks transform values; they do not validate later addresses. Repeated left shifts also do not imply retained mathematical precision. Under wrapping 32-bit interpretation, successive shifts can discard every original bit.

Dependency effects must remain ordered. No dependency is assumed to preserve globals, memory, ownership, or secondary machine state merely because its displayed result is unused.

### Initial direct-memory effects

The final composite dependency result contributes both words to the first direct stores. Other retained results supply shifted bases and payloads.

The first word publication is at offset 0x14 from the final high word and contains an earlier retained shifted value. A byte publication at offset 9 from another retained shifted base then contains the final high word’s low byte. The next word publication, at offset 0x54 from the final low word multiplied by 256, contains an earlier scaled result. Only afterward is the offset-9 byte replaced with the retained shifted value’s low byte.

Thus the first offset-9 publication is a distinct effect, not merely an intermediate value that may be omitted.

A word at offset 0x14 from the earlier scaled result is then cleared before a byte is read from that base plus 5. A doubled extracted-value base is cleared at offset 0x14; the previously read byte is published as a zero-extended word at offset 0x34 from the earlier base. A later word read from the earlier base plus 0x14 occurs after these stores and therefore must account for possible aliasing.

The doubled-base clear is repeated. Two subsequent equal-valued byte stores target that doubled base plus 0x15, overlapping the cleared word.

### Fixed storage and dependent-address mutation

A datum at absolute address 0x05 is read and widened to a 32-bit value used as a base. Its underlying declaration width is not supplied. This read precedes the byte write at absolute address 0x01 and the subsequent computed-base stores.

The following region combines:

- Reading a word at offset 0x54 before replacing it.
- Reading words at offset 0x74 and using them as further bases.
- Publishing a loaded address-like value through a destination derived from that value.
- Repeatedly publishing a base value at its own offset 0x24.
- Publishing shifted, doubled, fourfold, and eightfold retained values.
- Writing a word at absolute address 0x54 and a byte at absolute address 0x15.
- Alternating word writes at offset 0x14 with overlapping byte writes at offset 0x15.

One publication contains two represented reads from the same offset-0x74 storage, one contributing the destination and one the payload. They are separate reads. Their relative evaluation order and equality under external mutation are not established.

Further pointer chasing uses words at offsets 4 and 0x64, bytes at offset 5, and words at offset 0x74 from bases obtained from those bytes. Repeated reads of a byte at the same computed offset are not entry-time snapshots.

No ownership relation follows from this connectivity. In particular, a word used as a base is not thereby established as a live object pointer.

### Constant publications and the later global-base read

Before the repeated-publication region, the function writes 0x3A at three distinct computed destinations using byte, halfword, and word widths.

For the remaining description, the following terms identify retained value relationships, not object types:

- **Saved word:** the earlier word read at offset 0x54 before replacement.
- **Adjusted quantity:** four times the saved word, minus 0x96.
- **Adjusted base:** four times the adjusted quantity.
- **Doubled base:** twice the later word obtained through the byte-derived offset-0x74 lookup.
- **Fourfold base:** four times that same later lookup result.
- **Global base:** the 32-bit global value read immediately before the absolute-0x60 publication.

These arithmetic descriptions remain subject to the width and signed-arithmetic qualifications above.

At adjusted base plus 0x20, successive word publications contain 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from adjusted base plus 4, and 0x20 again. That byte read occurs after the 0x2F publication and before the third publication.

**The global-base read follows these four publications and precedes the word write of 0x20 at absolute address 0x60.** It is not a read deferred until after that fixed-address write. If the global storage overlaps absolute 0x60, the retained global base comes from the earlier read.

The subsequent repeated groups each contain, in order:

- A word publication at a scaled base plus 0x54.
- A byte publication of the adjusted base’s low byte at global base plus 0x19.
- A byte publication of the global base’s low byte at adjusted base plus 0x11.
- A word publication at global base plus 0x34.

The first group uses eight times the saved word for the scaled base and last payload. Later groups use eight times the adjusted quantity. Their first payload is the fourfold base.

The global base and both byte payloads are retained values, not fresh global reads on every group. Conversely, the repeated stores are distinct represented effects. Their exact multiplicity is not enumerated as an acceptance obligation here and remains **documented_only in this contract**; this does not license replacing the represented finite sequence with an arbitrary count or a single group.

### Final-region reads and the absolute-0x3A publication

After the repeated groups, a word is read at absolute address 0xA9. Next, a word is read at fourfold base plus 0x74.

**Both reads precede the byte write at absolute address 0x3A.** That byte contains the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30 under machine-bit interpretation.

**The word reads at doubled base plus 0x14 and adjusted base plus 4 follow the absolute-0x3A write**, in that order. Their results are retained for later publications and the final invocation.

This separation matters if either later read overlaps absolute 0x3A. Neither later word is specified as a value captured before that write.

The next publication cluster writes the adjusted base’s low byte at doubled base plus 0x0D, writes byte 0x6C at adjusted base plus 8, and publishes the global base’s low byte at adjusted base plus 0x0D and plus 9. A word publication at doubled base plus 0x54 then contains the doubled base itself.

### Absolute-0x67 storage: three separate reads

The function initially writes the low byte of the retained absolute-0xA9 word to absolute address 0x67. It subsequently performs **three separate byte reads from 0x67**. Their consumers and intervening effects are significant.

**First read: absolute-0x21 publication.** After the initial 0x67 write, a word is read at doubled base plus 0x44. Only then is byte storage at 0x67 read for the byte write to absolute address 0x21. That publication consumes the newly read byte, not an unconditional copy of the retained absolute-0xA9 word.

The loaded offset-0x44 word next supplies a byte at adjusted base plus 0x10, a word at global base plus 0x54, and a shifted byte at doubled base plus 1. The shift count is the adjusted base’s low byte. A second word publication at global base plus 0x54 replaces the first with the adjusted base.

**Second read: first later offset-9 publication.** A word is then read at adjusted base plus 0x40. The retained adjusted-base-plus-4 word is published at global base plus 0x74; the newly read offset-0x40 word is published at adjusted base plus 0x10; and the doubled base is published at global base plus 0x44. After those effects, byte storage at 0x67 is read again for a byte publication at adjusted base plus 9.

This second read may observe changes caused by any intervening aliased store. It is not reuse of the byte consumed by the absolute-0x21 publication.

**Third read: second later offset-9 publication.** The function next writes the adjusted base’s low byte at doubled base plus 9 and the retained absolute-0xA9 word at adjusted base plus 0x44. A word is then read at adjusted base plus 0x54, producing another address-like value. After that read, byte storage at 0x67 is read a third time for another byte publication at adjusted base plus 9.

The word publication at adjusted base plus 0x44 can itself overlap 0x67. The third byte read therefore need not equal either earlier byte read or the byte initially written to 0x67.

The value loaded from adjusted base plus 0x54 is retained for subsequent destination and payload use. Later modification of that storage does not by itself update the retained value.

### Final publications and unresolved invocation

The retained fourfold-base-plus-0x74 word supplies its low byte to the newly loaded destination plus 0x0D. The earlier doubled-base-plus-0x14 word is restored at doubled base plus 0x54. A word is then read at adjusted base plus 0x14, before further byte and word publications involving the retained loaded destination.

Those publications include the fourfold-base lookup’s low byte at doubled base plus 0x11, the retained destination’s low byte at adjusted base plus 9, and that destination as a word at adjusted base plus 0x44.

A later word read at doubled base plus 4 supplies another base. A halfword containing 0x65 is written at twice that base, followed by a word read at that base plus 0x64. Between this read and the later read at that base plus 0x74, the function publishes the adjusted-base-plus-0x14 word’s low byte at the retained destination plus 0x11 and decimal 100 as a word at its offset 0x54.

After the offset-0x74 read, another word is read at doubled base plus 0x64. The last publication cluster writes the newer base’s low byte at doubled base plus 9, publishes the first offset-0x64 result through the second offset-0x64 result plus 0x54, and publishes the second result at twice the first result.

The final unresolved invocation receives, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The word read at offset 0x64 from the base loaded at doubled base plus 4.
3. The earlier retained word from doubled base plus 0x14.
4. The word subsequently read at offset 0x74 from that newer base.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static obligations, not executed tests.

### Split-result width

For a composite result with low word 0x12345678 and high word 0x9ABCDEF0, a common split-result invocation supplies 0x34567800 and 0x9ABCDEF0 under wrapping 32-bit semantics. It does not supply one shifted 64-bit argument.

### Masked extraction

Masking 0xFFABCDEF to 24 bits and then logically shifting right by 20 yields 0xA. The original upper byte contributes nothing.

### Global-base capture

If global-base storage overlaps absolute 0x60, its retained value is read before the word publication of 0x20 there. Re-reading it afterward is not equivalent.

### Absolute-0x3A aliasing

If adjusted base plus 4 overlaps absolute 0x3A, its final-region word read occurs after the 0x3A byte write and must account for that write. The absolute-0xA9 and fourfold-base-plus-0x74 words were read earlier.

### Changing byte storage at 0x67

Suppose the initial 0x67 write contains 0x12. If an intervening computed store changes that byte to 0x34 before the second read, the corresponding adjusted-base-plus-9 publication consumes 0x34. If another intervening store changes it to 0x56 before the third read, the next publication consumes 0x56. Neither read is replaced by retained 0x12.

### Overlap and width

Clearing a word at offset 0x14 and later writing a byte at offset 0x15 modifies overlapping storage. Likewise, the byte, halfword, and word stores of 0x3A remain different-width effects.

### Partial progress

If a late destination faults, earlier completed effects have no visible rollback. No explicit failure result is produced.
