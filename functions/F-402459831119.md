# F-402459831119

## Purpose

F-402459831119 performs an ordered, memory-dependent transformation. It publishes bytes, halfwords, and words at addresses derived from incoming state and earlier observations. Some observations remain retained across later writes; others deliberately reacquire memory. Consequently, aliasing can change both subsequent payloads and subsequent destinations.

No application-level purpose, object model, or successful completion condition is established. Terms such as **active address**, **route**, **root**, and **link** describe dataflow roles only. They imply neither valid pointers nor allocated objects.

This contract distinguishes established static relationships from unresolved execution behavior. A late byte publication has no established initialized payload. The represented continuation remains **documented_only**; it is not a guarantee of defined execution beyond that boundary.

There is no established returned value, status code, recoverable error interface, or normal-return guarantee. Taking an argument-storage address does not establish that the storage belongs to the caller. Assigning argument-7 storage does not establish a caller-visible output parameter.

### Representation and preconditions

Bytes, halfwords, and words are respectively 8, 16, and 32 bits. A **selector** is a signed 16-bit observation promoted to signed 32 bits. A **character** is an eight-bit plain character whose signedness is unresolved.

All displacements are in bytes. Selectors contribute their signed numerical values without element scaling. Decimal 100 and hexadecimal 0x100 are different displacements.

**Low-byte extraction** means the numerical least significant eight bits. **Character conversion** is a distinct conversion with unresolved out-of-range behavior. Signed-halfword narrowing likewise has no established portable result for out-of-range inputs. Unknown byte order prevents assigning a numerical low byte to a particular address within a word.

Address arithmetic and products use represented signed 32-bit values. Validity requires that the reached arithmetic, including multiplication and signed left shifts, satisfy the applicable representation rules. Wrapping is not established. Neither pointer width nor lossless conversion between integers and pointers is established.

Every reached memory access requires adequate lifetime, extent, permissions, alignment, and address validity. A word-derived byte still requires the complete word observation.

## Behavior

### Observable model

An **observation** acquires a value at its specified point. A **publication** writes its specified width. The order below expresses represented precedence and data dependencies, not atomicity, volatile access counts, or visibility to concurrent observers.

A retained value is independent of subsequent changes to its source. A fresh observation can reflect preceding overlapping publications. No addressed regions, routing sources, or argument-storage regions are established to be disjoint.

### Entry contract

The entry publications and observations have the following precedence:

- Argument 4 is published at active address plus 0x11 before any entry observation.
- The entry observations retain, in order, the word at argument 1 plus decimal 100, the word at active address plus 0x44, and the selector at active address plus peer. Call the first two values **entry payload** and **entry reference**.
- Argument 2 plus 0x54 receives active address.
- Two byte publications at active address plus 0x11 both use the retained entry payload’s extracted low byte. Between those publications, peer is published at entry reference plus 0x44, then the word at selector plus 0x44 supplies **auxiliary base**, then the word at peer plus 0x14 supplies **entry anchor**.
- After the second byte publication, entry anchor is narrowed into the halfword at selector plus peer. The subsequent word observation at peer plus 0x44 supplies the first **relay**.

Active address is unchanged by entry.

### Transfer-phase invariants

Eleven distinct transfer phases follow. Their numbering denotes order, not a loop or an interchangeable operation.

Each phase has an incoming active address and relay. Its **initial route** and **renewal payload** are separate observations of that phase’s routing source. The following precedence applies to every phase:

1. The incoming active address is published at its own displacement 0x54 before either routing-source observation.
2. The first routing-source observation supplies initial route; the second supplies renewal payload.
3. Renewal payload is published at the phase’s renewal base plus 0x44 before incoming relay is published at initial route plus 0x14.
4. The opening byte is published at incoming active address plus 0x11 before the phase’s selector observation.

The selector address is always incoming active address plus initial route. Later replacement of either role does not revise that address.

The routing-source observations have no intervening addressed publication, but their equality is not guaranteed. These sources are data inputs, not established function invocations.

### Root, early link, and initial address replacements

Phase 1 establishes **root** from the word at selector plus 0x44, then **phase-1 anchor** from the word at initial route plus 0x14. A fresh auxiliary-base-plus-decimal-100 word supplies a character-converted byte at incoming active address plus 0x11.

After that byte publication, the root-plus-4 word is retained before the initial-route-plus-0x74 word, which establishes **early link**. The retained root-plus-4 word is published at incoming active address plus 0x54 before closure.

Phase 1’s closing destination uses multiplication by two, independently of the earlier left shift, and does not use the selector.

In phases 2 and 3, selector acquisition precedes publication of early link at root plus 0x54. Only after that publication does the word at initial route plus 0x14 supply replacement active address. Each replacement receives character-converted initial route at displacement 0x0d.

Phase 3 subsequently acquires effective route from incoming relay plus 0x14. Root is published at early link plus 0x54 before closure.

### Character-dependent transfers

Phases 4–6 acquire a character at selector plus incoming relay. Its promoted numerical value determines the closing payload and the following phase’s renewal base. Character signedness therefore affects later addresses.

Their additional obligations are:

- **Phase 4:** character-converted early link is published at initial route plus 0x1d before closure.
- **Phase 5:** after character acquisition, a fresh root-plus-decimal-100 word is published at selector plus 0x54. Character-converted incoming relay is then published at promoted character plus 0x0d. A subsequent word at initial route plus decimal 100 establishes **saved link**, before closure.
- **Phase 6:** after character acquisition, publications have this precedence: character-converted initial route at root plus 1; character-converted incoming relay at initial route plus 0x0d; saved link as a word at initial route plus 0x44; character-converted initial route at promoted character plus 0x0d; closing halfword.

Phase 6’s outgoing relay is a fresh observation after closure. It is not necessarily saved link: the intervening byte and halfword publications may overlap its source.

### Later routing and replacement constraints

Phase 7 acquires its selector before effective route is observed at incoming relay plus 0x74. Replacement active address is then observed at incoming active address plus 0x14. Character-converted effective route is published at the phase reference plus 0x0d before closure.

Phase 8 preserves active address. After selector acquisition, character-converted initial route is published at incoming relay plus 0x15, then incoming relay is published as a word at initial route plus 0x44. Only afterward does the word at phase reference plus 0x74 supply effective route.

Phases 9 and 10 obtain replacement active address from selector plus 0x14. Following that acquisition, their publications have this order:

|---|---|

| Initial route plus incoming relay | Saved link |
| Initial route plus 0x14 | Reference |
| Initial route plus 0x0d | Character-converted fresh word from saved link plus 0x44 |

The saved-link-relative observation occurs after the first three word publications.

Phase 10 additionally requires three publications between selector acquisition and replacement-address observation: incoming active address at incoming relay plus 0x54; the new snapshot’s extracted low byte at incoming active address plus 0x11; initial route at reference plus 0x44. Any overlap affecting selector plus 0x14 therefore affects the later replacement-address observation. The extracted byte remains retained for phase 11.

### Terminal arithmetic and initialization boundary

For phase 11, call initial route **terminal route**, its reference **terminal reference**, and its promoted selector **factor**. Active address remains unchanged.

Four word publications of factor at factor plus 0x54 precede the initialization boundary. The first two alternate with two character-converted active-address publications at terminal route plus 0x11.

After the second byte publication, three dependent word observations identify **holder**. Their addresses are, successively:

- Factor multiplied by terminal reference, plus 0x44.
- The preceding observed value plus 0x74.
- The preceding observed value plus 0x14.

The word at holder plus 0x14 is retained as **old link** before that location receives active address shifted left once. A subsequent fresh word at old link plus 0x14, multiplied by factor, forms **intermediate product**. Overlap with the holder-relative publication can affect this multiplicand.

The third factor publication follows that calculation. Character-converted old link is then published at terminal route plus 0x11. The word at intermediate product plus 0x74 establishes **late link**.

**Current product** is factor multiplied by intermediate product—not by late link. Its calculation precedes the fourth factor publication.

The next byte publication at terminal route plus 0x11 consumes an eight-bit value without established initialization. A later assignment does not establish this earlier payload. Neither a particular byte nor defined continuation is established at this boundary.

This subsection is **documented_only**.

A word at current product plus 0x44 is retained before a word at root plus 0x74. Late link is then published at terminal route plus decimal 100.

The first retained word supplies a character-converted byte at terminal route plus 9. A separate fresh word at current product plus 0x44 supplies a character-converted byte at current product plus 0x11. Both publications precede a fifth factor publication at factor plus 0x54.
