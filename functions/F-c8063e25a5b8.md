### Widths and arithmetic

The representation distinguishes 32-bit words from 64-bit result containers. Extracting an upper word differs from shifting an entire 64-bit result right by 34, 36, or 44 bits; all these forms occur.

Unsigned right shifts introduce zeros. Mask placement matters: restricting a field before shifting is not generally equivalent to masking afterward.

**[INFERENCE]** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, address additions, multiplications, and left shifts retain only the low 32 bits. Consequences include:

- The third argument’s explicit left shift by 24 retains only its low byte.
- A word shifted left by eight and then logically right by eight retains its original low 24 bits.
- Successive left shifts can eliminate all bits even when each individual count is below 32.
- Multiplication by 0x02000000 retains only seven source bits.
- A word shifted left by 16 has a zero low halfword.
- Byte and halfword stores retain the low eight and sixteen bits respectively.

These conditional simplifications do not settle signed overflow or signed-shift semantics in the supplied representation. In particular, the tail contains a signed right shift with a count taken from the low eight bits of a computed word. Counts outside the ordinary 32-bit range must not be silently reduced modulo 32.

- A word store at the final lower result multiplied by 0x100, plus 0x54, follows that byte store.
- The earlier byte destination is overwritten with a different retained value.
- A word at another computed base plus 0x14 is cleared before a byte at that base plus 0x05 is read.

This is an access-order contract, not merely a final-state description. Aliasing may cause a later read to observe an intervening write.

One computed base is formed by shifting a word left by eight, masking with 0x7FFF, shifting right by six, and doubling. **[INFERENCE]** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, this base is a multiple of eight from zero through 0x3F8. Another base is formed by multiplication by 0x02000000. Neither is checked before use.

### Early absolute accesses and address-bearing storage

The byte read at absolute address 0x00000005 occurs after the initial direct-write cluster and before the byte write at absolute address 0x00000001. The latter receives the low byte of the small computed base.

The retained byte from 0x00000005 is then used as an address component. A byte is stored at that byte-valued base plus 0x15. After intervening dynamic writes, a word is read at that base plus 0x54, then the same word destination is overwritten. The loaded word is retained and becomes an important seed for subsequent scaling and addressing.

Other address-bearing reads use offsets including 0x04, 0x14, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. A byte read at offset 0x05 can itself select the base of a word read at offset 0x74. This must not be replaced with a full-width pointer load at offset 0x05.

The absolute word store at 0x00000054 occurs amid dynamic mutations. It writes twice the retained seed word. Immediately before it, a word at small-base-plus-0x14 receives another retained value shifted left by 26. Immediately afterward, that dynamic word destination receives the seed word twice.

Two byte stores at small-base-plus-0x15 follow. The byte write at absolute address 0x00000015 then receives the low byte of the small base, before further dynamic word stores and address-bearing reads.

These absolute accesses are not a separate initialization pass. Their placement among dynamic accesses matters whenever destinations overlap.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Third-argument contribution.** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, arguments 0x000000AB and 0x123456AB yield the same explicit shifted contribution, 0xAB000000, at the two early sites. Whole-function equivalence is not established.

2. **Truncation.** Shifting 0x12345678 left by eight as a 32-bit word, then logically right by eight, yields 0x00345678.

3. **Unsigned widening.** A reached unsigned byte read of 0xFE supplies 0x000000FE to its widening word store, not 0xFFFFFFFE.

4. **Global capture before overwrite.** If the global address-word storage overlaps 0x00000060, the repeated region uses the address captured before the absolute 0x20 store. It must not use a replacement value obtained by moving the read afterward.

5. **Early absolute ordering.** An access observer records the byte read at 0x00000005 before the byte write at 0x00000001. The absolute writes at 0x00000054 and 0x00000015 retain their positions among dynamic accesses.

6. **Coincident 0x3A destinations.** If the relevant loaded word is zero and those accesses are supported, the byte and halfword stores begin at the same address. If it is eight, the halfword and word stores both begin at 0x40. All three stores remain separate and ordered.

7. **Reload-sensitive tail.** If the initial write to 0x00000067 stores 0xAA, but an intervening dynamic write changes that byte to 0xBB before a later read, that read supplies 0xBB. Reusing the initial retained byte is incorrect.

8. **Overwrite trace.** The update-base-plus-0x20 destination receives all four represented word stores. Keeping only its final 0x20 value loses access behavior.

9. **Interrupted execution.** If a prefix dependency does not return, later calls and direct writes are not reached. No cleanup is established.

10. **Final arguments.** Reaching the final invocation supplies the retained loads identified above, not automatically reloaded values after the last mutations.
