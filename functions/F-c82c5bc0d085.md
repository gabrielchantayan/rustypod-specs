## Behavior

The operation begins with a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. The first target is derived from incoming link-related execution state. Subsequent targets generally use locally established address bases and a displacement of 0xFFE.

The supplied identity map does not resolve these callees to additional opaque function IDs. Similar-looking invocations therefore remain distinct unresolved interactions.

- Explicit zero arguments remain explicit arguments, not omitted or unspecified values.

- Earlier captured results can supply arguments after intervening calls.

### Arithmetic boundaries

A conditional **32-bit bitvector model** is useful for describing the visible transformations: left shifts and multiplication retain the low 32 bits, and address arithmetic can wrap. This is not an assertion that every represented signed expression has portable wrapping semantics.

Intermediate truncation matters. Shifting left and then right need not recover the original value. A shift of a 64-bit result by more than 32 positions selects high-half information; it is not a shift of its low half.

A late compact field is formed from a 32-bit result shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, and logically shifted right by six. Under the bitvector model, its possible values are multiples of four from 0 through 508. Twice that field is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

Near completion, a represented signed word is right-shifted by a count selected from the low byte of a work address. Counts can exceed 31. Oversized-count behavior and right shifting a negative operand are **documented_only**; no masking-to-five-bits rule is established.

### Initial mutations and storage at 0x05 and 0x01

The initial memory ordering establishes several observable boundaries. A word store through the high half of the last dependency result, at offset 0x14, precedes a byte store through an earlier shifted result at offset 9. A word store of the primary base through the last result’s scaled low half, at offset 0x54, separates that byte store from its overwrite.

The primary-base word at offset 0x14 is then cleared. An unsigned byte read at primary-base offset 5 occurs before clearing compact-base offset 0x14. The captured byte is subsequently stored as a zero-extended word at primary-base offset 0x34.

A fresh read of primary-base offset 0x14 follows. It is not an unconditional reuse of the earlier zero: intervening aliases or environment-specific memory effects may matter.

After another compact-base clearing, the low byte of that freshly read word is written twice consecutively at compact-base offset 0x15. Only then is storage at fixed address **0x05** read and converted to a 32-bit unsigned value. Its declaration and actual read width are unavailable; it must not automatically be treated as an explicitly demonstrated byte load.

That read is immediately followed, in the represented memory sequence, by a byte assignment at fixed address **0x01**, receiving the low byte of the compact base.

The value obtained from 0x05 subsequently supplies a computed base. Before capturing the saved link at that base’s offset 0x54, the operation writes:

- The compact field’s low byte at that base’s offset 0x15.
- The captured token’s low byte at primary-base offset 0x11.
- The captured token as a word at offset 0x54 through the freshly read primary-base word.
- Zero at primary-base offset 0x11.

The saved link is then read, and its source word is overwritten. These accesses cannot be collapsed into a generic low-address update.

### Linking mutations and storage at 0x54 and 0x15

The middle phase performs word reads through offsets 0x74, 4, and 0x64, followed by stores through the resulting address-like values. It includes repeated self-reference writes and repeated byte stores.

One self-reference update contains two reads of the same compact-base offset-0x74 location. The representation does not establish their relative evaluation order, atomicity, or equality in changing memory.

A meaningful fixed-address boundary occurs after a word derived from the primary-base offset-0x74 read has been used for repeated link updates:

- A word at compact-base offset 0x14 receives a shifted captured scalar.
- Fixed-address storage **0x54** receives a word-valued assignment of twice the saved link.
- Compact-base offset 0x14 then receives the saved link twice.
- Compact-base offset 0x15 then receives the low byte of the fourfold link base twice.
- Fixed address **0x15** then receives a byte-valued assignment of the compact base.

Thus, the assignment at 0x54 precedes the assignment at 0x15, but computed-address word and byte writes intervene. Moving either fixed-address assignment across those writes requires unavailable non-aliasing evidence. The 0x54 assignment is represented as word-valued; its global declaration is not supplied.

Subsequent mutations connect saved, doubled, fourfold, and eightfold values through offsets 0x44 and 0x54. Some address bases come from unsigned bytes and therefore lie in 0–255 before offset addition, but the resulting accesses are wider word reads.

Repeated reads at offset 5 occur after intervening writes. They remain separate memory observations rather than one cached byte value.

### Constant-valued writes, anchor capture, and storage at 0x60

A later region writes 0x3A at three widths: a byte through a fourfold-scaled address, a halfword through an eightfold-scaled address, and a word at the fourfold-scaled address plus 0x20.

The anchor read supplies a represented 32-bit scalar, but its underlying global declaration and exact access width are unavailable. The 0x60 assignment is word-valued; its global declaration is also absent.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static obligations, not executed tests.

### Result-half separation

For low and high result halves 0x12345678 and 0x9ABCDEF0, a transformation requiring an eight-bit left shift of the low half and an unchanged high half supplies 0x34567800 and 0x9ABCDEF0 under the bitvector model. Carry propagation into the high argument would change the behavior.

### Compact-field boundary

A scalar whose low seven bits are all set produces compact field 508 and compact base 1016. A field value of 511 is not permitted by this transformation.

### Unsigned byte transfer

An explicitly unsigned byte read of 0xE2 supplies word value 0x000000E2, not 0xFFFFFFE2.

### Anchor capture across an alias

If anchor storage overlaps the destination at 0x60 and initially supplies one address, subsequent anchor-based accesses use that captured address. Reloading after the 0x20 assignment could select a different address and would violate the represented ordering.

### Fixed-address ordering

An observable trace places the read at 0x05 before the byte assignment at 0x01. It places the word-valued assignment at 0x54 before the assignment at 0x15 while retaining the intervening compact-base writes.

### Fresh 0x67 observations

If a computed write between the assignment at 0x67 and a later read changes that storage to 0xAB, the later byte transfer uses the value observed at that later read. Reusing the original low byte from 0xA9 is not generally equivalent.

### Repeated stores and failure

Where each device-memory write is observable, consecutive identical stores remain separate represented events.

If an invocation does not return or an access faults, no explicit local recovery path is shown. This establishes neither that preceding state changes persist forever nor that another component restores them.
