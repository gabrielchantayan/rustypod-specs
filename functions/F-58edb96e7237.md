# F-58edb96e7237

## Purpose

F-58edb96e7237 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, derives word-sized quantities from their results and implicit execution state, mutates memory, and finally invokes an unresolved target with four arguments.

The supplied representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Character-like constants, repeated offsets, and self-referential stores do not establish parsing, formatting, allocation, initialization, or collection semantics.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented accesses, widths, argument relationships, captured values, and ordering.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations that are not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior or details not fully specified here.

Requirements are conditional on the represented operations being reached and completing. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Dependency invocation phase

A long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations precedes the first explicit data-memory write. The first target depends on incoming link state plus `0xffe`; subsequent targets generally derive from embedded code-location values plus that displacement.

Invocation order and argument positions are significant. An invocation with no displayed arguments is not established to lack implicit inputs. Ignoring a result does not make its producer removable.

The first combined result supplies three arguments to the next invocation: its low word shifted left by 8, its unchanged high word, and its low word shifted left by 15. Another group supplies four arguments: low word shifted left by 8, unchanged high word, and two copies of low word shifted left by 21.

Later argument construction includes primary- and secondary-result shifts through 31 bits, unsigned right shifts, low-bit masks, combined-result extraction at bit positions 34, 36, and 44, and reuse across intervening invocations. Some argument positions receive repeated captured values or explicit zeros.

Zero-valued transformations do not remove surrounding invocations. In particular, a zero first argument with a nonzero third argument is not equivalent to putting the nonzero value first.

The complete invocation trace and dependency identities remain documented_only in this contract.

### Arithmetic constraints

Established unsigned 32-bit operations use bit-vector arithmetic. Left shifts discard bits outside the word; unsigned right shifts introduce zeros. Relevant bounds include:

- Low 24 bits shifted left by 6: at most `0x3fffffc0`.
- Low 22 bits shifted left by 8: at most `0x3fffff00`.
- Low 20 bits shifted right by 10: 0–1023.
- Low 18 bits shifted right by 11: 0–127.

A late restricted index comes from a word shifted left by 8, masked to its low 15 bits, then shifted right by 6. Under the 32-bit interpretation, the index ranges from 0 through 508 in multiples of four. Twice that index ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

Signed left and right shifts, signed multiplication, subtraction, and additions also occur. Their executable overflow and signed-shift semantics remain documented_only. Neither arbitrary-precision arithmetic nor portable signed-overflow assumptions are justified.

### Opening direct mutations

The following labels name captured quantities, not allocated objects:

- **Terminal words:** low and high halves of the last combined result before mutation.
- **Shifted base:** an earlier result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** another result multiplied by `0x02000000`.
- **Small base:** twice the restricted index.
- **Forwarded word:** an earlier result shifted left by 8.

The opening stores publish the shifted base as a word at terminal-high plus 20, then terminal-high’s low byte at shifted-base plus 9. A word at terminal-low multiplied by 256 plus 84 receives the scaled base. Shifted-base plus 9 then receives shifted-base’s low byte. The two stores to that byte destination are distinct events.

A read-before-clear relationship follows. Scaled-base plus 20 is cleared as a word, then the byte at scaled-base plus 5 is captured before the first word clear at small-base plus 20. Scaled-base plus 52 receives the zero-extended captured byte.

The word at scaled-base plus 20 is then captured. Small-base plus 20 is cleared again, followed by two byte stores of that captured word’s low byte at small-base plus 21.

Neither captured input may be replaced by a later reload. The offset-20 capture is the value observed at its actual read point, not contractually the nominal zero from an earlier clear.

### Fixed input at address 5

The address-5 input is read after both small-base offset-21 byte stores and before the byte store at address 1. Address 1 receives small-base’s low byte.

The captured address-5 value becomes a new unsigned base. Its offset-21 byte receives the restricted index’s low byte. Before reading its offset-84 word, the function writes forwarded-word’s low byte at scaled-base plus 17, stores the full forwarded word at the earlier offset-20 capture plus 84, and clears scaled-base plus 17 as a byte.

The address-5-base offset-84 word is captured before that location is overwritten with the earlier offset-20 capture. Later transformations reuse this captured word without reloading address 5.

### Pointer-like propagation

The middle region interleaves word and byte loads, scaled destinations, self-referential stores, duplicates, and fixed-address writes. These accesses do not establish pointer validity or object ownership.

After publishing the restricted index at small-base plus 52, the function loads scaled-base plus 116. That captured base receives a shifted value at offset 84, later replaced by the forwarded word, and repeated low-byte writes at offset 9. The address-5-derived captured word is twice stored at its own offset 36.

A nested operation reads small-base plus 116 twice: one occurrence determines a destination at offset 84; the other supplies the stored word. Both occurrences matter. Their relative evaluation order within the single represented operation remains unresolved.

Small-base plus 20 receives several successive words, including a shifted result and repeated copies of the address-5-derived capture. Address 84 receives twice that capture as a word; address 21 receives small-base’s low byte. Separate duplicate byte stores remain represented at small-base plus 21.

Fourfold and eightfold forms of the captured word feed subsequent links. The region includes a self-publication at the fourfold base’s offset 84, a doubled word loaded from small-base plus 116, and a byte-derived chain: a byte loaded at a derived base plus 5 becomes a zero-extended base for a word load at offset 116. Such a base ranges from 0 through 255; table-index semantics are not established.

Later loads at offsets 4, 68, and 100 participate in additional byte publications, self-referential word stores, and overwrites. A derived offset-5 byte is read at two distinct points, with writes between them; equality of those reads is not guaranteed.

There is no represented local branch selecting among these groups. Later operations require preceding operations to complete.

### Constants and publication

A later loaded word supplies destinations for three stores of `0x3a`: a byte at four times that word, a halfword at eight times that word, and a word at the first destination plus 32.

A previously derived fourfold quantity is reduced by 150 without a bounds or underflow check. Four times this adjusted quantity is the **adjusted fourfold base**.

The word at adjusted-fourfold-base plus 32 receives, in order, `0x20`, `0x2f`, a zero-extended byte read from adjusted-fourfold-base plus 4, and `0x20` again. The byte read occurs after the `0x2f` store.

Each publication group has four ordered stores:

1. A word at an eight-times-scaled destination plus 84 receives a fourfold value derived from the later loaded word.
2. Publication-base plus 25 receives adjusted-fourfold-base’s low byte.
3. Adjusted-fourfold-base plus 17 receives publication-base’s low byte.

### Final captures and byte destinations

For the remaining behavior:

- **Doubled link base** is twice an earlier loaded word.
- **Publication value** is the fourfold captured value written by the publication groups.

After publication, the function captures the word at address 169 as the **fixed captured word**, then the word at publication-value plus 116 as the **terminal field word**.

Address 58 next receives the low-byte result of eight times the adjusted quantity plus `0x30`. Only afterward are doubled-link-base plus 20 and adjusted-fourfold-base plus 4 captured as words.

Before publishing to address 103, the function performs these byte writes in order:

- Adjusted-fourfold-base’s low byte at doubled-link-base plus 13.
- `0x6c` at adjusted-fourfold-base plus 8.
- Publication-base’s low byte at **adjusted-fourfold-base plus 13**.
- Publication-base’s low byte at **adjusted-fourfold-base plus 9**.

Thus, publication base supplies the last two byte **values**, not their destination base. The offset-13 write precedes the offset-9 write. Neither destination is publication-base plus 13 or publication-base plus 9 merely because publication-base’s low byte is stored there.

Doubled link base is then stored as a word at doubled-link-base plus 84. Address 103 receives fixed-captured-word’s low byte.

Doubled-link-base plus 1 receives the low byte of a signed-right-shift result using the low eight bits of adjusted fourfold base as the shift count. Next, adjusted-fourfold-base plus 64 is captured. Publication-base plus 116 receives the earlier adjusted-fourfold offset-4 capture; adjusted-fourfold-base plus 16 receives the full offset-64 capture; publication-base plus 68 receives doubled link base.

The terminal field word’s low byte is written at the new base plus 13. Doubled-link-base plus 84 receives the earlier doubled-link offset-20 capture, after which adjusted-fourfold-base plus 20 is captured.

Further writes publish terminal-field-word’s low byte at doubled-link-base plus 17, overwrite adjusted-fourfold-base plus 9 with the new base’s low byte, and store the new base as a word at adjusted-fourfold-base plus 68.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static cases, not executed tests.

### Forwarding and masking

If the first combined result has low word `0x00000001` and high word `0x12345678`, the next argument sequence is `0x00000100`, `0x12345678`, `0x00008000`.

For unsigned result `0xff000001`, masking to the low 24 bits before shifting left by 6 produces `0x00000040`, not `0xc0000040`.

### Restricted index and capture timing

For an all-ones input to the restricted-index derivation, the shifted word is `0xffffff00`; the index is 508 and small base is 1016. An index of 511 does not satisfy that derivation.

If the opening offset-5 byte capture yields `0xe2`, the later offset-52 word store receives 226 even if the subsequent small-base clear changes the source storage.

The address-5 read occurs after both small-base offset-21 byte writes and before the address-1 write. The publication-base read occurs after all four adjusted offset-32 stores and before the address-96 store. Entry-time snapshots do not satisfy those boundaries.

### Publication byte values versus destinations

Suppose adjusted fourfold base is `0x2008` and publication base is `0x3070`, and the relevant accesses are valid.

In the final pre-address-103 group, publication-base’s low byte, `0x70`, is written first at `0x2015`, then at `0x2011`. These are adjusted-fourfold-base plus 13 and plus 9. Substituting destinations `0x307d` and `0x3079` fails the contract.

This describes those write events, not guaranteed final contents: later operations overwrite the adjusted offset-9 byte.

### Captured words versus fresh byte reads

Suppose address 169 yields a word whose low byte is `0x7a`. Address 103 initially receives `0x7a`. If an intervening overlapping write changes address 103, a subsequent read must supply the changed byte to its destination.

The full word later stored at adjusted-fourfold-base plus 68 remains the original address-169 capture. Neither reloading address 169 nor replacing all address-103 reads with the captured low byte preserves this distinction.

### Repetition and failure

For observable storage, the four adjusted offset-32 writes remain distinguishable from a single final `0x20` write. Duplicate byte stores likewise remain separate events.

If an early word store succeeds and a later byte access fails, partial mutation is permitted. Rollback and a returned error code are not established.
