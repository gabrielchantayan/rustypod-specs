# F-1b89e74f4780

## Purpose

F-1b89e74f4780 maps two 32-bit input words to a represented 64-bit result. Depending on the input, it:

- Constructs and right-shifts a two-word quantity, returning either that quantity or its two-word negation.
- Returns a sign-dependent boundary constant.
- Transfers control to an unresolved destination, selecting arguments from shared control data.

**Inferred intent:** the input layout, shift distances, and boundary constants are consistent with converting a binary64 representation to a signed 64-bit integer, with special handling for exceptional inputs. This interpretation does not replace the word-level contract. Oversized-shift behavior and the unresolved transfer remain material uncertainties.

All behavior below is derived from static evidence. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Construction and path selection

The extracted field is the upper input arithmetically shifted right by 20.

A constructed upper word combines:

- The upper input shifted left by 11, retaining 32 bits.
- The lower input logically shifted right by 21.

A constructed lower word is the lower input shifted left by 11, retaining 32 bits.

Bit 31 of the constructed upper word is set whenever \(E\) is nonzero. When \(E=-2048\), that bit is cleared afterward. Thus the most negative extracted field does not retain the inserted bit.

This construction requires no represented shared-data reads.

### Negative-field direct path

For \(E<0\), the mathematical candidate distance is:

\[
d=-962-E.
\]

Direct shifting is selected exactly for:

- Every \(E\) from −2048 through −963.
- \(E=-962\) only when the constructed upper word is `0x80000000` and the constructed lower word is zero.

All other negative-field inputs proceed to classification.

For an eligible input, distances greater than 79 are replaced by 80. The selected distance is therefore between 0 and 80, inclusive.

The shifted quantity is returned with two-word negation, as specified below.

### Nonnegative-field direct path

For \(E\geq0\), the mathematical candidate distance is:

\[
d=1086-E.
\]

Direct shifting is selected exactly for \(E\) from 0 through 1085. In particular, \(E=1086\) is rejected because its candidate distance is zero.

Eligible distances greater than 79 are replaced by 80. The selected distance is between 1 and 80, inclusive.

The shifted quantity is returned without negation. Fields from 1086 through 2047 proceed to classification.

### Shifted quantity

Let \(s\) be the selected distance after capping.

The shifted upper component is the constructed upper word logically shifted right by \(s\). The shifted lower component combines, using bitwise OR:

1. The constructed lower word logically shifted right by \(s\).
2. A contribution from the constructed upper word:

| Selected distance | Contribution to the shifted lower component |
|---|---|
| 0–31 | Constructed upper word shifted left by \(32-s\) |
| 32 | Constructed upper word shifted right by zero |
| 33–80 | Constructed upper word shifted right by \(s-32\) |

The represented shift counts retain their low eight bits. Within the selected range, this preserves the distances shown, including 32 and larger.

Several operations consequently require unresolved oversized-shift semantics:

- At \(s=0\), the lower-word contribution requests a left shift by 32.
- At \(s\geq32\), both initial right shifts request counts of at least 32.
- At \(s\geq64\), the upper-word contribution also requests a right shift of at least 32.

**Conditional interpretation:** if logical shifts of a 32-bit word by 32 or more produce zero, the combined operation is a logical right shift of the constructed 64-bit quantity by \(s\). This condition is not established independently by the supplied representation.

### Direct result and exact negation rule

For the nonnegative-field path, the shifted upper and lower components become the corresponding returned words unchanged.

For the negative-field path, let \(A\) be the shifted upper component and \(B\) the shifted lower component. Define \(c\) as 1 when \(B\) is nonzero and 0 otherwise. The returned words are:

\[
\text{returned lower}=(-B)\bmod 2^{32},
\]

\[
\text{returned upper}=\bigl(-(A+c)\bigr)\bmod 2^{32}.
\]

The addition occurs **inside the negation**. The rule is not “negate \(A\), then add \(c\).”

Consequently:

- When the shifted lower component is zero, the returned upper word is the modular negation of the shifted upper component.
- When the shifted lower component is nonzero, one is added to the shifted upper component before negating the entire sum.
- For shifted components upper zero and lower one, both returned words are `0xFFFFFFFF`.

The nonzero test uses the completed shifted lower component, including its upper-word contribution. This implements negation of the entire two-word quantity.

No rounding-mode lookup, remainder accumulation, or explicit exception-state update is represented on these direct paths.

### Classification and boundary results

Inputs rejected by both direct paths reach classification.

The classification word is formed from twice the upper input plus one when the lower input is nonzero, reduced to a 32-bit word. Comparisons of the resulting word are unsigned. The arithmetic presentation uses a signed upper input, so its correspondence to wrapping machine arithmetic remains a documented qualification.

When the classification word is at most `0xFFE00000`, the function returns:

| Upper-input sign | Returned 64-bit pattern |
|---|---|
| Nonnegative | `0x7FFFFFFFFFFFFFFF` |
| Negative | `0x8000000000000000` |

Separate less-than and equality branches are represented, but both produce the same sign-dependent result and show no differing side effects.

Under the binary64 interpretation, this includes out-of-range finite inputs and both infinities that reach classification. It does not establish that every input is range-checked before shifting: eligible shift-path inputs have already returned.

A classification word greater than `0xFFE00000` reaches shared-control dispatch.

### Shared-read ordering

On exceptional dispatch, the D-1 selector reference precedes the chosen branch’s additional references.

For selector 8 with bit `0x40` clear, the represented sequence is:

1. Read D-3.
2. Read D-2.
3. Inspect D-1 bit `0x10`.
4. If that bit is set, replace the first argument with zero and obtain the second argument from D-4.

Thus D-3 and D-2 are represented as read even when their argument values are superseded.

For selector 8 with bit `0x40` set, no D-2, D-3, or D-4 read is represented.

For selector 9, the control-bit check precedes either the transformed-control reference or the D-2 reference, according to the selected branch.

D-1 is referenced repeatedly rather than explicitly captured as one immutable snapshot. The representation does not establish actual load counts, volatility, atomicity, or concurrency guarantees. Later control references must not be assumed to observe the same value as the selector reference.

## Acceptance examples

These are static acceptance conditions, not executed tests. Conditions concerning oversized shifts or wrapping classification arithmetic are stated explicitly.

### Path boundaries

1. **Extracted field 1085:** the nonnegative direct path is selected with distance 1. No shared-control dispatch occurs.

2. **Extracted field 1086:** the zero candidate distance rejects the nonnegative direct path. Classification determines the outcome.

3. **Extracted field −963:** the negative direct path is selected with distance 1. The completed shifted quantity is negated.

4. **Extracted field −962, constructed upper `0x80000000`, constructed lower zero:** the negative direct path accepts distance zero. If oversized logical shifts produce zero, the returned pattern is `0x8000000000000000`.

5. **Extracted field −962 with any other constructed word pair:** the direct path is rejected. Classification is required.

### Negation and numerical interpretation

6. **Completed shifted components upper zero, lower one:** the negative result is `0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`. The upper-word calculation is the negation of \(0+1\), not the negation of zero followed by addition of one.

7. **Completed shifted components upper one, lower zero:** the negative result is `0xFFFFFFFF00000000`. The zero lower component contributes no increment inside the upper-word negation.

8. **Upper input `0x3FF80000`, lower input zero:** if oversized logical shifts produce zero, the result is `0x0000000000000001`, consistent with truncating binary64 positive 1.5 toward zero.

9. **Upper input `0xBFF80000`, lower input zero:** under the same shift condition, the completed shifted components are upper zero and lower one. The result is `0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`, consistent with truncating negative 1.5 toward zero.

10. **Both input words zero:** the selected distance is capped at 80. If oversized logical shifts produce zero, the result is zero.

### Classification and dispatch

11. **Upper input `0x7FF00000`, lower input zero:** classification equals `0xFFE00000`; the result is `0x7FFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`.

12. **Upper input `0xFFF00000`, lower input zero:** under 32-bit wrapping classification arithmetic, classification equals `0xFFE00000`; the result is `0x8000000000000000`.

13. **Upper input `0x7FF00000`, lower input nonzero:** classification exceeds the threshold. D-1 controls dispatch rather than a direct boundary return.

14. **Dispatch selector 8 with bit `0x40` set:** the represented transfer receives two zero words, without represented reads of D-2 through D-4.

15. **Dispatch selector 8 with bit `0x40` clear and bit `0x10` set:** D-3 and D-2 are read before the replacement decision; the represented arguments are zero and D-4.

16. **Dispatch selector 9 with D-1 consistently `0x00100009` across its references:** bit `0x100000` selects the transformed-control branch. The represented arguments are `0x00000008` and `0xFFF6FFFE`.
