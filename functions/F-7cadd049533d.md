## Behavior

### Trace conventions

**F-000000000001** labels unresolved indirect-call occurrences. It does not identify a known implementation or assert that different occurrences share a destination.

A **four-call group** means four consecutive calls with no explicit arguments shown. When a result is named, it is the represented result of the fourth call. Earlier calls still occur; their effects are not discarded merely because their results are unused. “No explicit arguments” does not mean that implicit machine inputs are absent.

A **selector-mediated byte store** first reads an unsigned byte from the stated selector address, then uses its numeric value plus `0x11` as the destination address. This is not a pointer-width load or a pointer dereference.

The phases below preserve the visible ordering of calls, reads, and writes. Every phase is conditional on execution reaching it.

### Entry and first field write

The first direct effect is a 32-bit store of C at the incoming base plus `0x24`. After X and Q are derived, a dependency receives X; its destination depends on the incoming return/link value. A four-call group follows.

Next, C is stored as 32 bits at X plus `0x24`. A 32-bit word is then read at X plus `0x74` and passed to a dependency. A further four-call group supplies a retained 32-bit result, called **R**.

Only after these calls does the routine read the selector byte at X plus one. It stores the low eight bits of A shifted right by 22 at that selector’s value plus `0x11`. The stored byte contains bits 22 through 29 of A; bits 30 and 31 do not survive truncation.

The two stores of C are separate events. Their addresses can coincide, and intervening dependencies can observe or alter the relevant storage.

### First selector clearing

A dependency receives R shifted left by two. A no-explicit-argument call then produces a 32-bit result that is forwarded to another dependency. A four-call group supplies **S**.

The selector at X plus one is read again, and zero is stored as one byte at the new selector value plus `0x11`. A dependency then receives S shifted left by two, followed by another four-call group whose result is not used explicitly.

The selector is not cached across these calls. The clear need not target the preceding field write’s destination.

### Q-derived clearing and first global assignment

The routine clears one byte at Q plus `0x0f`. It derives Y, reads the selector at Y plus one, and clears one byte at the selector value plus `0x11`.

The next dependency receives four explicit arguments: R shifted left by 23 three times, followed by R shifted left by 25. A no-explicit-argument call produces a word that is forwarded to another dependency. A four-call group supplies **T**.

The byte at Y plus one is then read again and retained as **E**. One byte is cleared at E plus `0x11`. A dependency receives T shifted left by two, and another four-call group occurs.

After those calls, the routine performs an explicit 32-bit zero store at E plus `0x44`. It then makes the **first zero assignment** to the represented global associated with `0x24`.

The word-store address uses the captured E, not a fresh selector read. The following global assignment has unresolved width and overlapping-storage coverage; it must not be described as another word store.

A no-explicit-argument call produces a word that is forwarded to another dependency. A second no-explicit-argument call produces another word, also forwarded to a dependency. A four-call group then supplies **U**.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static expectations, not executed tests. They assume the relevant operations are reached and the stated arithmetic interpretation applies.

1. **Initial field extraction.** With A equal to `0x3fc00000`, the first selector-mediated write stores `0xff`. With A equal to `0xc0000000`, it stores zero because the highest two bits are discarded by byte truncation.

2. **Fresh selector observations.** If the selector at X plus one is 5 for the first field write and 9 for the later clear, their destinations are `0x16` and `0x1a`, respectively. Reusing the first selector would not match the visible trace.

3. **Captured selector.** If E is 7, the later explicit word clear targets `0x4b`, even if intervening dependencies change the selector at Y plus one.

4. **Global-assignment boundary.** After that captured-selector word clear, the first represented-global zero assignment precedes the call receiving zero, `0x98`, Y, and `0x98`. Acceptance may require that ordering, but cannot require a 32-bit access at `0x24` or a particular overlapping-byte footprint without additional evidence.

5. **Retained store value.** With L equal to 3, N is 12 and its first word-store destination is `0x44`. If P is subsequently 5, the next word store writes the retained 12 at `0x28`.

6. **Maximum masked field.** With M equal to `0x3fffff`, M multiplied by `0x80` is `0x1fffff80`. The earlier two-argument call receives `0x03fffff0` as its second explicit argument.

7. **Final packed bytes.** With F equal to `0x12345678`, its two final-phase byte values are `0xe0` and `0x23`. Their destinations can differ because the selector is read separately.

8. **Unsigned address wrap.** With Q equal to 64, unsigned 32-bit calculation of Z yields zero. The corresponding selector read is at address one; no visible guard prevents it.

9. **Interrupted prefix.** If the first dependency never returns, the initial store of C has already occurred, but the second store of C is not reached. No rollback is shown.
