# F-b1bc54208e6b

## Purpose

F-b1bc54208e6b performs a long sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms dependency results, updates mixed-width derived and fixed-location storage, and reaches a terminal indirect invocation.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Allocation, formatting, serialization, initialization, and object-link maintenance are not established semantics. Character-like constants and address-like values do not establish those interpretations.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented operations, explicit operand widths, value relationships, and sequencing.
- **[INFERENCE]:** conclusions requiring an additional machine-arithmetic interpretation.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior without a sufficiently supported executable requirement.

Every later phase is conditional on execution reaching it. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Dependency progression and arithmetic

The initial invocation, F-dep-001, receives the first input unchanged and the fourth input shifted left by 25. Two invocations with no explicit arguments follow; the latter supplies a represented 64-bit result. Its low 32-bit word shifted left by 8 and high 32-bit word shifted left by 25 become arguments to the next invocation.

Further calls repeatedly consume transformed primary results, high result words, and unresolved secondary words. Early calls include the duplicated fourth-input shift-by-7 argument and the third-input shift-by-4 argument. Later calls contain duplicate transformed arguments, explicit zeros, masked fields, and unsigned right shifts.

“No explicit arguments” does not establish an input-free ABI. Unused explicit results do not make calls removable: dependency effects are unknown.

The represented progression contains no explicit conditional branch or loop. Dependencies themselves may branch, mutate memory, fail, or not return.

Numerical splitting of 64-bit results into low and high 32-bit words does not establish memory endianness. Unsigned shifts of combined results by 34, 36, and 44 select high-word material shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 respectively.

Masks and shifts select fields of different widths. For example, retaining 20 low bits and shifting right by 10 produces 0–1023; retaining 24 low bits and shifting right by 20 produces 0–15. Explicitly zero-valued contributions remain zero through the represented shifts.

**[INFERENCE]** With 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, left shifts and multiplication discard bits above bit 31. Sequential shifts can eliminate all contributing bits. This is not equivalent to unbounded arithmetic.

Signed shifts, signed multiplication, overflow, and out-of-range conversions do not acquire portable semantics from the representation. Address calculations must retain this uncertainty.

Under 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, the small field is a multiple of four from 0 through 508, and the small base is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

The opening effects establish these ordering boundaries:

- A 32-bit store places the packed base at the final dependency result’s high-word-derived address plus 0x14.
- Packed-base offset 9 receives an 8-bit conversion of that high word. A subsequent 32-bit store places the scaled base at 256 times the final low result word plus 0x54. Packed-base offset 9 is then replaced using the packed base’s own low-byte conversion.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial arguments:** Reaching F-dep-001 supplies the first input unchanged and the fourth input shifted left by 25, subject to the unresolved signed-shift interpretation.

2. **Small-base boundary:** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, 1016 is a possible small base; 1017 is not.

3. **Fixed-width restraint:** Capturing location 0xA9 into 32 bits does not justify requiring a 32-bit source read. An explicit 8-bit conversion assigned to 0x01, 0x15, or 0x67 does not justify requiring an 8-bit destination.

4. **Aliased readback:** The scaled-base-offset-0x14 readback is zero only if intervening overlapping effects leave that word zero.

5. **Global ordering:** The global-base capture precedes the fixed assignment at 0x60. A replacement that instead captures the base afterward is not established as equivalent.

6. **Pre-arithmetic character conversion:** If the adjusted scale is 0x100 and character conversion retains the low eight bits as zero, the promoted expression assigned at 0x3A is 0x30 before destination conversion—not 0x830. Its final storage footprint remains unresolved.

7. **Tail ordering:** Evaluation of 0xA9 precedes the fixed assignment at 0x3A. The companion-base-offset-0x14 and adjusted-base-offset-4 reads follow that assignment and may observe overlapping effects.

8. **Fresh fixed evaluations:** If intervening storage changes alter location 0x67, later consumers use their respective fresh evaluations. The latter two consumers write through explicit 8-bit destinations without establishing the source width.

9. **Mixed-width constants:** The indirect 0x3A stores retain separate 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. The later indirect 0x65 store remains 16 bits.

10. **Late overlapping byte:** After both final word captures, the companion-base-offset-9 byte store precedes both final word stores. If it overlaps a final destination, its effect occurs before that word replacement.

11. **Terminal arguments:** Reaching F-dep-003 supplies 0x72, the captured late-base-offset-0x64 word, the earlier companion-base-offset-0x14 word, and the captured late-base-offset-0x74 word, in that order.

12. **Failure:** An inaccessible reached destination has no promised validation response or rollback.
