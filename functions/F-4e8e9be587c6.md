### Nonnegative differences

For a representable, nonnegative difference, right shift by two produces the integer quotient on division by four, discarding the remainder.

Consequently:

- Differences zero through three produce zero.
- Differences four through seven produce one.
- A difference of eight produces two.

These results depend on the difference, not on the individual signs of the inputs. Two negative inputs, or inputs on opposite sides of zero, can produce the same nonnegative result if their subtraction is representable.

### Negative differences

When the second value is smaller than the first, the function applies the signed right shift directly to a negative difference. It expresses no separate negative-value policy.

Where the applicable language rules leave negative signed right shift implementation-defined, the target implementation determines the result. An arithmetic right shift commonly rounds the corresponding scaled value toward negative infinity. Under that rule, a difference of −5 produces −2.

That result differs from signed division by four with truncation toward zero, which would produce −1. Neither arithmetic shifting nor truncating division can be substituted as an unconditional guarantee without additional target evidence.
