# F-a08196eca202

## Purpose

F-a08196eca202 invokes an extended sequence of unresolved dependencies, transforms their results, mutates memory through computed and fixed addresses, and ends with an indirect transfer carrying four arguments.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation has two explicit inputs and no declared return value. Its enclosing sequence contains no visible input-dependent conditional branch, validation failure, or recovery path. Dependency behavior and indirect control transfers can nevertheless prevent execution from reaching later operations.

**Inferred intent:** masks, shifts, and offset-based accesses suggest packed-value processing and mutable linked state. They do not establish an application domain, public record format, or ownership model.

This is a static behavioral boundary, not a claim of executable equivalence. Unresolved dependency semantics, incomplete result conventions, and target arithmetic remain **documented_only**.

## Behavior

Supported argument transformations include:

- shifting a returned word left by eight;
- separating a 64-bit result into lower and upper words;
- forwarding an upper word both unchanged and shifted;
- repeating a transformed value in multiple argument positions;
- forwarding literal zeros beside dependency-produced values;
- retaining results across intervening invocations.

Masks select widths including 24, 22, 20, 19, 17, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. These are bit selections, not evidence of record capacities.

Selecting bits 10 through 19 and shifting the selection left by six produces multiples of 64 through 65,472. A corresponding left shift by five produces multiples of 32 through 32,736.

Near the end of the prefix, a returned word is shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, and shifted right by six. Under a 32-bit bitvector interpretation, the result is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Its doubled value is consequently a multiple of eight from 0 through 1,016. Later accesses using that doubled value can reach low addresses.

Several retained quantities are explicitly initialized to zero and subsequently only shifted. Their derived argument positions remain zero in the displayed dataflow. This does not eliminate the surrounding invocations.

No dependency is established to preserve globals, return normally, or avoid memory mutation. Reads after dependencies must use the storage then present, not assumed entry values.

### Initial mutations and early fixed-address accesses

The suffix first stores a retained word at offset 0x14 from the upper word of the last split dependency result, interpreted as an address. It then writes that upper word’s low byte at offset 9 from a retained base. A word store at offset 0x54 from the lower result word scaled by 256 intervenes before the same byte location is overwritten with another retained value’s low byte.

A computed field at offset 0x14 is cleared. A byte at offset 5 from that base is read, another offset-0x14 field using the small doubled base is cleared, and the promoted byte is stored at offset 0x34 of the first base. The first cleared field is then reread. Aliasing prevents assuming that this reread necessarily returns zero.

After another clear of the small-base field, the suffix performs two consecutive identical byte stores at its offset 0x15.

The next fixed-address boundary is explicit:

- A byte is read from address **5** and retained as a zero-extended address base.
- A byte is then written to address **1**, carrying the low byte of the small doubled base.
- The retained address-5 byte supplies the base for a byte store at offset 0x15.
- Computed stores follow, including an offset-0x11 byte write, a word write through the previously reread field, and clearing that offset-0x11 byte.
- A word is read at offset 0x54 from the retained byte-derived base, then replaced there.

The saved offset-0x54 word drives later scaling, addressing, and stored values. The fixed byte read is not interchangeable with a word-sized pointer read.

Subsequent operations follow offset-0x74 words, write through their results, and repeat self-referential offset-0x24 stores and offset-9 byte stores. Between these operations and later scaling:

- The small-base offset-0x14 field receives a shifted retained value.
- A full **32-bit word write at fixed address 0x54** receives twice the saved offset-0x54 word.
- The small-base offset-0x14 field receives the saved word twice.
- Two identical offset-0x15 byte stores carry the saved word scaled by four.
- A **byte write at fixed address 0x15** carries the small doubled base’s low byte.

These fixed stores are ordinary participants in the alias-sensitive sequence, not independent logical globals.

### Linked and scaled state changes

The saved word scaled by four becomes a recurring computed base. Operations write to its offsets 0x0d, 0x14, and 0x54, follow words at offsets 4 and 0x64, and use offset-0x74 results to construct doubled addresses.

A byte read at offset 5 from a computed base is zero-extended and used as the address base for an offset-0x74 word read. This narrow addressing behavior must not be replaced by following a presumed full-width pointer.

Offset-0x44 locations receive doubled link-derived values, promoted bytes, and other computed bases at different points. Offset-0x15 and offset-9 byte writes likewise carry different truncated quantities over time. Repeated offset-5 reads are fresh observations and can differ if preceding writes overlap their storage.

For the remaining description:

- **Saved word** means the earlier word captured before replacement at the byte-derived base’s offset 0x54.
- **Link value** means the word obtained from the later offset-0x74 read whose base comes from a fresh offset-5 byte read.
- **Doubled link base** is twice the link value.
- **Adjusted quantity** is four times the saved word, minus decimal 150.
- **Adjusted base** is four times the adjusted quantity.
- **Fixed-derived base** is the word later read from fixed address 0x087fbbbc.

These are descriptive dataflow labels, not established record types.

### Literal stores and repeated region

The value 0x3a is stored as a byte at four times the link value, as a halfword at eight times that value, and as a word at offset 0x20 from four times that value.

The adjusted base’s offset-0x20 word then receives, in order, 0x20, 0x2f, a promoted byte freshly read at offset 4 from that base, and 0x20 again. That read observes memory after the first two stores.

Next, the word at **fixed address 0x087fbbbc is read** and retained as the fixed-derived base. A **word containing 0x20 is then written at fixed address 0x60**, before the repeated region begins. If this store overlaps a later computed destination, that overlap is consequential. If it overlaps the just-read fixed word, the retained base is still the captured value.

Each repeated group preserves this internal order:

1. An offset-0x54 word store carries four times the link value.
2. A byte at the fixed-derived base’s offset 0x19 receives the adjusted base’s low byte.
3. A byte at the adjusted base’s offset 0x11 receives the fixed-derived base’s low byte.
4. A word at the fixed-derived base’s offset 0x34 receives a scaled quantity.

The first group uses eight times the saved word for both its initial destination base and final stored quantity. Later groups use eight times the adjusted quantity instead.

**documented_only:** this contract does not independently establish the exact repetition count. The supplied representation contains many separate groups. Their writes must not be collapsed into a single final-state update where intermediate accesses are observable.

### Final-region capture boundary

After the repeated region, the following order is established:

- The word at **fixed address 0xa9** is captured.
- A word at offset 0x74 from four times the link value is captured.
- A byte is written at **fixed address 0x3a**. Its value is the low byte of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30.
- Only afterward is the word at offset 0x14 from the doubled link base captured.
- The word at offset 4 from the adjusted base is then captured.

The address-0x3a write is therefore between two pairs of reads. Neither later read may be moved before it. The offset-0x14 capture supplies the final transfer’s third argument, subject to the target’s result convention.

Further mutations write the adjusted base’s low byte at the doubled link base’s offset 0x0d, literal 0x6c at the adjusted base’s offset 8, and the fixed-derived base’s low byte at its offsets 0x0d and 9. The doubled link base is stored into its own offset-0x54 word.

### Saved fixed word versus fresh byte reads

The low byte of the **captured address-0xa9 word** is written to **fixed address 0x67**. The doubled link base’s offset-0x44 word is then read.

The subsequent write to **fixed address 0x21** uses a **fresh byte read from 0x67**, not a direct extraction from the saved word. This distinction remains mandatory even when an ordinary non-overlapping memory example makes the two values equal.

After that fixed-byte copy, computed writes include:

- a byte at the adjusted base’s offset 0x10;
- a word at the fixed-derived base’s offset 0x54;
- a shifted byte at the doubled link base’s offset 1;
- replacement of the fixed-derived base’s offset-0x54 word with the adjusted base.

The shifted byte uses the signed offset-0x44 word and a shift count selected by the adjusted base’s low byte. Counts of 32 or greater and negative-word right shifts remain **documented_only**.

A word is then read from the adjusted base’s offset 0x40. The earlier offset-4 capture is stored at the fixed-derived base’s offset 0x74, and the newly read offset-0x40 word is written as a **full word starting at adjusted-base offset 0x10**. This can overwrite the earlier byte at that offset and adjacent bytes. Another word store follows at the fixed-derived base’s offset 0x44.

Only then does a **new byte read from 0x67** supply the first computed offset-9 store to the adjusted base.

Before the second such copy, intervening operations write a byte at the doubled link base’s offset 9, write the complete saved address-0xa9 word at the adjusted base’s offset 0x44, and read the adjusted base’s offset-0x54 word. The second adjusted-base offset-9 copy uses **another fresh byte read from 0x67**.

Thus the saved word and all three later byte observations have distinct roles. Computed writes can overlap 0x67; caching its original byte across them changes behavior.

### Remaining updates and final transfer

The captured link word’s low byte is stored through the base just read from adjusted-base offset 0x54. The previously captured doubled-link offset-0x14 word is written back at doubled-link offset 0x54.

A fresh adjusted-base offset-0x14 read supplies a later byte store. Other writes replace adjusted-base offsets 9 and 0x44 with values derived from its captured offset-0x54 base.

A word freshly read at doubled-link offset 4 supplies:

- the doubled address receiving halfword 0x65;
- the base of a subsequent offset-0x64 word read;
- the base of a later offset-0x74 word read;
- a low byte later written at doubled-link offset 9.

Between those reads, another computed offset-0x54 word receives decimal 100. A separate doubled-link offset-0x64 read supplies the destination base for a final offset-0x54 store. A word store through twice the earlier offset-0x64 result follows.

The final indirect transfer receives:

1. numeric constant 0x72;
2. the word captured through offset 0x64 from the freshly read offset-4 base;
3. the doubled-link offset-0x14 word captured after the fixed-address 0x3a write;
4. the later word captured through offset 0x74 from that offset-4 base.

9. **Mixed widths:** the later word store starting at adjusted-base offset 0x10 can replace the earlier byte there. Modeling both as independent fields is unacceptable.
