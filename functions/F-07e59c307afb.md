# F-07e59c307afb

## Purpose

F-07e59c307afb performs an in-place transformation of selected fields in a caller-supplied memory region. The visible operations reverse the byte order of selected 32-bit values, transform one 16-bit value, and construct four eight-byte results through an external dependency.

The apparent intent is endian conversion of a structured record. That intent is inferred, not an established record format: field names, protocol, conversion direction, and the external dependency’s implementation are unavailable.

Under the conditional assumption that the dependency synchronously copies exactly the requested bytes without other effects, each eight-byte transformation reverses all eight bytes. The routine is not established to have that behavior independently of the dependency.

## Interface

**Input:** One address identifying mutable storage.

**Output:** No explicit return value. Results are written into the supplied storage.

**Widths and extent:** The supplied representation uses four-byte unsigned words, two-byte unsigned halfwords, and eight-byte temporary regions. All offsets below are byte offsets from the input address.

The highest directly accessed word occupies offsets 76–79. Thus, at least 80 accessible bytes are required for the visible record accesses. Dependency accesses may impose additional requirements that are not established here.

### Preconditions

The caller must provide storage that supports the visible reads and writes. There is no null check, length check, alignment check, or record validation.

The address must satisfy the execution environment’s requirements for four-byte accesses. Two-byte access is also required at offset 40. The representation does not establish support for unaligned input.

For ordinary deterministic record conversion, the caller must also exclude concurrent mutation of the accessed storage. The eight-byte operations use repeated observations rather than a single snapshot.

### Ownership and lifetime

The routine operates on the supplied storage without explicitly allocating, replacing, or releasing it. No ownership-transfer operation is visible in its own body.

Temporary destinations and temporary result sources are passed to the external dependency. Their storage is local to the invocation and is not a persistent output. Safe use requires the dependency to complete relevant accesses before those temporaries cease to exist.

The dependency’s pointer-retention, memory-release, and ownership behavior are unknown. The visible argument passing alone does not establish that it borrows memory without retaining it.

## Behavior

### Overall mutation order

The visible sequence is:

1. Transform the four-byte fields at offsets 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, and 24, in that order.
2. Perform the eight-byte operation at offset 28.
3. Perform the halfword write at offset 40.
4. Transform the four-byte fields at offsets 44 and 56, in that order.
5. Perform the eight-byte operations at offsets 60 and 68, in that order.
6. Transform the four-byte field at offset 76.
7. Perform the eight-byte operation at offset 48.
8. Return without an explicit result.

Offset order is therefore not processing order. In particular, offsets 48–55 are processed after offsets 56–79.

There is no transaction boundary, rollback, or final commit covering the whole record. Earlier mutations have already occurred when later dependency calls execute.

### Four-byte transformations

Each explicitly transformed four-byte field is read, its four constituent bytes are rearranged in reverse order, and the result is written back to the same field before the next listed field is processed.

For a 32-bit numeric value of `0x12345678`, the resulting value is `0x78563412`.

The transformation uses shifts by 8 and 24 bits and byte-selection masks. With 32-bit unsigned arithmetic, bits shifted beyond the word width are discarded, and right shifts introduce zero bits. There is no widening to a 64-bit arithmetic field, sign extension, carry propagation between bytes, or saturation.

The visible operation is unconditional. Symmetric values, including zero and all-one bits, are still subject to the same read-and-write transformation.

### Halfword transformation at offset 40

This operation differs from the four-byte transformations:

- The input is expressed through accesses to the four-byte word beginning at offset 40.
- Only the low 16 numeric bits are used.
- Those two bytes are exchanged.
- The destination write is two bytes at offset 40.

The expression contains two word-access occurrences. Their relative evaluation order and whether they become separate physical loads are not established by the supplied representation. Under stable-memory conditions, both yield the same low halfword.

On a conventional little-endian target, this exchanges the bytes at offsets 40 and 41 and leaves offsets 42 and 43 unchanged.

That byte-level description is conditional on target endianness. On a conventional big-endian target, the low 16 numeric bits of the four-byte read come from offsets 42 and 43, while the two-byte destination remains at offset 40. The operation must therefore not be described unconditionally as exchanging the first two bytes at offset 40.

### Eight-byte dependency-mediated operations

Each eight-byte operation makes nine calls to the same dependency:

- Eight calls pass a temporary destination, the same record-field source address, and the numeric argument 8.
- One final call passes the record-field destination, an assembled temporary source, and the numeric argument 8.

The four operations therefore account for **36 dependency calls**.

The first eight calls use distinct temporary destinations within an operation. The routine selects byte-sized contributions from their two four-byte portions:

| Source portion examined | Contribution selected, in call order |
|---|---|
| First four bytes | Low byte, second byte, third byte, high byte |
| Second four bytes | Low byte, second byte, third byte, high byte |

Each contribution is placed in the opposite byte position within its output word. The assembled result places the transformed second word before the transformed first word.

If all eight calls provide identical copies of an unchanged eight-byte source, this is equivalent to reversing the complete eight-byte sequence. It is not merely reversing bytes independently inside two words: the word positions are exchanged as well.

For example, under that copy assumption, bytes `01 23 45 67 89 AB CD EF` become `EF CD AB 89 67 45 23 01`.

### Repeated observations and timing

The eight source calls must not be collapsed into one conceptual read when describing behavior without dependency assumptions.

If different calls observe different source contents, the output may combine contributions from different observations. The routine does not validate that the source remained stable or that the temporary results agree.

There is also a visible timing difference between groups:

- At offsets 28, 60, and 68, the first seven selected contributions are calculated after their respective calls and before subsequent calls. The eighth contribution is obtained after the eighth call, and the result is then assembled.
- At offset 48, all eight dependency calls occur before the selected contributions are calculated from the temporary regions.

That distinction can matter if the dependency retains temporary addresses and modifies earlier destinations during later calls. Such behavior is neither established nor ruled out by the supplied material.

The final writeback in every group is itself a dependency call. A successful record write cannot be inferred merely from the preceding assembly of the temporary result.

## Side effects

### Directly established effects

The routine explicitly writes the listed four-byte fields and a two-byte destination at offset 40. It also passes the four eight-byte record regions as writeback destinations to the dependency.

Within the first 80 bytes, offsets 20–23, 36–39, and 42–43 receive no explicit write from the routine’s own field assignments. Offsets 42–43 are nevertheless included in the word access at offset 40.

These are not unconditional preservation guarantees. The dependency could have additional effects, including effects on storage outside its apparent destination.

No explicit global access, allocation, release, logging, or I/O appears in the routine’s own body. None of those absences establishes that the dependency lacks such effects.

### Partial completion and errors

No explicit error value, exception handling, status branch, or recovery path is visible. Dependency return values, if any, are not inspected.

If an access faults or a dependency call does not return normally, the routine provides no visible rollback. Mutations completed before that point may remain observable.

A dependency that returns normally without populating the required temporary bytes leaves the subsequent arithmetic without an established initialized input. The routine contains no fallback for that case.

### Concurrency

The operation is not atomic at record level. Other observers may see a mixture of transformed and untransformed fields.

The repeated dependency calls also prevent an unconditional snapshot guarantee for each eight-byte field. No locking or synchronization is visible.

## Acceptance examples

These are static, conditional acceptance examples. No runtime tests have been performed.

### Selected four-byte field

Given an accessible record and a stable four-byte value `0x12345678` at offset 12, the value at that offset is `0x78563412` immediately after its direct transformation.

Its final value at return is also `0x78563412` only if subsequent dependency activity does not modify it.

### Eight-byte reversal under ordinary copying

Assume F-EXT-01 synchronously copies exactly eight bytes, has no other effects, and the record remains stable except for this routine’s writes.

If offsets 28–35 initially contain `01 23 45 67 89 AB CD EF`, they contain `EF CD AB 89 67 45 23 01` after the corresponding writeback.

The same conditional reversal applies independently to offsets 48–55, 60–67, and 68–75, in the processing order specified above.

### Halfword behavior on a little-endian target

Given little-endian execution and initial bytes `12 34 56 78` at offsets 40–43, the direct halfword operation produces `34 12 56 78`.

The unchanged final two bytes follow from the two-byte write width, not from an absence of reads covering them.

### Halfword behavior on a big-endian target

Given conventional big-endian execution and initial bytes `12 34 56 78` at offsets 40–43, the word’s low halfword is `0x5678`. Its exchanged value is `0x7856`, written as two bytes at offset 40.

The resulting four bytes are therefore `78 56 56 78`, assuming stable reads. This example distinguishes the visible expression from a target-independent two-byte exchange.

### Preservation outside selected fields

Under the ordinary-copy, no-other-effects dependency assumption, bytes at offsets 20–23, 36–39, and 42–43 retain their initial values.

Without that assumption, only the absence of explicit writes by the routine itself is established.

### Repeated application

With stable storage and ordinary-copy behavior, every four-byte and eight-byte reversal restores its initial bytes after two invocations.

On a little-endian target, the halfword exchange also restores its initial bytes. Thus the complete record returns to its initial contents under those conditions. This involution is inferred from the transformations, not experimentally verified.

### Interrupted processing

If the first dependency call for offset 28 fails to return normally, the six preceding four-byte transformations have already occurred. The routine does not defer those writes until dependency success or restore them afterward.

## Uncertainties

The following remain **documented_only**:

- The record’s identity, field meanings, and intended conversion direction.
- The actual target byte order and alignment rules.
- The authoritative identity and complete contract of F-EXT-01.
- Dependency effects on globals, ownership, pointer lifetime, and unrelated memory.
- Runtime fault behavior and error-reporting conventions.
- Concurrent-observer behavior beyond the visible lack of synchronization.
- Exact physical load/store counts and instruction-level atomicity.

Some temporary words are represented as signed values before a left shift by 24 bits. The apparent operation selects and relocates their low byte. However, signed left-shift semantics can be problematic in a portable high-level-language interpretation, especially for negative inputs or results outside the signed range.

The eight-byte reversal interpretation uses fixed-width 32-bit bit-pattern behavior. The supplied material does not independently establish how the original execution environment resolves every signed-shift case. This uncertainty does not justify silently substituting a portable-language guarantee.
