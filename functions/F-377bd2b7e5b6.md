# F-377bd2b7e5b6

## Purpose

F-377bd2b7e5b6 mutates memory reached through explicit arguments, implicit entry state, global values, and successive memory reads. After those mutations, it transfers control to an unresolved target with three visible arguments.

The established behavior is an ordered sequence of raw-memory accesses. The evidence does not establish an application-level data structure, text format, allocation protocol, or success result.

The four explicit arguments are insufficient to determine the behavior: additional entry values are consumed without visible initialization. This contract describes the supplied static representation, not a verified executable implementation.

No scalar result is explicitly returned. Outputs are memory effects and the terminal transfer.

Normal completion requires every reached address to support its requested access width and the terminal target to accept the actual calling state. No visible validation establishes accessibility, alignment, bounds, lifetime, or target validity.

## Behavior

### Interpretation and ordering

A **byte**, **halfword**, and **word** mean 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Unless explicitly qualified otherwise, reads and stores below are word accesses. A byte or halfword derived from a wider value retains its low 8 or 16 bits.

Bold descriptive names identify captured values or addresses for this document only. They do not imply types, ownership, distinct allocations, or non-aliasing. Once captured, a named value is not implicitly refreshed.

Within each phase, accesses occur in the order described; phases are ordered. A specified reread is a new access, even when it uses the same address as an earlier read.

Unsigned 32-bit arithmetic, including doubling the unsigned address used for the variable-shift read, is modulo `2^32` in the represented arithmetic. Signed address addition and doubling do not independently establish machine wraparound semantics. Byte order, alignment rules, and physical access atomicity are unknown.

### Entry writes and initial captures

The **combined address** is the third argument plus the implicit displacement. It first receives the low halfword of that displacement. Twice the second argument then receives the implicit halfword payload. The implicit base plus `0x45` receives byte `0x45`.

Next, the entry-base global is captured as the **entry base**, followed by the entry-arithmetic global, whose low halfword is interpreted as the represented 16-bit arithmetic value.

A word read from the combined address supplies a base whose offset `0x45` receives byte `0x45`. The combined address then receives the displacement’s low halfword again.

The entry base at offset `0x45` receives a halfword sum of:

- The implicit 16-bit arithmetic operand.
- The narrowed entry-arithmetic value.
- One if the unresolved target compares **strictly less than address `0x46`**, otherwise zero.

The result is narrowed to 16 bits. Equality with `0x46` contributes zero. The architectural meaning of that address comparison remains unknown; the strict predicate itself is explicit.

A second word read from the combined address captures the **entry record**. Its offset `0x45` receives word `0x45`. Only afterward does the entry base at offset `0x45` receive the fourth argument as a byte.

### Establishing the first traversal

A word at offset `0x14` from the third argument becomes the **seed**, and is stored at displacement-plus-`0x34`.

The entry base’s offset `0x74` supplies the **parent**; the entry record’s offset `0x14` supplies the **initial peer**. The parent receives the entry record at offset `0x54`; the initial peer receives its own address at offset `0x54`; the parent receives the seed at offset `0x44`. The entry record’s byte at offset `9` receives the parent’s low byte.

The parent’s offset `0x64` supplies the **bridge**. The bridge’s offset `0x14` supplies the **unsigned base**, interpreted as unsigned 32-bit. At unsigned-base offset `9`, two byte stores occur: first the initial peer’s low byte, then the bridge’s low byte.

**Immediately after that second byte store**, the initial-payload global is captured as the **initial payload**. This read precedes the subsequent read of the seed’s offset `0x14`, which supplies the **stem**. The stem plus `0x20` is the captured **advanced stem**.

A read at unsigned-base offset `0x64` supplies a destination whose offset `0x14` receives the parent. The seed’s byte at offset `0x11` receives the advanced stem’s low byte.

### Shift-derived bytes and transition to the child

The parent’s offset `0x44` is read twice. The first capture is the **first parent link**; the second supplies the **working record**. These are distinct reads.

A byte read at twice the unsigned base is promoted to signed 32-bit, shifted right by the unsigned base’s low eight bits, and narrowed to a byte: the **shift byte**. Negative-byte interpretation depends on the target’s plain-character signedness. Negative right shifts and counts of 32 or greater have no portable result established here.

The working record’s offset `8` receives the shift byte. The parent’s offset `0x74` receives the first parent link, and its byte at offset `0x11` receives the shift byte.

The seed’s offset `0x54` receives the advanced stem, then the initial payload. Its byte at offset `0x19` receives the advanced stem’s low byte. The working record’s offset `0x44` receives the parent, and the stem’s offset `0x74` receives the advanced stem.

The working record’s offset `0x14` supplies the **follower**. The parent’s byte at offset `0x11` receives the initial payload’s low byte. A word read at parent offset `0x14` supplies the byte stored at follower offset `0x15`. A separate reread of parent offset `0x14` supplies the **child**.

### Terminal arithmetic and transfer

The terminal-arithmetic global is captured as the **terminal arithmetic value**. Hub offset `0x74` then supplies the **terminal field**.

The following accesses occur in order:

1. A halfword store at terminal-field-plus-hub, using the terminal field’s low halfword.
2. A halfword store at twice the terminal doubled base, using the terminal arithmetic value’s low halfword.
3. A byte read at terminal-byte-offset-plus-hub, converted directly to unsigned 32-bit as the **terminal byte value**.
4. A halfword store at terminal-arithmetic-value-plus-terminal-field, using the terminal field’s low halfword.
5. A repetition of the halfword store at terminal-field-plus-hub.
6. A word `0x45` store at terminal-byte-value-plus-`0x45`.

The terminal-address global is then captured, followed by the terminal-payload global, followed by hub offset `0x44` as the **terminal argument field**.

The final stores are byte `0x44` at terminal-byte-value-plus-`0x44`, the captured terminal payload as a word at twice the captured terminal address, and the terminal address’s low halfword at terminal-offset-base-plus-terminal-address.

The unresolved target receives:

- The terminal byte value bitwise ORed with `0x44`.
- The captured terminal address.
- The terminal argument field.

The terminal argument field is captured before the final stores; it is not reread for the transfer.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance cases, not executed tests.

### Strict comparison contribution

Given implicit arithmetic operand `-1` and narrowed global arithmetic value `2`, the early arithmetic halfword is `2` when the target compares less than `0x46`, and `1` otherwise. A target equal to `0x46` belongs to the latter case.

### Initial-payload read timing

If initial-payload global storage overlaps the byte just written at unsigned-base offset `9`, an ordinary-memory interpretation must account for the second byte store before capturing that global. Moving the global read before either store, or after the subsequent seed-field read, does not preserve the specified order.

### Older and newer parent words

If intervening child writes cause the two parent-offset-`0x64` captures to differ, the parent-offset-`9` byte store uses the older capture. The child-offset-`9` byte store at that phase uses the newer capture. Later word stores to parent offsets `0x44`, `0x64`, and `0x34` use older, newer, and newer respectively.

These requirements describe particular stores, not final-memory values after every possible alias.

### Negative terminal byte

If the terminal byte is interpreted as signed and reads as `-1`, unsigned conversion produces `0xFFFFFFFF`. The first transfer argument remains `0xFFFFFFFF`.

Unsigned 32-bit addition places the associated word-`0x45` destination at address `0x44`, and the final byte-`0x44` destination at address `0x43`. Accessibility and successful completion are not implied.

### Unsupported shift count

If the shift count is 32 or greater, the result is **documented_only**. Count masking, saturation, and a zero result must not be invented.

### Interrupted execution
