# F-d664a431a762

## Purpose

F-d664a431a762 accepts four 32-bit words, performs an ordered chain of unresolved indirect invocations, and then mutates memory through fixed and computed addresses before a final indirect transfer.

**Established static evidence:** observable effects include mixed-width accesses, repeated overwrites, readbacks, address derivation from loaded values, and fixed-address storage updates. The operation has no declared return value.

**Inferred intent:** some accesses resemble interconnected-record updates; some constants coincide with character codes. Neither interpretation establishes a record-management, formatting, serialization, or text-output contract.

This contract describes represented behavior, not a proven executable replacement. Dependency semantics, some machine arithmetic, and access validity remain **documented_only**. Every later effect is conditional on preceding calls and accesses completing.

### Initial computed-memory effects

The direct-memory region begins with a word store at offset `0x14` through an upper-result-derived base, a byte store at another base’s offset `9`, and a word store at offset `0x54` through a scaled lower-result-derived base. The offset-9 byte is then overwritten.

Subsequent clears and readbacks are ordered effects, not bulk initialization:

- A derived base’s offset-`0x14` word is cleared before its offset-5 unsigned byte is read.
- A small computed base’s offset-`0x14` word is cleared before the saved byte is widened into an offset-`0x34` word.
- The first cleared word is read back before another small-base clear and two successive byte stores at offset `0x15`.

The small address source selects bits with `0x7FFF` after an eight-position left shift, then shifts right six positions. Under bit-vector assumptions it ranges from zero through 508 in multiples of four. Its doubled base ranges from zero through 1016 in multiples of eight.

A later fixed read at `0x5` supplies a value converted to a 32-bit unsigned working base. **The read width and source extension behavior are not established:** the fixed object’s declaration is absent. This access must not be characterized as an established byte read.

Through that converted base, an offset-`0x54` word is read and saved before replacement. Call the saved word the **saved record word**. Later effects include:

- Following offset-`0x74` words and writing through the resulting values.
- Repeated self-reference stores at offset `0x24`.
- Repeated byte writes at offsets `9` and `0x15`.
- Replacing offset-`0x14` words with shifted values and the saved record word.
- Deriving two-, four-, and eight-times-scaled bases from saved values.

Nested byte-derived addressing also occurs: an unsigned byte read at offset `5` becomes the base for an offset-`0x74` word read. This is not a full-width pointer load.

Further mutations use offsets `0x0D`, `0x14`, `0x44`, `0x54`, and `0x64`. Reads are interleaved with overwrites, including repeated offset-5 byte reads. Those reads cannot be assumed equal if intervening writes alias their storage.

### Address adjustment and constant-valued stores

For this phase, define these descriptive quantities:

- **Original working address:** four times the saved record word.
- **Adjusted working address:** the original working address minus decimal 150.
- **Adjusted base:** four times the adjusted working address.
- **Linked value:** the word reached through the later byte-derived offset-`0x74` read.
- **Doubled linked base:** twice that linked value.
- **Quadrupled linked base:** four times that linked value.

These names describe retained arithmetic values, not validated objects.

An 8-bit `0x3A` is written through the quadrupled linked base. The working address is then reduced by 150, before a 16-bit `0x3A` through eight times the linked value and a 32-bit `0x3A` at the quadrupled linked base’s offset `0x20`.

The adjusted base is derived from the **subtracted** value. Its offset-`0x20` word receives, in order:

1. `0x20`.
2. `0x2F`.
3. The zero-extended byte read at the adjusted base’s offset `4`.
4. `0x20`.

The fixed root read and fixed `0x60` write follow this sequence.

### Repeated-update region

### Late fixed storage and saved reads

Immediately after the repeated run, the visible read/write order is:

1. A 32-bit read at fixed address `0xA9`, retained as the **saved fixed word**.
2. A 32-bit read at the quadrupled linked base’s offset `0x74`.
3. An 8-bit write at fixed address `0x3A`.
4. A saved 32-bit read at the doubled linked base’s offset `0x14`.
5. A saved 32-bit read at the adjusted base’s offset `4`.

The byte written to `0x3A` derives from the adjusted working address, not an unresolved call. Under the stated arithmetic assumptions, it is the low byte of eight times that address’s low byte plus `0x30`. The subtraction of 150 therefore affects both subsequent computed destinations and this fixed byte value.

Next, byte stores affect the doubled linked base’s offset `0x0D` and the adjusted base’s offsets `8`, `0x0D`, and `9`; offset `8` receives `0x6C`. A word self-reference is stored at the doubled linked base’s offset `0x54`.

The doubled linked base’s offset-`0x44` word is read **before** a distinct 8-bit read of fixed `0x67` supplies an 8-bit write to fixed `0x21`. Thus even this first read of `0x67` is not an established reuse of its earlier written value: the intervening computed accesses may alias fixed storage.

### Remaining mutations and final transfer

The saved offset-`0x44` word supplies a byte at the adjusted base’s offset `0x10`, a word at the root’s offset `0x54`, and the late variable-count right shift stored as a byte at the doubled linked base’s offset `1`. The root’s offset-`0x54` word is subsequently replaced with the adjusted base.

A word read at the adjusted base’s offset `0x40` is retained before the root’s offset-`0x74` receives the previously saved offset-4 word. The retained offset-`0x40` value then replaces the adjusted base’s offset-`0x10` word, potentially overlapping its earlier byte store. A root offset-`0x44` word store follows.

The subsequent fixed-byte reads are separate events:

- A second read of `0x67` supplies a byte write at the adjusted base’s offset `9`.
- After a byte write at the doubled linked base’s offset `9`, a word write of the saved fixed word at the adjusted base’s offset `0x44`, and a word read at its offset `0x54`, a third read of `0x67` supplies another byte write at offset `9`.

No immutable saved-byte interpretation is established for these three reads.

The word read at adjusted offset `0x54` becomes another destination base. A byte from the saved quadrupled-linked offset-`0x74` word is written through it. The doubled linked base’s offset-`0x54` word is replaced with its saved offset-`0x14` word.

A new word is read at adjusted offset `0x14` before further byte updates and replacement of adjusted offset `0x44`. A word read at doubled-linked offset `4` supplies a late base through which a halfword `0x65` is stored at twice that base.

The tail reads that late base’s offsets `0x64` and `0x74`, stores a byte derived from the saved adjusted offset-`0x14` word, and writes word `100` at another base’s offset `0x54`. A word read at doubled-linked offset `0x64` supplies a further destination base. The late byte at fixed `0x3A` is `0x00`: eight times `250` plus `0x30` is `2048`, whose low byte is zero. Using the unadjusted working address would not satisfy this criterion.
