### Field extraction and bounded values

- A 32-bit value shifted left by 25 and subsequently left by seven becomes zero.
- A value masked to 20 bits and shifted right by ten lies between 0 and 1023.

### Entry into direct mutation

The following descriptive labels identify values, not object types:

- **Primary base:** an earlier dependency result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Secondary base:** twice the bounded index.
- **Shifted base:** a separate result shifted left by 25.
- **Final pair:** the low and high result words from the last paired-result invocation.

The initial accesses preserve several ordering relationships.

A word store at final-high-word offset 0x14 receives the shifted base. Next, shifted-base offset 9 receives the low byte of the final high word. A word store at offset 0x54 from the final low word multiplied by 256 receives the primary base. Only afterward is shifted-base offset 9 overwritten with the shifted base’s own low byte.

Primary-base offset 0x14 is then cleared before an offset-5 byte read. Secondary-base offset 0x14 is cleared before the saved byte is stored, zero-extended, at primary-base offset 0x34.

A subsequent word read of primary-base offset 0x14 supplies two separate byte stores to secondary-base offset 0x15, with another secondary-base offset-0x14 clear preceding those stores.

The word read cannot generally be replaced with a constant zero solely because an earlier access cleared its nominal location. Intervening overlapping stores, memory semantics, or external modification can affect the observation.

### Final saved state and global-byte publication

After the repeated region, the operation saves the 32-bit word read at fixed address 0xA9. Call this the **saved publication word**. This is a potentially unaligned word read.

The operation also saves a word from the preceding four-scaled value’s offset 0x74, writes a scaled-plus-0x30 byte to address 0x3A, and saves words from a doubled derived base’s offset 0x14 and the four-scaled base’s offset 4.

For the remaining description:

- **Final field base** is that four-scaled base.
- **Doubled base** is the doubled derived base retained into this region.
- **Repeated base** is the earlier saved global base.

Before publication, the operation writes low-byte fragments at doubled-base offset 0x0D and final-field-base offsets 0x0D and 9, writes 0x6C at final-field-base offset 8, and writes the doubled base into its own offset-0x54 word field.

The low byte of the saved publication word is then stored at fixed address 0x67.

**Address 0x21 does not receive a second direct extraction from the saved publication word.** Between publication and that assignment, the operation reads a word at doubled-base offset 0x44. The address-0x21 assignment then performs a fresh byte read of address 0x67 and stores that observed byte at address 0x21.

Thus, publication and propagation are separate memory events. Equality with the original saved low byte requires additional assumptions about intervening memory behavior.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

### Initial argument transformation

With a third argument of 1 and conventional 32-bit shifts, the first invocation receives 0x800 in both visible argument positions.

Third arguments sharing their low 21 bits produce the same first-call pair under truncating shifts. This does not establish equivalent complete executions or equivalent hidden inputs.

### Zero extension and bounded index

An offset-5 byte observation of 0xFF supplies 255 to its corresponding later word store, not a sign-extended negative value.

If the relevant index-producing result has low seven bits 0x7F, the bounded index is 508 and the secondary base is 1016 under the stated arithmetic model.

### Repeated-region base ordering

If execution reaches the repeated region, its global-base observation precedes the word store of 0x20 at address 0x60. A trace placing that observation afterward violates the represented ordering, even if both traces happen to obtain the same value.

### Publication is not cached propagation

If the saved publication word has low byte 0xA5, the publication store writes 0xA5 to address 0x67.

If address 0x67 instead yields 0x3C when the subsequent address-0x21 assignment reads it, address 0x21 receives 0x3C. Reusing 0xA5 would violate that read-dependent behavior.

### Intervening alias changes a later reload

Suppose the first global-sourced offset-9 store reads 0xA5 from address 0x67. If the intervening doubled-base offset-9 store aliases address 0x67 and writes 0x18, with no subsequent overlapping modification before the second reload, the second global-sourced offset-9 store receives 0x18.

This example requires two distinct offset-9 reloads. It does not posit an unsupported third one.

### Write history and failure

An observer capable of distinguishing successive stores must retain the separate offset-0x20 writes of 0x20, 0x2F, the byte-derived word, and 0x20.

If a later access fails, previously completed stores have no represented rollback guarantee.

### Final transfer

If all earlier operations complete and the final target is callable, its first visible argument is 0x72. Ordinary completion additionally requires that transfer to return.
