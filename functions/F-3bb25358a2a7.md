# F-3bb25358a2a7

## Purpose

F-3bb25358a2a7 derives six word-sized outputs from three ordered endpoint pairs and writes those outputs, followed by one cleared byte, into caller-provided storage.

Each pair selects a coefficient. Equal endpoints select zero without calling the coefficient-producing dependency. Unequal endpoints cause a call to F-dc13818ac82a with the second endpoint minus the first. An unconditional call to F-3d52feb78d54 precedes all three selections.

The output locations, coefficient doubling, and product scaling suggest a three-axis fixed-point transformation. This is **inferred intent**, not an established mathematical contract. In particular, the supplied body does not establish that F-dc13818ac82a computes a reciprocal or that the destination is a matrix.

The arithmetic requires two distinct qualifications:

- The apparent extraction of product bits 16–47 includes a **signed left shift**. The bit-slice interpretation is conditional where that shift, or the preceding conversion to a signed high word, lacks independently established behavior.
- The first two translation-like outputs negate an **unsigned 32-bit expression**. That negation is modular and does not itself incur signed-negation overflow. Conversion of its result into the signed destination word is a separate issue.

This contract is based solely on static evidence. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Product construction

The first two translation-like calculations form signed 64-bit products:

- Rₐ multiplied by the signed 32-bit sum A₁ + A₀.
- Rᵦ multiplied by the signed 32-bit sum B₁ + B₀.

The third forms a signed 64-bit product of R𝒸 and the signed 32-bit negation of C₁ + C₀.

In each case, the endpoint sum is evaluated at signed word width before widening. For the third pair, negation also occurs before widening.

The 64-bit product can represent the product of any two valid signed 32-bit operands. This does not make an overflowing sum or pre-widening negation valid.

### Extraction: established operations and conditional interpretation

For each product, the supplied expression combines two components:

1. The product is converted to unsigned 64-bit form and shifted right by 32 positions. That result is converted to a signed 32-bit high word, which is then shifted left by 16 positions.
2. The product is converted to unsigned 32-bit form, retaining its low word, which is shifted right by 16 positions.

The components are combined by a bitwise OR.

The second component has unsigned 32-bit type. Consequently, the OR has unsigned 32-bit type under the represented width and conversion model. The signed shifted component is converted to unsigned form for that combination.

The low-word component has a straightforward bit interpretation: it places product bits 16–31 in result bits 0–15.

The high-word component requires qualification:

- A high-word value exceeding the signed 32-bit range does not have an independently established signed conversion result.
- A negative signed high word is outside portable defined signed-left-shift behavior.
- A nonnegative signed high word can also have an unrepresentable shifted value. At 32-bit width, high words greater than 32,767 exceed the defined signed range when shifted left by 16.

Therefore, valid endpoint arithmetic and a representable 64-bit multiplication do **not** suffice to establish portable extraction behavior.

For products whose signed high-word conversion is representable and whose high word lies between 0 and 32,767 inclusive, the extraction is established as the unsigned word containing product bits 16–47. In other cases, this same bit-slice interpretation is **conditional on the target’s conversion and shift semantics**.

Call that conditional bit slice E(P), for product P. It retains bits 16–47 and discards bits 0–15 and 48–63. It is not truncation toward zero. Under ordinary two’s-complement, bit-preserving target behavior, E(−1) is the all-ones word; that negative-product result is not an unconditional portable consequence of the supplied expression.

### Translation negation and destination conversion

For the first two translation-like outputs, the extraction expression is negated **after** the OR.

Because the OR is unsigned 32-bit, this negation is unsigned arithmetic modulo 2³². In particular:

- Negating zero produces zero.
- Negating unsigned one produces 4,294,967,295.
- Negating the bit pattern 2,147,483,648 produces the same bit pattern.

The last operation is not signed-negation overflow. Whether the extraction that produced that pattern was itself defined is a separate question.

The unsigned negation result is then assigned to a signed destination word. Results greater than 2,147,483,647 require an unsigned-to-signed conversion whose target behavior is not independently established. A signed numerical interpretation such as −1 requires an additional conversion assumption.

The third translation-like output has no post-extraction negation. Its OR result is assigned directly to the signed destination word, with the same conversion qualification for out-of-range unsigned values.
