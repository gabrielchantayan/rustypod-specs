# F-0427cf7551c6

## Purpose

F-0427cf7551c6 performs an ordered chain of unresolved indirect calls, followed by mixed-width memory mutations and a final unresolved indirect transfer.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Describing the operation as a parser, formatter, allocator, initializer, or collection update would introduce unsupported requirements. Printable constants and recurring offsets do not establish text processing or a conventional object model.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented operations, widths, data dependencies, and ordering.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations that are not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior not resolved sufficiently to specify reliable implementation requirements.

Every later effect is conditional on execution reaching it. An unresolved dependency or unsuccessful memory access can prevent the remaining effects.

### Widths and arithmetic

The principal integer width is 32 bits. Some results are represented as 64-bit quantities whose halves are used separately:

- The low half contains the least significant 32 bits.
- The high half contains the most significant 32 bits.
- Whole-result logical right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 correspond to shifting the high half right by two, four, and twelve.

These relationships do not establish memory byte order.

Argument construction includes masking, shifting, and extraction of fields of several widths. Established examples include retaining a low 24-bit field before shifting left by six, retaining a low 22-bit field before shifting left by seven or eight, extracting fields from the low 24 bits, and placing an extracted single bit at position 16.

Unsigned right shifts are logical. Signed left-shift behavior, signed multiplication overflow, address wraparound, and a late variable-count signed right shift require environmental qualification.

Under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, truncation occurs at each 32-bit operation. Unlimited-precision algebra is not interchangeable: a word shifted left by 28 and then left by another 28 becomes zero under that interpretation.

Byte writes retain eight bits; halfword writes retain sixteen. Explicitly unsigned byte loads used as address components are zero-extended.

### Entry into direct memory mutation

The following descriptive terms identify captured values, not objects:

- **Wide result:** the final represented 64-bit dependency result.
- **Shifted base:** an earlier result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** a different earlier result multiplied by 33,554,432.
- **Shifted payload:** another earlier result shifted left by eight.
- **Small index:** a result shifted left by eight, masked to fifteen bits, then logically shifted right by six.
- **Small base:** twice the small index.

The small index can take multiples of four from zero through 508. The small base can take multiples of eight from zero through 1016. Consequently, the word access at small base plus 20 is four-byte aligned under ordinary integer addressing, although its validity remains unestablished. This does not establish alignment of other computed word accesses.

The opening mutations have these ordering requirements:

- A word write at the wide result’s high half plus 20 precedes a byte write at shifted base plus 9.
- A word write at the wide result’s low half multiplied by 256, plus 84, follows that byte write.
- Shifted base plus 9 is written again, this time with the shifted base’s low byte.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static deductions, not executed tests.

1. **Opening transformation.** With first input 1, the opening call’s first represented argument is 256. The second and third explicit inputs are unchanged.

2. **Small-index boundary.** A feeding result of 127 produces small index 508 and small base 1016. A feeding result of 128 produces zero. The transformation is not unrestricted multiplication by four.

3. **Overlapping clear.** If all accesses complete, clearing small base plus 20 precedes both byte writes at small base plus 21. Acceptance must preserve the separate widths and events, not merely a final word value.

4. **Read after clear.** With ordinary memory and no intervening aliasing write, the scaled-base offset-20 reread yields zero. The subsequent write based on that value targets address 84.

5. **Coincident decimal-58 destinations.** If the loaded scale value is zero and address-zero accesses are supported, the byte and halfword stores both begin at zero. The word store begins at 32. Rejecting this case merely because the first two expressions were assumed distinct changes the contract.

6. **Intermediate constant writes.** An observer capable of distinguishing the word writes at field base plus 32 sees 32, then 47, then the widened byte read, then 32, provided execution reaches each event.

7. **Stable address 103.** If the assigned byte is 7 and address 103 remains unchanged until all three rereads, those rereads supply 7 to address 33 and to the two later field-base offset-9 writes. The actual reads remain required by the represented sequence.

8. **Changed address 103.** If an intervening aliasing write changes byte 103 before the second reread, that reread uses the changed byte. The third reread independently supplies its then-current value. The later overwrite of field base plus 9 with the final node’s low byte does not erase these earlier events.

9. **Nonreturning dependency.** If an early dependency does not return, later direct stores and the final transfer are not required to occur.
