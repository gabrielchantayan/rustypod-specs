### Widths and arithmetic

Byte, halfword, and word accesses are respectively 8, 16, and 32 bits. Selected call results occupy represented 64-bit containers.

Extracting the upper word of a 64-bit result is distinct from extracting its low word. Unsigned shifts of such containers by 34, 36, and 44 positions select different high-order subsets; they must not be replaced by shifts of the low 32 bits.

Unsigned right shifts zero-fill. Byte loads widened to words are zero-extended. Byte stores retain the low eight bits; halfword stores retain the low sixteen bits.

Masks select fields including 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, and 24 bits. Their placement relative to shifts matters. For example:

- A low-24-bit field shifted right by 20 is between 0 and 15.
- The same field shifted right by 19 is between 0 and 31.
- A low-22-bit field shifted left by eight occupies at most 30 bits.
- A low-19-bit field shifted left by two occupies at most 21 bits.

Unsigned 32-bit computations use their represented width. Expressions represented as signed shifts, multiplication, or address arithmetic do not independently establish portable overflow behavior. Where this contract discusses truncating machine arithmetic, that interpretation is conditional: each 32-bit intermediate retains only its low 32 bits, rather than using unlimited precision.

Consequently, two successive left shifts by 28 produce zero under that model. They are not an unlimited-precision shift by 56.

A late signed-word right shift uses the low eight bits of a derived base as its count. Counts of at least 32 have unresolved target semantics. Right-shifting a negative signed value is also not given an unconditional arithmetic or logical interpretation here.
