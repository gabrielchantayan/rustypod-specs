- Assigning an explicitly narrowed 8-bit value to a fixed-location global establishes the supplied value's truncation, not an 8-bit destination store.

By contrast, explicit unsigned-byte-pointer loads establish 8-bit reads and zero-extension when widened. Explicit computed byte and halfword stores retain eight and 16 bits respectively.

Under a conditional 32-bit bit-vector interpretation:

- The fourth argument's first shift retains only its low four bits.
- Shifting that intermediate left another four positions produces zero.
- The subsequent extraction of bits 10–19 from that intermediate also produces zero.
- An eight-position left shift followed by an unsigned right shift loses the original high byte.
- Scaling, subtraction, and address calculations retain only their low 32 bits.

These are conditional arithmetic consequences, not proof of the machine semantics of every signed-looking expression. Signed overflow, negative left operands, and address wrapping remain **documented_only**.

A late right shift operates on a signed-represented word with a count taken from an address value's low eight bits. Negative operands and counts outside 0–31 have unresolved machine semantics.
