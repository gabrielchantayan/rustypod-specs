## Behavior

### Widths, layouts, and arithmetic

A byte access is 8 bits, a halfword access is 16 bits, and a word access is 32 bits. Dependency results represented as 64 bits are divided into a low 32-bit word and a high 32-bit word.

For readability:

- A **slot selected by an index** is a word location at four times that index, plus 20.
- A **selector write** first reads an unsigned byte at the supplied index plus 1, then writes a byte at that sampled byte plus 17. The write destination is therefore between 17 and 272 inclusive.
- A **field-byte copy at a base** reads a word at base plus 116 and writes its low byte at base plus 1.

These are address descriptions, not established object layouts. In particular, a selector-write destination is not relative to its selector-read address.

Unsigned right shifts are logical. Shifting a 32-bit unsigned value right by 22 leaves at most ten significant bits; shifting right by 23 leaves at most nine. Byte stores retain the low eight bits of their supplied values.

Some displayed scaling and left-shift expressions use signed types. Their portable signed-language behavior is not established for negative operands or overflowing results. Claims using wrapping arithmetic below are explicitly conditional on a 32-bit machine-bit interpretation; they do not establish that every displayed signed expression is defined.

There are no displayed conditional branches or loops. Calls may nevertheless branch internally, alter memory, fail, or not return. Intermediate calls remain part of the ordered path even where their arguments or results are not material to a direct access described below.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Byte truncation:** If the early byte-producing result is 65 and its selector byte is 7, the first selector write stores 4 at address 24. Shifting 65 left by two gives 260; the byte store retains 4.

2. **Upper-ten-bit extraction:** An all-ones fourth input yields 1023 after its logical right shift by 22. The corresponding selector read is at address 1024. A selector byte of 255 selects destination 272.

3. **Different word addresses:** A secondary index of 8 in the initial word-propagation phase causes a slot write at address 52, followed by a word read at address 28. Substituting a readback from address 52 is incorrect.

4. **Forced zero:** Every unsigned 32-bit first global sample becomes zero after successive right shifts by 23 and 23. Stores and selector indices derived from that twice-shifted value must reflect zero.

5. **Three fresh samples:** If the three established address-1 reads yield 2, 9, and 20, their selector destinations are respectively 19, 26, and 37. The retained base is 20. Its later field-byte copy reads at 136 and writes at 21, regardless of subsequent changes to address 1.

6. **Retained word:** A retained field word of hexadecimal 123456AB causes a byte store of hexadecimal AB while later word propagation retains hexadecimal 123456AB.

7. **Shift equivalence:** Under 32-bit machine-bit arithmetic, an underlying later-scale result of hexadecimal 40000001 scales to 4, shifts right by two to 1, and shifts left by 16 to hexadecimal 00010000. Directly shifting the original result left by 16 gives the same low 32 bits. This example does not claim the initial signed scaling is defined.

8. **Final composition:** A final byte result of hexadecimal 7E and a numeric 24-bit quantity of hexadecimal 123456 produce hexadecimal 1234567E in the write global. This is numeric composition, not an asserted endianness.

9. **Partial completion:** If the zero-address call cannot execute, normal return is not required and earlier writes are not undone.
