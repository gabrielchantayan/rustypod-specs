## Interface

### Explicit input and completion

The represented interface accepts one signed 32-bit integer and declares no result.

The input is initially shifted left six positions. Early argument positions apply another six-position left shift to that retained value. Under 32-bit bitvector semantics, those arguments contain the low 32 bits of the original input multiplied by 4096.

No input-range check, saturation, rejection branch, or diagnostic is represented.

Normal completion requires the final indirect target to return. No meaningful return-register value or guarantee of successful termination is established.

## Behavior

### Call-driven phase

The opening phase contains a long sequence of indirect calls interleaved with arithmetic. The initial target depends on incoming control-link state plus displacement 0xffe. Later targets generally combine represented control locations with that displacement.

No explicit loop or conditional branch is represented in this phase. A callee may nevertheless fault, block, modify state, or fail to return.

The sequence is significant: calls with unused results still occur; superficially similar calls are not interchangeable; explicit zeros differ from unresolved register-carried values; and results from different calls retain separate identities.

Arguments repeatedly use shifts, masks, separately extracted result portions, and retained earlier values. Masks select low fields of widths including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. No physical units, encodings, or semantic record fields are established.

Under 32-bit bitvector semantics:

- Two successive six-position left shifts discard the original high twelve bits.
- A left shift by eight followed by a logical right shift by eight retains the original low 24 bits.
- Successive valid left shifts totaling at least 32 positions discard all original bits.
- Scaling before address use may discard high bits.

Near the direct-memory phase, one retained base is a call result multiplied by \(2^{25}\). Another is formed by shifting a different result left eight positions, retaining 15 low bits, shifting right six, and doubling. This second, **small base** ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight. Neither base is established as an allocated-object address.

### Initial direct-memory effects

The first four explicit writes, in order, are:

- A retained shifted result, as a word at offset 0x14 from the high word of the latest represented multiword result.
- The low byte of that high word, at offset 9 from another retained base.
- The multiply-by-\(2^{25}\) base, as a word at offset 0x54 from the latest low result multiplied by 256.
- The low byte of the retained shifted result, at the same offset-9 byte destination used above.

The two writes to that byte destination remain separate effects.

The multiply-derived base’s offset-0x14 word is then cleared, followed by a byte read at its offset 5. The small base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared before the widened byte is written as a word at multiply-derived-base offset 0x34.

A subsequent word read at multiply-derived-base offset 0x14 is an actual read, not an unconditional substitution of the earlier zero. The small base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared again; its offset-0x15 byte then receives the low byte of that read-back word twice consecutively.

### Absolute storage and scaled-state updates

A byte from absolute address 0x05 is zero-extended for use as another base. After that read, absolute address 0x01 receives the low byte of the small base.

The byte-derived base’s offset-0x15 byte receives the low byte of half the small base. The multiply-derived base’s offset-0x11 byte receives the low byte of an earlier retained shifted call result. That retained result is also written as a word through the earlier read-back value at offset 0x54, before the multiply-derived offset-0x11 byte is cleared.

A word is saved from byte-derived-base offset 0x54 before that field is overwritten with the read-back value. This **saved base** supplies later scaling and addressing.

Further updates follow words at offsets including 0x04, 0x14, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. Bytes read at offset 5 also provide zero-extended bases for word reads. Some destinations receive their own bases; others receive scaled values, saved words, or truncated bytes. These accesses do not establish a conventional pointer layout.

Several ordering constraints are explicit:

- Absolute address 0x54 receives a word equal to twice the saved base, between writes to small-base offset 0x14.
- Later, small-base offset 0x15 receives the low byte of four times the saved base twice.
- Only after those two byte stores does absolute address 0x15 receive the low byte of the small base.
- That absolute byte store precedes the following offset-0x54 word update through four times the saved base.

The absolute-address-0x15 destination is distinct from the notation “base plus offset 0x15.”

Nested accesses establish data dependencies, but not necessarily a total evaluation order inside a compound expression. A field used both to obtain a destination base and to supply a stored value must not automatically be treated as one cached read.

### Distinct repeated-region entry

Suppose the saved base is 0x100 and the selected value is 0x20. The adjusted value is 0x36a, the working base is 0xda8, and the quadrupled selected base is 0x80.

After the global-base load and absolute-address-0x60 word store:

- The initial offset-0x54 word destination is 0x854, receiving 0x80.
- This store precedes the first loaded-global-base-offset-0x19 byte store, working-base-offset-0x11 byte store, and loaded-global-base-offset-0x34 word store.
- The first offset-0x34 assignment contains 0x800.
- Subsequent offset-0x54 destinations are 0x1ba4, receiving 0x80; subsequent offset-0x34 assignments contain 0x1b50.

If these destinations are valid and otherwise disjoint, replacing the initial 0x854 store with a store to 0x1ba4 leaves different memory contents and is unacceptable.

### Absolute ordering and disjoint fields

The absolute-address-0x15 byte store follows the two relevant small-base-offset-0x15 byte stores. The global-base load precedes the absolute-address-0x60 word store.

The final-region absolute-address-0xa9 word read precedes the quadrupled-base-offset-0x74 read, which precedes the absolute-address-0x3a byte write.

For working base 0x1000, the intervening byte read is at 0x1004; the four offset-0x20 word writes occupy 0x1020–0x1023. Ordinary address-range overlap cannot make those writes alter that byte.

### Rereads, dependency effects, and completion

If an intervening derived store changes absolute address 0x67, a later explicit read uses the changed byte under ordinary memory semantics. It must not reuse the original low byte saved from 0xa9. Equal read results do not eliminate the represented reads.

If a callee modifies global storage subsequently read here, that read uses the resulting state under the environment’s memory rules.

If all preceding calls return and accesses succeed, the final target receives the four explicit words described above. Normal completion still depends on that target returning.
