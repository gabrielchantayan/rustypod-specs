# F-28771be09d25

## Purpose

F-28771be09d25 computes an integer result by adding a rounded, weighted endpoint displacement to a first endpoint.

**Inferred intent:** interpolation using a signed weight with 28 fractional bits. Unity is \(2^{28}=268{,}435{,}456\); one half is \(2^{27}=134{,}217{,}728\). The function does not restrict the weight to the interval from zero through unity. Negative weights and extrapolation weights are not rejected.

Subject to the arithmetic and conversion conditions below, the numerical relationship is:

\[
R=a+\left\lfloor\frac{(b-a)w+2^{27}}{2^{28}}\right\rfloor,
\]

where \(a\) is the first endpoint, \(b\) is the second endpoint, and \(w\) is the weight.

This equation is not an unconditional signed-result guarantee for every input. Endpoint subtraction can exceed its signed range; displacement reconstruction contains a potentially overflowing signed operation; and unsigned-to-signed conversions require execution-environment qualifications. The function provides neither saturation nor an overflow indicator.

## Behavior

### Scale and rounding

For a representable endpoint difference, define:

\[
D=b-a,\qquad
A=Dw+2^{27},\qquad
Q=\left\lfloor\frac{A}{2^{28}}\right\rfloor.
\]

Here \(A\) is the biased wide quantity and \(Q\) is the intended rounded displacement.

Within the arithmetic and conversion domain specified below, rounding is to the nearest integer, with exact halfway cases directed toward positive infinity:

| Exact scaled displacement | Rounded displacement |
|---:|---:|
| \(+0.5\) | \(+1\) |
| \(-0.5\) | \(0\) |
| \(+1.5\) | \(+2\) |
| \(-1.5\) | \(-1\) |

The rule is neither truncation toward zero nor symmetric rounding away from zero.

A negative product does not necessarily make \(A\) negative. For example, a product of \(-2^{27}\) is exactly cancelled by the bias. This distinction determines whether the internal signed conversion is out of range.

No clamp is applied to the weight, displacement, or endpoint interval. Subject to the relevant representability and conversion conditions:

- Zero weight produces zero displacement.
- Unity weight produces the full endpoint displacement.
- Equal endpoints produce zero displacement for every weight.
- Negative weight reverses the displacement direction before rounding.
- A weight greater than unity permits extrapolation beyond the second endpoint.

These properties do not bypass endpoint subtraction or final return conversion.

### Endpoint subtraction

The endpoint difference is formed at the original signed width. Widening occurs only after this subtraction.

Consequently, the mathematical difference must fit in signed 32 bits for the subsequent wide-arithmetic interpretation to be established. A small or zero weight does not make an overflowing endpoint subtraction valid.

For example, subtracting the minimum signed endpoint from the maximum signed endpoint exceeds the signed 32-bit range, even though both individual endpoints are valid inputs.

The supplied body does not establish that such subtraction wraps, saturates, traps, or returns a particular value.

### Wide arithmetic

When \(D\) and \(w\) are both valid signed 32-bit values, their product fits in signed 64 bits. Adding \(2^{27}\) also fits.

Thus, once endpoint subtraction is valid, neither the wide product nor the bias introduces an additional signed-overflow boundary under the stated widths.

### Displacement representation and its boundary

The displacement is reconstructed from the wide biased quantity using a low four-bit field and a higher field. Under the expected signed conversions and within the signed-operation boundary, the resulting 32-bit representation corresponds to \(Q\).

More specifically:

- Bits 28 through 31 of the biased quantity supply the low four displacement bits.
- The upper 32-bit word supplies the remaining displacement bits after scaling by sixteen.
- The combination yields the 32-bit representation associated with division by \(2^{28}\), rounded downward after the bias.

This reconstruction is not an unrestricted logical extraction of bits. Scaling the signed interpretation of the upper word by sixteen is itself a signed operation. Its result must remain representable.

Under the expected interpretation of negative upper words, that condition is equivalent to:

\[
-2^{59}\le A\le 2^{59}-1,
\]

or, equivalently,

\[
-2^{31}\le Q\le 2^{31}-1.
\]

Outside this range, the source does not establish a portable displacement merely because only 32 result bits are eventually retained.

### Internal signed conversion

When \(A<0\), its unsigned 64-bit representation has an upper 32-bit word greater than the signed 32-bit maximum. Interpreting that word as signed therefore requires an out-of-range unsigned-to-signed conversion.

The expected negative displacement depends on the execution environment defining that conversion appropriately. This issue arises before final addition and can matter even when the final return is positive.

When \(A=0\), the upper word is zero. No out-of-range conversion is involved. In particular:

\[
(-1)\cdot134{,}217{,}728+134{,}217{,}728=0.
\]

Therefore, endpoints \(10\) and \(9\) with half weight produce displacement zero and return \(10\), without any out-of-range signed conversion under the stated widths.

Within the supported nonnegative range of \(A\), the upper-word conversion is also directly representable.

### Final addition and return

Where the displacement is established, final addition uses unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. Define its result representation as:

\[
U=(a+Q)\bmod 2^{32},
\qquad 0\le U<2^{32}.
\]

The return then converts \(U\) to the signed return type:

- If \(U\le2^{31}-1\), its numerical value is directly representable.
- If \(U>2^{31}-1\), the supplied material does not independently establish the signed result.
- With a bit-preserving two’s-complement conversion, the latter values return \(U-2^{32}\).

Unsigned reduction can also turn an out-of-range negative mathematical sum into a directly representable positive return. Thus, a nonnegative final unsigned value does not prove that the returned number equals the unbounded sum \(a+Q\).

There is no overflow flag, saturation result, or separate status value.
