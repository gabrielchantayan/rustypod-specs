## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **First-input forwarding.** If the first indirect invocation is reached, its displayed explicit argument equals the first input word. No preceding transformation of that word is visible.

2. **Fourth-input truncation.** Under 32-bit truncating shift semantics, a fourth-input bit pattern of `0x00000001` supplies `0x00100000` to F-a574095572d7. A pattern of `0x00001000` supplies zero. These examples do not resolve signed-shift validity at a source-language level.

3. **Zero top field.** If the address-producing result has top 12 bits equal to zero, the terminal store targets address zero, regardless of its lower 52 bits. Completion additionally requires every intervening operation to complete.

4. **Address gap and alignment.** Top-12-bit values of 3 and 4 yield addresses 3 and 5 respectively. Address 4 is skipped by this construction. Odd destinations are possible; acceptance cannot assume aligned stores.

5. **Maximum address.** A top field of `0xFFF` yields `4095 + 1023 = 5118`, or `0x13FE`. The displayed value written there is the halfword `0x00CD`.

6. **Low-result independence.** Two address-producing results with identical top 12 bits generate the same destination. Their overall executions need not be equivalent: low halves are also passed to dependencies.

7. **Interrupted traversal.** If a dependency does not return, later transfers and the terminal write are not required to occur. There is no visible recovery path.

8. **No successful-return guarantee.** Observing the terminal store alone would not establish normal completion; the next represented boundary is invalid instruction data.
