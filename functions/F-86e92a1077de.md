## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Entry-byte masking.** If the relevant entry word is `0x123456AB`, F-unknown-01 receives fourth argument `0x2AC00000`. Replacing the upper 24 bits while retaining `0xAB` must not change that argument.

2. **Eighteen-bit field.** If F-unknown-05 returns low half `0xFFFFFFFF`, the later masked four-argument call receives third argument `0x003FFFF0`, not the unmasked value shifted left by four.

3. **Discarded high bits.** Under 32-bit truncating shifts, shifting a word left by 27 and then left by 24 yields zero. Transfer 12 therefore has zero payload under that interpretation.

4. **Signed right-shift boundary.** If F-unknown-08 returns `0x04000000`, shifting left by five produces `0x80000000`. A later arithmetic right shift by five produces `0xFC000000`; a logical shift produces `0x04000000`. Acceptance requires preserving this unresolved distinction rather than assuming cancellation.

5. **Final address selection.** If the final address-producing 64-bit result has top 12 bits equal to five, the store address is six. Its lower 52 bits do not affect that address.

6. **Maximum final address.** If those top 12 bits are all set, the store targets `0x13FE` and writes `0x00CD`, subject to all preceding operations completing.

7. **Unaligned outcome.** Top 12 bits equal to one produce address one. The representation supplies no alignment correction. Whether the halfword write succeeds or faults is environment-dependent.

8. **No premature terminal claim.** If an earlier dependency does not return, neither the final store nor the invalid-instruction boundary is reached.
