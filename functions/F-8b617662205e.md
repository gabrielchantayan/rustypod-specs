## Behavior

The stored byte is H’s low eight bits. A zero low byte produces a zero write; there is no nonzero-value requirement.

**Second selector.** Retain A’s low 30 bits, shift that unsigned value right by 21, and add one. This yields the numeric address of the second selector, ranging from 1 through 512, inclusive.

The byte read there selects another numeric destination by addition of 17. That destination again ranges from 17 through 272. The stored byte is H’s bits 22–29, which may also be zero.

**Final two-byte destination.** Retain A’s low 30 bits and shift right by seven. This yields a numeric address from 0 through 8,388,607, inclusive.

This address is not necessarily aligned to two bytes. Its least significant bit comes from bit seven of A. Nonzero A does not guarantee a nonzero destination.
