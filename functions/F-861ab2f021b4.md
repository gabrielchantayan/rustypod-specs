# F-861ab2f021b4

## Purpose

F-861ab2f021b4 performs a long, ordered sequence of unresolved indirect calls, derives integers and addresses from their results, mutates calculated-address and fixed-address storage, and finishes with an unresolved four-operand indirect transfer.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation exposes integer transformations, explicit access widths for calculated-address dereferences, retained memory values, and ordering between direct-access groups. It does not establish an application-level purpose or certify correspondence with executable instructions.

**Inferred intent:** some updates resemble manipulation of interconnected records. This does not establish record types, valid links, text output, device operations, or memory ownership.

This contract is based on static evidence only. Unresolved properties are marked **documented_only**.

## Behavior

### Dependency interactions and transformations

A long straight-line indirect-call sequence precedes the first explicit direct data-memory store. Calls have zero to four represented operands. Operand construction includes shifted results, duplicated values, retained earlier results, secondary machine results, and explicit zeros.

The first call receives two copies of the incoming word shifted left by seven positions. Its target is derived from incoming control state with displacement 0xFFE. Later targets are generally derived from represented code-location values using that displacement. Callable identities and target encoding are **documented_only**.

Calls whose results are unused remain distinct interactions. Matching operands do not justify merging calls. Absence of explicit operands does not establish absence of hidden inputs.

Transformations mask low fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. Unsigned 64-bit results are split into words or shifted right by 34, 36, or 44 positions. These latter extractions retain at most 30, 28, or 20 significant bits; they are not 32-bit shifts.

Under the conditional arithmetic interpretation:

- Successive left shifts by 25 and seven positions produce zero.
- Successive left shifts by 28 and 28 positions produce zero.
- Left shift by eight followed by unsigned right shift by eight retains the original low 24 bits.
- A low-24-bit mask followed by a six-position left shift can retain 30 significant bits.

These deductions do not remove intervening dependency effects. The complete operand-by-operand call trace remains **documented_only** in this contract.

### Initial captures and mutations

The following names describe retained values, not verified objects:

- **Packed base:** a late primary result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Shifted base:** an earlier low result shifted left by 25.
- **Narrow index:** a later result shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, then shifted right by six.
- **Small base:** twice the narrow index.
- **Shifted payload:** another late result shifted left by eight.
- **Final result pair:** the last combined dependency result before direct stores.

Under 32-bit truncation, the narrow index is a multiple of four from 0 through 508; the small base is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

Initial mutations place the shifted base as a word at final-high-word offset 0x14, followed by a byte at shifted-base offset 0x09 containing the final high word’s low byte. A word at 256 times the final low word, offset 0x54, receives the packed base. The shifted-base byte at offset 0x09 is then overwritten with the shifted base’s own low byte.

Packed-base offset 0x14 is cleared by a word store. An explicit unsigned byte read at packed-base offset 0x05 supplies a zero-extended word payload for packed-base offset 0x34. A zero-word store at small-base offset 0x14 occurs between that byte read and the widening store.

Packed-base offset 0x14 is then captured as the **saved initial word**. Under ordinary byte-addressed storage without external modification, this capture is zero:

- The intervening small-base word store writes only zeros, even if it overlaps the cleared location.
- Packed-base offsets 0x14 and 0x34 are separated by 0x20 bytes and their four-byte spans do not overlap, including under modular 32-bit addressing.

Ordinary calculated-address overlap therefore cannot make this capture nonzero. A nonzero result would require unresolved external memory behavior, concurrency, nonordinary address mapping, or a failure of the conditional interpretation.

Another zero-word store at small-base offset 0x14 follows the capture. Two byte stores at small-base offset 0x15 then carry the saved initial word’s low byte.

Only afterward is the global at fixed address **0x05** converted to an unsigned 32-bit value and retained as the **fixed-source base**. Its source width and signedness are **documented_only**. If that global is an unsigned eight-bit object, this is a zero-extended byte capture; otherwise that description is not established.

The capture precedes assignment to the global at **0x01** of the small base narrowed to eight bits. The retained fixed-source base then determines an explicit byte destination at offset 0x15, carrying the narrow index’s low byte.

The shifted payload supplies a byte at packed-base offset 0x11 and a word at saved-initial-word offset 0x54. The former byte is subsequently cleared. A word at fixed-source-base offset 0x54 is captured as the **old retained word**, then replaced by the saved initial word.
