A, B, and C first write the low byte of the active base shifted left once to active offset 17. Each subsequently reads a signed halfword at active-base-plus-external-address, then a byte at signed-halfword-plus-incoming-word.

Each ends by converting its captured lookup byte to a halfword and writing it at signed-halfword-plus-external-address. Intervening writes do not cause the lookup to be repeated.

The distinguishing effects are:

- **A:** After the lookup, write the entry byte at external offset 29; then perform the concluding halfword write.
- **B:** After the lookup, read a fresh word at first-argument offset 100 and write it at signed-halfword offset 84. Next, write the incoming word’s low byte at promoted-lookup-byte offset 13. Capture a word at external offset 100, then perform the concluding halfword write.
- **C:** Capture first-argument offset 100 after the active-offset-17 write but before either lookup. After the lookups, write, in order:
  1. External address’s low byte at first-argument offset 1.
  2. Incoming word’s low byte at external offset 13.
  3. B’s retained external-offset-100 word at external offset 68.
  4. External address’s low byte at promoted-lookup-byte offset 13.

  The concluding halfword write follows.

B’s external-offset-100 capture remains a retained numeric value for F and G. C’s first-argument-offset-100 capture supplies D’s initial byte write.

### D–E: traversal and replacement

D writes the low byte of C’s first-argument-field capture at active offset 17, then freshly captures first-argument offset 100. D next reads, in order:

1. Active offset 68.
2. A signed halfword at active-base-plus-external-address.
3. A word at incoming-word offset 116.
4. A word at active offset 20.

The third read supplies D’s traversal result. The fourth selects the active base for E and F.

D writes its traversal result’s low byte at offset 13 through its captured active-offset-68 word. It then writes that captured word’s narrowed halfword at signed-halfword-plus-traversal-result.

E writes the low byte of D’s fresh first-argument-field capture at active offset 17. It captures active offset 68, then reads a signed halfword at active-base-plus-external-address.

E then performs two writes before its traversal read:

1. Write the external address’s low byte at incoming-word offset 21.
2. Write the incoming word at external offset 68.

Only afterward does E read a word at offset 116 through its captured active-offset-68 value. This is E’s traversal result. Either preceding write can alter that read through aliasing.
