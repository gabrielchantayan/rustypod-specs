# F-0b6012a62dac

## Purpose

F-0b6012a62dac performs an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect invocations, transforms their outputs, mutates shared memory through calculated and fixed addresses, and finishes with a four-argument indirect control transfer.

Its established result is an interaction and memory-effect trace, not a pure calculation or a defined success value. No application-level purpose, object model, character encoding, or synchronization protocol is established.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** behavior represented explicitly in the supplied material.
- **[INFERENCE]:** conclusions conditional on an execution model or interpretations of apparent intent.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which this contract supplies no invented implementation semantics.

All observations are static. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

The initial region contains a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations interleaved with arithmetic. No explicit loop or conditional branch is represented in that region.

Represented argument lists contain zero through four word-sized values. Arguments include explicit zeros, repeated copies of transformed outputs, retained values from earlier invocations, and secondary machine outputs whose signatures are incomplete.

Some results are represented as 64-bit quantities. Extracting their high word is distinct from shifting the complete result right by 34, 36, or 44 bits. Truncating to a low word before such an extraction would change behavior.

Masks also precede particular shifts. Retaining 24 bits and shifting right by 20 yields a value from 0 through 15; retaining 22 bits and shifting left by eight yields at most `0x3FFFFF00`.

A word shifted left by 25 and subsequently by 11 is therefore zero, rather than a retained 36-bit mathematical result. Signed overflow and signed-shift execution semantics cannot be recovered solely from the textual representation.

Invocations must not be removed, merged, cached, or reordered because their argument shapes repeat. Unknown callees can affect later memory, implicit inputs, and secondary outputs. A call with no represented arguments is not established to have no inputs; an ignored primary result does not establish absence of side effects.

### Computed address quantities

Several values from the end of the invocation region become address bases:

- A **shifted base** is a word result multiplied by `0x02000000`.
- A **small quantity** is obtained by shifting another word result left by eight, retaining its low 15 bits, and shifting that unsigned masked value right by six.
- The **small base** is twice the small quantity.
- A further retained call-derived word, already shifted left by eight, supplies several early publications.

Under the represented 32-bit interpretation, the small quantity has its lowest two bits clear. Its possible values are multiples of four from 0 through 508. Equivalently, it is four times the original word’s low seven bits. The small base ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

These quantities are used as addresses, not checked collection indices. No allocated container surrounding them is established.

For the later memory discussion:

- The **captured link** is a word read at offset `0x54` from the zero-extended byte obtained at fixed address `0x05`.
- The **quadrupled link** is four times that captured link.
- The **adjusted base** is the quadrupled link minus decimal 150.
- The **publication base** is four times the adjusted base.
- The **shared base** is a retained word read from fixed address `0x087FBBBC`.
- The **nested word** is a later word obtained through an offset-`0x74` access using an unsigned byte as its base.

### Initial mutations and fixed low-address access

The first explicit store publishes a retained call-derived word at offset `0x14` from the high word of the last wide invocation result. A byte at another retained computed base plus `0x09` is then written twice, with a word publication at a separately computed base plus `0x54` between those byte writes.

The shifted base’s offset-`0x14` word is cleared before its offset-`0x05` byte is read. That unsigned byte is later published as a word at shifted-base offset `0x34`; a clear of small-base offset `0x14` intervenes between the byte load and publication.

The operation then reads shifted-base offset `0x14`, clears small-base offset `0x14` again, and writes the retained word’s low byte twice at small-base offset `0x15`.

Only after those computed writes does it read the byte at **fixed address `0x05`**. The resulting zero-extended value is retained as an address base. The operation next:

- Updates **fixed byte `0x01`** with the small base’s low byte.
- Writes the small quantity’s low byte at offset `0x15` from the retained fixed-byte-derived base.
- Writes the retained call-derived word’s low byte at shifted-base offset `0x11`.
- Publishes that call-derived word at offset `0x54` from the earlier retained word read.
- Clears the byte at shifted-base offset `0x11`.

Thus the fixed `0x05` read is neither an entry-state snapshot nor a read that may be moved ahead of the preceding computed writes.

The captured link is subsequently read at offset `0x54` from the retained fixed-byte-derived base. That source word is replaced with the earlier retained word, but the captured link continues to be used.

### Address-bearing state and additional fixed updates

A nested publication uses two reads of small-base offset `0x74`: one determines a destination whose offset `0x54` is written, and another supplies the published word. The representation does not establish a single snapshot or the relative evaluation order of those two reads.

A meaningful fixed update occurs between publications to small-base offset `0x14`: after publishing a transformed earlier call result there, the operation writes twice the captured link as a word at **fixed address `0x54`**. It then publishes the captured link at small-base offset `0x14` twice.

After two byte publications of the quadrupled link at small-base offset `0x15`, it updates **fixed byte `0x15`** with the small base’s low byte. Computed publications resume afterward.

The intervening region derives further bases from current memory. It reads offset `0x74` from the small base and doubles that word; propagates this value through offset `0x44`; obtains another word through a byte-derived base plus `0x74`; and later obtains the nested word through another such byte-derived access.

After that fixed update, it captures words at paired-base offset `0x14` and publication-base offset `0x04`. These snapshots remain distinct from later current-memory values.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

### Attainable small-base values

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, an originating word whose low seven bits are `0x7F` produces small quantity 508 and small base 1016. Small quantity 511 and small base 1022 are unattainable. Every attainable small base is divisible by eight.

### Current fixed-byte input

If a preceding computed store changes fixed byte `0x05` to `0xE1`, the subsequent fixed-byte read uses `0xE1` as its zero-extended address-bearing value. Substituting its entry-state value violates the read boundary.

### Shared-word capture boundary

If the word read at `0x087FBBBC` yields a particular base, later repeated publications use that retained base even if the following fixed word write at `0x60` overlaps and changes its source storage.

### Changing `0x67` through aliasing

If the retained `0xA9` word’s low byte is `0x12`, but an intervening computed store changes `0x67` to `0xA5` before the second read, the associated computed byte publication uses `0xA5`. Reusing `0x12` is incorrect.

If another intervening write changes `0x67` again before the third read, that third publication uses the newly observed byte.

### Width and trace preservation

An unsigned byte load of `0xE1` published as a word yields `0x000000E1`. Repeated byte stores and the ordered `0x20`, `0x2F`, byte-derived, `0x20` word publications remain distinct accesses even when final memory happens to match a collapsed implementation.

### Partial progression and final arguments

If a dependency does not return, later effects need not occur; rollback is not established. If execution reaches the final transfer, its retained snapshot arguments must not be replaced with arbitrary current field contents.
