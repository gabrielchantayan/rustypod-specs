### Word transformation

The function traverses the five-word record in reverse order: fifth, fourth, third, second, then first.

At each position it reads the corresponding unsigned 32-bit word, shifts it right by one bit, and writes the result back to that position. The shift is logical: the newly introduced high bit is zero. An input’s least significant bit is discarded.

For each word considered separately, this operation produces the integer quotient of the unsigned input divided by two, rounded down. It does not propagate bits between adjacent record words and does not treat the 20-byte record as a single large integer.

Exactly five words participate. The storage immediately after the record is not an additional input to the transformation.

After all five shifts, the function modifies a 16-bit portion of the fifth word. Under the low-order-field interpretation indicated by the supplied representation, it takes the fifth word’s least significant byte, widens that byte to 16 bits without sign extension, and replaces the word’s low 16 bits with that widened value.

Under that interpretation, the fifth output word has:

- Bits 0–7 retained from the shifted fifth word.
- Bits 8–15 cleared.
- Bits 16–31 retained from the shifted fifth word.

Thus, for unsigned 32-bit inputs \(x_1\) through \(x_5\), the logical record delivered to the second dependency is:

- Words 1–4: \(x_i \mathbin{\mathrm{>>}} 1\).
- Word 5: \((x_5 \mathbin{\mathrm{>>}} 1) \mathbin{\&} \text{0xFFFF00FF}\).

The fifth-word formula depends on the low-order-field interpretation. Exact physical byte serialization remains subject to the layout uncertainty described below.
