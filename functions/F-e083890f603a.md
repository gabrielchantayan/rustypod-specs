# F-e083890f603a

## Purpose

F-e083890f603a accepts three represented 32-bit arguments, executes an extensive ordered sequence of unresolved indirect calls, and then performs mixed-width memory mutations before a final unresolved indirect transfer.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Character-sized constants do not establish text processing; recurring offsets do not establish a particular object model, container, or device interface.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** explicit data flow, represented widths, constants, and access ordering.
- **[INFERENCE]:** consequences requiring an additional arithmetic, ABI, or execution-environment interpretation.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which this contract makes no executable guarantee.

The memory phase is order-sensitive. It is not adequately specified by its final stored values: earlier writes can affect subsequent reads through aliasing, and access multiplicity may itself be observable.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

The first target is formed by adding 0xFFE to the incoming return-link value.

The second call receives the first primary result shifted left by eight positions and the third explicit argument shifted left by two. The third receives the second call’s low result word shifted left by eight, its high result word unchanged, and the same transformed explicit argument.

The remaining call phase is represented as a long straight-line sequence, without explicit input-dependent branches or loops in this function. This says nothing about control flow or termination inside dependencies.

Observable call-boundary behavior includes:

- Forwarding primary results after eight-bit left shifts.
- Passing low and high words of represented 64-bit results separately.
- Forwarding secondary result words not explained by ordinary return declarations.
- Passing zero words and duplicated arguments.
- Masking, shifting, and extracting fields before forwarding.
- Retaining earlier results across intervening calls.

Calls whose results are unused remain observable. No-argument calls are not established to have no implicit inputs, and no dependency is established to be pure.

The complete ABI-level call trace and independent identities of the targets remain **documented_only**.

### Widths and arithmetic

“Byte,” “halfword,” and “word” mean 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively for explicitly typed dynamic accesses. Represented 64-bit results are split numerically into bits 0–31 and bits 32–63; this does not establish memory endianness.

Unsigned right shifts are zero-filling. Shifts of an unsigned 64-bit result by 34, 36, and 44 positions correspond to shifting its high word right by 2, 4, and 12 positions.

The call phase uses masks of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. Masking and shifting order must be preserved. A truncating left shift followed by a right shift does not generally recover the original value.

Explicit byte writes retain the low eight bits. Explicit unsigned byte reads widen without sign extension. Halfword writes of 0x3A and 0x65 affect 16 bits, not merely one byte.

**[INFERENCE]** Under 32-bit modular arithmetic:

- Successive left shifts by 25 and 7 produce zero.
- Successive left shifts by 31 and 30 produce zero.
- A word shifted left by 16 has a zero low halfword.
- Scaled address calculations retain only the low 32 bits.

These are conditional interpretations, not unconditional guarantees for represented signed expressions. Signed overflow, address wrapping, and shifts of negative signed values remain unresolved.

Fixed-address symbols require an additional qualification: their declarations are absent. A 32-bit receiving value or byte-valued assignment establishes represented value flow, but does not independently prove the symbol’s physical access width.

### Initial direct mutations

Direct accesses begin after the last call of the extensive call phase. Their opening order is:

- A retained word is stored at offset 0x14 from the final result’s high word.
- A byte is stored at offset 9 from a retained shifted address.
- A retained scaled word is stored at offset 0x54 from the final result’s low word multiplied by 256.
- The byte at the retained address plus 9 is overwritten.

A further clear and two consecutive equal byte writes follow. None of these accesses establishes that the involved regions are distinct.

### Early fixed-address state and linked fields

After the consecutive byte writes, the function reads the fixed symbol at **0x05**, converts its value to an unsigned 32-bit quantity, and then performs a byte-valued write at **0x01**. The converted value supplies a base for subsequent offset accesses. The declaration of the symbol at 0x05 is unavailable; its read must not be described as an established unsigned-byte load.

Subsequent operations update bytes at offsets 0x15 and 0x11, write an offset-0x54 word through another loaded base, clear the offset-0x11 byte, and then read the word at offset 0x54 of the base derived from 0x05. That word is saved before the field is replaced.

For the remainder of this description, **anchor** means that saved offset-0x54 word. This is a specification label, not an object type.

The linked-field portion includes:

- Offset-0x74 word reads whose results become address bases.
- Self-referential word stores at offsets 0x24, 0x54, and 0x14.
- Repeated word stores and duplicate consecutive byte stores.
- Scaling loaded values by two, four, and eight.
- Byte reads at offset 5 whose widened numeric results become bases for word reads at offset 0x74.

The byte-to-address transitions are explicit. They must not be replaced with presumed full-width pointer loads.

Within this portion, a word-valued fixed assignment at **0x54** receives twice the anchor. It occurs after a dynamic offset-0x14 write and before two further writes to that dynamic word. Later, after two equal dynamic byte writes at offset 0x15, a separate byte-valued fixed assignment occurs at **0x15**. The fixed access at 0x15 is not the same operation as either preceding offset-based write.

For the fixed assignments at 0x54 and 0x15, the supplied representation establishes respectively a 32-bit computed value and a low-byte value. Actual symbol storage widths remain **documented_only** without their declarations.

### Constants, adjusted addresses, and repeated updates

A loaded value determines three stores of 0x3A: a byte at four times that value, a halfword at eight times it, and a word at offset 0x20 from the four-times-scaled address.

A separate retained quantity is reduced by 0x96 and then multiplied by four. Call this result the **adjusted base**. Its offset-0x20 word receives, in order, 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte freshly read from offset 4 of the adjusted base, and 0x20 again.

The intermediate read and writes remain part of the behavior. The last value does not justify removing earlier accesses.

Next, the function loads a 32-bit base value from the fixed symbol at **0x087FBBBC**. Only after that load does it perform the word-valued fixed assignment of 0x20 at **0x60**. Thus an overlapping write at 0x60 cannot retroactively change the already saved base.

Call the loaded value the **repetition base**. An expanded run repeatedly writes four fields in this order:

- An offset-0x54 word at a scaled destination.
- An offset-0x19 byte relative to the repetition base.
- An offset-0x11 byte relative to the adjusted base.
- An offset-0x34 word relative to the repetition base.

The initial group uses eight times the anchor for its final word. Subsequent groups use the later adjusted eight-times-scaled quantity. The byte payloads are saved low-byte values, not repeated loads of the two bases.

No element increment is represented. The repetitions must not be recast as traversal or collapsed into a single final-state update. Their numeric enumeration is **documented_only** here; this is not permission to change their supplied multiplicity.

### Final mutation block: entry interleaving

For this block, **linked base** means the retained doubled address derived from the last byte-based offset-0x74 lookup.

The opening access order is specifically:

| Order | Access |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Read the fixed symbol at **0xA9**, retaining a represented 32-bit word. |
| 2 | Read a word at offset **0x74** from the retained four-times-scaled value used as the repeated word payload. |
| 3 | Perform a byte-valued fixed write at **0x3A**, using the low-byte result of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30. |
| 4 | Read and save the word at **linked base + 0x14**. |
| 5 | Read and save the word at **adjusted base + 0x04**. |

The write at 0x3A precedes both saved dynamic reads. If it overlaps either field, the later read observes the resulting memory state. The representation does not support moving all these reads ahead of mutations.

The declaration of the symbol at 0xA9 is absent. Its value is retained in a 32-bit word, but its physical read width is **documented_only**.

### Final mutation block: saved values versus fresh reads

The function next writes bytes at linked-base offset 0x0D and adjusted-base offsets 8, 0x0D, and 9. The offset-8 byte receives 0x6C. A self-referential word is stored at linked-base offset 0x54.

A byte-valued assignment at **0x67** then receives the low eight bits of the saved 0xA9 word. Afterward, a word is read at linked-base offset 0x44. A fresh read of the fixed symbol at **0x67** supplies a byte-valued assignment at **0x21**.

The intervening offset-0x44 access is a read, not another load from 0xA9. Whether it has external read effects is unknown.

Dynamic writes then occur at adjusted-base offset 0x10, repetition-base offset 0x54, and linked-base offset 1. The last uses a signed right-shift result whose count is the low eight bits of the adjusted base. Another word replaces repetition-base offset 0x54.

The word at adjusted-base offset 0x40 is read before being copied to offset 0x10. Between that read and copy, repetition-base offset 0x74 receives the previously saved adjusted-base offset-4 word. Repetition-base offset 0x44 then receives the linked base.

A **fresh 0x67 read** now supplies the byte written at adjusted-base offset 9. Next come a byte write at linked-base offset 9 and a word write of the saved 0xA9 value at adjusted-base offset 0x44. The word at adjusted-base offset 0x54 is then read as another base. A **second fresh 0x67 read** supplies another byte write at adjusted-base offset 9.

These reads are not unconditional propagation of the original saved low byte from 0xA9. Any intervening overlapping dynamic write can change 0x67. Even the first write at adjusted-base offset 9 can affect the second read if that destination is 0x67.

The fixed-symbol declarations at 0x3A, 0x67, and 0x21 are unavailable. Their represented flows are byte-valued; physical symbol access widths remain **documented_only**.

### Final mutations and transfer

The newly loaded base receives the low byte of the earlier saved offset-0x74 word at offset 0x0D. Linked-base offset 0x54 is restored from the saved linked-base offset-0x14 word.

The function then reads adjusted-base offset 0x14, writes another byte at linked-base offset 0x11, replaces the byte at adjusted-base offset 9, and replaces the word at adjusted-base offset 0x44.

A word loaded from linked-base offset 4 becomes a further base. The halfword 0x65 is written at twice that base before its offset-0x64 word is read. The low byte of the saved adjusted-base offset-0x14 word is written at offset 0x11 of the other late-loaded base, whose offset-0x54 word then receives 100.

Next, the function reads offset 0x74 from the base used for the offset-0x64 read, and reads offset 0x64 from the linked base. A byte write at linked-base offset 9 precedes a word write at offset 0x54 of this last loaded address. Immediately before the final transfer, a word is written at twice the saved offset-0x64 value used as the transfer’s second argument.

No represented cleanup follows the transfer.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Argument forwarding.** If the third argument is 3 and the early calls return, the second and third calls receive 12 in their argument-derived positions.

2. **Word truncation.** Under 32-bit modular shifting, shifting 0x12345678 left by eight yields 0x34567800.

3. **Unsigned widening.** An explicit offset-5 byte read of 0xFE supplies numeric 254 when widened into a word. This criterion does not establish the undeclared fixed symbol at 0x05 as a byte.

4. **Early fixed ordering.** The 0x05 read precedes the byte-valued 0x01 write. The saved converted value is used afterward, even if that write overlaps the fixed symbol’s storage.

5. **Saved repetition base.** If the 0x087FBBBC load returns a particular base, the subsequent 0x60 assignment does not change that saved value.

6. **Final entry interleaving.** If the 0x3A write overlaps either saved dynamic field, acceptance uses the post-write contents for the later offset-0x14 or offset-4 read, not a prefetched value.

7. **Fresh 0x67 reads.** If the saved 0xA9 word ends in 0xAB, the first 0x67 assignment receives 0xAB. If an intervening overlapping write changes 0x67 to 0xCD, a later dynamic byte write fed by a fresh 0x67 read uses the newly observed value.

8. **Intermediate effects.** The adjusted offset-0x20 sequence includes all four writes and its intervening byte read; merely ending at 0x20 is insufficient.

9. **Transfer and failure.** Reaching the final transfer requires first argument 0x72. Unsupported earlier accesses do not require graceful completion or an error result.
