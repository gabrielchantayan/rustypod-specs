# F-14bbe60ba144

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven phase

The first invocation receives the first explicit argument and the implicit incoming word shifted left by five positions. After two further invocations, a represented paired result supplies the low word shifted left by eight, the high word unchanged, and the third explicit argument shifted left by five to the next invocation.

A lengthy straight-line sequence follows. Its arguments include captured results, secondary result channels, shifted or masked fields, and explicit zeros. No visible input-dependent branch, error-result check, or loop termination condition appears in this phase.

Captured values remain associated with their original producing invocations across intervening calls. A later result cannot replace an earlier capture merely because the same transformation recurs. Calls represented without explicit arguments are not established to have no implicit inputs or side effects.

Invocation order is significant. Repeated-looking invocations cannot be merged or reordered on an assumption of purity, equal targets, or commuting global effects. The exhaustive invocation trace and individual target identities are **documented_only** in this contract.

### Width and arithmetic rules

The representation distinguishes 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, 32-bit words, and 64-bit result pairs. Byte and halfword stores retain the corresponding low bits. Unsigned byte values converted to words are zero-extended.

For paired results, the low word comprises bits 0–31 and the high word bits 32–63. An unsigned extraction beginning at bit 36 yields the high word shifted right by four; it does not yield the full high word. Extractions beginning at bits 34 and 44 likewise differ from high-word extraction.

Transformations include shifts through 31 positions and masks selecting, among others, 24-, 22-, 21-, 20-, 19-, 18-, 17-, 16-, 15-, 14-, 13-, 12-, 11-, and single-bit fields. Masking before shifting is not interchangeable with an unmasked shift. Left shifting and subsequently right shifting need not recover discarded bits.

Where a **conditional 32-bit machine-word interpretation** is used, each word-sized arithmetic result is retained modulo \(2^{32}\). Wider intermediates must not restore discarded bits. Signed-looking shifts, multiplication overflow, address wraparound, and the variable-count signed-looking right shift are not fully resolved by the representation; their exact executable semantics remain **documented_only**.

### Entry into direct memory processing

The last paired result supplies a high-word base and a low-word scaled destination. Earlier captures supply a retained shifted value, a scaled base, and a narrow value obtained by masking a shifted word with 0x7FFF and logically shifting right by six. Under 32-bit interpretation, that narrow value lies between zero and 0x1FC in multiples of four. Its doubled value supplies another base.

The first direct-memory effects occur in this order:

- A word store at the final high-word base plus 0x14.
- A byte store at the retained shifted base plus 9, carrying the low byte of the final high word.
- A word store at the final low word multiplied by 256, plus 0x54.
- A second byte store at that same offset-9 destination, carrying the retained shifted value’s low byte.
- A zero word store at the earlier scaled base plus 0x14, followed by a byte read at that base plus 5.

The byte read follows those stores and therefore reflects any applicable aliasing. After a zero word store at the doubled narrow base plus 0x14, the byte is zero-extended into a word store at the scaled base plus 0x34. A subsequent word read at the scaled base plus 0x14 is a fresh read, not an assumed zero. Another zero word store and two separate byte stores at the doubled narrow base plus 0x15 follow.

Duplicate stores remain distinct represented events.

### Fixed-location effects before the constant region

The fixed locations are part of the state transition, not merely unspecified external storage.

**Fixed 0x05 and 0x01.** After the two offset-0x15 byte stores just described, fixed location 0x05 is read and its value converted to a 32-bit unsigned value used as a base. The underlying access width and extension behavior are **documented_only**, because the supplied material does not declare that fixed symbol’s type.

Next, a byte is written at fixed 0x01, containing the low eight bits of the doubled narrow base. A byte store through the base obtained from fixed 0x05 then writes the narrow value’s low byte at offset 0x15.

Further intervening effects include a derived offset-0x11 byte store, an offset-0x54 word store through the earlier captured word, and a zero byte store at the offset-0x11 destination. Only afterward is a word captured from the fixed-0x05-derived base plus 0x54; that location is then overwritten with the earlier captured word.

For the remainder of this contract, **retained word** means the word captured by this last offset-0x54 read.

**Fixed 0x54.** Subsequent processing includes an offset-0x34 word store, chained offset-0x74 reads and offset-0x54 writes, and repeated offset-0x24 word and offset-9 byte stores. A word is then written at the doubled narrow base plus 0x14 using a retained dependency value shifted by 26 positions. Immediately afterward, fixed 0x54 receives a **32-bit word containing twice the retained word**. The doubled narrow base plus 0x14 is then written twice with the retained word.

**Fixed 0x15.** After those two word stores, the doubled narrow base plus 0x15 receives two byte stores containing the low byte of four times the retained word. Fixed 0x15 then receives a **byte containing the low byte of the doubled narrow base**. This precedes the following offset-0x54 and offset-0x34 word stores.

These fixed accesses may alias computed destinations. In particular, a later access through a zero-valued computed base can address the same storage as a fixed-location access. No separation is guaranteed.

### Chained updates and constants

The next region uses four times the retained word as a recurring base. Word and byte reads at offsets 4, 5, 0x64, and 0x74 supply further addresses. It performs self-valued word stores, byte copies of truncated address-like values, and successive writes at offsets 0x14, 0x44, and 0x54.

Repeated reads of the same location remain fresh reads when intervening stores can affect it. The complete intermediate access trace is **documented_only** here; the fixed-location ordering specified above remains required.

A later offset-0x74 chain produces a word used to define:

- **Constant base:** four times that word.
- **Terminal base:** twice that word.
- **Adjusted value:** four times the retained word minus decimal 150.
- **Working base:** four times the adjusted value.

Subject to the arithmetic qualifications above, the constant writes are a byte containing 0x3A at the constant base, a halfword containing 0x003A at twice the constant base, and a word containing 0x0000003A at the constant base plus 0x20, in that order.

At the working base plus 0x20, successive word stores contain 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended fresh byte read from the working base plus 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read follows the first two stores.

### Stored repetition base and repeated writes

After the last working-base-plus-0x20 store, the stored repetition base is read as a 32-bit word. **Only after that capture does fixed 0x60 receive the 32-bit constant 0x20.**

Thus, even if the stored-base location overlaps fixed 0x60, repetition uses the captured base rather than a reload of the newly written constant. The stored-base location’s independently established address is **documented_only** here.

The initial four-write group performs, in order:

- An offset-0x54 word store through eight times the retained word, carrying the constant base.
- An offset-0x19 byte store through the stored repetition base, carrying the working base’s low byte.
- An offset-0x11 byte store through the working base, carrying the stored repetition base’s low byte.
- An offset-0x34 word store through the stored repetition base, carrying eight times the retained word.

Following groups preserve the same widths and order, but use eight times the adjusted value for both the scaled destination and the offset-0x34 stored word.

The sequence is explicitly repeated without intervening represented calls. Exact multiplicity is **documented_only** in this contract. Final-state equivalence alone does not justify collapsing the writes.

### Terminal captures and fixed-byte state

After repetition, a **32-bit word at fixed 0xA9** is captured first. A word at the constant base plus 0x74 is captured next. Both precede the **byte write at fixed 0x3A**, whose stored value is the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30.

Only after that fixed-byte write are words captured from the terminal base plus 0x14 and the working base plus 4. These captures can therefore reflect aliasing with fixed 0x3A; they must not be moved before its write.

The following stores write the working base’s low byte at the terminal base plus 0x0D, 0x6C at the working base plus 8, and the stored repetition base’s low byte at working-base offsets 0x0D and 9. A self-valued word store at the terminal base plus 0x54 follows.

Fixed 0x67 then receives the low byte of the captured fixed-0xA9 word. A word is freshly read from the terminal base plus 0x44. **After that read, fixed 0x67 is read as a byte and copied to fixed 0x21.** This is a memory read, not substitution of the original fixed-word capture.

The fresh offset-0x44 word supplies a byte store at the working base plus 0x10 and a word store at the stored repetition base plus 0x54. A byte store at the terminal base plus 1 uses that word right-shifted by a count equal to the working base’s low byte. Counts of at least 32 and right shifts of negative represented values remain **documented_only**. The stored repetition base plus 0x54 is then overwritten with the working base.

A fresh word read at the working base plus 0x40 precedes three word stores: the earlier working-base-plus-4 capture at the stored repetition base plus 0x74; the fresh offset-0x40 value at the working base plus 0x10; and the terminal base at the stored repetition base plus 0x44.

Only then does the **first subsequent fresh byte read of fixed 0x67** supply a byte store at the working base plus 9.

Before the next fixed-0x67 read, there are a byte store at the terminal base plus 9, a word store of the captured fixed-0xA9 word at the working base plus 0x44, and a fresh word read at the working base plus 0x54. The **second fresh byte read of fixed 0x67** then supplies another byte store at the working base plus 9.

These two reads cannot be replaced with each other or with the originally captured low byte: intervening computed stores may overlap fixed 0x67.

### Terminal loaded-address updates and transfer

The fresh working-base-plus-0x54 word becomes another destination base. The low byte of the earlier constant-base-plus-0x74 capture is written at its offset 0x0D. The earlier terminal-base-plus-0x14 capture is written as a word at terminal-base offset 0x54.

A fresh word at working-base offset 0x14 is captured before further byte and word stores. These include terminal-base offset 0x11, working-base offset 9, and working-base offset 0x44.

A subsequent word read at terminal-base offset 4 supplies a new base. A halfword containing 0x0065 is stored at twice that base before its offset-0x64 word is loaded. The fresh working-base-plus-0x14 capture supplies a truncated byte store through the other loaded destination, which also receives the word constant decimal 100 at offset 0x54.

The new base’s offset-0x74 word is loaded next, followed by a word at terminal-base offset 0x64. Final stores write a truncated base value at terminal-base offset 9, the offset-0x64 capture through the last loaded base plus 0x54, and that last loaded base as a word at twice the offset-0x64 capture.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

- **Initial shift:** With a valid initial target and an implicit incoming word of 3, the initial invocation receives 96 as its second represented argument. The first explicit argument is forwarded unchanged.

- **Word truncation:** Under modulo-\(2^{32}\) interpretation, third-argument bit pattern 0x08000001 shifted left by five produces 0x00000020, not a widened value retaining the discarded bit.

- **Paired extraction:** A high word of 0x12345678 yields 0x01234567 when the unsigned pair is shifted right by 36.

- **Fixed-word update:** If the retained word is 0x12345678 and relevant accesses succeed, fixed 0x54 receives a 32-bit store of 0x2468ACF0 at its specified position. This does not guarantee its final value if later computed writes overlap it.

- **Stored-base capture:** If the stored repetition base overlaps fixed 0x60 and initially contains a valid base, repetition uses that captured base despite the subsequent word store of 0x20 to fixed 0x60.

- **Capture boundary:** If fixed 0x3A overlaps the later terminal-base-plus-0x14 word, that word is captured after the byte write. The fixed-0xA9 and constant-base-plus-0x74 captures precede the write.

- **Fresh fixed-byte reads:** If an intervening computed store changes fixed 0x67 between its two reads supplying working-base-plus-9 stores, the stores use their respective freshly read bytes. Equality with the original fixed-word low byte is not required.

- **Dependency failure:** If an invocation does not return normally, later effects are not guaranteed. No status conversion or rollback is established.
