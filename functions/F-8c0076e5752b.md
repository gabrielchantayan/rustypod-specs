| Doubled primary base plus `0x54` | 32 bits | First argument |

Within each complete occurrence, the writes follow the table’s order. After the final complete occurrence, the opening portion ends with another write to the first destination followed by another write of the doubled primary base to the second destination.

This is a finite repeated sequence, not an input-controlled loop in the supplied representation. Its exact numerical repetition count is not asserted here; preserving the represented multiplicity remains a `documented_only` requirement for trace-level equivalence.

There are no visible memory reads or validation branches within this repeated prefix.

It next writes one byte at absolute address `0x3A`. The byte is:

\[
(2 \times \operatorname{low8}(\text{primary base}) + 0x30) \bmod 256.

The evidence does not establish character formatting or digit validation despite the addition of `0x30`.

The function then captures:

- A word at the tertiary base plus `0x14`.
- A word at the primary base plus `0x04`.

These reads occur after the fixed-address byte write. If any address relationships overlap, that order matters.

### Early field and fixed-address updates

- The primary base’s low byte at the tertiary base plus `0x0D`.
- The byte `0x6C` at the primary base plus `0x08`.

- The previously captured word from the primary base plus `0x04` is stored at the secondary base plus `0x74`.
- The newly read word is stored at the primary base plus `0x10`.

### First derived destination

- The low byte originating from the first argument plus `0x74` is written at the tertiary base plus `0x11`.
- The derived destination base’s low byte is written at the primary base plus `0x09`.
- The derived destination base is stored as a word at the primary base plus `0x44`.

The function next writes:

- The 32-bit value `100`, at the derived destination base plus `0x54`.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional specification examples, not executed tests.

This does not imply that the byte remains `0xAB` after the later overlapping word store at primary displacement `0x10`.

### Derived fixed-address byte

If the primary base’s low byte is `0x80`, the write at absolute address `0x3A` carries `0x30`, because the arithmetic result is truncated to eight bits.

This example does not establish that the primary base denotes valid memory.

### Replacement of the second argument

Assume the opening destinations are writable and non-overlapping. If the primary base is `0x00001000` and the second argument is `0xDEADBEEF`, the word destination at secondary displacement `0x34` receives `0xDEADBEEF` initially and `0x00002000` on subsequent opening writes.

A final-state-only check cannot establish preservation of the initial write.

### Defined shift case

If the shift-source word is nonnegative `0x00000100` and the primary base’s low byte is `8`, the shifted byte written at tertiary displacement `0x01` is `0x01`, under ordinary 32-bit right-shift semantics.

No corresponding expected value is asserted for a count of `32`.

### Captured invocation argument

### Partial failure
