## Behavior

### Eligibility checks

The checks are short-circuited in this order:

1. Compare the record discriminator with the external discriminator value.
2. If they match, require the selector to be nonzero.
3. If the selector is nonzero, require it to be no greater than the record limit after conversion of that limit to unsigned.
4. If the bound check passes, require the gate to be nonzero.

The first failed check determines the rejection path. Rejection returns zero.

Consequently:

- A discriminator mismatch prevents direct reads of the limit, gate, and stride.
- A matching discriminator with selector zero prevents direct reads of the limit, gate, and stride.
- A failed bound check prevents direct reads of the gate and stride.
- A zero gate prevents the dependency call and the direct stride read.

The discriminator is checked even when the selector is zero. The function therefore does not provide a null-pointer-safe or invalid-pointer-safe zero-selector shortcut.

The discriminator comparison requires reading both the record discriminator and the external discriminator value. Their relative read order within that comparison is not specified by the supplied expression. Both belong to the first check; the later checks are conditional on its result.

### Unsigned bound semantics

The limit is converted to unsigned before comparison. It is not validated as a nonnegative signed count.

For a nonnegative representable limit, the accepted selector range is one through that limit, inclusive, subject to the other checks.

A negative limit converts to its unsigned residue. Under a 32-bit interpretation, a limit of negative one becomes 4,294,967,295. With a matching discriminator and nonzero gate, every nonzero 32-bit selector then satisfies the bound check.

A zero limit rejects every selector: zero fails the nonzero check, and every nonzero selector fails the bound check.
