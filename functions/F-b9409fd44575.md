# F-b9409fd44575

## Behavior

### Indirect invocation phase

The first target is the incoming control-location value plus 0xFFE. Its first represented argument is an implicit incoming word shifted left by 22 bits; its other arguments are the explicit inputs in reverse order.

An extended straight-line invocation sequence follows. Most targets derive from embedded control locations plus 0xFFE. Invocations without represented arguments are interspersed with invocations receiving transformed results.

The sequence includes:

- Left shifts of 32-bit results, frequently by eight bits.
- Extraction of low and high 32-bit components from 64-bit containers.
- Retention of components across intervening invocations.
- Masking and shifting of primary and secondary result channels.
- Repeated values in different argument positions.
- Invocations whose represented primary results are unused.

Unused results do not make invocations removable or reorderable. Their implicit-result, memory, device, and control-flow effects remain unresolved.

No explicit loop, conditional branch, zero-result exit, or status-checking branch is represented in this function. Dependencies may independently branch, fault, fail, or not return.

### Arithmetic and truncation

Masks select fields of widths including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. Represented unsigned right shifts introduce zeros.

Right shifts of a 64-bit container by 34, 36, or 44 bits extract upper-container information. They must not be interpreted as shifts of its low 32-bit component.

Under a **conditional 32-bit machine-bit interpretation**, left shifts and multiplication retain the low 32 bits. Successive shifts preserve the truncation of each intermediate result; their counts must not be combined modulo 32. Scaling and subtraction have no represented overflow or range checks.

Some operands have signed-looking representations. Signed overflow, signed left-shift validity, negative-value right shifts, and address wraparound are not independently established as language-level guarantees.

A late variable right shift uses the low eight bits of the working base as its count. Counts of 32 or greater and shifts of negative operands remain **documented_only**; a particular processor’s count-masking rule must not be assumed.

Byte stores retain an eight-bit payload. Halfword and word stores retain 16-bit and 32-bit payloads respectively, subject to the unresolved machine interpretation where arithmetic precedes truncation.

### Initial direct-memory mutations

The following descriptive names identify retained values, not source-level objects:

- **High-shift base:** a retained call-result component shifted left by 25 bits.
- **Multiplication base:** another call result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Small value:** a result shifted left by eight bits, masked to 15 bits, then shifted right by six.
- **Small base:** twice the small value.

Under 32-bit machine-bit arithmetic, the small value ranges from zero through 508 in multiples of four. The small base ranges from zero through 1016 in multiples of eight.

The first direct write stores the high-shift base as a word at offset 0x14 from the high word of the final 64-bit result. A byte is then written at high-shift-base offset 0x09, followed by a word containing the multiplication base at offset 0x54 from a separately scaled result. Another byte write follows at high-shift-base offset 0x09.

The first offset-0x09 byte payload comes from the final high result word. The second comes from the high-shift base and is zero under the machine-bit interpretation.

The multiplication-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared before a byte is read from its offset 0x05. The small-base word at offset 0x14 is then cleared before the captured byte is zero-extended into the multiplication-base word at offset 0x34.

A subsequent word load from multiplication-base offset 0x14 is saved. Another small-base clear precedes two distinct byte writes at small-base offset 0x15, both carrying the saved word’s low byte.

These requirements concern ordered accesses, not just final values. Intervening stores may alias previously cleared locations.

### Early absolute accesses and captured linking value

After those two offset-0x15 byte writes, the function **reads a byte at absolute address 0x05**, zero-extends it as an address base, and only then **writes a byte at absolute address 0x01**. The write carries the small base’s low byte.

The captured 0x05 byte drives later accesses at offsets 0x15 and 0x54. It cannot be replaced by an assumed constant or by a read moved after the 0x01 write.

Subsequent computed mutations include:

- Byte writes at multiplication-base offset 0x11, first with a retained shifted-result fragment and later with zero.
- A word load from offset 0x54 of the base captured from 0x05, followed by replacement of that word.
- A word write at small-base offset 0x34.
- A word load from multiplication-base offset 0x74 feeding further mutations.
- Two represented reads of small-base offset 0x74 within one pointer-derived update.
- Repeated self-valued word stores and byte stores of address fragments.

The word captured by the offset-0x54 load is the **captured link**. This name asserts neither valid pointer semantics nor ownership. The two reads within the pointer-derived update remain distinct; their internal evaluation order is not independently established by the supplied representation.

After a computed word write at small-base offset 0x14, the function **writes a word at absolute address 0x54**, carrying the captured link shifted left by one bit.

It then writes the captured link twice to small-base offset 0x14. Two byte writes at small-base offset 0x15 carry the low byte of four times the captured link. Only afterward does it **write a byte at absolute address 0x15**, carrying the small base’s low byte.

Further computed word writes follow at fourfold-link offset 0x54 and small-base offset 0x34. The absolute writes at 0x54 and 0x15 therefore occupy distinct positions among computed accesses.

### Scaled mutations and constant payloads

Further mutations use twofold, fourfold, and eightfold values derived from the captured link and subsequent loads. Zero-extended bytes can become address bases for word loads at offset 0x74. Other loaded words feed accesses at offsets including 0x04, 0x05, 0x09, 0x0D, 0x14, 0x15, 0x44, 0x54, and 0x64.

Repeated loads remain separate observations. In particular, two byte loads from an offset-0x05 expression may differ because intervening stores can overlap their location.

A later lookup word determines three constant destinations. In order, its fourfold address receives an **8-bit 0x3A**, its eightfold address receives a **16-bit 0x3A**, and its fourfold address plus 0x20 receives a **32-bit 0x3A**.

For subsequent descriptions:

- **Adjusted link:** four times the captured link minus 0x96.
- **Working base:** four times the adjusted link.
- **Lookup base:** twice the later lookup word.
- **Scaled target:** four times that lookup word.

Working-base offset 0x20 receives four separate word payloads: 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte loaded from working-base offset 0x04, and 0x20. The byte load occurs after the first two stores.

The function next **reads a word at absolute address 0x087FBBBC**, retaining it as the **global-derived base**, and then **writes a word containing 0x20 at absolute address 0x60**. Both accesses precede the repeated linking region.

The global read observes memory after the preceding mutations. Later computed stores may overlap 0x60.

### Repeated linking region

The repeated region preserves a fixed four-access group:

- A word store at scaled-link offset 0x54.
- A byte store at global-derived-base offset 0x19.
- A byte store at working-base offset 0x11.
- A word store at global-derived-base offset 0x34.

Within each group, these accesses occur in that order. The first word receives the scaled target. The byte payloads are retained low bytes of the working base and global-derived base.

The first group uses eight times the captured link as its scaled-link base and final word payload. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted link.

The addresses do not advance through an array. This is a fixed straight-line expansion, not an input-controlled loop. Its numerical multiplicity is not enumerated in this contract and remains **documented_only** here; no claim that the body contains a variable repetition count follows. Separate accesses must not be collapsed into final assignments.

### Final absolute reads and dependent mutations

After the repeated region, the function **captures a word from absolute address 0xA9**. It then captures a word from scaled-target offset 0x74 before **writing a byte at absolute address 0x3A**. That byte carries the truncated result of eight times the adjusted link plus 0x30.

The captured 0xA9 word remains available for later payloads; it is not reloaded from that address.

Words are next saved from lookup-base offset 0x14 and working-base offset 0x04. Computed byte writes follow at lookup-base offset 0x0D and working-base offsets 0x08, 0x0D, and 0x09. The offset-0x08 payload is 0x6C.

A self-valued word store at lookup-base offset 0x54 precedes **a byte write at absolute address 0x67**, carrying the captured 0xA9 word’s low byte.

The function loads a word from lookup-base offset 0x44 and then **reads the current byte at 0x67 for a byte write to absolute address 0x21**. This is not unconditional reuse of the earlier 0x67 payload.

Fragments of the loaded word are stored at working-base offset 0x10 and lookup-base offset 0x01. The latter uses the variable right shift. Global-derived-base offset 0x54 receives the loaded word and subsequently the working base.

**The next interleaving is significant:** the function first captures the word from working-base offset 0x40. It then stores the previously saved working-base offset-0x04 word at global-derived-base offset 0x74. Only after that store does it write the captured offset-0x40 word to working-base offset 0x10. It then stores the lookup base at global-derived-base offset 0x44.

The offset-0x40 value is therefore captured before either intervening destination is updated. The offset-0x74 store precedes the offset-0x10 store; these writes must not be reversed. Their destinations are not established as disjoint. The word write at working-base offset 0x10 supersedes the earlier byte write there, subject to other overlapping accesses.

Only after the global-derived-base offset-0x44 store does the function **read 0x67 again** for a byte store at working-base offset 0x09.

A byte write at lookup-base offset 0x09 and a word write of the captured 0xA9 value at working-base offset 0x44 intervene before a word load from working-base offset 0x54. After that load, the function **reads 0x67 a third time** for another byte store at working-base offset 0x09.

The three 0x67 reads are distinct. Computed stores between them may alias 0x67, so their payloads need not agree.

### Terminal preparation and handoff

The remaining mutations propagate low-byte fragments of the saved scaled-target offset-0x74 word and restore the saved lookup-base offset-0x14 word to lookup-base offset 0x54.

The function captures working-base offset 0x14. The word previously loaded from working-base offset 0x54 supplies further destinations, including byte and word updates.

A word loaded from lookup-base offset 0x04 supplies an address whose doubled value receives a **16-bit 0x65**. A word is then loaded from that address’s offset 0x64. Further mutations include a byte carrying the captured working-base offset-0x14 fragment and a **32-bit decimal 100** at a derived offset 0x54.

A word from the same loaded address’s offset 0x74 is retained for terminal use. A later lookup-base offset-0x64 load supplies another destination base. Final word stores place the offset-0x64 result at that base’s offset 0x54 and place the newly loaded destination base at twice the offset-0x64 result.

The terminal invocation receives four represented arguments:

1. 0x72.
2. The recent word obtained through the offset-0x64 load.
3. The saved lookup-base offset-0x14 word.
4. The recent word obtained through the offset-0x74 load.

No validation or status processing intervenes before that invocation. Return is represented only if it returns.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Input order:** Explicit inputs 0x11223344 and 0x55667788 require first-invocation argument positions two and three to carry 0x55667788 and 0x11223344.
- **Small-value boundary:** Under 32-bit machine-bit arithmetic, a contributing result of 0x7F produces small value 508 and small base 1016; 0x80 produces zero.
- **Early capture:** The byte read at 0x05 precedes the byte write at 0x01. Subsequent address uses depend on the captured byte.
- **Absolute-store placement:** The word write at 0x54 precedes the later byte write at 0x15. The word write at 0x60 follows the global-derived-base read and precedes repeated linking.
- **Saved payload:** If the 0xA9 read returns 0x123456AB, the later explicit byte write to 0x67 carries 0xAB even if 0xA9 changes afterward.
- **Independent rereads:** If an intervening computed store changes 0x67, a later read-dependent destination receives the current byte, not an automatically cached earlier value.
- **Required interleaving:** Suppose global-derived-base offset 0x74 exactly aliases working-base offset 0x10. If the saved offset-0x04 word is 0x11223344 and the captured offset-0x40 word is 0x55667788, the shared destination receives those two word payloads in that order. Immediately after the second store it contains 0x55667788 in ordinary memory. This is not a claim about its value after subsequent potentially overlapping stores.
- **Capture before overwrite:** If the offset-0x74 destination overlaps working-base offset 0x40, that store cannot change the already captured offset-0x40 payload used for the later offset-0x10 write.
- **Read after clear:** The saved multiplication-base offset-0x14 load observes zero only if intervening effects leave its four bytes unchanged.
- **Mixed widths:** Overlapping byte, halfword, and word accesses follow the target byte order; they are not independent fields.
- **Repeated payloads:** Working-base offset 0x20 receives 0x20, 0x2F, the loaded-byte value, and 0x20 as separate word stores.
- **Terminal arguments:** The handoff uses the identified saved and recent loads, not guessed constants or current reloads of their original locations.
