# F-aa4e585f285c

## Purpose

The established behavior is an ordered sequence of reads and writes through incoming and loaded addresses, followed by arithmetic-addressed stores and an unresolved indirect transfer. Values are propagated as 32-bit words, truncated bytes, and truncated 16-bit halfwords. Several fields receive addresses, including self-references.

No application-level purpose is established. The accesses do not establish an object model, allocation protocol, initialization operation, or destruction operation. “Root,” “link,” and similar terms below distinguish captured values; they do not imply domain semantics.

This contract describes static evidence from the supplied representation. The function boundary, complete calling convention, and interpretation of the final transfer as a call are not independently established.

### Widths and addressing

Offsets are in bytes. A **byte**, **halfword**, and **word** mean 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Address-bearing storage is represented as 32 bits.

“Low byte” and “low halfword” mean numeric truncation to 8 or 16 bits. They do not identify a particular memory byte without knowing byte order.

The tail derives an unsigned 32-bit value **U** from a byte read as a plain character. Both U-based additions, `U + 0x45` and `U + 0x44`, wrap modulo \(2^{32}\). Other address expressions involve signed-looking values; their overflow semantics are unresolved.

### Preconditions

Completion requires every executed access to be valid for its width and every reached transfer target to be executable under the actual calling convention. This includes addresses obtained by traversal, addition, doubling, and conversion from U.

No checks for bounds, null addresses, lifetime, alignment, arithmetic overflow, or target validity are represented.

Disjoint storage is not an established precondition. Aliasing can change later reads and destinations. Publishing an address does not establish allocation, ownership transfer, or permission to release storage.

## Behavior

### Ordering rules

The phases below preserve the represented access sequence. Reads observe preceding overlapping writes according to the actual memory model and byte order. Captured values are not refreshed merely because their source storage changes.

Repeated accesses remain distinct. This includes repeated stores and a word read followed by a write of the same captured value to the same address. No atomicity or synchronization is established.

The sequence describes static memory dependencies and represented operations, not independently recovered instruction-level timing.

### Initial publications and captures

The routine first writes:

1. B’s low byte to C’s offset `0x0D`.
2. The third explicit argument as a word to C’s offset `0x54`.
3. A as a word to C’s offset `0x14`.

It captures B’s word at offset `0x64` as the **initial payload**.

It then writes byte `0x74` to A’s offset `0x10`, word `0x00000074` to B’s offset `0x54`, and word `0x00000074` to A’s offset `0x10`, in that order.

A’s word at offset `0x74` is captured as the **early link**. C’s offset `0x54` receives C as a word, and A’s offset `0x30` receives word `0x00000074`. Thus the third argument’s store precedes its replacement at the same nominal field; it is not absent from the behavior.

Next:

- B’s offset `0x01` receives D’s low byte.
- D’s offset `0x09` receives the initial payload’s low byte.
- B’s offset `0x74` receives the full initial payload.
- B’s offset `0x11` receives byte `0x74`.

A word is captured from A’s offset `0x60`. B’s offset `0x11` then receives D’s low byte, B’s offset `0x34` receives the initial payload, and A’s offset `0x10` receives the low byte of the capture from A’s offset `0x60`. This last access is only a byte store.

### First referenced destinations

B’s word at offset `0x14` selects the **first referenced base**.

The routine writes the fourth explicit argument to C’s offset `0x11`, writes C as a word to B’s offset `0x44`, and writes the initial payload’s low byte to B’s offset `0x11`. It captures B’s word at offset `0x74`, then writes C’s low byte to B’s offset `0x0D`.

The following stores occur in order:

1. Word `0x00000066` to the first referenced base’s offset `0x10`.
2. C as a word to B’s offset `0x44` again.
3. Byte `0x66` to the first referenced base’s offset `0x10`.
4. Word `0x00000066` to B’s offset `0x54`.
5. Word `0x00000066` to the first referenced base’s offset `0x60`.

A fresh read of B’s word at offset `0x74` selects the **second referenced base**. B’s offset `0x74` is then replaced with C.

The second referenced base’s offset `0x40` receives word `0x00000066`. B’s offset `0x0D` receives the low byte of the earlier capture from B’s offset `0x74`. The routine captures the second referenced base’s word at offset `0x44`.

B’s offset `0x54` receives B. The early link’s offset `0x54` receives the early link itself.

The second referenced base’s word at offset `0x14` selects another destination. C’s offset `0x11` receives the low byte captured from the second referenced base’s offset `0x44`. The routine then reads C’s word at offset `0x14` and writes its low byte to the selected destination’s offset `0x15`.

Without interfering aliases, the fourth explicit argument’s byte is therefore transient.

### Root traversal and separate payload observations

A fresh word read from C’s offset `0x14` selects the **current root**.

The routine captures B’s word at offset `0x64` as the **first payload reread**, then writes word `0x00000073` to the current root’s offset `0x20`.

It reads B’s word at offset `0x64` again as the **second payload reread**, whose low byte is written to the current root’s offset `0x09`. These reads are separate; the intervening store can affect the second through aliasing.

The current root’s word at offset `0x74` selects the **next root**. Its offset `0x0D` receives B’s low byte.

The next root’s word at offset `0x54` is read and then written back to that same address. Its offset `0x09` next receives byte `0x74`. B’s offset `0x44` receives the first payload reread, and the next root’s offset `0x34` receives B.

### Branch traversal and payload replacement

The next root’s word at offset `0x04` selects the **branch base**. Its offset `0x24` receives the second payload reread. B’s offset `0x19` receives the first payload reread’s low byte.

Three words are then captured in order:

1. The **branch link**, from the branch base’s offset `0x74`.
2. The **replacement payload**, from the branch base’s offset `0x04`.
3. The **active base**, from B’s offset `0x74`.

The active base’s word at offset `0x14` selects a destination whose offset `0x54` receives the branch link. The active base’s word at offset `0x04` selects a second destination.

B’s offset `0x0D` receives the second payload reread’s low byte twice consecutively. B’s offset `0x64` receives the replacement payload. The second destination’s offset `0x30` receives word `0x00000074`.

The active base’s offset `0x04` is read again. Reading the resulting base’s word at offset `0x74` obtains the **downstream base**. This traversal uses the fresh read, not necessarily the previous destination.

A fresh word read from B’s offset `0x64` supplies a **retained payload byte** by truncation. That byte is immediately written to B’s offset `0x1D`.

The routine reads B’s word at offset `0x44`, then reads offset `0x14` of the selected base to obtain the **replacement root**. The downstream base’s offset `0x34` receives the branch link, followed by the replacement root as a word at B’s offset `0x14`.

### Downstream byte propagation

A word is captured from B’s offset `0x54`. The replacement root’s offset `0x09` receives B’s low byte.

The downstream base’s word at offset `0x04`, followed by the resulting base’s word at offset `0x74`, selects the **terminal base**. Its offset `0x15` receives the branch link’s low byte.

The routine then writes:

- B to B’s offset `0x54` as a word.
- The downstream base’s low byte to B’s offset `0x11`.
- The low byte captured earlier from B’s offset `0x54` to the terminal base’s offset `0x11`.

The terminal base’s word at offset `0x64` is captured as the **terminal payload**. B’s offset `0x09` receives the retained payload byte, without rereading its original source.

A fresh word read from the downstream base’s offset `0x04` selects the **byte-oriented base**. Its offset `0x09` receives B’s low byte. A word is captured from its offset `0x50`. Its offset `0x11` receives the terminal payload’s low byte, then its offset `0x09` receives B’s low byte again.

B’s offset `0x14` receives the terminal payload as a word. B’s offset `0x0D` receives the byte-oriented base’s low byte. Offset zero of the byte-oriented base receives the low byte captured from its offset `0x50`.

### Final linked writes

Two words are captured from the downstream base: the **late link** at offset `0x74`, then the **late root** at offset `0x14`.

The byte-oriented base’s offset `0x54` receives that base itself as a word. The late root’s word at offset `0x04` selects the **final node**.

Stores then occur in this order:

1. Byte `0x64` to the final node’s offset `0x10`.
2. Word `0x00000064` to B’s offset `0x54`.
3. Word `0x00000064` to the final node’s offset `0x40`.
4. The terminal payload’s low byte to B’s offset `0x0D`.
5. The final node’s low byte to the late root’s offset `0x09`.
6. The terminal payload as a word to B’s offset `0x24`.
7. B’s low byte to the late root’s offset `0x11`.

### Arithmetic-addressed tail

Three fixed storage locations are labeled **G1**, **G2**, and **G3**. Their names imply neither immutability nor ownership.

The routine reads G1 as a word, then reads B’s word at offset `0x74` as the **final link**.

It writes the final link’s low halfword at the address formed by adding the final link and B. It next writes the captured G1 word’s low halfword at the address formed by doubling the final node.

A byte is then read at the address formed by adding the late link and B. Its interpretation as a plain character determines U, an unsigned 32-bit value:

- Unsigned plain character: U is between `0x00000000` and `0x000000FF`.
- Signed plain character: bytes below `0x80` retain their values; bytes `0x80` through `0xFF` yield U values `0xFFFFFF80` through `0xFFFFFFFF`.

The final link’s low halfword is written at captured G1 plus final link. The halfword store at final link plus B is repeated.

Word `0x00000045` is written at address `U + 0x45`, modulo \(2^{32}\).

The routine next captures, in order, the word at G2, the word at G3, and the word at B’s offset `0x44`.

Three final stores precede the transfer:

1. Byte `0x44` at address `U + 0x44`, modulo \(2^{32}\).
2. The captured G3 word at the address formed by doubling the captured G2 word.
3. The captured G2 word’s low halfword at branch link plus captured G2.

The unsigned wrapping rules for U do not resolve overflow semantics for the other address calculations.

### Final transfer

The unresolved target **F-000000000001** receives three represented arguments:

1. U bitwise-ORed with `0x44`.
2. The captured G2 word.
3. The captured word from B’s offset `0x44`.

The first argument uses bitwise OR, not addition or dereferencing. The third remains the captured value even if one of the final stores overlaps its original location.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Incoming roles.** A is the incoming value initially stored at C’s offset `0x14`. B supplies the initial word read at offset `0x64`. C receives the first three stores. D supplies the byte stored at B’s offset `0x01` and addresses the subsequent store at offset `0x09`. No label is assigned by encounter order.

2. **Transient argument.** With noninterfering storage, C’s offset `0x54` receives the third argument and later C. Both stores remain part of the behavior.

3. **Partial replacement.** If A’s captured word at offset `0x60` has low byte `0xAB`, the later store at A’s offset `0x10` replaces one byte with `0xAB`, not the entire word.

4. **Aliased payload reread.** If the current-root store at offset `0x20` changes B’s offset `0x64`, the second payload reread observes the resulting storage, rather than reusing the first capture.

5. **Repeated accesses.** The consecutive stores of the second payload reread’s low byte to B’s offset `0x0D` remain two stores. The next root’s offset `0x54` has a read followed by a write, not an omitted operation.

6. **Character boundary.** A tail byte of `0x80` gives U equal to `0xFFFFFF80` with signed plain characters or `0x00000080` with unsigned plain characters.

7. **Unsigned wrapping.** For a tail byte of `0xFF` with signed plain characters, U is `0xFFFFFFFF`. The word containing `0x45` is addressed at `0x00000044`; the later byte containing `0x44` is addressed at `0x00000043`. With unsigned plain characters, those addresses are `0x00000144` and `0x00000143`.

8. **OR versus addition.** If U is `0x44`, the first transfer argument is `0x44`, not `0x88`.

9. **Captured argument.** If a final store changes B’s offset `0x44`, the third transfer argument still uses the earlier capture, provided execution reaches the transfer.

10. **Failure after mutation.** An inaccessible derived address can stop execution after preceding writes. No rollback or error-value guarantee follows.
