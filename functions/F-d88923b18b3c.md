## Acceptance examples

These are conditional review examples, not executed tests.

1. **Explicit unsigned-byte widening.** If Q + 5 yields byte 0xE3, the following word payload derived from it is 227, not a sign-extended negative value.

2. **Unresolved global width.** A replacement is not justified in restricting G to 0–255 merely because its source is associated with address 0x05. Acceptance requires an independently established declaration before imposing that restriction.

3. **Intermediate overwrite.** If P + 9 is externally observable, the low-byte-of-H store must precede the low-byte-of-P store. Matching only final contents is insufficient.

4. **Read before fixed write.** If the shared-base read and fixed-address 0x60 store alias under the environment’s mapping, J must reflect the earlier read, not a read moved after the 0x20 write.

5. **Mixed widths.** The three 0x3A stores remain 8-, 16-, and 32-bit operations. Three byte stores are not equivalent.

6. **Conditional extraction boundary.** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, S may equal 508 but cannot equal 511.

7. **Global reread after aliasing.** Suppose U’s low byte is 0xAB, but an intervening computed write changes the global at 0x67 to 0xCD before a later read. That later B + 9 byte store must use the value obtained by the read, not cached 0xAB.

8. **Separate later observations.** If the two later reads of 0x67 observe different values, their B + 9 stores may differ. They must not be collapsed into reuse of one retained global value.

9. **Terminal arguments.** On reaching F-000000000002, the first argument is 0x72 and the third is retained E, even if D + 0x14 has since changed.

10. **Non-returning dependency.** If an early dependency does not return, subsequent effects are not required to occur. No cleanup obligation is established.
