# F-e38b6f7b7229

## Purpose

F-e38b6f7b7229 describes a low-level operation that interleaves 22 dependency invocations with six coprocessor-transfer operations, derives arguments through fixed-width shifts, and finally attempts a 16-bit write to a calculated address.

The available static representation does not establish a higher-level purpose. In particular, it does not establish that the operation is initialization, device configuration, decoding, or memory management.

The visible sequence ends at an explicitly marked invalid-instruction boundary. Successful normal return is not established. This contract therefore describes the visible prefix and its conditional effects, not a verified executable routine with a known completion convention.

## Interface

### Entry state

The supplied representation declares no explicit parameters. Its behavior nevertheless depends on incoming machine state:

| Input | Width | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Incoming register R3 | 32 bits | Supplies two shifted arguments to a dependency. |
| Incoming register R4 | 32 bits | Supplies the first coprocessor payload and one shifted dependency argument. |
| Incoming register R5 | 32 bits, unsigned interpretation | Supplies a logical right-shifted argument. |
| Incoming link-register value | Address-width interpretation not established | Determines the first dependency target by an offset of `0xC3C`. |
| Four incoming auxiliary operands, designated C5, C9, C10, and C15 | Represented as 32-bit values | Participate in coprocessor operations. |

These names identify entry-state operands, not a verified calling convention. The representation does not establish how a caller must initialize them or whether the containing execution environment gives them their apparent register meanings.

Dependency results used by the visible sequence have either 32-bit or 64-bit representations. No evidence establishes their signedness, domain meaning, validity constraints, or ownership.

### Outputs

There is no established return value.

Visible outputs consist of:

- Six coprocessor-transfer requests.
- The ordered dependency invocations and their explicit argument bit patterns.
- A final attempted 16-bit store of `0x00CD`.
- Any additional effects caused by dependencies, which are unknown.
- Reaching the represented invalid-instruction boundary, if all preceding operations complete.

The source does not check these conditions. It provides no fallback, retry, error return, or recovery path for their failure.

## Behavior

### Arithmetic model

For describing the apparent data flow, 32-bit left shifts below mean a shift of the operand’s bit pattern with bits above bit 31 discarded. Logical right shifts insert zero bits. A 64-bit result is split numerically into its low and high 32-bit halves.

Some source expressions apply left shifts to signed integer representations. Their behavior is not generally portable as a high-level language program. The bit-vector interpretation here describes the apparent machine-level intent; verification against actual instructions is absent.

The dependency identifiers below identify invocation sites only. Different identifiers do not establish distinct implementations or distinct effective addresses.

Each later operation is conditional on earlier operations completing and returning control where required.

4. **F-000000000003.** Invoke the next dependency and retain its represented 32-bit result.

5. **Second coprocessor transfer.** Request a transfer with selectors `(0, 6, 7)`, the preceding result as payload, and auxiliary operands C9 and C15.

6. **F-000000000004.** Invoke the next dependency without explicit arguments or a consumed result.

8. **Third coprocessor transfer.** Request a transfer with selectors `(0, 5, 7)`, payload A shifted left by one, and auxiliary operands C10 and C15.

9. **F-000000000006.** Invoke with four ordered arguments:

10. **F-000000000007**, then **F-000000000008**. Invoke these dependencies in that order. Neither has explicit arguments or a consumed result in the supplied representation.

11. **Fourth coprocessor transfer.** Request a transfer with selectors `(0, 7, 7)`, zero payload, and auxiliary operands C9 and C15.

12. **Fifth coprocessor transfer.** Request a transfer with selectors `(0, 0, 7)`, zero payload, and C15 in both auxiliary positions.

13. **F-000000000009.** Invoke with one explicit argument, zero. Retain its 32-bit result as **C**.

14. **Sixth coprocessor transfer.** Request a transfer with selectors `(0, 2, 7)`, payload B shifted left by eight, and auxiliary operands C9 and C15.

15. **F-000000000010.** Invoke with three ordered arguments:
   - B shifted left by eight.
   - B shifted left by twelve.
   - C shifted left by nine.

   No result is consumed.

16. **F-000000000011.** Invoke without explicit arguments and retain a represented 64-bit result, **D**.

17. **F-000000000012.** Invoke with:
   - The low 32 bits of D.
   - The high 32 bits of D.
   - The low 32 bits of D shifted left by one.

   No result is consumed.

18. **F-000000000013.** Invoke with one zero-valued argument and retain its 32-bit result.

   This argument is necessarily zero under the stated bit-vector model: B is formed by a left shift of four, so its bottom two bits are zero. Masking those bits and shifting the result cannot produce a nonzero value.

19. **F-000000000014.** Pass the preceding 32-bit result as its sole explicit argument. Retain the new 32-bit result.

20. **F-000000000015.** Pass that new result as its sole explicit argument. No result is consumed.

21. **F-000000000016.** Invoke without explicit arguments or a consumed result.

22. **F-000000000017.** Invoke without explicit arguments and retain its represented 64-bit result, **E**.

23. **F-000000000018.** Invoke with:
   - The low 32 bits of E.
   - The high 32 bits of E.
   - The high 32 bits of E shifted left by eleven.

   Retain its represented 64-bit result, **G**.

24. **Derive the final address component.** Logically shift G right by 41 bits, yielding a value between zero and `0x7FFFFF`, inclusive. Retain this value independently of later dependency results.

25. **F-000000000019.** Invoke with the low 32 bits of G. No result is consumed.

26. **F-000000000020.** Invoke without explicit arguments and retain its represented 32-bit result.

27. **F-000000000021.** Invoke with that result as the first argument and zero as the second argument. The second argument originates from the same zero-valued masked quantity described at step 18; further shifting leaves it zero.

28. **F-000000000022.** Invoke without explicit arguments or a consumed result.

29. **Final memory write.** Take bits 52 through 63 of G as an unsigned 12-bit integer **K**. The destination address is:

   **K + floor(K / 4)**

   Attempt a 16-bit write of `0x00CD` at that address.

30. **Invalid-instruction boundary.** The visible representation terminates at a boundary explicitly marked as bad instruction data. No ordinary return or subsequent behavior is established.

### Final address properties

The apparent signed right shift used during address construction receives a nonnegative value: G shifted right by 41 fits in 23 bits. Consequently, its signed interpretation does not change the result.

The destination range is zero through `5118`, inclusive (`0x0000` through `0x13FE`). Not every address within that range is generated. Across each group of four consecutive K values, the formula generates four consecutive addresses and skips the next.

The store is 16 bits wide and may be unaligned. Its first byte is at the calculated address; an ordinary byte-addressed implementation would also access the following byte. Byte order, unaligned-access support, and the address space selected by this pointer conversion are not established.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance examples, not executed tests.

1. **Shift truncation at entry.** If incoming R3 is `0xFFFFFFFF` and R4 is `0x00000001`, F-000000000005 receives `0xFFC00000`, `0xFFFFF800`, and `0x00400000`, respectively, under the 32-bit bit-vector interpretation.

2. **Unsigned R5 shift.** If incoming R5 is `0xFFFFFFFF`, the second argument to F-000000000006 is `0x07FFFFFF`, not `0xFFFFFFFF`.

3. **Derived zero arguments.** For every 32-bit result A, the argument to F-000000000013 and the second argument to F-000000000021 are zero. A nonzero value at either position would contradict the visible arithmetic model.

4. **64-bit numerical splitting.** If F-000000000011 returns `0x0123456789ABCDEF`, F-000000000012 receives `0x89ABCDEF`, `0x01234567`, and `0x13579BDE`. This specifies numerical halves, not an in-memory byte layout.

5. **Lowest destination.** If G has all of its upper twelve bits clear, K is zero and the final store targets address zero. The representation contains no null-address guard.

6. **Address discontinuity.** If K is three, the destination is three. If K is four, the destination is five. Destination four is skipped by this transition.

7. **Highest destination.** If the upper twelve bits of G are all set, K is `4095` and the destination is `5118`.

8. **Irrelevance of lower result bits to the destination.** Two G values with identical upper twelve bits produce the same final address, even if their lower 52 bits differ. Their arguments to F-000000000019 may still differ.

9. **Interrupted execution.** If F-000000000022 does not return, the final explicit store is not reached. Earlier coprocessor and dependency effects need not be undone.
