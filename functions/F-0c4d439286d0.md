For compactness:

- A **four-call group** means four consecutive indirect calls with no displayed arguments. Only the fourth ordinary result is subsequently consumed unless stated otherwise.
- A **relay pair** means one call with no displayed arguments, followed by another call receiving that call’s word result.
- A **two-argument relay pair** additionally passes a specified second argument to the receiving call.

These descriptions preserve call occurrences. They do not mean that calls share a target, lack hidden arguments, or are free of side effects. A result not explicitly consumed may still accompany observable dependency effects.

### Unconditional repetition

After store 6, a dependency receives twice the initial loop word. A subsequent call with no displayed arguments supplies the **retained loop result** for the first iteration.

Each iteration has the following observable structure:

- A dependency receives the retained loop result and the current loop word logically shifted right eight bits.
- A four-call group occurs.
- The function freshly reads the byte at the fixed second selector-read address and writes zero at address 17 plus that byte.
- A dependency receives the latest group result shifted left two bits.
- Another four-call group occurs.
- Its final result supplies the next retained loop result by a two-bit left shift, and the next loop word by a twenty-six-bit left shift.

There is no represented exit condition.

The first iteration’s second displayed argument is always zero because the initial loop word is at most 31. Under conditional 32-bit wrapping semantics, subsequent loop words contain only the low six bits of their producing result, moved into bits 26–31. Their right shift by eight therefore has eighteen low zero bits.

The second selector-read address remains fixed across iterations. Its contents are read again on every iteration.
