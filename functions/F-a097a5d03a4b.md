## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests. They assume the 32-bit machine interpretation, ordinary successful accesses to the specified storage, and returning callees unless stated otherwise.

### Complete initial-write aliasing

With input word **4** and slot word **1**:

- The first write targets **20 + 4 × 1 = 24** and stores 4.
- The following word read targets **4 + 20 = 24**.
- It captures the just-written word 4.
- The later shifted-word write stores zero, because 4 logically shifted right by 12 is zero.

This example does not require knowing the previous contents of address 24.

### Equal inputs do not imply aliasing

With both input word and slot word equal to **4**:

- The first write targets **36** and stores 4.
- The subsequent read targets **24**.

These four-byte accesses do not overlap in the stated address model. The captured word depends on the readable contents at address 24, not on the write at address 36. The later shifted-word write is therefore not necessarily zero.

### Logical right shift

If the captured word is hexadecimal **FFFFFFFF** and the first result is **3**, the shifted-word write targets address **32** and stores hexadecimal **000FFFFF**.

Storing hexadecimal FFFFFFFF through an arithmetic right shift would not match the unsigned read and shift.

**20 + 16 × 5 = 100**
