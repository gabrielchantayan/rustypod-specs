# F-780d6593c541

## Behavior

### Gate arithmetic

For the gate calculation, interpret the second argument's bit pattern as an unsigned 32-bit value. Add three using unsigned 32-bit arithmetic, retaining only the low 32 bits. Shift that result right by two bits with zero fill.

Equivalently, the compared quantity is:

- The second argument's unsigned bit pattern plus three, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).
- Then divided by four, discarding the remainder.

The shift occurs after the addition. The resulting quantity ranges from zero through 1,073,741,823.

Compare this quantity to the widened unsigned 16-bit field. Equality passes: the gate accepts when the derived quantity is less than or equal to the field.

For nonnegative second arguments, the addition cannot overflow 32 bits because the largest signed 32-bit input is 2,147,483,647. In that nonnegative domain, the calculation is ordinary upward rounding to a multiple-of-four unit count.

That interpretation does not extend unchanged to negative inputs. Unsigned conversion and modular addition remain part of the contract; there is no preliminary negative-value rejection.
