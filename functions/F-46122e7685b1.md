# F-46122e7685b1

## Purpose

The supplied static representation establishes an interaction and memory-access contract, not an application-level purpose. It does not establish text formatting, object initialization, collection management, or a particular protocol. Character-like constants and recurring offsets do not establish those interpretations.

This document distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** displayed calls, arithmetic, access widths, value dependencies, and access order.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations not strong enough to impose behavioral requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved details that this contract does not replace with assumptions.

All completion-dependent behavior is conditional on preceding calls returning and preceding accesses succeeding.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Eleven-bit extraction.** A contributing word of 0xFFFFFFFF produces 0x001FFC00 after selecting its low eleven bits and shifting left ten places.

2. **Five-bit extraction and truncation.** Selecting bits 19–23 from 0x00F80000 yields 31. Its 31-place left shift yields 0x80000000 under 32-bit truncation; a further thirty-place left shift yields zero.

3. **Restricted small base.** An all-ones contributing word produces I = 508 and Q = 1016. Q must not be specified as an arbitrary allocated pointer.

4. **Distinct early byte stores.** If H’s low byte is 0x7E, the two early stores at B + 9 supply 0x7E and then zero. A single zero store does not preserve their access trace.

5. **Early zero read, coincident case.** With ordinary stable memory and P = Q = 0, the word at address 20 is zero when captured: both relevant preceding word stores supply zero.

6. **Early zero read, disjoint case.** With P = 0x02000000 and Q = 1016, the offset-20 ranges are disjoint. The store at P + 52 is also disjoint from P + 20. The captured word at P + 20 remains zero in ordinary stable memory.

7. **Fixed-base capture order.** An access trace must place the read at 0x087FBBBC after the last store of 32 at A + 32 and before the word store of 32 at address 96.

8. **Late overlap observes the byte store.** Suppose T + 116 is readable and C + 20 equals address 56. The C + 20 word read spans address 58 and occurs after the byte store there. Its value must reflect that store under ordinary stable-memory semantics; the byte’s numeric position depends on byte order.

9. **Repeated publication.** Final-state equality does not justify replacing repeated publication with one occurrence when individual stores are observable.

10. **No completion guarantee.** If D-003 does not return, the displayed trailing return does not establish normal completion or an error result.
