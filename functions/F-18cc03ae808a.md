## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Selection boundaries:** third inputs 0 and 127 select `0x14`; 128 selects `0x18`; `0xffffffff` selects `0x08000010`. The global sample is written twice, without a second global read.

2. **Fresh selectors:** if an initial selector yields 3, its destination is 20. If the later read yields 8, its destination is 25, regardless of the earlier value.

3. **Entry extraction:** entry word `0xffffffff` supplies `0xff` to both initial byte writes, without implying equal destinations.

4. **Primary call arguments:** with first producer 3, the initial store writes 12 at address 68, followed by the primary word read at address 32. Group 05’s first call receives, in order, 48, 12, `0x0c000000`, and 12.

5. **Secondary writeback:** secondary sample `0x89abcdef` is later written back as the full word `0x0089abcd`.

6. **Primary-derived offset:** primary sample `0x12345678` produces offset `0x0091a2b0`, not eight times only `0x56`.

7. **Distinct value and destination producers:** if the seventh producer is 2 and Group 19’s last result is 7, the relevant stores write 8 first at address 52 and later at address 48. The later write does not store 28.

8. **Highest-byte word store:** primary sample `0x89abcdef` supplies full word `0x00000089` at the second destination producer’s fourfold address, after the eighth producer’s separate sixteenfold-address store.

9. **Direct-byte boundary and argument:** a primary sample with bit 31 clear selects address 28 and gives Group 27’s first call argument 0. With bit 31 set, those values are address 29 and argument 1.

10. **Loop transition:** the first Group 30 second argument is zero. Under 32-bit wrapping semantics, a Group 31 result of 1 makes the next carried value 4 and the next Group 30 second argument `0x00040000`.

11. **Noncompletion:** if all accesses and dependencies continue normally after loop entry, calls and selector-directed zero writes continue indefinitely.
