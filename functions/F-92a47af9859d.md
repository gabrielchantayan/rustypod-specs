## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance cases, not executed tests.

1. **Entry truncation.** Two first arguments differing only above bit 6 produce the same fourth argument to the initial call under 32-bit shifting. Their unchanged first arguments still differ.

2. **Observable zero-argument work.** The early left-25/left-30 composition yields zero. The receiving call still occurs in sequence.

3. **Field placement.** A relevant low word of `0xFFFFFFFF`, masked to 18 bits and shifted left by four, produces `0x003FFFF0`.

4. **Signed reconstruction.** An original word of `0x04000000` produces `0xFC000000` after left-five followed by arithmetic-right-five, not the original pattern.

5. **Minimum destination.** If the address-driving result’s bits 52–63 are clear, the computed destination is zero regardless of its lower 52 bits. Those lower bits can still affect the first of the four final calls through the result’s low word.

6. **Skipped address.** A top 12-bit field of 3 produces destination 3; a field of 4 produces destination 5. Destination 4 is not produced.

7. **Maximum destination.** A top 12-bit field of 4095 produces destination 5118. If all intervening operations complete and the store succeeds, the stored value is the 16-bit `0x00CD`.

8. **Four-call tail.** After an address-driving result is obtained, the required sequence is: consume its low word; obtain a new word from a no-displayed-argument call; pass that word with zero; execute another no-displayed-argument call; then attempt the halfword store.

9. **Tail interruption.** If the fourth final call does not return, the store need not occur even though the destination has already been determined. No cleanup is established.

10. **Transfer interruption.** If a coprocessor operation faults, later calls and the final store need not occur.
