# F-7524c1278315

The supplied static representation establishes these mechanical effects, not a domain-level purpose. It does not establish formatting, serialization, allocation, initialization, or container management. Constants resembling characters do not establish textual output.

This contract distinguishes:

## Behavior

### Indirect-invocation phase

A long straight-line invocation phase precedes the direct mutations. The first target depends on incoming control-flow state. Most subsequent targets are constructed from fixed anchors using a common displacement of `0xffe`.

The opening sequence includes the entry-derived invocation, then an invocation receiving the incoming machine value shifted left by 12. Later calls repeatedly forward primary results shifted left by eight, sometimes alongside high-word results, saved earlier values, additional shifted values, or explicit zeroes.

Established transformation families include:

- Fixed left shifts, including shifts by 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and larger counts.
- Masks selecting low-width fields before further shifting.
- Logical right shifts of explicitly unsigned words.
- Unsigned extraction from paired results at bit positions 34, 36, and 44.
- Reuse of saved results across intervening invocations.
- Explicit zero propagation and transformations that become zero under 32-bit bit-vector semantics.

For example, extraction at bit 44 yields the high word logically shifted right by 12. Successive 32-bit left shifts must not silently retain bits in wider intermediates.

There is no represented retry loop, conditional error branch, or early successful return in this phase. An invocation without represented arguments is not necessarily argument-free under the actual ABI and is not assumed pure.

The complete invocation count, target-by-target argument schedule, and exact provenance of ambiguous secondary machine results are **documented_only** rather than fully enumerated here. The established invocation order is not permission to reorder or eliminate calls.

### Initial mutation ordering

The high word of the final pair supplies the base for a word store at offset `0x14`, receiving the secondary base. Its low byte is then stored at secondary base plus nine. A separate word destination, formed from 256 times the final pair’s low word plus `0x54`, receives the primary base. The secondary-base byte at offset nine is subsequently overwritten with the secondary base’s low byte.

The primary-base word at `0x14` is cleared before the byte at primary base plus five is read. That byte is later stored as a zero-extended word at primary base plus `0x34`. Between these operations, the doubled-selector word at `0x14` is cleared.

The primary-base word at `0x14` is read back and saved. Another clear of doubled selector plus `0x14` precedes two identical byte stores at doubled selector plus `0x15`, using the saved read-back value’s low byte. Aliasing and externally mutable memory prevent an unconditional assertion that the saved read-back word is zero.

The converted input is then obtained from the `0x05` global. The absolute byte at `0x01` receives the doubled selector’s low byte before the byte at converted input plus `0x15` receives the selector’s low byte.

The saved shifted result participates in word and byte writes, including a byte write at primary base plus `0x11` that is subsequently replaced by zero. The saved loaded word is captured from converted input plus `0x54` before that field receives the earlier primary-base read-back word.

After those writes, the global-derived base is loaded. Absolute word `0x60` then receives `0x20`, before the repeated-update region begins.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional specification examples, not executed tests.

### Different constructions with equal values

If the saved loaded word is decimal 50, the fourfold base is 200 and the adjusted value is 50. The first repeated group’s base is \(8 \times 50 = 400\); subsequent groups’ bases are also \(8 \times 50 = 400\), without overflow.

Acceptance must allow equality. It must not require the first group’s final word value or first destination to differ.

### Fresh absolute-byte observations

Suppose the saved word from `0xa9` has low byte `0x12`. The write to `0x67` uses `0x12`.

If an intervening aliased mutation changes `0x67` to `0x34` before its read for `0x21`, that copy uses `0x34`. If storage changes again before either later read for working base plus nine, each store uses its own fresh observation.

Caching `0x12` for all destinations is not equivalent.

### Converted input without byte-width evidence

If the unresolved global declaration permits a value of `0x1234`, conversion to unsigned 32-bit form preserves `0x1234`. This contract does not justify truncating it to `0x34` before computing destinations.

Whether that value is actually representable by the global remains **documented_only**.

### Observable absolute stores

Given permitted memory and observers, the word writes at absolute `0x54` and `0x60` remain required even if later aliased writes replace their contents. Byte writes at `0x01`, `0x15`, `0x3a`, `0x67`, and `0x21` likewise cannot be reduced to an unspecified storage footprint.

### Width and overwrite preservation

Reaching the literal region requires distinct 8-, 16-, and 32-bit stores of `0x3a`. The working-base word sequence must preserve `0x20`, `0x2f`, the freshly loaded byte value, and `0x20`, including the byte read.

### Unsigned extraction and truncation

A high word of `0x80000000`, logically shifted right by three, produces `0x10000000`, not a sign-extended value.

Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, a word shifted left by 28 and then left by another 28 becomes zero. A retained 56-bit intermediate is not equivalent.

### Failure and final arguments

A non-returning opening invocation does not require later direct writes or rollback.

If execution reaches the final invocation, its saved arguments must retain their captured values despite intervening aliased mutations. Reloading all argument sources at transfer time is not generally equivalent.
