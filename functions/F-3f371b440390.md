# F-3f371b440390

## Purpose

F-3f371b440390 performs an ordered transformation of interconnected, addressable state and then transfers control through external dependencies. The supplied static evidence establishes memory-access behavior, but not an application-level operation, a conventional data structure, or a normal completion outcome.

The observable contract includes access widths, retained observations, repeated reads, and ordering. Final memory contents alone are insufficient: aliasing can make an intervening write affect a later read, including a read from a designated global source.

All effects are conditional on execution reaching them. No validation, rollback, or recovery behavior is established.

Routing bytes have unresolved plain-byte signedness. Bits `0xFF`, for example, may promote to −1 or 255. That distinction affects both stored halfwords and subsequent addresses.

## Behavior

### Contract vocabulary

The main transformation has eleven fixed **routing phases**, followed by a product-addressed tail.

Each phase has its own designated global word source. Two distinct observations of that source are required:

- The first supplies the phase’s **route**.
- The second supplies a word published at another base’s offset 68.

The first observation precedes the second; the second precedes its publication. The retained route is not replaced by the second observation. Earlier writes may affect either observation.

The following retained values connect phases:

- **Early reference:** an entry-time word later used for an offset-100 read.
- **Anchor:** a word acquired during phase 1 and reused as an address base.
- **Early payload:** a phase-1 route-relative word.
- **Continuing payload:** a phase-5 route-relative word.
- **Sample:** a retained anchor-offset-100 observation.
- **Field:** a phase-specific context-offset-68 observation.
- **Carry:** a retained word supplied to a later phase.

Retention means reuse of an observed value, not a live view of its original location. It establishes no ownership or lifetime extension.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes valid preceding accesses, suitable arithmetic and representation semantics, and execution reaching the identified event.

1. **Entry retention.** With input 4 equal to `0xA7` and the input-1-offset-100 observation equal to `0x12345678`, the designated entry context-byte payloads are `0xA7`, `0x78`, and `0x78`. Later changes to the original word do not replace the captured payload.

2. **Signed selector boundary.** Selector bits `0xFFFF` represent −1, not 65535. This interpretation applies to selector-relative addressing and the final narrow base.

3. **Routing-byte promotion.** Routing-byte bits `0xFF` may promote to −1 or 255. Subject to conversion semantics, the corresponding halfword bits are `0xFFFF` or `0x00FF`; the following publication base also differs.

4. **Payload is not a dereference.** A phase-5 incoming carry whose low byte is `0x42` supplies byte `0x42` at promoted-byte offset 13. No incoming-carry-offset-13 read is required.

5. **Replacement identity.** With distinct valid locations, phase 7 follows old-context offset 20; phases 9 and 10 follow selector offset 20. Route-offset-20 replacements would violate the contract.

6. **Aliasing-sensitive carry.** If phase-6 writes overlap route offset 68, outgoing carry reflects the later word observation, not an assumed copy of the continuing payload.

7. **Independent shifts.** For anchor `0x123`, assuming valid shifts, the four-bit and eight-bit captures are `0x1230` and `0x12300`. The eight-bit family is not derived by shifting the four-bit result again.

8. **Unresolved tail payload.** Neither zero nor the tail result’s low byte is established for the byte write following the fourth narrow-base publication.

9. **Dependency mutation.** If F-U1 changes the later global source and returns, F-cdec09f5d432 receives the subsequent observation. If F-U1 does not return, the post-call assignments are not reached.
