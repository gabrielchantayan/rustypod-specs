# F-a016e2527d4a

### Widths and arithmetic

The explicitly represented accesses establish two 16-bit writes, three 8-bit writes, and four 32-bit writes. The remaining write, to G, has an unresolved width.

The literal 0xEAEC does not establish G’s width. In particular, it is insufficient evidence for classifying the sixth operation as a 16-bit write.

Narrow writes retain the stated low-order bits of their source values. This describes the stored bit pattern without assigning an unsigned interpretation to every intermediate expression.

Several destination calculations require special care:

- Multiplication by two or sixteen may exceed the signed 32-bit range.
- Adding offsets or subtracting constants may also exceed that range.
- A left shift by 16 or 29 positions is not automatically valid signed arithmetic merely because the shift count is below 32.
- Negative input values and nonrepresentable shifted results require semantics not established by the fragment alone.
- Converting an integer result into a destination address requires the target’s address-conversion rules.

Under the explicitly conditional 32-bit wrapping model:

These properties do not establish that the corresponding addresses are mapped, writable, or suitably aligned. In particular, E is not necessarily aligned for a 32-bit access.

- R = 0x00001000;
- W = 0x89ABCDEF;

- P = 0x20000040;
