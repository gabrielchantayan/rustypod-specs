# F-75007d99bc32

## Behavior

### Execution structure

The represented path consists of an invocation-heavy preparation phase, a direct memory-mutation phase, and the final indirect transfer. No explicit data-dependent branch, loop, retry, or recoverable-error path appears in the supplied representation.

Repeated accesses are distinct represented operations. A fault or nonreturning dependency prevents later operations from being reached. Dependencies may themselves branch, mutate state, or fail.

### Dependency preparation

The preparation phase repeatedly invokes calculated targets and passes transformed results to subsequent invocations. Visible argument groups include:

- A low result component shifted left eight, its high component unchanged, and that high component shifted left six.
- Single shifted words, duplicated arguments, and explicit zero arguments.
- Retained primary or secondary result words shifted by various counts through 31.
- Masked fields followed by left or logical right shifts.
- Fields extracted from earlier combined results rather than necessarily the most recent result.

The visible masks include low fields of 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, and 24 bits. Ordering of masking and shifting is significant. For example, selecting a low 20-bit field, shifting right ten, then left six places the selected ten-bit field in bits 6–15.

An unsigned combined result shifted right 36 selects its high component logically shifted right four. Other represented extractions shift combined results right 34 or 44; these likewise are not shifts of the low component alone.

Some retained quantities are explicitly zeroed and later shifted. Their resulting visible arguments remain zero under ordinary fixed-width interpretation, but the associated dependency invocations remain observable.

### Initial direct mutations

For descriptive purposes, the **large shifted base** is a late dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000. The **masked quantity** is obtained by shifting another result left eight, selecting its low 15 bits, and shifting logically right six. The **small base** is twice that masked quantity.

Conditional on the represented intermediate shift producing a word value, the masked quantity is a multiple of four from 0 to 508. The small base is consequently a multiple of eight from 0 to 1016.

After the final preparatory invocation, the initial memory effects have this order:

- A retained word is stored at offset 0x14 from the final result’s high component.
- The low byte of that high component is stored at offset 9 from another retained base.
- A retained highly shifted word is stored at an address formed from the final result’s low component, scaled by 0x100, plus 0x54.
- The same offset-9 byte destination is overwritten with a retained word’s low byte.
- The word at large shifted base + 0x14 is cleared before the byte at large shifted base + 5 is loaded.

The small-base word at offset 0x14 is then cleared, the loaded byte is widened and stored at large shifted base + 0x34, and the word at large shifted base + 0x14 is reloaded. Another clear at small base + 0x14 precedes two byte stores at small base + 0x15 using the reloaded word’s low byte.

These operations must not be reduced to final values. Calculated addresses can alias, and the byte at offset 0x15 overlaps the four-byte range beginning at offset 0x14.

A byte is subsequently read from fixed address 0x05 and widened into another base. Fixed address 0x01 receives the small base’s low byte.

### Linked and scaled mutations

The byte-derived base participates in a word load at offset 0x54. Call the captured result the **linked seed**. Subsequent operations overwrite that same offset-0x54 location; the linked seed remains the earlier captured value.

This region interleaves:

- Word traversal at offsets 0x04, 0x14, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.
- Word stores at offsets 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54.
- Byte stores at offsets 0x09, 0x0d, 0x11, and 0x15.
- Doubled, quadrupled, and eightfold forms of captured words.

Two word reads at small base + 0x74 supply, respectively, an address and the value for a nested offset-0x54 store. Both reads are represented; neither may be replaced by an assumed common value without additional memory assumptions.

The fixed-address mutations within this region have specific ordering:

- After repeated self-referential offset-0x24 stores through the linked seed and offset-9 stores through another loaded base, small base + 0x14 receives a dependency-derived word shifted left 26.
- **Fixed address 0x54 then receives a word equal to the linked seed shifted left once.**
- Small base + 0x14 subsequently receives the linked seed twice.
- Two byte stores at small base + 0x15 receive the low byte of four times the linked seed.
- **Fixed address 0x15 then receives the low byte of the small base**, which is the doubled masked quantity.
- A derived offset-0x54 word store through four times the linked seed follows, before an offset-0x34 word store through the small base.

The fixed 0x54 write is a 32-bit access; the fixed 0x15 write is an 8-bit access. They are not merely shorthand for the surrounding derived-address fields. The signed-shift and multiplication qualifications remain applicable.

Later traversal uses unsigned bytes loaded at offset 5 as numerical bases for offset-0x74 word reads. One offset-5 byte is loaded twice at distinct points separated by writes. No stable object layout or cached-byte equivalence is established.

A later offset-0x74 word read supplies the **terminal link** used below. Twice the terminal link becomes the **tail base**.

### Tail mutations and final transfer

Before further updates, it captures words from tail base + 0x14 and companion base + 4. Later effects include writing byte 0x6c at companion base + 8 and storing the tail base into its own offset-0x54 field.

The accesses involving fixed address 0x67 are distinct:

1. Fixed address 0x67 receives the low byte of the word previously read at 0xa9.
2. A word read at tail base + 0x44 occurs before the byte at 0x67 is read and propagated to fixed address 0x21.
3. After additional mutations—including two writes at publication base + 0x54, a word load at companion base + 0x40, and stores at publication base + 0x74, companion base + 0x10, and publication base + 0x44—the byte at 0x67 is read again for a byte store at companion base + 9.
