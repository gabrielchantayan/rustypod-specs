# F-dca73609ec09

## Purpose

F-dca73609ec09 constructs a multiword arithmetic result in private workspace, adjusts that result through a comparison-driven reduction loop, and copies it to a caller-provided destination.

The arithmetic has the structure of a radix-\(2^{32}\) Montgomery squaring operation: each operand word selects a multiplication row, a context word selects a modulus-cancellation multiple, and each row discards one low word. This mathematical interpretation is **inferred intent**, not an unconditional contract. The supplied material does not establish the representation constants, the context’s mathematical validity, or the behavior of the comparison and mutation dependencies.

The established contract is the sequence of word-level arithmetic, workspace updates, dependency calls, and destination writes described below. There is no explicit return value or status result.

### Representation factors

Four global 32-bit quantities affect interpretation:

- **Extent factor:** multiplies object headers before their signs are examined.
- **Decode factor:** multiplies stored data words before arithmetic.
- **Header factor:** multiplies the final provisional word count.
- **Encode factor:** multiplies arithmetic words before they are stored in the accumulator.

Their values are not supplied. They must not be silently treated as one, as inverses, or as immutable throughout dependency calls.

A stored data word \(w\) contributes the low 32 bits of its product with the decode factor. An accumulator write similarly stores the low 32 bits of the arithmetic value multiplied by the encode factor.

### Widths and workspace

Established arithmetic widths are:

The private accumulator has 130 data-word slots. A provisional header is immediately before those slots and is passed together with them as a multiword object. A separate 524-byte private region is passed to dependencies as a second object.

## Behavior

### 1. Read and classify the headers

The function first derives the modulus extent from the extent factor and the first modulus word. It replaces a negative result with its negation.

It then derives the operand extent from the extent factor and the first operand word.

This ordering matters: even when the operand extent is zero, the modulus header has already been read and its extent arithmetic performed.

If the operand extent is zero:

- exactly one 32-bit zero word is written directly to the destination;
- no arithmetic-context word is read by the visible body;
- no operand data word is read by the visible body;
- none of the four dependencies is called;
- the function returns.

No clearing of destination words beyond the first is visible.

For a nonzero operand extent, its sign is also discarded by negation when negative. Consequently, representable positive and negative extent headers with equal decoded magnitude select the same number of arithmetic rounds. This does not establish a general signed-number representation contract.

### 2. Initialize the accumulator

Let \(n\) be the absolute decoded modulus extent and \(m\) the absolute decoded nonzero operand extent.

Accumulator data words at indices 0 through \(n\), inclusive, are initialized to zero. The extra word at index \(n\) holds the upper carry state.

The first operand data word is decoded and retained for use in the low-word calculation of every multiplication row.

Only the stated accumulator range is explicitly initialized. The separate 524-byte region is not explicitly initialized before it is passed to F-bcd78fb452c3.

### 3. Perform operand-driven multiplication and cancellation rows

There are \(m\) outer multiplication rows.

Each row decodes the corresponding operand data word. The low-word calculation combines:

- that row’s operand word multiplied by the retained first operand word; and
- the decoded current low accumulator word.

The resulting 64-bit sum is separated into low and high 32-bit components.

The low component is multiplied by the 32-bit context word at byte offset 8. Only the low 32 bits of this product become the row’s cancellation coefficient.

The first modulus data word is multiplied by that coefficient. The high product word and the carry from adding the low product word to the previously obtained low component establish the initial cancellation carry. The combined low word is not stored as an output word.

For subsequent operand positions, the row combines:

- the decoded accumulator word at that position;
- the multiplication carry;
- the decoded operand word at that position multiplied by the row operand word;
- the cancellation carry; and
- the cancellation coefficient multiplied by the corresponding decoded modulus word.

The combined low word is encoded and written one accumulator position lower. Multiplication and cancellation carries are maintained separately.

If operand positions have ended but positions below the modulus extent remain, processing continues without an operand-product contribution. Those positions still combine the existing accumulator, multiplication carry, cancellation carry, and modulus multiple.

At the row’s upper boundary:

- the remaining two carries and decoded extra accumulator word determine the encoded word at index \(n-1\);
- the carries from those additions determine the encoded extra word at index \(n\).

Thus, under the intended extent relationship, each row advances the multiplication while shifting the accumulated result down by one 32-bit position.

The implementation does not check that \(m \le n\). If \(m>n\), the operand-driven inner loop still follows the operand extent, while the final row stores remain indexed by \(n\). Such inputs must not be assigned the normal modular-squaring meaning without additional evidence.

### 4. Perform remaining cancellation-only rows

When \(m<n\), another \(n-m\) outer rows are performed. When \(m\ge n\), this phase is skipped.

Each such row obtains a cancellation coefficient from the decoded low accumulator word and the context word at byte offset 8. It combines that multiple of the modulus with the accumulator, propagates unsigned carries, and stores encoded low words one position lower.

The final two stores again update the word at index \(n-1\) and the extra word at index \(n\).

The context field is read during successive outer rows rather than being visibly cached once for the whole operation. There are no dependency calls during these arithmetic phases.

### 5. Construct the provisional object

Starting at the original modulus extent, the function examines decoded accumulator words downward. It decreases the index while:

- the index is greater than zero; and
- the decoded accumulator word at that index is zero.

The provisional header is the header factor multiplied by one more than the resulting index.

This construction retains at least one data word. In particular, an entirely zero accumulator is presented to later dependencies with a provisional one-word extent, scaled by the header factor. The body does not directly change that representation to a zero header.

### 6. Prepare the adjustment object

F-bcd78fb452c3 is called with:

1. the separate private region as destination;
2. the modulus-like object as source.

The call’s shape suggests an object-copy operation. Its actual initialization, allocation, metadata, ownership, and normalization behavior are not supplied.

A direction state is initialized to positive one.

### 7. Adjust until comparison reports below the modulus

The function repeatedly calls F-08a778c50842 with the provisional result first and the modulus-like object second.

A negative return ends this loop. A zero or positive return enters an adjustment iteration.

Within an iteration, the provisional result is compared with the private adjustment object:

- If that comparison is negative, the direction becomes negative one. No F-aa5c6722c7c4 call occurs in that branch.
- Otherwise, F-aa5c6722c7c4 is called with the provisional result first and the private adjustment object second.

The direction does not visibly return to positive one after becoming negative.

The adjustment object is then conditionally passed to F-796113959089 with the current direction:

- In positive direction, after the nonnegative inner comparison and F-aa5c6722c7c4 call, this mutation occurs unconditionally.
- In negative direction, the adjustment object is first compared with the modulus-like object. The mutation occurs only if that comparison returns strictly positive.

The outer result-versus-modulus comparison is then repeated.

The dependency contracts are essential to termination. The visible body imposes no iteration limit, progress check, cancellation, or fallback.

### 8. Publish the result

After the outer comparison returns negative, F-bcd78fb452c3 is called with the caller’s destination first and the provisional result second.

The function then returns without an explicit result value.

The nonzero path therefore does not directly publish intermediate accumulator states through the destination. The final copy dependency controls the observable destination representation and write extent.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not observed executions.

### Zero operand extent

- the destination’s first word becomes zero;
- later destination words are not directly touched;
- the context and operand data words are not directly read;
- no dependency call occurs.

The modulus-header read still precedes this outcome.

### Equal-magnitude signed extents

This criterion excludes the most-negative signed 32-bit value and overflowing header multiplication.

### Operand shorter than modulus

This is a statement about processing extent, not proof of a numeric result.

- the initial modulus-to-adjustment call has already occurred;
