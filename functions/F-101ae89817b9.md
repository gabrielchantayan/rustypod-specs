## Side effects

Direct effects include all specified byte, halfword, and word reads and writes; an unresolved-width first-global clear; a full-word second-global replacement; and its terminal byte/24-bit-fragment replacement.

Aliasing is not excluded. Selector sources and destinations, direct fields, indexed storage, globals, and global fragments may overlap. The terminal destination-byte clear may therefore affect the subsequently read 24-bit fragment if they alias.

Argument reads complete before the receiving dependency is invoked. Unknown dependencies may modify later-read memory, including globals and selector bytes. A captured value remains the earlier value despite those modifications.

No explicit allocation, release, reference-count operation, or ownership transfer is established. Passing an address does not establish ownership, retention permission, or a release obligation. The stack-relative address is invocation-relative storage; safe use beyond that invocation requires an external contract not supplied here.

No synchronization, transaction boundary, or rollback is represented.

10. **Byte truncation.** A word value of 65 shifted left by two gives 260 under modular arithmetic. Its byte store writes 4, while a word argument can still contain 260.

12. **Repeated field copies.** An offset-116 word of 0x123456AB produces byte 0xAB at offset one. A later specified copy rereads the word and may copy a different byte.

13. **Terminal concatenation.** A final byte of 0xAB and then-read 24-bit fragment of 0x123456 produce second-global value 0x123456AB. This specifies bit placement, not memory byte order.

14. **Terminal failure.** If the address-zero call faults, the destination-byte clear, concatenated global replacement, and zero-halfword store have already occurred, provided their accesses completed.
