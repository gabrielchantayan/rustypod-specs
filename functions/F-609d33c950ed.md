# F-609d33c950ed

## Purpose

F-609d33c950ed performs an ordered sequence of computed memory accesses and unresolved dependency calls. It accepts three 32-bit arguments, directly mutates byte-, halfword-, and word-sized storage, updates two global locations, and finally attempts a call to address zero with three zero arguments.

The supplied static representation establishes data dependencies, access widths, and important sampling boundaries. It does not establish an application-level purpose. Allocation, initialization, reference management, dispatch, and cleanup are not established semantics.

All operations described below are conditional on preceding accesses completing and dependencies returning control. No runtime tests have been performed.

### Widths and address descriptions

A **word** is 32 bits, a **halfword** is 16 bits, and a **byte** is 8 bits.

For address descriptions:

- **S4(x)** means four times the selecting value, plus 0x14.
- **S16(x)** means sixteen times the selecting value, plus 0x14.
- **B(x)** means reading an unsigned byte at address x, then selecting the byte destination at that byte value plus 0x11.

B(x) therefore selects an address from 0x11 through 0x110. It does not load a pointer-sized address from x.

Byte stores retain only the low eight bits of their supplied value. An unsigned byte retained as a 32-bit value is zero-extended. Unsigned right shifts are logical. Splitting a represented 64-bit result produces its low and high 32-bit portions without establishing a memory layout or byte order.

These formulas do not establish arrays, object boundaries, or valid mapped addresses.

## Behavior

### 1. Initial mutation and word-slot processing

The first direct effect is a word store of the first argument at S4(first argument). It precedes every dependency call.

F-U001 is then attempted at a target derived from the incoming return-link value plus 0xFFE. Its four explicit arguments are, in order: first argument, first argument, third argument, first argument.

Subsequent unresolved call sequences produce selectors for three ordered word mutations:

1. Store the first argument at S4(a returned selector).
2. Store four times a later returned selector at S16(that selector).
3. Store zero at S4(another returned selector).

Calls between these stores remain observable and cannot be treated as transparent.

### 2. First retained selector and byte-selected writes

A later 32-bit result becomes a retained selector. Its masked value, using mask 0x3FFFFFFF, supplies several selector-byte addresses.

In order, with dependencies interposed, the routine:

- Stores the low byte of four times the retained selector through B(masked selector shifted right by 16, plus 1).
- Clears B(masked selector shifted right by 17, plus 1).
- Supplies a newly returned value shifted left by two to a call.
- Supplies another result shifted left by two and the third argument shifted left by 31 to a call.
- Reads an unsigned byte directly at the retained selector shifted left by three, then passes that byte four times to one dependency.
- Clears B(masked selector shifted right by seven, plus 1).
- Reads the current byte at absolute address 1 and clears the destination at that byte plus 0x11.

Later calls receive two equal scaled results together with the retained direct-read byte shifted left by 31, and a secondary output logically shifted right by 18.

The address-1 access here is a fresh sample for its own clear. It is not the retained address-1 sample used much later around the first G-B assignment.

### 3. Split results, indirect reads, and stack exposure

Further dependency results drive byte-selected clears and indirect loads.

A represented 64-bit result is split. Its high half is forwarded to a dependency while its low half is retained. A later word read uses the retained low half plus an offset obtained by shifting that low half right by 19 and then left by 31. Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, that offset is zero or 0x80000000, determined by original bit 19.

Other established operations in this phase include:

- A byte read at a secondary-output-derived address plus 0x0E, passed four times to a dependency.
- A clear through B(a retained result multiplied by 0x1000, plus 1).
- A call receiving one secondary output in argument positions one, two, and four, and its logical right shift by 19 in position three.
- A call receiving the same stack-frame address in all four explicit positions.

No initialization of the exposed stack storage is visible. Its extent and the dependency’s access pattern are unknown.

After further calls and split-result processing, a byte is read at the sum of a retained low half and a secondary output. That byte is forwarded four times. A later **halfword** read uses another secondary output shifted right by 19, plus that retained byte; the halfword is also forwarded four times. The access remains 16 bits despite the byte-sized address contribution.

### 4. Word slots and offset-0x0D writes

A subsequent split result supplies a high-half selector for a word store at S4(high half). The stored value is a retained secondary output shifted right by 19. A call receives the split result’s low half, high half, and zero. After more calls, the same retained shifted value is stored at another dependency-selected S4 destination.

The split result’s retained low half supplies a base for two byte writes at offset 0x0D:

1. The low byte of twice that base.
2. The constant 4.

Dependencies and a word read separate these writes. The first value is therefore observable before replacement.

The intervening word read uses four times a retained selector plus 0x44. After the constant-4 write, another call receives a word read at the masked selector shifted right by 20, plus 0x44.

Further stores include:

- Four times the retained selector at a destination formed from a dependency result multiplied by 0x100, plus 0x14.
- A dependency result masked with 0x03FFFFFF and shifted right by 13 at an S4 destination.
- Repeated S16 stores of four times their selecting result.
- S4 stores carrying earlier scaled values.

Offsets alone do not establish named fields or object layouts.

### 5. Offset-0x24 and offset-0x74 processing

The routine clears a word at a scaled base plus 0x24, then reads a word at the same scaled base plus 0x74. That loaded word becomes a retained address.

After calls, another result shifted right by 20 is stored at the retained address plus 0x24. A word at that retained address plus 0x74 is then read and forwarded.

The routine subsequently stores a truncated masked-and-shifted value through B(retained address plus 1). After further calls, it reads the selector byte again and clears B(retained address plus 1). The second selector read is fresh: an intervening change can redirect the clear.

Three distinct bytes are read at dependency-derived addresses plus 0x15. Their bases are not established as identical. The final byte is later zero-extended and stored as a word through a secondary-output-selected S4 destination.

Several clears use B(a retained right-by-20 value multiplied by 0x400, plus 1). Calls also receive that retained value shifted right by another 21 bits. Because the first shift leaves at most 12 significant bits, the further-shifted argument is necessarily zero.

### 6. Repeated scaled stores and explicit G-A clear

The routine also reads words at computed addresses plus 0x14 and forwards extracted upper bits.

G-A is explicitly cleared. This does not establish its value at either later G-A sampling point: unresolved calls and computed stores intervene.

Several retained unsigned values are explicitly set to zero. Expressions derived solely from those retained zeros produce zero arguments or selector-byte reads at address 1. Such local conclusions do not permit replacing global reads or fresh memory reads with constants.

### 7. Chained reads and the first G-A snapshot

A retained dependency result is passed twice to F-U003. Later, a byte is read at that result plus 1; another byte is subsequently read at the first byte’s retained value plus 1. The second address lies between 1 and 256.

Subsequent offset-0x14 accesses mix scaled and unscaled bases. They must remain distinct.

- A dependency-selected S4 word slot receives a retained earlier result shifted left by two.
- **Immediately after that store, before the following dependency sequence, G-A is read and its 32-bit value retained.** If the store aliases G-A, the sample observes the post-store contents.
- Five dependency calls intervene before that retained sample is shifted right by 23.
- Another retained indirect-load value is shifted right by 22 and stored through a secondary-output-selected S4 destination.

### 8. Retained address-1 sample, first G-B assignment, and second G-A snapshot

After additional calls and byte-selected writes, the routine makes a distinct **retained address-1 sample**:

- It reads the byte at absolute address 1 and zero-extends it into a retained 32-bit base.
- It immediately clears the byte at that retained base plus 0x11.
- Seven dependency calls occur before a later 32-bit result is assigned to G-B.
- That same result is passed to another dependency; one further dependency call follows.
- Only then does the routine read a **word at the retained base plus 0x74** and write its low byte at **the retained base plus 1**.

The base for all three accesses is the single captured address-1 byte. It is not recomputed after calls or the G-B assignment. Changes to address 1 may affect other fresh reads, but do not redirect this word-to-byte transfer. The word at retained base plus 0x74 is nevertheless sampled late, after the intervening calls.

The routine next performs word-to-byte transfers at additional dependency-derived bases: each reads a word at base plus 0x74 and stores its low byte at base plus 1. One retained base is processed twice with calls between operations; both word reads are required.

A later offset-0x74 word is retained and stored through both an S16 destination and an S4 destination. Other mutations include a store at an address formed by clearing a retained scaled value’s low two bits and adding 0x14, followed by explicit zero stores.

**The second G-A snapshot also has a specific boundary:**

- A represented 64-bit result is split. Four times its low half is stored at S4(its high half).
- **Immediately after that store, G-A is read and retained as an address.**
- Five dependency calls follow.
- Another dependency-selected S4 word slot receives the same scaled low-half value.
- **After that intervening store, a word is read at the captured G-A address plus 0x14.**

The indirect read uses the captured address, not a fresh G-A value. Its contents are sampled after the dependencies and intervening word-slot store. Thus the address is retained early, while the pointed-to word is read later. The intervening store may change G-A or the pointed-to word through aliasing; neither possibility permits moving either read.

### 9. Terminal sequence

The word obtained through the second G-A snapshot supplies two later byte-selected stores:

- Its low byte is stored through a selector derived from a previously retained offset-0x74 word.
- After additional calls and split-result processing, its right-by-22 value is truncated and stored through another byte-selected destination.

Further calls include one target calculation using displacement 0xB0 rather than the prevalent 0xFFE.

The final ordered direct effects are:

1. Obtain a byte-sized dependency result and a secondary-output-derived byte destination.
2. Clear that destination.
3. Replace all 32 bits of G-B with a value whose high 24 bits come from the three-byte quantity associated with absolute address 9 and whose low eight bits are the newly obtained result.
4. Clear a halfword at the address formed by masking a retained split-result low half with 0x3FFFFFFF and shifting right by seven.
5. Attempt F-U004 at address zero with three zero arguments.

The final G-B assignment is a full replacement, not preservation of the earlier G-B high bytes. Normal return is represented only after F-U004 returns.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static requirements, not executed tests.

1. **Initial store:** With first argument 3, the initial successful mutation stores word 3 at address 0x20, before F-U001.

2. **Byte selection:** A selector byte of 0xFE selects destination 0x10F. A supplied value 0x1234 stores byte 0x34 there.

3. **Third-argument shift:** Under 32-bit machine-shift semantics, third arguments 2 and 3 contribute 0 and 0x80000000 respectively to the left-by-31 call argument.

4. **Fresh selector:** If a dependency changes a selector byte between a store and a clear, the later fresh selector read can redirect the clear.

5. **First G-A snapshot:** If G-A is 0xFF800000 immediately after the specified word-slot store, then a following dependency changes G-A to zero, the retained right-by-23 value remains 0x1FF. Its further right-by-23 value is zero.

6. **Second G-A snapshot:** If the immediate post-split-store G-A sample is 0x2000, a dependency later changes G-A to 0x3000, and the intervening store updates word address 0x2014, the indirect read uses the then-current word at 0x2014—not 0x3014 and not an earlier 0x2014 value.

8. **Word-to-byte width:** A relevant offset-0x74 word value of 0x123456AB produces byte 0xAB at offset 1.

9. **Terminal fault:** If the address-zero call faults, the terminal byte clear, full G-B assignment, and halfword clear precede that attempt. No normal return is required.
