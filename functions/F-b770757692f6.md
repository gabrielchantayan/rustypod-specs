## Behavior

The opening phase is a long, straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. The first destination uses the entry control-link value plus 0xFFE. Later destinations use represented control-location values plus the same displacement.

These are destination calculations; they do not establish a memory-resident function-pointer table. Distinct call sites are not established to target the same dependency.

Invocation order remains significant:

- Repeated arguments, zero arguments, or equal transformed results do not make invocations redundant.
- Values explicitly retained across calls are not replaced with later result-channel contents.
- No unresolved dependency is assumed to preserve globals or own, retain, or release memory.

Argument preparation uses low and high portions of paired results, saved words, shifts, and masks. Common operations include an eight-bit left shift of a low result word and six- or seven-bit shifts of other contributors. Other represented shifts extend through 31 bits. Masks include 24-, 22-, 21-, 20-, 19-, 18-, 17-, 16-, 15-, 14-, 13-, 12-, and 11-bit fields.

Transformation order matters. A mask applied before a shift is not generally interchangeable with one applied afterward. A right shift following a truncating left shift cannot recover discarded bits.

Some retained intermediates are explicitly set to zero. Their subsequent represented shifts remain zero, but calls receiving those values remain observable.

No branch or loop is represented in this phase. Dependencies may themselves branch, fail, or fail to return; their internal behavior is unresolved. This contract does not claim a fully resolved per-invocation dependency trace.

### Entry into direct memory modification

The direct-memory phase uses several retained contributors:

- A result scaled by \(2^{25}\) as a computed region base.
- A separate saved result shifted left eight bits.
- Another saved result shifted left 25 bits.
- A selector formed by shifting a result left eight bits, masking to 15 bits, and shifting right six bits.
- The last paired result’s low and high words.

Under the 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, the selector is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. Its doubled value is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016.

The first memory effects have a specific ordering and payload provenance:

1. A word store at the last paired result’s high word plus 0x14 publishes the saved 25-bit-shifted word.
2. A byte store at that saved shifted address plus 0x09 uses the low byte of the last paired result’s **high word**.
3. A word store at the last paired result’s low word scaled by 256, plus 0x54, publishes the separately retained scaled region base.
4. The earlier byte destination is written again, now using the low byte of the **saved shifted word**.

The two byte payloads have different provenance. Their numerical values are not established to differ. Both writes remain part of the access history even if the payloads are equal.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Equal early byte payloads.** If the last paired result’s high word and the saved shifted word have equal low bytes, both early byte stores write the same value. Two ordered writes remain required; differing provenance does not require differing values.

2. **Unsigned promotion.** If an unsigned byte read yields 0xE3, its word-sized promotion is 0x000000E3, not 0xFFFFFFE3.

3. **Selector boundary.** Under the 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, an input word of 0xFFFFFFFF produces selector 508 and doubled base 1016.

4. **Shift truncation.** Under that interpretation, shifting 0x01000000 left eight bits yields zero. A later right shift cannot recover its discarded bit.

5. **Constant-store aliasing.** If working-base +0x04 overlaps the preceding +0x20 stores, the promoted byte reflects the read after the 0x20 and 0x2F stores, not an entry snapshot.

6. **Publication-base capture.** If the shared publication-base source overlaps the word store at absolute 0x60, later publications still use the base captured before that store. Re-reading the modified source would change the represented behavior.

7. **First fresh 0x67 read.** If the intervening linked-base +0x44 read has no effect and ordinary byte memory at 0x67 remains unchanged, the write to 0x21 receives the published low byte. That equality depends on the stated conditions.

8. **Different later 0x67 payloads.** If the first computed copy reads 0x12, and the intervening linked-base +0x09 byte store aliases 0x67 and writes 0xA5, the second computed copy reads 0xA5, provided the intervening word store and read do not change that byte.

9. **Repeated observable stores.** For write-observable storage, identical publication groups remain multiple ordered effects, not one final-state update.

10. **Interrupted execution.** If an access fails before the final transfer, neither the transfer nor restoration of earlier writes is required.

11. **Final argument provenance.** If execution reaches the final destination, its first displayed argument is 0x72. Its other arguments use the specified retained reads, not convenient fresh reads of those locations.
