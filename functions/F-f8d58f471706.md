## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static consequences, not runtime observations.

1. **Selector-derived addresses.** If the entry selector is 255, the first byte destination is 272 and the initial word destination is 275. Neither adds the selector-read base.

2. **Retained selector.** If the initial selector is 7 and intervening calls change its memory location to 9, the initial word destination remains 27. A later fresh read may observe 9.

3. **Word truncation.** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, a third input of 0x40000001 produces 0x00000004 for the initial word store.

4. **Byte truncation.** A retained shared word of 0xFFC00000 shifts right by 22 to 1023. Its byte store writes 255.

5. **Payload projection.** A first payload of 0x3FF00000 produces 1023 after the specified mask and shift. The projected byte is 255, not a ten-bit stored field.

6. **Wide-result ordering.** Distinct low and high halves remain distinguishable: scaling uses the low half, while calls forwarding both halves receive low first and high second.

7. **Mutation after clear.** A dependency changing updated address-like state plus 1 after its direct clear affects the later tail selector read. The clear does not justify replacing that read with zero.

8. **Tail assignment ordering.** If the final dependency does not return, the code-address-state assignment has already occurred in the represented sequence. No next iteration follows unless control returns and reaches the back-edge.

9. **Earlier nonreturn.** If the second of the two calls immediately preceding that assignment does not return, the assignment and final call are not reached.

10. **Iteration continuity.** If all operations succeed and dependencies return compatibly, the next iteration continues with updated working state rather than restoring the original inputs.
