# F-cf590c90c115

## Purpose

F-cf590c90c115 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency calls, derives address-bearing quantities from incoming state and dependency results, mutates computed-address and fixed-address storage, and finishes with an unresolved indirect transfer.

No application-level purpose is established. Character-like constants do not establish text output; repeated offsets do not establish object layouts or allocation boundaries.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented calls, explicit access widths, conversions, arithmetic dependencies, captures, reloads, and ordering.
- **Inferred machine interpretation:** 32-bit arithmetic retaining the low 32 bits, where explicitly assumed below.
- **documented_only:** behavior not established by the supplied representation, including missing storage declarations and dependency semantics.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Entry state and result

The represented interface has no explicit parameters and no explicit return value. It establishes no returned object, status code, or success indication.

Implicit inputs include an incoming unsigned 32-bit quantity, incoming return-link state, dependency results, secondary register outputs, and memory contents. Some dependency results are represented as 64-bit pairs. Secondary register outputs are separate inputs to later calls, not interchangeable with ordinary return values.

The actual ABI, required entry state, and completeness of represented call signatures are **documented_only**.

### Widths and arithmetic

Explicit pointer accesses establish:

| Access | Width and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Unsigned-byte read | 8 bits, yielding 0–255 |
| Byte store | 8 bits |
| Halfword store | 16 bits |
| Word read or store | 32 bits |
| Selected paired result | 64 bits |

A pair’s low word is its least significant 32 bits; its high word is its most significant 32 bits. This does not establish memory byte order.

Explicit byte and halfword stores retain the corresponding low bits. A narrow conversion supplied to named storage does **not** establish that storage’s transaction width.

Arithmetic examples assume addresses and arithmetic results wrap modulo \(2^{32}\). This is a machine interpretation, not a claim of portable signed-overflow or signed-left-shift semantics. Negative signed right shifts, excessive variable-shift counts, and character signedness remain **documented_only**.

### Preconditions

Every executed indirect target must be callable. Every executed memory access must be valid for its actual width and alignment requirements.

No checks are represented for null addresses, bounds, alignment, overflow, writability, or addresses derived from small integers. No non-aliasing guarantee is established. Missing named-storage declarations prevent complete determination of access footprints.

## Behavior

### Dependency preparation

Preparation is a fixed straight-line call sequence, with no represented data-dependent branch or loop. The first target derives from incoming return-link state plus 0xFFE. Subsequent targets derive from embedded anchors using the same displacement.

Calls remain ordered even when their ordinary results are unused: their effects are unknown.

After the first three calls, the incoming unsigned word is shifted right by 15. Relevant descendants select:

- Incoming bits 15–28 through a 0x3FFF mask.
- Incoming bit 15 through a low-bit extraction.
- Incoming bit 15 placed at bit 31 through a left shift by 31.

Other represented extraction patterns include:

| Transformation | Selected source bits |
|---|---|
| Mask 0xFFFFFF, then unsigned right shift by 8 | 8–23 |
| Mask 0x3FFFF, then unsigned right shift by 11 | 11–17 |
| Mask 0x1FFFF, then unsigned right shift by 4 | 4–16 |
| Left shift by 8, then mask 0x1FFFF | 0–8, placed at 8–16 |

Arguments include explicit zeros, repeated values, shifted ordinary results, separately supplied high words, masked fields, and secondary register outputs. Several four-argument calls combine a low word shifted left by eight, its corresponding high word, and two further derived values.

Under 32-bit truncation, successive left shifts by 31 and 30 yield zero; successive shifts by 14 and 23 also yield zero. Explicit zero assignments constrain their arithmetic descendants until reassignment, but do not imply that intervening dependencies preserve memory or globals.

### Values used by the memory phases

The following descriptive labels identify captured quantities, not object types:

| Label | Derivation |
|---|---|
| **Shifted address** | A paired result’s low word shifted left by 25 |
| **Scaling result** | A later ordinary dependency result |
| **Scaled base** | Scaling result multiplied by 0x02000000 |

The small index equals four times its source’s low seven bits: 0–508 in multiples of four. The doubled index is 0–1016 in multiples of eight. These ranges do not establish valid storage.

### Initial typed-memory effects and zero capture

Initial publication writes the shifted address as a word at final-pair-high plus 0x14, then writes the high word’s low byte at shifted-address plus 9. A word at 256 times final-pair-low plus 0x54 receives the scaled base. The byte at shifted-address plus 9 is then overwritten with the shifted address’s low byte, which is zero under the stated arithmetic interpretation.

The scaled-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared. An unsigned byte at scaled-base plus 5 is captured. Next, the doubled-index word at offset 0x14 is cleared, and the captured byte is zero-extended into a word store at scaled-base plus 0x34.

Only then is the scaled-base word at offset 0x14 read. This result is the **initial captured word**.

For ordinary coherent memory without external mutation, the initial captured word is zero:

- The intervening doubled-index store writes only zero, even if it overlaps the cleared word.
- The four-byte field at scaled-base plus 0x34 cannot overlap the four-byte field at scaled-base plus 0x14 under the 32-bit address interpretation.

Those intervening stores therefore cannot introduce nonzero bits into the cleared field. Memory-mapped read behavior, external mutation, or nonordinary address mappings are separate environmental uncertainties, not an alias-based explanation involving these stores.

After the capture, the doubled-index word at offset 0x14 is cleared again. Two distinct byte stores at doubled-index plus 0x15 receive the initial captured word’s low byte.

### Root capture and pointer propagation

The converted base from 0x05 is captured before the assignment at 0x01 and is not reloaded.

Before reading its word at offset 0x54, the function:

- Stores the small index’s low byte at captured-base plus 0x15.
- Writes the shifted payload’s low byte at scaled-base plus 0x11.
- Stores the shifted payload as a word at initial-captured-word plus 0x54.
- Clears the byte at scaled-base plus 0x11.

The subsequent word read at captured-base plus 0x54 produces the **root word**. That field is then replaced with the initial captured word. The root remains a capture independent of this replacement.

The small index is published as a word at doubled-index plus 0x34. A word loaded from scaled-base plus 0x74 supplies another base: its word at offset 0x54 first receives the scaling result shifted left by 26.

A self-address store through doubled-index plus 0x74 contains two represented reads of that field: one supplies the destination base and one supplies the stored value. Their relative evaluation order is unresolved; substituting one cached read is not generally equivalent.

Two word stores of the root at root plus 0x24 are interleaved with two byte stores of the root’s low byte through the scaled-base offset-0x74 capture, at offset 9. A shifted-payload word store at that loaded base plus 0x54 intervenes between the pairs.

The doubled-index word at offset 0x14 then receives, in order, the scaling result shifted left by 26 and two stores of the root. The named assignment at 0x54 occurs between the first and second of these three word stores.

Two byte stores at doubled-index plus 0x15 receive the low byte of four times the root. The named assignment at 0x15 follows.

### Root-derived reads and late pointer result

Four times and eight times the root drive further propagation:

- Four-times-root plus 0x54 receives the shifted payload, then four times the root, with further repeated publication of that root-derived value.
- Doubled-index plus 0x34 receives four times the root.
- A word read at doubled-index plus 0x74 is doubled; this **doubled chain value** is published at root plus 0x44.
- An unsigned byte from four-times-root plus 5 supplies a zero-extended base for a word read at offset 0x74. That loaded word receives the doubled chain value at offset 0x44.
- A word read at four-times-root plus 0x64 supplies destinations at offsets 0x0D and 0x14. These stores target the loaded base, not four-times-root plus 0x64.

Intervening effects include low-byte stores at offsets 9, 0x0D, and 0x15, self-address word publication at four-times-root plus 0x14, and alternating word values at doubled-chain-value plus 0x44.

A word read at four-times-root plus 4 supplies a byte-source base. Its unsigned byte at offset 5 is read twice, separated by stores. The first capture is published as a zero-extended word through the earlier byte-derived pointer at offset 0x44. The second read supplies a base for another word read at offset 0x74.

This last word is the **late pointer result**. Its doubled and quadrupled forms are used independently. Neither the repeated byte read nor the later word read may be replaced with an earlier capture without additional memory assumptions.

### Final captures and replacement dependencies

For this phase, **doubled late base** means twice the late pointer result.

The named value at 0xA9 is captured into 32 bits. Next, a word at four-times-late-pointer-result plus 0x74 is captured as the **late offset-0x74 word**. The named assignment at 0x3A follows. Only afterward are words captured from doubled-late-base plus 0x14 and publication-base plus 4.

Initial byte effects in this phase publish:

- Publication base’s low byte at doubled-late-base plus 0x0D.
- 0x6C at publication-base plus 8.
- Fixed-storage base’s low byte at publication-base plus 0x0D and plus 9.

The word at doubled-late-base plus 0x54 receives that base itself.

The named assignment at 0x67 receives the narrowed 0xA9 capture. A word is then loaded from doubled-late-base plus 0x44 before the named assignment at 0x21 reads 0x67.

That loaded word supplies a byte at publication-base plus 0x10, a word at fixed-storage-base plus 0x54, and a shifted byte at doubled-late-base plus 1. The shift count is the publication base’s low eight bits. Results for negative operands or excessive counts are **documented_only**.

Three later typed reads have essential capture points:

1. **Publication offset-0x40 capture.** Fixed-storage-base plus 0x54 is first replaced with the publication base. The function then reads the word at publication-base plus 0x40. After publishing the earlier publication-offset-4 capture at fixed-storage-base plus 0x74, it replaces publication-base plus 0x10 with this offset-0x40 capture as a word. Thus the earlier byte store at plus 0x10 precedes its word replacement.

2. **Loaded destination base.** Fixed-storage-base plus 0x44 receives the doubled late base. A fresh named read from 0x67 supplies a byte at publication-base plus 9, and doubled-late-base plus 9 receives publication base’s low byte. The full 32-bit 0xA9 capture is then stored at publication-base plus 0x44. Only afterward is the word at publication-base plus 0x54 read and captured as the loaded destination base. Another fresh read from 0x67 supplies the next byte at publication-base plus 9. A byte at loaded-destination-base plus 0x0D receives the late offset-0x74 word’s low byte.

3. **Publication offset-0x14 capture.** The earlier doubled-late-base offset-0x14 capture is restored as a word at doubled-late-base plus 0x54. The function then reads the word at publication-base plus 0x14. Its low byte is used later at loaded-destination-base plus 0x11.

These are explicit 32-bit pointer reads, not unresolved named-storage accesses. Each observes memory at its own position, including effects of earlier overlapping stores.

After the third capture, doubled-late-base plus 0x11 receives the late offset-0x74 word’s low byte. Publication-base plus 9 receives the loaded destination base’s low byte, and publication-base plus 0x44 is replaced with that base as a word.

### Terminal propagation and transfer

A word read at doubled-late-base plus 4 supplies the **terminal base**. A halfword at twice the terminal base receives 0x0065. The terminal-base word at offset 0x64 is then captured.

Next, loaded-destination-base plus 0x11 receives the publication offset-0x14 capture’s low byte, and loaded-destination-base plus 0x54 receives decimal 100 as a word.

The terminal-base word at offset 0x74 is captured after those stores. A separate word read at doubled-late-base plus 0x64 supplies the **final destination base**. A byte at doubled-late-base plus 9 receives the terminal base’s low byte. The final destination’s word at offset 0x54 receives the terminal offset-0x64 capture; a word at twice that capture receives the final destination base.

The unresolved final transfer has four represented arguments:

1. 0x72.
2. Terminal-base offset-0x64 capture.
3. Earlier doubled-late-base offset-0x14 capture.
4. Terminal-base offset-0x74 capture.

A return is represented afterward. Whether the target returns is unresolved.

## Side effects

The contract includes intermediate accesses and their ordering, not merely final contents. Captures remain independent of later changes to their source storage; reloads observe their own positions in the sequence.

Typed and named accesses may overlap. Ordinary aliasing can affect many later reads, but does not invalidate the initial zero-capture proof: the relevant nonzero-capable store is disjoint from that cleared word.

Endianness, atomicity, volatility, synchronization, memory-mapped behavior, and external mutation are **documented_only**.

No direct allocation, release, reference-count operation, or ownership transfer is established. Unknown dependencies may acquire, retain, release, or invalidate resources; no ownership or global-preservation guarantee is inferred.

No status checking, rollback, exception handling, or failure cleanup is represented. A fault or nonreturning dependency may leave earlier effects in place.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

### Incoming-bit boundary

If only incoming bit 15 is set, the unsigned right-shifted quantity is 1; relevant descendants include 0x00010000 and 0x80000000. If only bit 14 is set, that quantity is zero.

### Pair and index boundaries

A pair with low word 0x12345678 and high word 0x9ABCDEF0 supplies shifted-low/high values 0x34567800 and 0x9ABCDEF0 under 32-bit truncation.

A small-index source whose low seven bits are 0x7F produces 508 and doubled index 1016. Higher bits do not change this result.

### Initial zero capture

For ordinary coherent memory without external mutation, the initial captured word is zero even when the doubled-index zero store overlaps it. The captured-byte word store at scaled-base plus 0x34 cannot make it nonzero.

Consequently, the later shifted-payload word store through initial-captured-word plus 0x54 targets address 0x54 under this interpretation. Successful access still requires writable storage there.

### Named-source conversion

If declaration evidence establishes an unsigned 8-bit source containing 0x80 at 0x05, conversion yields 0x00000080. A signed 8-bit source with that bit pattern instead converts to 0xFFFFFF80. Without declarations, the base is not determined by that byte pattern alone.

### Ordered typed captures

If publication-base plus 0x40 equals fixed-storage-base plus 0x54, the offset-0x40 capture sees the just-stored publication base in ordinary memory. Its later word publication at offset 0x10 uses that capture, not a pre-store value.

If publication-base plus 0x14 equals doubled-late-base plus 0x54, its read sees the restored earlier offset-0x14 capture, absent external mutation. The loaded destination’s byte at offset 0x11 uses that newly read word’s low byte.

### Loaded destination and reloads

If the ordered read at publication-base plus 0x54 yields a usable address, that captured address supplies the later byte destinations at offsets 0x0D and 0x11 and the word destination at offset 0x54 receiving decimal 100.

Fresh named reads at 0x67 need not equal the original narrowed 0xA9 capture if intervening effects change contributing storage.

### Nonreturning transfer

If the final target does not return, no normal return or rollback is required. Earlier accesses have already been attempted.
