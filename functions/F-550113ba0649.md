# F-550113ba0649

## Purpose

A higher-level purpose is **documented_only**. The evidence does not establish that this function allocates objects, serializes a format, updates a device, or implements any particular application operation.

This contract describes the visible execution prefix, conditional on the recovered control flow being accurate and each preceding operation completing. The supplied representation reports invalid instruction data and ends at an unrecovered instruction boundary. It does not establish a normal return.

### 2. Second word store and paired arguments

### 3. Third word store and first byte update

1. Invoke **F-U14**, **F-U15**, and **F-U16**, in order, with no visible arguments.
2. Invoke **F-U17** with no visible arguments and retain a third 32-bit index result.
3. Store the ambient selector logically shifted right by three bits as a 32-bit word at the address obtained by scaling the third index by four and adding 20.
4. Invoke **F-U18**, passing the third index scaled by four.
5. Invoke **F-U19**, **F-U20**, and **F-U21**, in order, with no visible arguments.
6. Invoke **F-U22** with no visible arguments and retain its unsigned 32-bit result as the **first byte-source word**.
7. Calculate the **selector-derived base** by multiplying the ambient selector shifted right by three by 524,288.
8. Read one unsigned byte at the selector-derived base plus one.
9. Add 17 to that byte value to obtain the destination address.
10. Store the low eight bits of the first byte-source word shifted left by two at that destination.
11. Invoke **F-U23**, passing the 32-bit shifted value, not merely the stored byte.

Under 32-bit modular multiplication, the selector-derived base is a multiple of 524,288, and depends only on bits 3 through 15 of the ambient selector. The expression is therefore not an unrestricted wide-address multiplication. The arithmetic-recovery qualifications below still apply.

### 4. Second byte update

1. Invoke **F-U24** with no visible arguments and retain its 32-bit result.
2. Invoke **F-U25**, passing that result unchanged.
3. Invoke **F-U26**, **F-U27**, and **F-U28**, in order, with no visible arguments.
4. Invoke **F-U29** with no visible arguments and retain its 32-bit index result.
5. Read one unsigned byte again at the selector-derived base plus one.
6. Add 17 to this newly read value to obtain the destination address.
7. Mask the retained first byte-source word to its low 30 bits, logically shift it right by 20, and store the low eight bits of that result at the destination.
8. Invoke **F-U30**, passing the result of **F-U29** shifted left by two.

The address-component byte is reread after the intervening dependencies. It must not be replaced with the earlier byte value.

Although the intermediate shifted value can contain ten bits, the byte store retains only eight. The stored value is therefore original bits 20 through 27 of the first byte-source word. Bits 28 and 29 do not reach memory through this store.

The result of **F-U29** contributes to the following call argument, not to this store’s address or byte value.

### 5. Third byte update and retained address component

1. Invoke **F-U31**, **F-U32**, and **F-U33**, in order, with no visible arguments.
2. Invoke **F-U34** with no visible arguments and retain its unsigned 32-bit result as the **second byte-source word**.
3. Read one unsigned byte at the selector-derived base plus one.
4. Retain this byte value.
5. Store the low eight bits of four times the second byte-source word at the address equal to the retained byte plus 17.
6. Invoke **F-U35**, passing the retained byte zero-extended to 32 bits.

The argument to **F-U35** is the byte read before the store. It is neither the destination address nor the byte just written.

This distinction remains important if the byte-store destination aliases the address-component read location: the call uses the retained pre-store byte.

### 6. Fourth byte update and final calls

1. Invoke **F-U36** with no visible arguments and retain its 32-bit result.
2. Invoke **F-U37**, passing that result unchanged.
3. Invoke **F-U38**, **F-U39**, and **F-U40**, in order, with no visible arguments.
4. Invoke **F-U41** with no visible arguments and retain its 32-bit index result.
5. Calculate a new read address by multiplying the retained second byte-source word by eight and adding one.
6. Read one unsigned byte at that address.
7. Add 17 to the byte value to obtain the destination address.
8. Mask the second byte-source word to its low 30 bits, logically shift it right by 20, and store the low eight bits at the destination.
9. Invoke **F-U42**, passing the result of **F-U41** shifted left by two.
10. Invoke **F-U43**, **F-U44**, **F-U45**, and **F-U46**, in order, with no visible arguments.
11. Reach the unrecovered invalid-instruction boundary.

The final address-component read is based on the second byte-source word, not on the selector-derived base used for the preceding three byte updates.

### Arithmetic limits

Unsigned right shifts of the ambient selector and byte-source words are logical shifts.

Byte-store truncation is established independently of signed character interpretation: the stored bit pattern is the low eight bits.

Some index scaling and left shifts are represented using signed 32-bit intermediates. The evidence does not establish a portable overflow rule for those expressions. For inputs whose calculations remain representable, the stated mathematical scaling applies directly. For overflowing cases, exact machine-level address and argument behavior is **documented_only** pending instruction-level confirmation.

Where unsigned 32-bit arithmetic is established, overflow retains the low 32 bits. In particular, multiplying the second byte-source word by eight is not evidence of a wider multiplication.
