# F-d732f7f548d1

## Purpose

F-d732f7f548d1 conditionally acquires a byte-region descriptor, derives two quantities from selected bytes, requests an eight-byte result, publishes two result words, and invokes a descriptor-release dependency.

The established operation has two explicit early-return conditions:

- An initial two-word quantity that is zero or negative produces status 11.
- A nonzero acquisition status is returned unchanged.

After successful acquisition, the wrapper checks no further dependency status. If the remaining calls and memory accesses complete normally, it performs two output stores, invokes release, and returns zero.

The apparent intent is to extract an item near the end of an acquired region. This is inferred from the address calculation, not an established format or object-level guarantee.

Descriptor fields and some local quantities are accessible to dependencies. Their later reads are not necessarily acquisition-time or decoding-time snapshots. In particular, the quantity passed to the size dependency need not equal the quantity subsequently passed to the extraction dependency.

## Behavior

### Initial quantity and gate

The wrapper initializes two adjacent 32-bit words to zero, then calls F-66c392410142 with the handle and the address of the first word. No return value from that call is used.

The resulting words are interpreted as a low 32-bit word and a signed high 32-bit word. The gate accepts exactly these combinations:

- The signed high word is greater than zero.
- The high word is zero and the low word is nonzero.

A negative high word, or two zero words, causes return 11. This is a signed two-word positivity test, not a test that either word contains nonzero bits.

On rejection, the wrapper does not acquire a descriptor, decode bytes, perform its direct output stores, or call the release dependency. Effects already caused by F-66c392410142 are not rolled back.

## Side effects

### Direct writes and exposed storage

The wrapper directly initializes the initial quantity and selected descriptor fields, maintains local quantity slots, and publishes two result words.

Decoder output slots and extraction-result storage are not shown initialized before their respective dependency calls. Defined consumption requires the dependencies to supply the values subsequently read.

Addresses exposed to dependencies include:

- The initial two-word quantity storage.
- The descriptor storage.
- A first-quantity slot when first decoding is extended.
- A second-quantity slot when second decoding is extended.
- The extraction-result storage.

Passing these addresses does not establish ownership transfer or preservation. It permits no assumption that an unknown dependency cannot retain or later access them while the storage remains live.

### Output on early returns

On initial rejection and acquisition failure, the wrapper performs no direct stores through the output argument.

That is narrower than a guarantee that the destination remains unchanged. Dependency effects and aliases are unspecified.

### Resource lifecycle

There is one explicit release invocation, after both output stores, on the successful-acquisition path.

There is no explicit release on initial rejection or acquisition failure. Between successful acquisition and release, no checked error branch provides cleanup.

The release role is inferred from its placement and descriptor argument. Its implementation is unknown. The call does not prove that the wrapper owns the region, that release frees it, or that copied result words represent independent values rather than references.

### External state

No direct global-variable access is established in the wrapper. Dependencies may read or modify globals, handle-associated state, descriptor fields, byte storage, exposed locals, or aliased caller storage. Purity, global preservation, reentrancy, and thread safety are not established.
