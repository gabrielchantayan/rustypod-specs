### Arithmetic boundaries

The unsigned operations on the low word and entry payload have determinate bit-level meaning under the displayed 32-bit interpretation:

- The first argument transformation discards the low word’s top two bits when shifting left by two.
- The second lookup uses exactly low-word bits 21–29.
- The final two-byte-store address uses exactly low-word bits 7–29.
- The second payload byte uses exactly entry-payload bits 22–29.

The left shifts applied to the results of F-DEP-09 and F-DEP-14 are different: those results are represented as signed integers. For nonnegative results no greater than hexadecimal `0x1FFFFFFF`, shifting left by two produces a representable signed 32-bit result.

For negative results or larger positive values, the supplied representation does not establish a portable signed-arithmetic contract. A target-level wrapping interpretation may be plausible, but it is not established here.

### First lookup is numeric indirection

### Second lookup boundaries

If the low word is zero, the second lookup reads address 1.

If the low word is `0x3FFFFFFF`, it reads address 512. Setting low-word bits 30 or 31 does not change that lookup address.

If the entry payload is `0xFFFFFFFF`, the second payload store writes `0xFF`, regardless of the lookup byte’s value. The lookup byte determines only the destination.

### Observable aliasing between lookups

Suppose the low word selects address 17 for the second lookup, the first lookup byte is zero, and the entry payload is `0x00000007`.

The first payload store writes 7 at address 17. If intervening dependencies leave that location unchanged, the second lookup then reads 7, and the second payload store writes zero at address 24.

This outcome depends on intervening memory effects; it is not guaranteed solely by entry memory.
