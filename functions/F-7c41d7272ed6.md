# F-7c41d7272ed6

## Purpose

F-7c41d7272ed6 performs an ordered series of indirect dependency invocations, derives fixed-width values from dependency results, mutates memory through computed and fixed addresses, and ends with a four-argument indirect invocation.

The supplied static material does not establish a domain-level purpose. It does not justify identifying the operation as initialization, serialization, allocation, rendering, or maintenance of a particular data structure.

This contract distinguishes three evidence levels:

- **Established static evidence:** widths, data relationships, and ordering explicitly represented in the supplied material.
- **[INFERENCE]:** conclusions conditional on a machine model not independently established.
- **documented_only:** behavior left unresolved rather than assigned an unsupported rule.

Descriptions of access order concern the represented execution. They do not establish hardware atomicity, bus transactions, or visibility guarantees for concurrent observers.

## Behavior

### 1. Dependency-driven preparation

The preparation region is a long, straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. No conditional branch, loop, retry, or explicit recovery path is represented.

Invocation order is significant even when a call has no represented arguments or its primary result is unused. Dependencies may affect memory, global state, secondary results, or control flow.

The opening relationships establish that:

- Two invocations precede the first captured 64-bit result.
- The next invocation receives that result’s low portion shifted left eight bits, its high portion unchanged, and two zeros.
- After an intervening invocation and another captured result, a later invocation receives zero, the new high portion, the new low portion shifted left eight bits, and the same low portion shifted left eleven bits.

Subsequent preparation repeatedly forwards shifted dependency results, retains values across intervening calls, duplicates transformed values in multiple argument positions, and supplies explicit zeros.

Represented argument lists do not establish complete physical machine interfaces. Secondary results are consumed even where their production is incompletely described.

### 2. Numerical transformations

The represented transformations include:

- Left shifts of low and high result portions.
- Logical right shifts of explicitly unsigned values.
- Masks selecting low-order fields before further shifts.
- Scaling by powers of two.
- Numerical extraction from 64-bit containers.
- Reuse of earlier captured values rather than fresh dependency results.

Examples of established field relationships include:

- Mask 0xFFFFFF retains the low 24 bits.
- Mask 0x3FFFFF retains the low 22 bits.
- Mask 0xFFFFF followed by a logical right shift of ten selects original bits 10 through 19.
- An unsigned 64-bit result shifted right 36 bits yields its high 32-bit portion shifted right four bits.
- An unsigned 64-bit result shifted right 44 bits yields its high portion shifted right twelve bits.

A later right shift does not recover bits lost in an earlier fixed-width left shift.

Several retained values are explicitly assigned zero before subsequent transformations. Their represented shifts and masks remain zero. This arithmetic fact does not make neighboring dependency calls removable.

The complete preparation invocation trace is not enumerated here. Resolved callee identities and physical calling conventions remain documented_only; the transformation families are not permission to replace the sequence with an unordered computation.

### 3. Initial direct mutations

The last captured dependency result supplies low and high 32-bit portions for direct memory operations. Earlier retained values supply additional bases and stored values.

The initial access relationships include:

- A word store through the final high portion at offset 0x14 precedes a byte store at offset 0x09 of an earlier shifted base.
- A word store through the final low portion scaled by 256, at offset 0x54, precedes a second byte store to that offset-0x09 location.
- Those two byte stores have different represented value sources and remain separate accesses.
- A word clear at offset 0x14 of another derived base precedes an unsigned byte load from that base’s offset 0x05.
- A word clear through a small doubled base precedes storage of the widened byte at offset 0x34 of the former base.
- The former offset-0x14 word is captured before another clear through the small doubled base.
- Two consecutive byte stores write the captured word’s low byte at offset 0x15 of the small doubled base.

The small quantity is derived by shifting a dependency result left eight bits, retaining bits selected by 0x7FFF, and logically shifting right six bits.

**[INFERENCE]:** under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, this quantity is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Its doubled base is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

After the consecutive offset-0x15 stores, the global value associated with absolute address 0x05 is evaluated and converted to unsigned 32-bit form. That captured converted value becomes an address component, including for accesses at offsets 0x15 and 0x54. It is not an established byte-derived base.

A byte write to absolute address 0x01 follows this capture. Therefore, the converted input must not be replaced with a later reevaluation if fixed-address storage overlaps.

### 4. Computed-address mutations

The following region combines explicit byte operations with word accesses at offsets including 0x04, 0x09, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x14, 0x15, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.

Established access patterns include:

- Storing a base into its own offset-0x24 or offset-0x14 location.
- Repeating word stores with byte stores between them.
- Loading offset-0x74 words and using loaded values as bases for offset-0x54 stores.

Some explicitly loaded unsigned bytes become bases for further word reads. Those particular bases are limited to 0 through 255. This limitation does not apply to the global input associated with 0x05.

No list, tree, object type, or field ownership is established by apparent self-references.

Earlier zero stores do not unconditionally determine later reads. Intervening writes may overlap cleared storage. Likewise, earlier captured words must remain captured values rather than being replaced with fresh reads after intervening mutations.

### 5. Constants and repeated writes

A later region stores 0x3A at three widths:

- One byte through a loaded value scaled by four.
- One halfword through that value scaled by eight.
- One word at offset 0x20 of the four-times-scaled base.

Another derived base receives an ordered offset-0x20 overwrite sequence: word value 0x20, word value 0x2F, a word containing the zero-extended byte read from offset 0x04, and word value 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the 0x2F store and before the third word store.

**The separately stored 32-bit publication base is captured before the word store of 0x20 to absolute address 0x60.** Subsequent accesses use that captured base. The representation does not read the base after the 0x60 store, and equivalence must not depend on assuming the store cannot overlap its storage.

The repeated region then uses an ordered four-access pattern:

1. A word store at offset 0x54 of a scaled destination.
2. A byte store at offset 0x19 of the captured publication base.
3. A byte store at offset 0x11 of another derived base.
4. A word store at offset 0x34 of the captured publication base.

The two byte values are retained low-byte values, not repeated global loads. The initial occurrence differs from later occurrences in both the scaled destination and the value stored at the captured base’s offset 0x34.

These are explicit repeated operations, not a represented data-dependent loop. The region ends after its terminal offset-0x34 word store.

### 6. Final mutation region

For this section:

- **Current derived base** means the derived base used for the offset-0x20 overwrite sequence and subsequent accesses.
- **Retained doubled base** means the previously retained doubled value used for late offset-0x14, offset-0x44, and offset-0x54 accesses.
- **Earlier scaled base** means the earlier four-times-scaled value read at offset 0x74 immediately after the repetitions.

These are descriptive roles, not asserted object types.

The opening interleaving is significant. The represented execution captures the word at absolute address 0xA9, then captures the earlier scaled base’s offset-0x74 word. It next writes the computed byte to absolute address 0x3A. **Only after that write** does it capture the retained doubled base’s offset-0x14 word and the current derived base’s offset-0x04 word.

The computed byte is the low-eight-bit result of multiplying the retained adjusted index by eight and adding 0x30. The write must not move past the subsequent reads: address 0x3A may overlap their storage.

Subsequent mutations include byte stores at offsets 0x0D, 0x08, and 0x09, with constant 0x6C stored at the current derived base’s offset 0x08. The retained doubled base is written into its own offset-0x54 word.

The treatment of absolute address 0x67 requires separate reads:

- The low byte of the saved 0xA9 word is initially written to 0x67.
- A word is then read from offset 0x44 of the retained doubled base.
- Only afterward is 0x67 read for the byte assignment to absolute address 0x21.
- Later, 0x67 is read again for a byte store at offset 0x09 of the current derived base.
- After further pointer-derived writes and an offset-0x54 word capture, 0x67 is read once more for another byte store at that offset 0x09.

These are three separate reads of 0x67 after its initial write, not propagation of an invariant saved byte. Intervening computed-address writes may overlap 0x67. No claim that the three read values match is unconditional.

Between these global reads, the function also performs offset-0x10 byte and word stores, offset-0x54 overwrites through the captured publication base, and offset-0x74 and offset-0x44 word stores. The saved 0xA9 word is reused for a later word store; that reuse is distinct from reevaluating 0x67.

One byte-store value depends on a signed right shift of a captured word. The shift count is the low eight bits of the current derived base. Counts exceeding 31 are possible.

**documented_only:** the target rule for oversized signed shifts is unresolved. Count masking, saturation, rejection, or another rule must not be invented.

Near completion, a word captured from offset 0x04 of the retained doubled base becomes another base. A halfword value 0x65 is stored at twice that value. Later accesses capture words at offsets 0x64 and 0x74, store decimal 100 at offset 0x54 of another loaded base, and perform a word store through a doubled captured value.
