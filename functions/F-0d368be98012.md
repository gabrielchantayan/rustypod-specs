# F-0d368be98012

### Width and arithmetic requirements

The representation uses principally 32-bit arithmetic and address values, 64-bit result containers, and 8-, 16-, and 32-bit memory accesses.

Established transformations include fixed left shifts up to 31 positions, unsigned right shifts, low-field masking, scaling by powers of two, and subtraction of decimal 150 before additional scaling.

Unsigned 32-bit operations retain only their low 32 bits. Intermediate width matters: a left shift followed by a right shift may discard information and must not be replaced with an unbounded-precision calculation. Extracting the high word of a 64-bit container is distinct from shifting its low word.

Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Byte loads represented as unsigned are zero-extended when used as word values or address bases.

Signed arithmetic, signed left-shift overflow, and exceptional shift counts require executable-level confirmation. In particular, a late byte store uses a signed right shift whose count comes from the low eight bits of a computed base. Its behavior for negative operands or counts outside 0–31 is **documented_only**; no count-masking rule is established.

### Initial direct-memory effects

The final prefix invocation supplies a represented low word and high word. The initial direct stores have this order:

- A word is written at the high-word-derived base plus 0x14.
- A byte at a previously computed base plus nine receives the low byte of that high word.
- A word at the low word scaled by 256, plus 0x54, receives an earlier computed value.
- The byte at the earlier base plus nine is overwritten with another previously computed value.

These are ordered accesses, not merely final-state assignments.

A subsequent region clears a word at a computed base plus 0x14, reads a byte at that base plus five, clears another computed word, stores the zero-extended byte at the original base plus 0x34, and then reads the original offset-0x14 word.

That readback is not unconditionally zero. Intervening computed stores may overlap the cleared word. The same region also includes repeated identical word clears and repeated identical byte writes; these remain separate represented accesses.

### Fixed-address 0x05 capture

A meaningful byte read occurs at fixed address 0x05 after the repeated byte writes just described and before the byte write to fixed address 0x01.

The byte is zero-extended to a 32-bit value and captured as a memory base. It is not represented as an index into a separate table.

The subsequent byte write to 0x01 does not replace or refresh this captured base. Later accesses use the captured value:

- A byte is written at the captured base plus 0x15.
- After intervening accesses through other bases, a word is read at the captured base plus 0x54.
- That same offset-0x54 location is then overwritten with an earlier word readback.

The word obtained from the offset-0x54 read contributes to later scaled addresses, self-value stores, and linked accesses.

The byte-read width, zero-extension, and read-before-write ordering are established. Whether 0x05 denotes ordinary memory, device storage, or another mapped entity is **documented_only**. If intervening stores overlap 0x05, they do not change the already captured base, although they may affect later independent reads.

### Repeated-store region

Each repeated group writes, in order:

Word stores at scaled addresses plus 0x54 separate the groups. The initial scaled destination differs from the recurring later destination; the initial offset-0x34 value likewise uses the earlier scaling.

The global base is captured, not visibly reloaded for every group. This does not establish that the backing global storage remains unchanged.

### Late captures and fixed-address sequence

For the late region, **linked base** denotes a previously computed doubled value obtained through the earlier byte-based word-read chain. It is distinct as a value role from the working and publication bases; actual address disjointness is not established.

The late capture order is significant:

- The word at fixed address 0xA9 is captured first.
- A computed word at a scaled base plus 0x74 is captured next.
- A byte is written to fixed address 0x3A.
- The word at the linked base plus 0x14 is captured.
- The word at the working base plus four is captured.

The 0x3A byte is derived from eight times the adjusted value formed in the literal-store region, plus 0x30, retaining the low eight bits. It must not be assumed to be a decimal character or range-limited digit.

Subsequent byte writes affect the linked base plus 0x0D and the working base plus eight, 0x0D, and nine. The working-base-plus-eight byte receives 0x6C. A word at the linked base plus 0x54 then receives the linked base itself.

Only after those writes does fixed address 0x67 receive the low byte of the earlier word captured at 0xA9. Thus the stored byte comes from the earlier capture, not from a new 0xA9 read.

### Conditional zero readback

A cleared word reads back as zero only if no intervening overlapping write or external effect changes its bytes. The representation does not justify unconditional constant substitution.

### Transfer, not restoration

If the late linked-base-plus-0x14 capture is 0x11223344, the later transfer writes that captured word at linked-base-plus-0x54. A different prior value at the destination does not change the transferred value.

### Separate 0x67 observations

If the word captured at 0xA9 has low byte 0xAB, the explicit 0x67 store writes 0xAB. With ordinary non-aliasing memory and no external changes, the following three reads yield 0xAB.

If an intervening computed store changes 0x67 to 0xCD, a subsequent read must observe the then-current byte under the actual memory model. Reusing 0xAB solely because it was stored earlier does not satisfy the represented dependency.

### Literal access trace

The four word writes in the literal region remain 0x20, 0x2F, a promoted byte, and 0x20 in that order. The three 0x3A writes retain their byte, halfword, and word widths.

### Final invocation
