## Behavior

### Dependency phase

The initial phase is a straight-line series of indirect invocations, without a visible conditional branch, retry loop, or early return.

The first destination depends on incoming control context plus displacement 0xFFE. Later destinations use embedded control references with that displacement. Verified dependency identities are unavailable.

Invocations remain observable even when their primary results are unused. Dependencies may modify global memory, affect external state, change secondary result registers, fail, or not return. None may be treated as inert solely from its argument list.

Transformations include:

- Word results shifted left by eight.
- Independent use of lower and upper words of paired results.
- Unchanged upper words passed alongside transformed lower words.
- Duplicate arguments containing the same transformed value.
- Explicit zero arguments.
- Captured values retained across intervening invocations.
- Masking before subsequent scaling or shifting.

Masks cover several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit. Their placement matters: masking a previously shifted value differs from masking the original result.

Established unsigned bounds include:

- A 20-bit mask followed by a right shift of ten: 0–1023.
- A 24-bit mask followed by a right shift of twenty: 0–15.
- A 22-bit mask followed by a left shift of eight: at most 0x3FFFFF00.
- A 19-bit mask followed by a left shift of two: at most 0x001FFFFC.

Under 32-bit bitvector semantics, successive left shifts can discard all contributing bits. A left shift of 25 followed by eleven more positions yields zero under that interpretation. This does not independently resolve the semantics of represented signed expressions.

### Captured values entering direct memory access

The following descriptive labels identify captured values, not source identifiers:

- **Shifted seed:** a dependency-result lower word shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** a later dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Forwarded word:** another dependency result shifted left by eight.
- **Compact seed:** an eight-bit-shifted result masked with 0x7FFF, then shifted right by six.
- **Final pair:** the last paired dependency result before direct accesses.

Under represented 32-bit bit operations, the compact seed ranges from 0 through 508 in multiples of four. Its double ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

### Initial stores and read-after-clear

The opening direct accesses preserve this order:

1. A word store places the shifted seed at upper-final-pair plus 0x14.
2. A byte store places the low byte of that upper word at shifted-seed plus 9.
3. A word store places the scaled base at 256 times the lower-final-pair plus 0x54.
4. The same byte destination from item 2 receives the low byte of the shifted seed.
5. The word at scaled-base plus 0x14 is cleared.
6. A byte is captured from scaled-base plus 5.
7. The word at twice-compact-seed plus 0x14 is cleared.
8. The captured byte is stored, zero-extended to a word, at scaled-base plus 0x34.
9. The word at scaled-base plus 0x14 is read.

Under stable ordinary-memory semantics, the last read yields zero. The nonzero-capable word store at offset 0x34 uses the **same captured scaled base** as the clear and read at offset 0x14. Their four-byte ranges are separated by 0x20 bytes and do not overlap. The other intervening store writes zero, so ordinary overlap with it cannot introduce nonzero bytes.

Device-specific read behavior or concurrent modification can invalidate the stable-memory conclusion. The represented address arithmetic does not support an offset-0x34 alias explanation for a nonzero result.

The compact-derived word is cleared again after that read. Two consecutive byte stores then place the low byte of the read word at twice-compact-seed plus 0x15.

### Earlier fixed-storage accesses

The next fixed-storage interaction is a **byte read at 0x05**, zero-extended and retained as a selector. It precedes the byte write at **0x01**, whose value is the low byte of twice the compact seed.

Before the selector-based word capture, derived stores occur in this order: the selector-plus-0x15 byte receives the low compact seed; scaled-base plus 0x11 receives the low forwarded word; read-after-clear-result plus 0x54 receives the forwarded word as a word; and scaled-base plus 0x11 is cleared as a byte.

The word at selector plus 0x54 is then captured. Call this the **linked value**. That location is subsequently overwritten with the read-after-clear result. The earlier selector capture and linked-value capture must not be replaced by later reloads.

Further linked accesses and repeated self-referential stores intervene before the **word write at fixed address 0x54**, which receives twice the linked value. In particular, those intervening accesses include offset-0x74 traversal, stores through traversed bases, repeated stores at linked-value plus 0x24, and overwrites of the compact-derived offset-0x14 word.

After the fixed 0x54 write, the compact-derived offset-0x14 word receives the linked value twice. Two consecutive byte writes at twice-compact-seed plus 0x15 receive the low byte of four times the linked value. Only then does the **byte write at fixed address 0x15** receive the low byte of twice the compact seed.

These fixed accesses may overlap derived accesses. Fixed address 0x54 is not a protected global independent of other stores.

### Linked and scaled access region

The subsequent region combines word traversal, byte selectors, self-referential stores, and multiplication by two, four, or eight.

Important consumer-visible properties are:

- Words loaded through offset 0x74 become further address bases.
- An unsigned byte loaded through offset 5 can itself become the base for a word read at offset 0x74.
- A value is stored at offset 0x24 from that same value.
- Several offset-0x14 words are repeatedly overwritten.
- Byte stores retain only low bytes of larger captured values.
- Repeated loads remain separate observations when stores intervene.

Observed byte offsets include 1, 5, 9, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19. Word offsets include 4, 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.

A byte used as an address base is neither sign-extended nor visibly validated as a pointer or array index. The detailed sequencing of linked accesses not explicitly constrained in this contract remains documented_only.

### Constant stores and repeated writes

For the remaining regions, define these captured arithmetic relationships:

- **Adjusted value:** four times the linked value, minus 0x96.
- **Working base:** four times the adjusted value.
- **Traversal value:** the late word reached through an unsigned offset-5 selector and offset 0x74.
- **Double traversal:** twice the traversal value.
- **Quadruple traversal:** four times the traversal value.

These relationships use represented word arithmetic, subject to the arithmetic qualifications above.

The constant 0x3A is written as a byte at quadruple traversal, a halfword at eight times the traversal value, and a word at quadruple traversal plus 0x20, in that order.

At working-base plus 0x20, successive word stores contain 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from working-base plus 4, and 0x20. That byte read occurs after the 0x2F store and before the third store.

Next, a word is captured from fixed storage at **0x087FBBBC**. Call it the **repetition base**. This capture occurs **before** the word store of 0x20 at fixed address **0x60**. Even if those storage regions alias under the execution environment, the later store does not retroactively change the captured repetition base.

The expanded repeated region uses four-write groups:

- A word destination at an eight-times-scaled base plus 0x54 receives quadruple traversal.
- Repetition-base plus 0x19 receives the low byte of the working base.
- Working-base plus 0x11 receives the low byte of the repetition base.
- Repetition-base plus 0x34 receives the eight-times-scaled base.

The first group scales the linked value; subsequent groups scale the adjusted value. Within each group, the four accesses retain the order stated. The captured repetition base is reused rather than reloaded.

The exact number of expanded groups is not enumerated here and remains documented_only. No runtime repetition counter is visible. Replacing the expanded accesses with one final group is not justified.

### Final fixed-storage capture and publication

The final region begins with a **word capture from fixed address 0xA9**. Call this the **fixed captured word**. A derived word read from quadruple-traversal plus 0x74 follows before the byte write at **0x3A**. That byte receives the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30.

After the 0x3A write, two more words are captured, in order:

- Double-traversal plus 0x14: the **saved traversal word**.
- Working-base plus 4: the **saved working word**.

The following stores then precede publication at 0x67: double-traversal plus 0x0D receives the low working-base byte; working-base plus 8 receives 0x6C; working-base plus 0x0D and then plus 9 receive the low repetition-base byte; and double-traversal plus 0x54 receives double traversal as a word.

Only after those derived stores does the **byte at fixed address 0x67** receive the low byte of the fixed captured word. Thus neither the word capture from 0xA9 nor this byte publication may be moved across the intervening accesses.

### Separate reads of 0x67 and later updates

After publication at 0x67, a word is captured from double-traversal plus 0x44. Then the byte at **0x67 is read again** for the byte write at **0x21**. This is a memory read, not an unconditional reuse of the original fixed captured word’s low byte.

The newly captured derived word supplies a byte at working-base plus 0x10, a word at repetition-base plus 0x54, and a signed variable-distance right shift whose low byte is stored at double-traversal plus 1. The shift distance is the low eight bits of the working base. The repetition-base-plus-0x54 word is then overwritten with the working base.

A word read from working-base plus 0x40 precedes restoration of the saved working word at repetition-base plus 0x74. The newly read word is stored at working-base plus 0x10, followed by a word store of double traversal at repetition-base plus 0x44.

The next byte store at working-base plus 9 uses a **fresh read of 0x67**. It is followed by a byte store at double-traversal plus 9 and a word store of the fixed captured word at working-base plus 0x44. A word is then loaded from working-base plus 0x54 to form another base.

Only after that load does a second byte store at working-base plus 9 use **another fresh read of 0x67**. Intervening derived writes can alias 0x67; these two reads and the earlier read for 0x21 are not generally interchangeable.

The low byte of the earlier quadruple-traversal-plus-0x74 capture is then stored at offset 0x0D from the newly loaded base. The saved traversal word is restored at double-traversal plus 0x54. A word capture from working-base plus 0x14 precedes a byte store at double-traversal plus 0x11 using that same earlier offset-0x74 capture.

Working-base plus 9 and plus 0x44 subsequently receive, respectively, the low byte and full word of the newly loaded base.

### Final traversal and transfer

A word loaded from double-traversal plus 4 becomes the **terminal base**. A halfword store of 0x65 occurs at twice that value before a word is captured from terminal-base plus 0x64.

The recently captured working-base-plus-0x14 word supplies a byte at offset 0x11 from the newly loaded base. A word store of decimal 100 follows at that base plus 0x54.

A word is then captured from terminal-base plus 0x74, followed by a word capture from double-traversal plus 0x64. Later stores write the terminal base’s low byte at double-traversal plus 9, place the terminal-base-plus-0x64 capture at offset 0x54 from the second captured base, and store that second base at twice the terminal-base-plus-0x64 capture.

The final unresolved transfer receives, in represented argument order:

1. 0x72.
2. The captured word from terminal-base plus 0x64.
3. The saved traversal word.
4. The captured word from terminal-base plus 0x74.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static expectations, not executed tests.

- **Read after clear:** With stable ordinary memory and successful accesses, the initial scaled-base-plus-0x14 read yields zero. Its offset-0x34 neighbor cannot make it nonzero through ordinary overlap.
- **Byte truncation:** A word value 0x123456AB reaching a byte store contributes 0xAB, not four bytes or hexadecimal text.
- **Compact seed:** If the compact-extraction input has its low seven bits set, the compact seed is 508 and its double is 1016 under represented bit operations.
- **Captured repetition base:** If a later fixed or derived store changes the storage supplying the repetition base, repeated writes still use the previously captured word.
- **Fixed capture versus publication:** If a derived store changes address 0xA9 after its word capture but before publication at 0x67, publication still uses the captured word’s low byte.
- **Independent 0x67 reads:** If an intervening derived store changes byte 0x67, a later read for a working-base-plus-9 store observes the changed byte under ordinary memory semantics. Reusing the original publication byte would not preserve that behavior.
- **Unsigned selector:** An offset-5 byte of 0xFF contributes address base 255, not negative one. Accessibility remains a separate precondition.
- **Interruption:** A nonreturning dependency prevents later direct accesses. A fault establishes no rollback of earlier effects.
- **Final transfer:** Reaching the final transfer establishes first argument 0x72 and the specified captured provenance of the remaining arguments, not their application-level interpretation.
