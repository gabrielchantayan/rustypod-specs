### Entry values

The four contributions are combined by bitwise exclusive OR. There is no visible addition, carry propagation, or substitution of inclusive OR.

No dependency result is explicitly restricted to eight bits before combination. The eight-bit restriction applies to call arguments, not to returned values.

Under a 32-bit bit-pattern interpretation:

- The first contribution can retain only the original result’s lowest eight bits after its 24-bit shift.
- The second contribution can retain the original result’s lowest sixteen bits after its 16-bit shift.
- The third contribution can retain the original result’s lowest twenty-four bits after its 8-bit shift.
- The fourth contribution can affect any of the thirty-two stored bits.

Bits shifted beyond the word width do not contribute under that interpretation. These observations do not grant portable signed-shift behavior to every possible operand.

When all four results are between 0 and 255, their bit positions do not overlap: the first supplies the highest byte, the second the next byte, the third the next, and the fourth the lowest byte. This describes numeric placement within a word, not increasing-address byte order.

When results are wider than a byte, contributions can overlap. Exclusive OR cancels a bit set by an even number of contributions and retains a bit set by an odd number. An implementation that masks every result to a byte, adds contributions, or uses inclusive OR would not generally preserve the visible behavior.

The supplied representation treats the first three dependency results as signed integers. The shifts occur on those results before combination with the unsigned fourth result. The unsigned contribution does not retroactively make preceding signed shifts portable.

| Contribution | Largest nonnegative operand whose shifted value fits a signed 32-bit integer |

| Shift by 24 bits | 127 |

Thus, byte-confined results are sufficient for nonoverlapping contributions, but are not sufficient for portable signed-shift semantics: a first result between 128 and 255 still exceeds the first bound.
