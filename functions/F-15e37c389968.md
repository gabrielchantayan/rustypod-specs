# F-15e37c389968

## Purpose

F-15e37c389968 accepts three 32-bit inputs, performs an extended sequence of unresolved indirect interactions, and modifies memory through fixed and computed addresses. It ends with another unresolved indirect interaction carrying four arguments.

Application-level purpose is **documented_only**. Character-like constants and recurring offsets do not establish text generation, serialization, allocation, or collection behavior.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented widths, arithmetic, value dependencies, captures, and access relationships.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations requiring assumptions beyond that evidence.
- **documented_only:** behavior that the supplied material does not resolve.

Access occurrences described here belong to the supplied representation. They do not prove physical memory transactions, hardware ordering, or actual path reachability.

## Behavior

### Preparation and arithmetic model

A long straight-line preparation phase precedes the explicit memory-update phase. No explicit loop or conditional branch is represented in preparation; dependency-internal control flow remains unknown.

Preparation consumes 32-bit primary results, represented 64-bit paired results, and secondary result state whose production and preservation conventions are unresolved. Arguments include zeros, repeated values, shifted low words, high words, and masked fields. Some interactions receive several differently transformed versions of one result.

Ignored results do not make their producing interactions removable. An interaction without displayed arguments does not establish a complete zero-argument interface.

Explicit computed accesses use 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. Paired results use 64 bits. Unsigned right shifts zero-fill; extractions above bit 31 occur before narrowing. Masks select fields including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits, plus a separate single-bit extraction.

Many left shifts and multiplications have signed representations. Negative operands, signed overflow, and overflowing address calculations have no established portable interpretation. Bit-vector arithmetic examples below are conditional on 32-bit modular treatment.

Explicit unsigned-byte loads at computed addresses yield 0–255. **That rule does not resolve the untyped global read associated with `0x05`.**

Despite its name, “publication byte base” is an address role, not a statement that all accesses through it are bytes.

### Opening updates and global capture

Opening updates place the shifted base at offset `0x14` from the high word of the final paired preparation result. Shifted-base offset 9 receives the low byte of that high word and, later, the low byte of the shifted base itself. Between those byte writes, the large base is stored at offset `0x54` from a destination formed by multiplying the paired result’s low word by 256.

Large-base offset `0x14` is cleared before an explicit unsigned-byte observation at large-base offset 5. Double-small-base offset `0x14` is cleared before that captured byte is stored, widened unsigned, at large-base offset `0x34`.

The subsequent word observation at large-base offset `0x14` must not simply be replaced with zero: intervening accesses may overlap. Another double-small clear precedes two byte writes at double-small-base offset `0x15`, both using the low byte of that observed word.

Only afterward is the global associated with `0x05` evaluated and converted to unsigned 32 bits. This retained conversion is the captured global base. Its source access width and extension are **documented_only**; “low base” or “byte-derived base” would impose an unsupported range.

Literal address `0x01` then receives the double-small base’s low byte. Accesses through the captured global base follow that fixed write.

Before anchor capture:

- Captured-global-base offset `0x15` receives the small quantity’s low byte.
- Large-base offset `0x11` receives the prepared word’s low byte.
- The prepared word is stored at offset `0x54` from the earlier large-base word observation.
- Large-base offset `0x11` is cleared.

The anchor is captured from captured-global-base offset `0x54`; that location is subsequently overwritten with the earlier large-base word observation. The global associated with `0x05` is not reread to choose a replacement base.

### Two observations at double-small-base offset `0x74`

Before the repeated anchor stores, the small quantity is published at double-small-base offset `0x34`. A word loaded from large-base offset `0x74` supplies a destination for an offset-`0x54` store of a preparation result shifted left 26 positions.

The following update contains **two separate 32-bit read occurrences at double-small-base offset `0x74`**:

- One observation supplies the destination base; the write targets offset `0x54` from that observed value.
- The other observation supplies the complete word stored there.

Both observations are required by the represented expression. They are not one retained capture, and their values are not required to match.

**Their relative evaluation order is documented_only.** The supplied expression does not establish whether destination selection or stored-value acquisition occurs first. It establishes the two roles and their contribution to the update, not an ordered pair of reads.

The update precedes two anchor self-stores at anchor offset `0x24`, with low-byte stores at offset 9 of the previously loaded large-base-linked destination and an intervening offset-`0x54` prepared-word update.

### Fixed writes and further linking

Double-small-base offset `0x14` receives the preparation result shifted left 26 positions before literal address `0x54` receives the anchor shifted left one.

After that fixed write, double-small-base offset `0x14` receives the anchor twice. Two byte writes at double-small-base offset `0x15` then use the low byte of four times the anchor. Literal address `0x15` subsequently receives the double-small base’s low byte.

These fixed writes cannot be treated as movable initialization: computed accesses can overlap them.

Further linking uses four- and eight-fold anchor scalings, words at offsets 4, `0x64`, and `0x74`, and explicit unsigned-byte observations at offset 5. Relevant relationships include:

- Quarter-scale offset `0x54` is updated first with the prepared word and later with quarter-scale values.
- Double-small-base offset `0x34` receives four times the anchor.
- Twice a word observed at double-small-base offset `0x74` is published through anchor offset `0x44` and another byte-index-selected destination.
- Loaded bases receive low-byte links at offsets 9 and `0x0D`, self-related words at offset `0x14`, and offset-`0x44` updates.
- Two offset-5 byte observations through a later loaded base have intervening mutations. They are separate observations, not a cached index.

The final byte-index-selected offset-`0x74` observation supplies the **linked value**. Twice it becomes the **closing branch base**; four times it becomes the **publication word**.

### Final updates and terminal interaction

The earlier publication-word offset-`0x74` capture supplies low-byte writes at newly captured offset-`0x54`-base offset `0x0D` and closing-branch offset `0x11`.

Between those writes, closing-branch offset `0x54` is restored from its earlier offset-`0x14` capture, and publication-byte-base offset `0x14` is captured. The newly captured base is subsequently published through publication-byte-base offsets 9 and `0x44`.

Closing-branch offset 4 supplies a late base. A halfword `0x65` is stored at twice that base before its offset-`0x64` word is captured as the terminal second argument.

The low byte of the publication-byte-base offset-`0x14` capture is written at newly captured base offset `0x11`; that base’s offset `0x54` receives the word 100. The late base’s offset `0x74` is then captured as the terminal fourth argument.

Finally, closing-branch offset `0x64` supplies a destination base. After a closing-branch offset-9 byte update, destination-base offset `0x54` receives the terminal second argument. A word at twice that argument receives the destination base. The terminal interaction follows.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Input forwarding:** A third input of 3 produces an initial third argument of 96. The other two words remain unchanged.
- **Overflow qualification:** Under 32-bit modular interpretation, shifting `0x08000000` left five yields zero. This does not resolve validity of the represented signed-overflow case.
- **Small-quantity boundary:** Producing-result low seven bits of `0x7F` yield 508 and a double-small base of 1016 under the stated arithmetic interpretation.
- **Unsigned global value:** If the global associated with `0x05` evaluates numerically to 255, its unsigned 32-bit conversion is 255. This condition alone says nothing about access width.
- **Signed global alternative:** If that global has a signed type and evaluates to −1, conversion to unsigned 32 bits yields `0xFFFFFFFF`, not 255. Subsequent address validity and arithmetic remain conditional.
- **Retained global capture:** A later change to the global associated with `0x05` does not replace the already captured base.
- **Independent offset-`0x74` observations:** If the destination-role observation yields `0x1000` and the stored-value observation yields `0x2000`, the update writes the word `0x2000` at `0x1054`, assuming valid addressing. Neither role is required to be evaluated first.
- **Publication capture:** An overlapping `0x60` write does not cause the publication base to be reloaded.
- **Separate `0x67` observations:** If the three observed values contribute low bytes `0x11`, `0x22`, and `0x33`, the `0x21` publication uses the first; the two subsequent computed byte publications use the second and third.
- **Ordered overwrite:** Publication-byte-base offset `0x20` receives `0x20`, `0x2F`, the later unsigned-byte observation, and `0x20`, in that order.
- **Partial failure:** No rollback is established for writes preceding a fault.
- **Terminal arguments:** Earlier designated captures supply the final arguments even if their originating memory changes before the terminal interaction.
