The ordering is significant. Both byte stores occur after the first dependency returns and before the second dependency is invoked. The first store precedes the second. The indirect operation follows the second dependency’s return.

The function does not visibly read either destination byte before writing it. It does not compare old and new values, skip equal-value updates, combine the two values, or branch on their contents.

### Byte-store semantics

Each direct store modifies one byte. The visible operations do not constitute a two-byte assignment, a read-modify-write of a larger word, or an explicitly atomic pair.

Consequently, the function’s own direct writes affect offsets 76 and 77 without directly writing their neighboring bytes. This does not constrain writes performed by dependencies.

The pair’s visibility to other execution contexts is unknown. If F-D01 and F-D02 implement effective synchronization, they may constrain observation of the update. Without that dependency contract, another observer cannot be assumed to see the pair atomically.

No endianness interpretation is needed to describe these two independent byte stores. Treating them as a single multibyte number would introduce semantics absent from the evidence.

The represented indirect invocation receives three arguments:
