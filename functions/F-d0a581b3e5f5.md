## Behavior

### Normal execution order

The wrapper establishes this call order:

1. F-ab4e0c01f068 is called with no explicit arguments.
2. After that call returns normally, F-75a3efca3d72 is called with no explicit arguments.
3. The 32-bit result of F-75a3efca3d72 becomes the wrapper’s selected result.
4. F-e5e0e3dec811 is called with the original 32-bit wrapper input.
5. After the final dependency returns normally, the wrapper returns the previously selected result.

The final dependency therefore executes before the caller receives the wrapper result. Computing that result does not, by itself, complete the wrapper operation.

There are no visible branches, loops, retries, fallback calls, or optional calls. On an ordinary completed invocation, each of the three dependencies is called once.

### Argument and result handling

Only the middle dependency supplies the wrapper’s returned value. No result from the first or final dependency is visibly used to determine the wrapper result. Whether those dependencies have meaningful return values at their own interfaces is not established.

The selected result is obtained before the final call. The wrapper does not visibly reread a global, repeat the middle call, or recompute the result afterward. Under ordinary execution respecting the represented call interfaces, a later change to dependency-managed state does not replace the already selected result.

- Effects completed by the first dependency precede invocation of the middle dependency.
- Effects completed by the middle dependency precede invocation of the final dependency.
- Effects completed by the final dependency precede the wrapper’s normal return.

The exact reads, writes, addresses, access widths, and internal ordering within any dependency are unknown. Cross-thread visibility, atomicity, and synchronization guarantees are also unknown. Call sequencing alone is not evidence of a concurrency protocol.
