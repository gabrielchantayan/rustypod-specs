This contract distinguishes:

## Behavior

The opening phase is a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. No explicit loop or conditional branch is represented.

The first target depends on incoming control state plus 4094. Later targets generally use embedded continuation-like locations with the same displacement. This establishes target construction, not dependency identity.

Observable invocation order matters even when a primary result is discarded. Such an invocation can affect memory, auxiliary result channels, external state, or control flow. An invocation with no displayed arguments is not established to consume no hidden state.

### Field transformations

The representation uses numerous distinct masks and shifts rather than one established record layout. Visible masks include widths of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit.

Significant relationships include selecting:

- A 20-bit portion before shifting right by ten and repositioning the resulting field.
- A low result’s 24 bits before shifting left by six.
- A high result’s 22 bits before shifting left by seven or eight.
- Upper-result portions through 64-bit logical shifts by 34, 36, or 44.
- Portions of masked 24-bit words through several right shifts.
- One bit before positioning it at bit 16.

Truncation order is significant. Repeated left shifts operate on values that may already have lost high bits. A later right shift does not restore them.

### Direct-memory phase: terminology

The following descriptive labels identify value provenance, not source identifiers or structure types:

- **Shifted word:** a late primary result shifted left by eight.
- **Scaled base:** another late result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Small field:** a result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, then logically shifted right by six.
- **Small base:** twice the small field.
- **Fixed selector:** the unsigned byte read from address 0x05.
- **Saved link:** the word loaded from fixed-selector-plus-0x54 before that location is overwritten.

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, the small field has seven potentially nonzero bits, occupying positions 2–8. Its maximum is **0x1fc**, and the small base’s maximum is **0x3f8**. The number of meaningful bits is not the numerical bound.

### Entry mutations and fixed-location relationships

The direct-memory phase begins with a word store at offset 0x14 from the final combined result’s high word. Its value comes from an earlier shifted result.

A result-derived offset-9 byte location receives first the narrowed high word and later the narrowed earlier shifted result. Between those byte writes, a word at offset 0x54 from the final low word multiplied by 256 receives the scaled base.

The scaled-base region then has its offset-0x14 word cleared and its offset-5 byte loaded. A zero word is written at small-base-plus-0x14 before the loaded byte is zero-extended into scaled-base-plus-0x34. The scaled-base offset-0x14 word is subsequently read and saved, followed by another zero word write at small-base-plus-0x14. The saved word’s low byte is written twice consecutively at small-base-plus-0x15.

The **fixed byte read at 0x05 follows those two byte writes and precedes the fixed byte write at 0x01**. Address 0x01 receives the low byte of the small base. The saved selector then determines an offset-0x15 byte destination, which receives the low byte of the small field. Later changes to address 0x05 do not change this saved selector.

A byte at scaled-base-plus-0x11 receives the shifted word’s low byte, then is cleared after an intervening computed word write. The saved link is loaded from fixed-selector-plus-0x54 before that location receives the saved offset-0x14 word.

Further computed operations precede this important fixed-location sequence:

- A word at small-base-plus-0x14 receives a result shifted left by 26.
- **Address 0x54 receives the saved link shifted left once as a 32-bit word.**
- The small-base offset-0x14 destination receives two copies of the saved link.
- The low byte of four times the saved link is written twice at small-base-plus-0x15.

These fixed accesses cannot be treated as disjoint from computed destinations.

### Constant sequence and anchor snapshot

The quantity four times the saved link is reduced by 150. Call the result the **adjusted quantity**; four times that quantity is the **work base**.

At work-base-plus-0x20, four ordered word writes supply:

2. 0x2f.
3. The zero-extended byte read at work-base-plus-4 at that point.

**After the fourth write, a word is read from fixed address 0x087fbbbc and retained as the anchor base. Only after this snapshot is address 0x60 assigned the word 0x20.**

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples. No runtime tests have been performed.

### Word truncation

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, shifting either 0x00123456 or 0xff123456 left by eight yields 0x12345600. Retaining the discarded high byte through a wider intermediate violates this relationship.

Selecting 24 bits from 0xffffffff before shifting left by six yields 0x3fffffc0, not 0xffffffc0.

### Combined-result extraction

For combined result 0x89abcdef01234567, the low word is 0x01234567 and the high word is 0x89abcdef. A relationship requiring the shifted low word and unchanged high word supplies 0x23456700 and 0x89abcdef.

### Small-field bound

For a result of 0x7f, shifting left by eight gives 0x7f00. Masking to 15 bits and shifting right by six yields **0x1fc**. Doubling gives **0x3f8**.

Seven meaningful bits occupy positions 2–8; they do not imply a maximum of 0x7f.

### Fixed-read timing

If address 0x67 is initialized to 0x12 and an intervening permitted computed store changes it to 0x34 before the second read, that read supplies 0x34 to work-base-plus-9. A further change before the third read must likewise affect that third read.

Without intervening aliasing or external mutation, all three reads may agree. Equality is conditional, not guaranteed.

### Snapshot boundaries

If an offset-0x20 store aliases the fixed anchor-word location, the anchor snapshot observes the state after that store sequence. The later 0x60 write cannot retroactively change the saved anchor.

If the 0x3a byte write overlaps storage used by a later offset-0x14 snapshot, that snapshot uses post-write memory. The preceding 0xa9 snapshot remains saved.

### Repeated and overlapping writes

An observer of the work-offset-0x20 word destination sees 0x20, 0x2f, the byte-derived word, then 0x20. Emitting only the final value does not preserve the stated trace.

An overlapping computed byte write can affect a later word load. Acceptance uses memory at the actual read position, subject to the unresolved platform’s byte order and access rules.
