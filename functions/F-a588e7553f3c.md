## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

The initial phase contains a long straight-line sequence of indirect calls, without a represented explicit loop or conditional branch.

The first target derives from incoming control-link state plus 0xFFE. Later targets derive from control-location-related values with the same displacement. These target expressions do not establish dependency identities.

The first 32-bit result is shifted left by eight before being passed onward. Subsequent calls receive combinations of shifted low words, unmodified or shifted high words, explicit zeros, extracted fields, saved earlier values, and additional register outputs.

### Entry into direct mutation

Several values from the final call sequence drive the opening address graph:

- A saved result scaled by 2²⁵.
- A low result word shifted left by 25.
- A saved word produced by an eight-bit left shift.
- The low and high words of the final paired result.
- An index obtained by shifting a returned word left eight, retaining 15 bits, and shifting right six.

Under 32-bit truncation, the index is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Its doubled base is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016. The index cannot take every value from 0 through 511.

The opening mutations include a word store at the final high-word-derived base plus 0x14. A different derived byte at offset 9 is written twice, with a word store between those writes. The intervening word destination uses the final low result scaled by 256, plus 0x54.

A word at the 2²⁵-scaled base plus 0x14 is cleared. A byte at that base plus 5 is sampled and expanded into a word store at offset 0x34. The cleared word is subsequently loaded, but intervening accesses can affect it if destinations overlap.

The doubled-index base plus 0x14 is also cleared repeatedly. Two consecutive byte stores then write the low byte of the sampled word at doubled-index base plus 0x15. Both stores belong to the represented trace.

### Fixed-address input at 0x05

The fixed-address read at **0x05 occurs after those initial duplicate byte stores and before the byte write at fixed address 0x01**.

Its value is converted to unsigned 32-bit form and retained as a subsequent address base. The immediate and later consequences are:

- Fixed address 0x01 receives the low byte of the doubled-index base.
- The converted 0x05 value supplies the base for a byte store at offset 0x15; the stored value is the low byte of the index.
- The same base supplies a word read at offset 0x54.
- That word location is subsequently overwritten with the earlier word sampled from the 2²⁵-scaled base plus 0x14.

The offset-0x54 word read saves an address-driving value before replacement. That saved value feeds later scaling, self-referential stores, and pointer traversal.

These operations are not immediately adjacent: other derived byte and word stores intervene before the offset-0x54 read. The 0x05 input must nevertheless retain its represented sampling position and conversion.

Neither the address 0x05 nor the unsigned conversion establishes a byte-sized source. In particular, the derived base is not proven to lie within 0–255.

### Fixed-address input position

If execution reaches the initial duplicate byte stores, the read at 0x05 follows both stores and precedes the byte write at 0x01. Sampling 0x05 at entry or after the 0x01 write is not equivalent without an additional preservation guarantee.

### Converted fixed-address base

### Global sampling before 0x60

### Intermediate writes and mixed widths

If accesses are independently observable, both duplicate byte writes and all four derived-offset-0x20 word writes belong to the trace. The 0x3A cluster must retain byte, halfword, and word widths.

### Aliased cleared word

### Dependency mutation and failure
