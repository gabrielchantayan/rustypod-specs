## Behavior

### Preparation and dependency results

Before its first direct data-memory store, the function performs a long straight-line sequence of indirect calls. Calls have zero through four explicitly represented arguments. No visible loop or conditional branch selects among these calls.

An argument-free call is not effect-free. A call whose primary result is ignored may still change memory, globals, external state, or control flow. Such calls must not be removed merely because no explicit result is consumed.

Preparation repeatedly shifts primary results left by eight positions, combines shifted and unshifted secondary results, passes duplicate transformed arguments, and retains earlier values across intervening calls. Some result containers are represented as 64 bits, with their low and high 32-bit portions consumed separately. Masks and shifts select smaller fields before further transformation.

These are established dataflow characteristics, not evidence of a particular packed record or protocol. The complete preparation call trace is **documented_only** in this contract; no resolved callee contracts are supplied.

### Widths and arithmetic

Direct accesses distinguish:

- Bytes: 8 bits.
- Halfwords: 16 bits.
- Words: 32 bits.
- Preparation result containers: 64 bits where explicitly represented.

Narrow stores retain the corresponding low-order bits. Byte loads explicitly widened for word stores are zero-extended, not sign-extended.

Unsigned right shifts introduce zeros. Extracting a 64-bit result after right shifts of 34, 36, or 44 positions yields at most 30, 28, or 20 bits respectively. These operations are not interchangeable with shifts of an already truncated 32-bit value.

Other established field boundaries include:

- Masking to 20 low bits and shifting right by 10 selects a ten-bit field.
- Masking to 24 low bits permanently discards the original upper eight bits.
- Masking to 22 low bits before left shifts of seven or eight positions confines the mathematical result to 29 or 30 bits.
- Sequential 32-bit left shifts can discard bits that an unbounded combined computation would retain.

Signed left shifts and signed multiplication can exceed the represented signed 32-bit range. Their executable overflow semantics are **documented_only**. Address formulas below describe the represented arithmetic; they do not impose an unverified unbounded-integer or modulo-address model.

A terminal signed right shift uses the low byte of the working address as its count. Counts outside 0–31, and right shifts of negative values, require target-level interpretation.

### Initial stores and overlapping fields

The first direct word store places the packed address at offset 0x14 from the high word of the final preparation result container. A byte at packed-address offset 9 then receives the low byte of that high word.

Next, a word destination formed from 256 times the final low word plus 0x54 receives the packed base. Packed-address offset 9 is then overwritten with the low byte of the packed address. Both byte stores are represented effects.

Packed-base offset 0x14 is cleared before packed-base offset 5 is read as a byte. Index-base offset 0x14 is cleared before that captured byte is published, zero-extended, at packed-base offset 0x34. The word at packed-base offset 0x14 is then read and retained. A second index-base clear follows, then two byte stores at index-base offset 0x15 using the low byte of the retained word.

These accesses overlap within index-base storage. They can also alias packed-base storage. Consequently, the retained word must reflect memory at its actual read, including intervening stores.

### Constant publication and global-base capture

- A 16-bit 0x003A at eight times that word.
- A 32-bit 0x0000003A at four times that word plus 0x20.

**The dedicated global word is read after the final working-address offset-0x20 store and before the word store of 0x20 at fixed address 0x60.** Its captured value becomes the captured global base. The first scaled-address publication follows the fixed-address 0x60 store.

The captured global base is reused throughout the repeated region; the representation does not reread the global for each association. Later modification of its backing location does not, by itself, change the retained base.

The first association uses eight times the retained link as its scale basis. Subsequent associations use eight times the adjusted scale. ### Intervening terminal write
