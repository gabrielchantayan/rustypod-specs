## Behavior

### Dependency-driven phase

The first invocation derives its destination from incoming control-link state plus 0xFFE. Subsequent invocations use other derived destinations. Two further invocations occur before the first returned word is shifted and forwarded.

The long invocation phase contains calls with no displayed arguments and calls with up to four displayed arguments. All remain potentially observable. No dependency contract permits eliminating calls, treating them as pure computations, or assuming preservation of globals or memory.

Established transformation patterns include:

- Left-shifting returned words by 8 before forwarding.
- Passing the same transformed word in multiple argument positions.
- Supplying explicit zero arguments.
- Separately using the low and high words of represented 64-bit results.
- Retaining values across intervening calls.
- Using secondary register values independently of primary results.

The complete phase cannot be reduced to a pure address calculation. A dependency may mutate memory, release resources, or fail to return. Later behavior is required only when execution reaches it.

### Width and arithmetic rules

The representation distinguishes 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, 32-bit words, and 64-bit results.

High-word extraction selects bits 32–63 of a 64-bit result. Right shifts of the whole result by 34, 36, and 44 leave at most 30, 28, and 20 significant bits respectively. These are not shifts of the low word.

Masks select fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. Masking before shifting must not be replaced with shifting an unrestricted value.

Unsigned byte reads are zero-extended when used as words. Byte stores retain the low eight bits; halfword stores retain sixteen bits.

**Conditional 32-bit interpretation:** when word arithmetic follows ordinary 32-bit bit-vector semantics, left shifts and multiplication discard bits above bit 31, and address calculations wrap modulo 2³². Under that interpretation:

- The third argument’s successive shifts contribute its value shifted left by 10.
- Its shift by 24 depends only on its low byte.
- Successive shifts can discard every bit even when each individual count is below 32.
- Multiplication by 0x02000000 retains only the multiplicand’s lowest seven bits.

Displayed signed operands do not establish portable signed-overflow behavior. Exact machine semantics remain **documented_only** where instruction-level evidence is absent.

| Role | Meaning |

### Initial writes and zero read-back

The direct-memory region first stores Packed base as a word at Final pair’s high word plus 0x14. It then writes that high word’s low byte at Packed base plus 9, stores Shifted base at Final pair’s low word multiplied by 256 plus 0x54, and overwrites Packed base plus 9 with Packed base’s own low byte.

Next, Shifted base plus 0x14 is cleared as a word. A byte is read at Shifted base plus 5. Small base plus 0x14 is cleared as a word, and the captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at Shifted base plus 0x34. The cleared word at Shifted base plus 0x14 is then read.

**For ordinary stable memory, this read-back is zero.** The intervening clear writes only zero bytes even if it overlaps the first clear. The other intervening store is 0x20 bytes away from the read-back destination; the two four-byte ranges are disjoint under the stated 32-bit interpretation.

Concurrent modification, special mappings, or read side effects could invalidate ordinary stable-memory reasoning. They are separate uncertainties, not evidence that an intervening represented store introduces nonzero bytes.

### Early derived and absolute accesses

After the read-back, Small base plus 0x14 is cleared again. Two consecutive byte stores at Small base plus 0x15 receive the read-back’s low byte.

The function then reads an unsigned byte at absolute address 5 and writes Small base’s low byte at absolute address 1. Through the byte-derived base it writes at offset 0x15. It writes Working word’s low byte at Shifted base plus 0x11, stores Working word at the read-back-derived base plus 0x54, and then clears the byte at Shifted base plus 0x11.

Only afterward does it capture the word at the byte-derived base plus 0x54 and overwrite that location with the read-back value. This captured value is the **Captured word**. Under ordinary stable-memory conditions, the earlier read-back-derived word store targets absolute address 0x54; this is distinct from the explicit absolute write below.

Subsequent accesses update Small base plus 0x34, read Shifted base plus 0x74, perform linkage-like stores through loaded words, and repeat stores through Captured word plus 0x24 and a loaded base plus 9.

The following absolute mutations have specific positions:

- After those repeated stores, Small base plus 0x14 receives the earlier dependency word shifted left by 26.
- **Absolute address 0x54 then receives a 32-bit word containing twice Captured word.**
- Small base plus 0x14 receives Captured word twice consecutively.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Third-argument fields.** For 0x123456AB, the low byte shifted left by 5 is 0x1560. Under 32-bit semantics, shifting the argument left by 24 yields 0xAB000000.

2. **Whole-result extraction.** For a 64-bit result of 0x123456789ABCDEF0, the high word is 0x12345678; shifting the whole result right by 36 yields 0x01234567.

3. **Small-base boundary.** Relevant returned words 0x7F and 0x80 produce Small bases 1016 and zero respectively under 32-bit semantics. Neither establishes address safety.

4. **Unsigned extension.** A byte 0xE1 read at Shifted base plus 5 supplies the word 0x000000E1 at offset 0x34.

5. **Intermediate constant stores.** Keeping only the final 0x20 at Related base plus 0x20 is insufficient: the preceding stores and fresh offset-4 byte read belong to the represented access ordering.

6. **Initial zero read-back.** With ordinary stable memory and valid accesses, Shifted base plus 0x14 reads as zero after its clear, including when the intervening Small-base clear overlaps it. A nonzero result requires an additional cause, such as concurrent mutation or special-memory behavior.

7. **Absolute-write placement.** The explicit word write at 0x54 occurs between the dependency-shifted store and the first Captured-word store at Small base plus 0x14. The byte write at 0x15 follows the two fourfold-capture byte stores and precedes the next Fourfold-capture-plus-0x54 store.

8. **Alias-sensitive final reads.** If absolute 0x3A overlaps Doubled scale plus 0x14, the captured field reflects that preceding byte mutation under the chosen endian model.

9. **Fresh 0x67 reads.** If an intervening derived store changes absolute 0x67, the next Related-base-plus-9 store uses the changed byte, not the original 0xA9 byte.

10. **Interrupted execution.** A non-returning dependency or invalid memory access does not imply that later mutations occur or that earlier effects are undone.
