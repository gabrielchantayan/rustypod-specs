# F-21815dd4ba07

## Purpose

F-21815dd4ba07 transforms caller-owned storage in place. Under the integer-width assumptions stated below, it reverses the order of the four octets in each of three consecutive 32-bit unsigned fields. It also reverses the two octets of the numeric low half of a fourth 32-bit field and writes that 16-bit result at the fourth field’s starting address.

The final operation is not an unconditional reversal of the next two addressed octets. Its input is selected by numeric significance, while its destination is selected by address. These coincide on a conventional little-endian layout but not on a conventional big-endian layout.

**Inferred intent:** byte-order conversion for a record containing three full-width fields followed by a half-width field.

**Established distinction:** the final input is accessed as a full-width field despite the half-width destination. A record with only 14 accessible octets does not satisfy the source-level access requirements under the stated width model.

## Behavior

### First three fields

Each of the first three fields is replaced by its independent four-octet reversal.

Expressed in numeric significance order, a field whose octets are A, B, C, D becomes D, C, B, A. For example, `0x12345678` becomes `0x78563412`.

This is an octet-order transformation, not a reversal of the bits within each octet. All 32 input bits participate, and no field’s result depends on an adjacent field.

Under the stated unsigned 32-bit model, no arithmetic overflow error, saturation, or carry propagation forms part of this transformation. The result is the exact 32-bit permutation described above.

On either conventional little-endian or conventional big-endian storage, the four addressed octets of each of these fields are reversed. Thus, the first three fields have the same address-order reversal property on both layouts, even though their numeric interpretation differs between layouts.

### Final field

The final result is the octet reversal of the fourth field’s numeric low 16 bits. The destination is the two-octet region beginning at offset 12.

For a fourth-field value of `0xA1B2C3D4`, the selected half is `0xC3D4`, and the half-width result is `0xD4C3`. The numeric upper half does not contribute to that result.

The final destination conversion retains only 16 result bits. Under conventional 32-bit integer promotions, the intermediate arithmetic is representable, and the narrowing yields the stated two-octet reversal. Other promotion environments require separate confirmation; the widths alone do not establish every intermediate-arithmetic property.

Byte order determines which original information is overwritten:

| Layout | Fourth numeric value before | Fourth numeric value after |
|---|---|---|
| Little-endian | `0xA1B2C3D4` | `0xA1B2D4C3` |
| Big-endian | `0xA1B2C3D4` | `0xD4C3C3D4` |

On little-endian storage, the selected low half occupies the destination region. The transformation exchanges the two octets already there.

On big-endian storage, the selected low half occupies offsets 14–15, while the destination occupies offsets 12–13. The transformation therefore replaces the original numeric upper half with a reversed copy derived from the lower half.

For either layout, offsets 14–15 are not written by the function.

### Access ordering and stability

The source-level sequence completes each of the first three field updates before proceeding to the next field. The fourth field is consulted only after those three updates.

Each of the first three transformations derives its result from a captured full-width input value. The final expression instead contains two full-width input evaluations of the fourth field. Their relative evaluation order is not established. Both precede the final half-width write.

For stable ordinary memory, the two final input evaluations yield the same value, so their relative order does not affect the result. This contract does not extend that conclusion to concurrently changing storage or device registers.

These statements describe source-level sequencing and access expressions. They do not guarantee a particular number of emitted loads, stores, bus transactions, or processor instructions. The interface supplies no volatile or atomic access contract.

### Repeated application

For stable valid storage, each of the first three field transformations is self-inverse: two completed calls restore those fields.

On the described little-endian layout, two completed calls restore the entire 16-octet region because the final operation exchanges the same two octets on each call.

On the described big-endian layout, the final operation is not generally reversible. It leaves its input half unchanged and overwrites the other half. After one completed call, another call produces the same final four octets rather than recovering the original upper half.
