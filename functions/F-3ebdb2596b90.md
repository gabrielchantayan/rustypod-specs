# F-3ebdb2596b90

## Purpose

This contract establishes word-level behavior, visible dependency arguments, memory-access timing, and output publication. Dependency names are opaque identifiers; their mathematical operations are not established.

**Inferred intent:** Sign manipulation, exponent-like fields, split coefficients, and refinement thresholds suggest numerical range reduction. This does not establish floating-point types, numerical accuracy, rounding behavior, or a mathematical interpretation of the return value.

## Interface

## Behavior

### Configuration and read terminology

The function uses auxiliary initialization words, three dispatch boundaries, an equality marker, coefficient storage, refinement-table and scaling-table configuration, and several constant pairs.

Three address sources have distinct lifetimes:

- The coefficient base is captured after auxiliary initialization and retained for later accesses.
- The refinement-table base is read separately for each continuing refinement stage.
- The scaling-table base is read after the scaling-index dependency returns.

Effective addresses also include fixed addressing biases and, where applicable, index displacements. These calculations do not establish valid allocation extents.

A **pair read** means two 32-bit reads supplying one dependency invocation. Their relative evaluation order and atomicity are not established. Reads supplying later invocations are not assumed to reuse earlier values.

### Entry initialization and dispatch

The auxiliary high initialization word is read and retained before either output write. The original input pair is also assembled.

The auxiliary low initialization word is then read and written at offset 8. The retained auxiliary high initialization word is written at offset 12. Consequently, aliasing may affect storage read later, but cannot retroactively change the retained high initialization value.

After those writes, the equality marker is captured, followed by the coefficient base. Clearing bit 31 of the original high word produces the **magnitude high word**, ranging from zero through `0x7fffffff`.

Dispatch uses signed 32-bit comparisons in this order:

1. A magnitude high word at or below the first boundary selects direct copying.
2. Otherwise, a magnitude high word strictly below the second boundary selects short transformation.
3. Otherwise, a magnitude high word strictly above the third boundary selects extended transformation.
4. Otherwise, iterative refinement applies.

Only reached comparisons read their respective boundaries. Boundary values and ordering are not supplied. The input low word does not participate directly in dispatch.

### Direct copying

The original low word is written at offset 0, followed by the original high word at offset 4. The return is zero.

This path has no visible dependency invocation and no further explicit auxiliary write.

#### Publication and stopping conditions

There are at most three stages. At each stage, F-8a693c103d1c receives the working pair first and the correction pair second. Its result is immediately stored as the primary output.

Stage three stops without evaluating the cancellation condition or reading another refinement record. Earlier stages evaluate cancellation from the returned pair itself, not by rereading the output memory.

The cancellation fields are:

- **Input field:** magnitude high word shifted right by 20 bits.
- **Result field:** stage result shifted logically right by 52 bits, converted to unsigned 32 bits, then masked with `0xfffff7ff`.

The input field minus the result field is unsigned 32-bit subtraction. The resulting bits are interpreted as signed 32 bits for comparison. Stage one stops at a difference of at most 16; stage two stops at a difference of at most 49.

These are width-specific operations, not unrestricted integer subtraction. Primary publication occurs before the stopping decision.

#### Negative-input completion

2. Write the entry-retained auxiliary high initialization value with bit 31 toggled at offset 12.

The auxiliary-high replacement neither reloads its initialization source nor reads the current auxiliary-high output. There is no corresponding explicit auxiliary-low replacement.

Scaling occurs only when the field exceeds `0x3ff` and its unsigned difference from `0x400` exceeds `0x68`. Together, these conditions select field values at least `0x469`.

## Side effects

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance cases, not executed tests.

1. **Boundary precedence:** Equality with the first boundary selects direct copying. After excluding that path, equality with the second boundary excludes short transformation. After excluding both earlier paths, equality with the third selects refinement.

2. **Low-word independence:** With unchanged high word and dispatch configuration, changing only the input low word does not change initial path selection. Transformation results may still differ.

3. **Short equality marker:** Within either short subpath, matching the captured marker adds one invocation of that dependency family without changing the subpath’s return sign.

4. **Refinement termination:** A stage-one signed cancellation difference of 16 stops after one publication and no refinement-record read. A stage-one difference above 16 followed by a stage-two difference of 49 produces two publications. Differences above both thresholds produce three publications, with no record read after stage three.

5. **Late second coefficient:** If one of the first four continuing-stage invocations modifies record offsets 8–15, the fifth invocation reads the later contents, assuming valid accesses. That fifth invocation is F-c4b34006a13c with the retained reused pair first and the later-read coefficient pair second. Reversing these operands does not satisfy this contract.

6. **Changed refinement base:** A changed refinement-table base affects the next continuing stage’s newly computed address. It does not redirect second-pair reads through an already-retained record address.

7. **Scaling cutoff and timing:** Field `0x468` does not scale. Field `0x469` supplies zero followed by `0x36` to F-d7be9cfb4000. A scaling-base change made by that dependency affects the subsequent coefficient lookup.

8. **Repeated constants:** If the first C-consuming invocation changes C’s storage, the next C-consuming invocation reads the changed contents. The corresponding later-read rule also applies to A, B, and E.

9. **Negative refinement:** Completion toggles the current primary-high sign bit. Auxiliary high instead comes from the retained entry seed with its sign bit toggled, even if the seed’s source or auxiliary output changed meanwhile.
