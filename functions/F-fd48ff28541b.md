Word-sized calculations are 32-bit. Scaled-address formulas below describe low-32-bit machine arithmetic; they do not establish portable semantics for signed overflow or signed left shifts. Unsigned right shifts are logical. Byte and halfword stores retain the low 8 and 16 bits.

A **selected-byte publication** reads an unsigned selector byte at a specified lookup address and writes a byte at selector plus 0x11. The destination is therefore within 0x11–0x110, not relative to the lookup address. A **selected clear** is such a publication with value zero.

Except for an explicitly retained selector, each publication performs a fresh selector lookup at its stated position. Intervening calls or aliasing writes may change the selected destination.

A **four-call interval** contains four successive calls without explicit arguments. Its fourth call supplies the primary result subsequently described. These are separate observable calls, not interchangeable or presumed pure operations.

A **link relay** writes a word at four times a destination index plus 0x14, calls a dependency with four times that index, and crosses a four-call interval. A **self-link** writes four times an index at sixteen times that index plus 0x14. These names describe access patterns, not object types or ownership.
