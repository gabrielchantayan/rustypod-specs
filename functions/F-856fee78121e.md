## Behavior

### Ordering and aliasing

All effects below are conditional on execution reaching them. Reads occur after preceding writes, not against a snapshot of entry memory. Earlier writes can therefore change later address selection, including through partial overlap.

A loaded value retained for later use remains that earlier value even if its source location is subsequently modified. Conversely, a later explicit reread must not be replaced with the earlier value without a valid non-aliasing argument.

Repeated identical stores are part of the represented access sequence. Treating them as redundant is not justified for device memory, externally observed storage, or fault-sensitive execution. The supplied material does not establish volatility, atomicity, or concurrency semantics.

### Initial mutations and address discovery

The first observable effect is a word store of the first payload at the second address plus 0x14. A word containing twice the primary seed is then stored at the first address plus 0x54.

The second-address word at offset 0x14 is replaced with the primary seed. Between this replacement and a second identical replacement, the low byte of the second address is stored at the first address plus 1.

The initial byte updates then place:

- The low byte of the primary region at secondary-region offset 0x15, twice consecutively.
- The low byte of the secondary region at first-address offset 0x15.

The second payload is stored as a word at primary-region offset 0x54. The primary region is stored at secondary-region offset 0x34. Only then is a word read from secondary-region offset 0x74 and doubled. Call the resulting value the **first derived region**.

The word at primary-region offset 0x54 is replaced with the primary region. The low byte of the expanded seed is stored at primary-region offset 0x15, and the first derived region is stored at primary-seed offset 0x44.

A byte is read from primary-region offset 5. A word is then read at the address equal to that zero-extended byte plus 0x74. Call this word the **first linked region**. This is an absolute low-address lookup, not a lookup relative to the primary region.

The first linked region receives the first derived region at offset 0x44. The primary-region byte at offset 0x15 is changed to the low byte of the primary region. The low byte of the first linked region is stored at primary-region offset 0x0D; word offsets 0x14 and 0x54 receive the primary region.

A word loaded from primary-region offset 0x64 selects an additional record. That record receives the low byte of the first linked region at offset 0x0D and its own loaded address value at word offset 0x14.

### Linked updates and a second lookup

A word read from primary-region offset 4 selects the **second linked region**.

Before reading its selector byte, the function writes the low byte of the first linked region to first-derived-region offset 9 and the expanded seed to first-derived-region offset 0x44.

The byte at second-linked-region offset 5 is read. Its zero-extended value is stored as a word at first-linked-region offset 0x44, after an intervening byte store of the first derived region to primary-region offset 9.

The primary region then receives the expanded seed at word offset 0x14. The first derived region receives the low byte of the second linked region at offset 0x15 and the primary region at word offset 0x44.

The selector byte at second-linked-region offset 5 is read again. Between that read and its dependent word lookup, the low byte of the expanded seed is written to primary-region offset 0x15. The word lookup uses the newly read, zero-extended selector plus 0x74. Call its result the **lookup value** and twice that result the **doubled lookup region**.

The subsequent updates replace primary-region offset 0x15 with the low byte of the second linked region, set word offset 0x54 to the primary region, place the low byte of the doubled lookup region at offset 9, and set word offset 0x14 to the expanded seed.

The two selector reads and the earlier primary-region selector read are distinct observations. Aliasing may make their values differ.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial overwrite order.** If the first four destinations do not overlap, the second-address word at offset 0x14 first receives the first payload, then receives the primary seed twice. Final-state equality alone does not demonstrate the intermediate writes.

2. **Byte-selected lookup.** If a selector read yields 0xFE, its dependent word lookup begins at address 0x172. Interpreting the selector as −2 would violate the explicit zero extension.

3. **Independent selector observations.** If an intervening store overlaps second-linked-region offset 5, its second selector read may differ from the first. Reusing the first selector is not acceptable without a non-aliasing guarantee.

4. **Literal widths.** With valid, disjoint destinations, the three 0x3A publications are respectively a byte, a halfword, and a word. Their numeric equality does not make their access widths interchangeable.

5. **Low-byte truncation.** If the working-region value ends in 0xAB, byte publications of that value store 0xAB, not a complete address.

6. **Repeated publication.** With ordinary stable memory and no overlap, repeated groups may leave unchanged final values. An access-sensitive observer can nevertheless distinguish the represented sequence from one consolidated group.

7. **Terminal arguments.** If all accesses succeed and the indirect invocation is reached, its third argument is the earlier retained doubled-lookup-region-offset-0x14 word, not a new read at invocation time.

8. **Mid-execution failure.** If a late derived address is inaccessible, earlier successful stores are not covered by any visible rollback guarantee. No particular exception or status value is established.
