# F-a6e0049d7055

## Purpose

The established behavior is effectful. No domain-level purpose is established. Descriptions such as initialization, formatting, allocation, or collection processing would add unsupported semantics.

## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

The first target is derived from incoming control-link state plus 0xFFE and receives zero. Further indirect calls follow in the displayed order, mostly using embedded references plus the same displacement. No visible conditional branch selects an alternative path through this phase.

Argument construction includes:

- Primary words shifted left by eight.
- Shifted low words paired with unchanged high words.
- Additional fields derived from either half of combined results.
- Equal arguments derived from one saved value.
- Explicit zeros interleaved with result-derived arguments.
- Secondary machine-result values whose complete production contract is unresolved.

Near the beginning, one primary word is shifted left by eight and supplied as both the first and third arguments of a call, with zero between them. A later consumer receives a field derived from that **saved shifted value**: retain its low fourteen bits, discard four low bits, then shift left eighteen. A newer call result does not replace that dependency.

Other transformations use masks spanning 1, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, and 24 bits. Some composed left shifts discard all useful bits under the conditional bit-vector interpretation. These fields determine arguments or address calculations; they do not establish a serialized format.

### Opening direct effects

The last combined result before direct stores supplies terminal low and high words. The opening effects preserve this order:

- A word containing the shift base is written at terminal-high-word plus 0x14.
- The low byte of the terminal high word is written at shift-base plus nine.
- The product base is written as a word at terminal-low-word times 256 plus 0x54.
- Shift-base plus nine is overwritten with the low byte of the shift base.
- Product-base plus 0x14 is cleared before reading the byte at product-base plus five.
- Compact-base plus 0x14 is cleared before the byte just read is stored, zero-extended, at product-base plus 0x34.
- Product-base plus 0x14 is read as a word. Compact-base plus 0x14 is cleared again, followed by two byte stores of the read word’s low byte at compact-base plus 0x15.

The fixed byte read at 0x05 occurs next. Its saved value supplies a computed base; it is not subsequently refreshed from 0x05.

Before reading the root word, the function writes the low byte of the compact base to absolute address 0x01, writes the compact index’s low byte at saved-low-address-byte plus 0x15, and performs computed effects involving product-base plus 0x11 and the previously read word plus 0x54. Product-base plus 0x11 first receives the saved shifted word’s low byte and later zero.

The root word is then saved from saved-low-address-byte plus 0x54. That location is overwritten afterward; later uses of the root word retain the saved value rather than rereading the overwritten location.

### Intermediate computed effects and fixed updates

Further effects include word reads at relative offset 0x74, stores through the resulting addresses, repeated self-valued stores at root-word plus 0x24, and repeated byte writes at computed offset nine.

The word at compact-base plus 0x14 first receives the earlier product-producing primary word shifted left 26. The absolute word at 0x54 then receives twice the root word. Two later stores at compact-base plus 0x14 contain the root word itself.

After two byte stores of the scaled root’s low byte at compact-base plus 0x15, absolute byte 0x15 receives the compact base’s low byte.

The following computed region derives doubled and quadrupled values, follows words at offsets 0x04, 0x44, 0x64, and 0x74, and reads bytes at offset five. A byte read may become the base for a word read at offset 0x74. Self-valued word stores and byte stores at offsets 0x0D, 0x09, and 0x15 remain distinct observable effects.

The chain word is obtained from the later byte-to-word chain. The function writes 0x3A:

- As a byte at four times the chain word.
- As a halfword at eight times the chain word.
- As a word at four times the chain word plus 0x20.

At working-base plus 0x20, four word writes occur in order: 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte freshly read at working-base plus four, and 0x20. The intervening access at offset four is **a byte read**, distinct from a later word read at that offset.

The fixed word at 0x087FBBBC is saved after these writes. Absolute word 0x60 then receives 0x20, before the repeated-store region.

### Repeated-store region

A lead-in group contains four stores:

- A word at eight-times-root-word plus 0x54 receives the quadrupled chain.
- A byte at saved-fixed-base plus 0x19 receives the working base’s low byte.
- A byte at working-base plus 0x11 receives the saved fixed base’s low byte.
- A word at saved-fixed-base plus 0x34 receives eight times the root word.

The sustained group uses eight times the **adjusted root** instead of eight times the root word for its first destination base and fourth stored value. Its internal order remains word, byte, byte, word, at the corresponding offsets 0x54, 0x19, 0x11, and 0x34.

This is a finite, statically displayed sequence, not evidence of traversal over different objects. The final full sustained group ends with the word store at saved-fixed-base plus 0x34, immediately before the absolute word read at 0xA9.

### Late memory phase and terminal ordering

After saving the 0xA9 word, the function saves a word from quadrupled-chain plus 0x74. The arithmetic-derived byte write at 0x3A follows. It then saves a word from doubled-chain plus 0x14 for the terminal third argument, and a separate word from working-base plus four.

Mixed byte stores at offsets 0x0D, eight, and nine precede the self-valued word store at doubled-chain plus 0x54. These include 0x6C at working-base plus eight and low-byte values derived from the working base and saved fixed base.

After assigning byte 0x67, the function reads a word from doubled-chain plus 0x44. The fresh byte read supplying 0x21 follows. The loaded word then participates in computed stores, including a byte at working-base plus 0x10 and a word at saved-fixed-base plus 0x54. That latter word location is subsequently overwritten with the working base.

A byte at doubled-chain plus one receives a right-shifted signed-looking word, with shift count equal to the working base’s low byte. Counts above 31 are possible in the representation; their execution semantics remain documented_only.

The function reads a word at working-base plus 0x40. It writes the **previously saved word from working-base plus four** at saved-fixed-base plus 0x74, then writes the newly read 0x40 word at working-base plus 0x10. Offset 0x74 therefore has a word write as well as reads.

A word store at saved-fixed-base plus 0x44 precedes the second fresh read of byte 0x67, which supplies a byte store at working-base plus nine. Further effects include a byte store at doubled-chain plus nine and a word store of the saved whole 0xA9 value at working-base plus 0x44.

The word read next at working-base plus 0x54 establishes the **late destination base**. A third fresh read of byte 0x67 supplies another byte store at working-base plus nine. Intervening computed stores can alias 0x67; neither later byte read is required to equal the initially assigned byte.

The saved word from quadrupled-chain plus 0x74 supplies byte stores at late-destination-base plus 0x0D and doubled-chain plus 0x11. The earlier saved terminal-third word is restored at doubled-chain plus 0x54. A word is freshly saved from working-base plus 0x14 before further byte and word stores using the late destination base.

The terminal chain has these ordering constraints:

- A word read at doubled-chain plus four establishes the **terminal source base**; a halfword containing 0x65 is written at twice that value.
- A word read at terminal-source-base plus 0x64 establishes the **terminal second argument**.
- The byte at late-destination-base plus 0x11 receives the low byte of the saved working-base-plus-0x14 word.
- The word at late-destination-base plus 0x54 receives 100.
- **Only afterward**, a word is read at terminal-source-base plus 0x74 for the terminal fourth argument.
- A further word read at doubled-chain plus 0x64 establishes the final destination base.
- A byte at doubled-chain plus nine receives the terminal source base’s low byte. The final destination’s offset 0x54 receives the terminal second argument, and twice the terminal second argument is used as the address of a word store containing the final destination base.

The unresolved terminal target receives:

1. 0x72.
2. The word saved from terminal-source-base plus 0x64.
3. The earlier word saved from doubled-chain plus 0x14.
4. The late word read from terminal-source-base plus 0x74.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not runtime results.

- **Saved extraction:** An early primary word of 0x12345678 produces shifted value 0x34567800. The first three-argument consumer receives that value, zero, and that value. The later saved-value extraction produces 0x0E000000.
- **Combined result:** High word 0x89ABCDEF and low word 0x01234567 produce 0x23456700 and unchanged 0x89ABCDEF in a shifted-low/unchanged-high consumer.
- **Compact boundary:** Low seven bits all set produce compact index 508 and compact base 1016, not 511 and 1022.
- **Arithmetic byte:** If the adjusted root is one under the conditional arithmetic interpretation, the byte written at 0x3A is 0x38.
- **Saved versus fresh:** If the saved 0xA9 word is 0x123456AB, byte 0x67 initially receives 0xAB. If an intervening computed store changes 0x67 to 0xCD, a subsequent read supplies 0xCD. The later whole-word store still uses saved value 0x123456AB.
- **Overwrite sequence:** Where individual writes are observable, the word writes 0x20, 0x2F, the zero-extended freshly read offset-four byte, and 0x20 must remain ordered.
- **Terminal alias:** If late-destination-base plus 0x54 equals terminal-source-base plus 0x74 in ordinary memory, with no intervening modification, the terminal fourth argument observes 100. Reading it before the 100 store violates the established order.
- **Partial effects:** If the terminal target does not return, no success result or rollback is implied.
