### Opening bases and memory acquisition

The following descriptive names identify retained values, not recovered types:

- **Shifted base:** a dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000, equivalent to a 25-bit left shift under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation.
- **Small field:** bits 6–14 selected from another dependency result after an eight-bit left shift.
- **Small base:** twice the small field.
- **Opening payload base:** an earlier retained dependency value shifted left by 25 bits.

Under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, the small field is a multiple of four between 0 and 508, and the small base is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016.

The direct-memory region first writes the opening payload base as a word at offset 0x14 from the final paired result’s high word. It then writes that high word’s low byte at opening-payload-base offset 9. A word store at offset 0x54 from 256 times the paired result’s low word carries the shifted base. The byte at opening-payload-base offset 9 is then overwritten with the opening payload base’s own low byte. Both byte writes are represented events.

The next acquisition window has a precise order:

The shifted-base offset-0x14 word is cleared **before** the shifted-base offset-5 byte is read. After that byte acquisition, the small-base offset-0x14 word is cleared for the first time. The acquired byte is then written, zero-extended, as a word at shifted-base offset 0x34. Only then is the shifted-base offset-0x14 word read and retained as the **opening readback**.

The small-base offset-0x14 word is cleared a second time **after the opening readback has been acquired**. Two consecutive byte stores at small-base offset 0x15 then carry the opening readback’s low byte.

The readback is an actual represented observation. The second small-base clear cannot retroactively alter its retained scalar value. Whether replacing a read with a constant is valid requires a memory model excluding relevant aliasing, external mutation, and read-sensitive storage effects.

### Early fixed state and displaced-word capture

After the two small-base offset-0x15 byte stores, the byte at fixed address **0x05** is read and zero-extended as a retained address base. The subsequent byte write to fixed **0x01** carries the small base’s low byte; it does not replace the captured 0x05-derived base.

That captured base is used for an offset-0x15 byte store carrying the small field’s low byte. The function also writes an earlier eight-bit-left-shifted dependency value to shifted-base offset 0x11 as a byte and through opening-readback offset 0x54 as a word. The shifted-base offset-0x11 byte is then cleared.

Next, the word at offset 0x54 from the captured 0x05-derived base is read and retained as the **displaced word**. Its source location is subsequently overwritten with the opening readback. Later uses of the displaced word consume the retained scalar, not a reload of this overwritten location.

Further propagation updates the small base and destinations reached through offset-0x74 word reads. Some word payloads equal their destination bases. The represented region includes duplicate stores at displaced-word offset 0x24 and duplicate byte stores at offset 9 of another loaded base.

After a small-base offset-0x14 word update, fixed **0x54** receives the 32-bit payload formed by shifting the displaced word left once. Small-base offset 0x14 then receives the displaced word twice. Two byte stores at small-base offset 0x15 carry the low byte of four times the displaced word. Fixed **0x15** subsequently receives the small base’s low byte.

These fixed writes can overlap computed accesses; they are not established as independent bookkeeping.

1. **Third-input extraction.** Third arguments 0x000003FF, 0x00000400, and 0x000FFFFF produce transformed arguments 0, 64, and 65472 respectively. This constrains that argument only.

5. **Captured fixed byte.** If fixed 0x05 yields 0x80, its retained address base is 0x80. Subsequent modification of 0x05 or the write to 0x01 does not retroactively replace that scalar.

6. **Derived fixed writes.** With displaced word 7 and nonoverflowing arithmetic, the fixed 0x54 word payload is 14. The later fixed 0x15 byte payload is the small base’s low byte, not the low byte of 28 used by preceding computed stores.

7. **Late copy under aliasing.** Suppose retained-global-base offset 0x74 and working-base offset 0x40 designate the same word. If that word yields 0x11223344 at acquisition and the saved working word is 0x55667788, the global store writes 0x55667788, but the subsequent working-base offset-0x10 store carries 0x11223344. 9. **Retained transfer arguments.** Later stores overlapping an argument’s source do not change an already acquired argument. In particular, the third argument remains the saved companion word.

10. **Repeated effects and partial progress.** Equal final contents do not establish equivalence to repeated writes. A late invalid destination has no represented rollback restoring earlier effects; actual fault handling remains environment-dependent.
