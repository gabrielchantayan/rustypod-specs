## Behavior

### Dependency-driven preparation

The initial and largest portion consists of sequential indirect calls. The first target depends on incoming control-flow state; subsequent targets are formed from embedded target anchors and a common displacement of 4094 bytes.

Calls must be treated as ordered events, not interchangeable calculations. Calls whose explicit arguments or results appear unused may still have observable effects.

The preparation phase exposes these data transformations:

- Incoming words are shifted left by five, six, and twelve positions for early arguments.
- Many primary results are shifted left by eight positions before being passed onward.
- Combined results often supply a shifted low word and an unchanged high word to the next invocation.
- Some argument groups duplicate the same transformed value in multiple positions.
- Other arguments use shifted secondary results from preceding calls.
- Explicit zero arguments occur throughout, including groups of several zero-valued arguments.
- Certain intermediate values are explicitly reset to zero before further shifts or masks.

“No explicit arguments shown” does not establish that a dependency reads no implicit inputs. Likewise, an apparently unused return does not establish that a call is disposable.

### Packed-value transformations

The visible preparation includes masks selecting low fields of 24, 22, 20, 19, 18, 17, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. These fields are shifted both left and right and passed to dependencies or retained for later address calculations.

Unsigned right shifts are distinguishable from signed operations in several places. Examples include extracting the high result word shifted right by two, four, or twelve positions through a 64-bit unsigned shift by 34, 36, or 44 positions.

Several transformations discard information:

- A 32-bit left shift by eight followed by an unsigned right shift by eight retains only the original low 24 bits.
- Masking to 24 bits before shifting right by twenty produces a four-bit value.
- Masking to 22 bits before shifting left by seven produces a value occupying at most 29 bits.
- A byte store never preserves the full address or word from which its value was obtained.

Arithmetic must not be silently widened. Under a 32-bit modular interpretation, repeated left shifts can eliminate all input bits. For example, shifting a 32-bit word left by 28 positions and later shifting that retained result left by another 28 produces zero. That interpretation is conditional: the supplied signed expressions alone do not independently prove executable overflow semantics.

### Transition to direct memory mutation

The last preparation calls produce values used as both data and addresses. For clarity, this contract names a few **semantic roles**, not recovered source variables:

- **Packed base:** a retained low result word shifted left by 25 positions.
- **Scaled base:** another result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Small selector:** a result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, then shifted right by six.
- **Doubled-selector base:** twice the small selector.
- **Saved shifted word:** a retained result shifted left by eight.
- **Final pair:** the low and high words of the last combined dependency result before direct stores.

Under 32-bit modular arithmetic, the packed and scaled bases each retain only seven original low bits. The small selector is more constrained than its mask alone suggests: its bottom two bits are zero, and its range is 0 through 508. Thus the doubled-selector base ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

These constraints do not make the resulting addresses valid.

2. A byte at packed-base offset 9 receives the low byte of that high word.
3. A word at an address formed from the final low word multiplied by 256, plus offset 84, receives the scaled base.
4. The same packed-base byte is written again, this time from the packed base itself.
5. Scaled-base offset 20 is cleared.
6. A byte is read at scaled-base offset 5.
7. A word at doubled-selector-base offset 20 is cleared before the earlier byte is stored, zero-extended, at scaled-base offset 52.

These are access-order requirements, not merely final-state requirements. In particular, clearing a word and later reading it does not justify substituting zero if intervening accesses can alias that word or the memory is externally mutable.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria. **No runtime tests have been performed.**

1. **Byte truncation:** if a byte-store value has low eight bits equal to 171, the stored byte is 171 regardless of the upper 24 bits.

2. **Combined-result extraction:** if a dependency result has low word 305419896 and high word 2596069104, a later unchanged high-word argument retains 2596069104. It is not replaced with the low word or a memory-endian interpretation.

3. **Selector boundary:** under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, a source value whose low seven bits are all set produces a small selector of 508 and a doubled-selector base of 1016. Higher source bits do not enlarge this selector.

4. **Ordered overwrites:** observing the packed-base offset-9 byte during the initial mutation group must distinguish its first high-word-derived value from its later packed-base-derived value, unless those bytes happen to be equal.

5. **Literal access widths:** the stores of 58 must retain their respective 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. Converting them all to byte writes is not equivalent.

6. **Aliasing:** if a write overlaps a later loaded field, that load observes the then-current storage subject to the execution environment. A precomputed snapshot is not an established substitute.

7. **Repeated publication:** memory-mapped or otherwise observable destinations may expose every repeated publication group. Final memory equality alone is insufficient evidence of equivalent behavior.

8. **Failure:** an invalid early indirect target does not require the terminal dependency to be invoked, and no successful return or rollback is promised.

9. **Terminal arguments:** if finalization completes and the terminal target is reached, its first explicit argument is 114 and its remaining three arguments retain the stated provenance and order.
