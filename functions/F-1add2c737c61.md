No application-level purpose is established. The address calculations and stored values do not identify a data structure, protocol, device, allocator, or ownership model.

### Width and arithmetic rules

The representation uses 32-bit unsigned arithmetic for U and the two unsigned dependency results designated **X** and **Y** below. For these expressions, reduction modulo \(2^{32}\) is established within the supplied representation:

\[
S = U \gg 3
\]

\[
W = (X \ll 2) \bmod 2^{32}
\]

\[
H_u = (S \times 0x80000) \bmod 2^{32}
\]

\[
Z = (8Y + 1) \bmod 2^{32}
\]

Both right shifts of U are logical. Masked right shifts of X and Y are also unsigned.

In particular, the multiplication producing \(H_u\) is unsigned **before** its result is assigned to a signed address-basis value. Unsigned reduction is therefore established independently of whether that later signed conversion is fully specified.

The following remain separate concerns:

- Converting an unsigned result outside the signed 32-bit range into the signed address-basis type.
- Signed multiplication, addition, or left shift outside its defined range.
- Integer-to-pointer conversion and validity of the resulting address.
- Whether the recovered operations match actual instructions.

No universal wrapping rule is established for those concerns.

Each selector read produces an unsigned 8-bit value. Its destination calculation, selector plus `0x11`, lies between `0x11` and `0x110` inclusive. Byte stores are described by their retained eight-bit patterns; recovered character conversions do not establish a portable character signedness or runtime ABI.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static-contract examples, not test results. They assume that preceding calls return, relevant conversions have the stated outcomes, and required accesses are valid.

### Distinct word-store value and address

If B is 3, T is 12. The second word store writes 12 at `0x44`.

If C is subsequently 5, the third word store writes that same value 12 at `0x28`, then **F-000000000010** receives 20.

Writing 20 in the third store would violate the represented dependency on B.

### Logical shifts and unsigned multiplication reduction

If U is `0x80000000`:

- S is `0x10000000`.
- **F-000000000017** receives `0x02000000` as its second argument.
- \(H_u\) is zero because the unsigned multiplication reduces modulo \(2^{32}\).
- Conversion of that zero to H preserves zero, so the first three selector-address expressions are 1.

The arithmetic result does not establish that address 1 is accessible.

### Unsigned shift reduction versus byte truncation

If X is `0xC0000041` and J₁ is `0x20`:

- W is `0x00000104`, after unsigned 32-bit reduction.
- The first byte store writes `0x04` at `0x31`.
- **F-000000000021** receives `0x00000104`.

Neither an unbounded shifted integer nor the truncated byte is the represented call argument.

### Masked extraction

If X is `0x3FF00000`, the second byte-store expression before byte narrowing is `0x3FF`; the stored pattern is `0xFF`.

The final byte retains original bits 20 through 27. It does not hold a ten-bit field.

### Fresh selectors

If the three H-based reads return `0x02`, `0x09`, and `0x04`, their byte-store destinations are `0x13`, `0x1A`, and `0x15`.

**F-00000000002D** receives 4, independently of the third stored byte.

### Unsigned final-address wrapping

If Y is `0x20000003`, Z is `0x19` after unsigned multiplication and addition reduce modulo \(2^{32}\).

The final selector read therefore uses the pointer interpretation of `0x19`, independently of H. The final stored byte pattern is zero: the masked shifted value is `0x200`, whose low byte is zero.

### Unresolved signed conversion

If U is `0x00008000`, S is `0x00001000`, and \(H_u\) is `0x80000000`.

That unsigned result is established. The subsequent signed conversion is not assigned a particular outcome by this contract, so no unconditional H-based selector address follows.

### Interrupted execution
