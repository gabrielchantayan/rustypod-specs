### Inherited-payload phase

The early direct stores use the inherited eight-bit payload. Their common destination is eight times an address-producing word.

Each explicit byte store writes one byte. An eight-times-scaled address does not mean that eight bytes are written.

The first byte store precedes a call consuming four times the same address-producing word. That call supplies the early aggregate result whose consumer receives low, high, low.

Many subsequent inherited-payload stores are followed by a call receiving two equal arguments, each formed by shifting the store’s address-input word left by 14 bits.

Exceptions include:

- An intervening call consumes a four-times-scaled word and returns a new word; the next byte store uses twice that new word, followed by two equal 12-bit-left-shifted arguments.
- One store is followed by a four-argument call. Its first, second, and fourth arguments are equal 14-bit-left-shifted values. Its third argument comes from the additional inherited word shifted left by 10 bits.
- A following call consumes a secondary output shifted left by three bits.
- A later inherited-payload store is followed by two equal arguments scaled by four rather than shifted left by 14 bits.

The inherited payload is not established to be zero, a boolean, or a sentinel.

### Explicit-zero phase

These are conditional contract examples, not executed tests. Unless stated otherwise, they assume successful preceding execution, valid memory, and arithmetic that represents the stated values without overflow.

2. **Literal-zero independence.** A nonzero inherited payload does not change the six explicit-zero sites: each still writes zero if reached.

3. **Retained derived payload.** An unsigned payload-producing result of 35 establishes payload 24, because eight times 35 is 280 and its low eight bits are 24. Its initial destination is address 560. Later stores reuse 24 rather than deriving a new payload from their destinations.

4. **Retained address provenance.** If an exceptional derived-byte store’s retained call-derived input differs from the newest primary result, its destination must follow the retained input’s represented scaling. Substituting the newest result or an embedded target constant is not equivalent.

5. **Global assignment boundary.** If the retained payload is 24, that eight-bit value supplies the distinguished-global assignment before the two-zero-argument call. This does not require a one-byte destination store or preservation of adjacent bytes; the destination type and storage width are **documented_only**.

6. **Bit-extraction narrowing.** For a retained unsigned word of 268,435,456, retaining 29 bits and shifting right by 19 gives 512. Narrowing to eight bits produces zero, not 512.

7. **Three-bit extraction.** A retained high byte of 224 produces seven when shifted right by five. Repeated derivations from that same retained high byte continue to produce seven, not zero from repeatedly shifting seven.

8. **Aggregate ordering.** Low word 17 and high word 34 produce arguments 17, 34, 17 at the early consumer and 17, 34, 34 at either high-duplicating consumer.

9. **Read timing.** If an intervening dependency changes a later halfword-read location, the later read consumes its then-current content. Hoisting the read before that dependency is not equivalent.

11. **Dependency non-return.** If F-U001 fails to return at an intermediate site, subsequent accesses and F-U002 need not occur.
