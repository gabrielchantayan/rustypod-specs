### Retained lineage and three-bit extraction

One earlier dependency-derived quantity supplies a pair of equal arguments after a 14-bit left shift. That shifted argument remains relevant much later:

- A byte-write destination uses it after a further 16-bit left shift.
- A following invocation receives it twice after a further 27-bit left shift.
- A separate retained quantity is formed by multiplying it by 32,768.

The multiplication adds 15 bits of scaling to the earlier 14-bit shift: **29 bits of total scaling, not 32 or more**.

The product later supplies a byte-write destination after a further 12-bit shift, two equal arguments after a further 23-bit shift, and an upper-byte extraction. Later small-value writes use the extracted upper byte’s top three bits—equivalently, bits 29–31 of the retained product. The resulting byte is in the range 0–7.

**Conditional wrapping interpretation:** if the shift and multiplication are interpreted as wrapping 32-bit bit operations, the product is the original quantity multiplied by \(2^{29}\), reduced modulo \(2^{32}\). Its upper three bits contain the original quantity’s low three bits, in the same order. Consequently, the three-bit write value equals the original quantity modulo eight.

Nonzero values are permitted. An original quantity of one gives a shifted argument of 16,384, a product of 536,870,912, an extracted upper byte of 32, and a three-bit value of one. The product is zero under this interpretation exactly when the original quantity’s low three bits are zero.

This is a conditional numerical relationship, not an unconditional guarantee about represented signed operations. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, and integer-to-address conversion still require target-level semantics.

A separate byte write selects bits 19–26 of the retained unsigned dependency result. The represented mask preserves 29 low bits; the subsequent right shift can leave ten bits before byte narrowing discards the upper two. This byte is independent of both the low-five-bit-derived retained byte and the product-derived three-bit value.
