## Behavior

### 1. Entry and initial field updates

An entry-only indirect call uses a target formed from incoming return-link-related state. Its 32-bit result becomes the **iteration index**.

Each iteration begins with a selector-field update through the current selector base. The stored byte is the low byte of initial-data bits `20` through `29`; consequently, only bits `20` through `27` reach memory.

The following calls and effects occur in order:

1. A dependency receives the current shifted-input word shifted left by 12.
2. Another dependency returns a word, which is forwarded to a two-argument dependency alongside the iteration index shifted left by 30.
3. A dependency supplies a 64-bit result. The next dependency receives its low half followed by its high half.
4. One intervening call occurs, followed by a call returning another word.
5. The selector byte is read again through the current selector base, and the selected field is cleared.
6. A dependency receives the latest returned word shifted left by two.

The two selector reads are separate observations. An intervening dependency may change which destination is selected.

### 2. Initial paired word writes

After three intervening calls, another dependency returns a word. Four times that word becomes the first payload.

The first payload is stored at the iteration index multiplied by `16384`, plus `20`. A dependency then receives four arguments, in order:

- The iteration index multiplied by `4096`.
- That same value again.
- The iteration index multiplied by `16384`.
- The first payload.

After three intervening calls, another dependency returns a destination index. The first payload is stored at four times this index plus `20`; four times the index is then passed to a dependency.

The two stores are separated by calls and are not an atomic pair.

### 3. Two self-derived payload stages

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns an index. A payload equal to four times that index is stored at sixteen times the index plus `20`. A dependency receives that payload three times.

After three further intervening calls, another dependency returns a destination index. The same payload is stored at four times that destination index plus `20`; four times the destination index is passed to a dependency.

This pattern then repeats with fresh results:

- Three intervening calls precede the payload-producing dependency.
- Four times its returned index becomes the second self-derived payload.
- That payload is stored at sixteen times its producing index plus `20`.
- A dependency receives the payload three times.
- Three intervening calls precede another destination-producing dependency.
- The payload is stored at four times the returned destination index plus `20`.
- Four times that destination index is passed to a dependency.

Two distinct values from this second stage remain significant:

- The **direct-clear base** is the payload-producing index multiplied by `1073741824`. Under 32-bit modular arithmetic, it has four possible bit patterns.
- The **retained paired destination index** is the later dependency result used for the second store. This unscaled index remains the source of the later shift-left-by-31 store.

The retained paired destination index is not the payload-producing index, a fixed call-location value, or the index returned immediately before that later store.

### 4. Repeated direct byte clearing

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns a word retained for a later table read.

The routine directly clears one byte at the direct-clear base plus `17`, then one byte at the direct-clear base plus `1`. No selector read determines these two destinations.

A dependency receives the retained table-address word shifted left by two. Another dependency returns a word, which is forwarded to the next dependency.

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns another word. The direct clears occur again, in the same order: base plus `17`, then base plus `1`. A dependency then receives the latest returned word shifted left by two.

The repeated stores are not safely removable: intervening calls may mutate memory, and accesses may themselves be observable.

### 5. Retained-index high-bit store and table read

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns a fresh index. Four times this fresh index is retained as a later call argument.

A 32-bit store occurs at sixteen times the fresh index plus `20`. Its value is the **retained paired destination index from §3 shifted left by 31**.

Under 32-bit modular arithmetic:

- An even retained paired destination index produces `0x00000000`.
- An odd retained paired destination index produces `0x80000000`.

The fresh index determines this store’s destination, not its value. The stored word is not necessarily `0x80000000`.

Next, a 32-bit table word is read from address `20` plus bits `20` through `29` of the word retained before the direct clears. This is a ten-bit byte offset, giving possible starting addresses `20` through `1043`. It is not multiplied by four.

The routine then:

1. Passes the retained four-times-fresh-index value to a dependency.
2. Obtains another dependency result, retained as the **table-phase result**.
3. Calls a dependency with the table word shifted right by 22 and then left by 28.

Under 32-bit truncation, the last argument contains original table-word bits `22` through `25` in bits `28` through `31`.

### 6. Zero word and selector-field reconstruction

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns an index. A zero word is stored at four times that index plus `20`, and four times the index is passed to a dependency.

After three more intervening calls, another dependency returns a new data word. The following sequence uses the direct-clear base as selector base:

1. Read the selector and write the low byte of four times the new data word to its selected field.
2. Pass four times the data word to a dependency.
3. Obtain a dependency result and forward it to another dependency alongside the data word shifted left by 30.
4. Make three intervening calls, then obtain another result.
5. Read the selector again and retain the selector byte itself.
6. Write the low byte of data-word bits `20` through `29` to that selected field.
7. Pass the latest dependency result shifted left by two to a dependency.
8. Make four intervening calls.
9. Store the table-phase result shifted left by two at the cached selector byte plus `20`.

The last store uses the cached byte. It does not re-read its original selector storage after the four calls.

### 7. Global snapshot and another field-clear sequence

The routine reads the 32-bit global location and retains its value.

It passes the four-times-fresh-index value from §5 to a dependency, makes three intervening calls, and obtains a 64-bit result, here called the **global-phase wide result**. Four times its low half becomes a retained scaled value.

The routine then:

1. Reads the selector through the direct-clear base.
2. Writes the low byte of the global snapshot shifted right by 22 to the selected field.
3. Passes the wide result’s high half to a dependency.
4. Obtains another result and forwards it alongside the retained scaled value shifted left by 28.
5. Makes three intervening calls and obtains another result.
6. Re-reads the selector through the direct-clear base and clears the selected field.
7. Passes the latest returned word shifted left by two to a dependency.

The snapshot-derived byte contains original global bits `22` through `29`. Subsequent global changes do not replace the captured value.

### 8. Propagation of another payload

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns a word. That word shifted left by two is passed twice to the next dependency.

After three additional intervening calls, another dependency result is multiplied by four to form a new payload. This payload is stored at the retained scaled value from §7 multiplied by `4096`, plus `20`.

A dependency receives that retained scaled value shifted left by 14.

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns another destination index. The same payload is stored at four times that index plus `20`, and four times the index is passed to a dependency.

After three more intervening calls, a dependency returns a 64-bit result, here called the **transition wide result**. Its low half controls the subsequent selector-base transitions.

### 9. Selector-base transitions

The routine performs a selector-field update through a base equal to the retained scaled value from §7 multiplied by `16384`. The stored byte is the low byte of payload bits `20` through `29`.

It then:

1. Passes the transition wide result’s high half to a dependency.
2. Obtains another result and forwards it to a dependency.
3. Obtains a further 64-bit result and passes its low and high halves to another dependency.
4. Makes one intervening call and obtains another result.
5. Clears a field selected through the transition wide result’s low half multiplied by `1073741824`.
6. Passes the latest returned word shifted left by two to a dependency.

The further wide result in step 3 does not replace the transition wide result as the source of these selector bases.

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns a word retained as the **final propagation result**.

The current selector base is replaced with sixteen times the transition wide result’s low half. A selector-field update through this new base writes zero.

### 10. Final propagation and repetition

A dependency receives the transition wide result’s low half shifted left by 12. Another dependency returns a word, which is forwarded alongside the table-phase result shifted left by 28.

After three intervening calls, another dependency returns a word. The routine re-reads the selector through the current selector base, clears its selected field, and passes the returned word shifted left by two to a dependency.

Four intervening calls follow.

The final propagation result shifted left by 14 becomes the **propagated word**. It is stored at the final propagation result multiplied by `16384`, plus `20`.

A dependency receives the final propagation result shifted left by 16. After another call, a two-argument dependency receives:

- The propagated word shifted left by 28.
- The additional register-derived value of unresolved provenance.

Under 32-bit modular arithmetic, the first argument is zero because the propagated word already has fourteen zero low bits.

After three intervening calls, a dependency returns another destination index. The propagated word is stored at four times that index plus `20`, and four times the index is passed to a dependency.

After three further intervening calls, a dependency supplies the next iteration index. Execution repeats without an exit test.

The next iteration retains the updated selector base. Both the data word and shifted-input word become the propagated word. The entry-only call is not repeated.
