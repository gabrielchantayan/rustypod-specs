# F-54e74a330e81

## Purpose

No application-level purpose is established. Character-like constants and recurring offsets do not establish formatting, serialization, allocation, initialization, or a container operation.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** explicitly represented value widths, address expressions, captures, and relative event order.

- **documented_only:** behavior not established here, including underlying access types of undeclared named storage.

## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

The indirect-call sequence precedes the first explicitly represented direct store. Dependencies may themselves access or mutate memory before that store.

The extended phase combines calls with no displayed explicit arguments, word and paired results, explicit zeros, transformed prior values, and additional machine-result words. Unused results do not make calls inert. Missing displayed arguments do not exclude implicit machine inputs.

### Arithmetic and extraction

The representation distinguishes 32-bit transformations from logical shifts of 64-bit paired results. Paired-result shifts by 34, 36, and 44 bits extract high-component-derived fields, not fields obtained by shifting only the low component.

Representative unsigned field bounds are:

| Transformation | Result range |
|---|---:|
| Retain low 24 bits | 0–0xFFFFFF |
| Retain low 22 bits | 0–0x3FFFFF |
| Retain low 20 bits, then shift right by 10 | 0–1023 |
| Retain low 19 bits, then shift left by 2 | 0–0x1FFFFC |
| Retain low 17 bits, then shift right by 4 | 0–8191 |
| Retain low 14 bits, then shift left by 16 | 0–0x3FFF0000 |

**[INFERENCE]:** Under a 32-bit bit-vector model, arithmetic wraps modulo \(2^{32}\), and left shifts discard bits beyond bit 31. Successive shifts can therefore remove every original bit. This model is not a portable guarantee for represented signed arithmetic.

Locally saved zeros remain zero under their displayed local shifts until replaced. That fact does not establish preservation of external machine or memory state.

Near the direct-memory transition, one word is multiplied by \(2^{25}\). A separate word is shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, then logically shifted right by six.

**[INFERENCE]:** Under the bit-vector model, the latter transformation places original bits 0–6 in result positions 2–8. The resulting small quantity is a multiple of four between 0 and 508; its double is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016. These are numeric bounds, not allocation or mapping guarantees.

### First direct mutation and readback

After the final paired-result-producing call before direct mutation, the first direct store writes a word at the pair’s high component plus 0x14. Its value is a previously saved shifted word.

A computed byte destination at another base plus 9 next receives the low byte of the pair’s high component. A word destination at 256 times the pair’s low component plus 0x54 receives the \(2^{25}\)-scaled word. The same byte destination then receives the low byte of the saved shifted word. Different source expressions do not guarantee different stored bytes.

Early mutation also establishes the following relationships:

- The word at the \(2^{25}\)-scaled base plus 0x14 is cleared before an explicitly unsigned byte read at that base plus 5.
- A word at twice the small quantity plus 0x14 is cleared.
- The saved byte is zero-extended into a word at the first base plus 0x34.
- The first base’s +0x14 word is read again.
- Another clear of the small-base destination and two identical-source byte stores follow.

The later word read is a separate memory event, not reuse of the earlier stored zero.

**[INFERENCE]:** In stable ordinary memory, suitable nonaliasing can make a preceding clear determine a later read. Such conditions are not established. Overlap, external mutation, or access-sensitive memory can distinguish these events.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

### Mask boundary

An incoming masked value of 0xFFFFFFFF produces 0xFFC00000. An incoming value of 0x003FFFFF produces zero. Neither example establishes dependency success.

### Explicit unsigned-byte widening

If an explicitly unsigned computed +5 byte read yields 0xE7, its widened word publication is 0x000000E7, not 0xFFFFFFE7. This criterion does not apply automatically to named storage at fixed 0x05.

### Fixed read-before-write without a width assumption

A trace reaching the relevant early region must evaluate named storage at 0x05 before assigning named storage at 0x01. No eight-bit read or zero-extension requirement follows.

If additional declarations establish an unsigned eight-bit source, 0xE7 converts to 0x000000E7. If they instead establish a signed eight-bit source with value −25, conversion to unsigned 32 bits produces 0xFFFFFFE7. If the source is wider, a converted base above 255 is not excluded.

### Other named-storage ordering

The assignment at 0x54 precedes the later assignment at 0x15. The destination-base source is captured before the assignment at 0x60. These constraints remain valid without specifying the named accesses’ widths.

### Fresh reads after aliasing

Assume supplied declarations establish ordinary unsigned-byte storage at 0x67 and a compatible byte destination at 0x21. If 0x67 receives 0x12, then an intervening computed write changes it to 0xA5 before the fresh read for 0x21, that assignment receives 0xA5.

Without those declarations, fresh evaluation remains required, but the underlying read width and destination conversion remain documented_only. Each later computed byte store uses the value obtained by its own read, narrowed to eight bits.

### Repeated stores and ordered replacement

Equal low bytes from the two different early byte-store sources are accepted; a value change is not required.

Where accesses are observable, the derived +0x20 word destination receives 0x20, 0x2F, the widened computed +4 byte, and 0x20 in that order. Keeping only the last store does not preserve the trace.

### Arithmetic boundaries and saved arguments

Overwriting the earlier +0x14 snapshot in memory before the final transfer does not replace the saved third argument.
