## Behavior

### Execution and arithmetic model

The represented sequence consists of dependency-driven preparation, direct memory mutation, a fixed repeated-write region, and concluding reads, writes, and transfer. No explicit conditional branch, retry, or data-dependent loop is represented. Every later requirement is conditional on earlier operations completing.

The representation mixes signed and unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. **32-bit interpretation**, used below for conditional arithmetic examples, means retaining the low 32 bits of arithmetic and left-shift results. It is not a claim that overflowing signed expressions have portable language-level semantics.

Under this interpretation:

- A left shift by 8 discards the original highest 8 bits.
- A left shift by 30 retains only the original lowest 2 bits.
- Two left shifts by 14 have the low-32-bit effect of a shift by 28.
- A left shift by 14 followed by one by 23 produces zero.
- Multiplication by a power of two can discard bits before subsequent extraction.

Explicit byte accesses use 8 bits, halfword stores use 16 bits, and word accesses use 32 bits. Narrow stores retain the corresponding low bits. Loads explicitly represented as unsigned bytes are zero-extended.

The concluding variable right shift uses the low 8 bits of a computed quantity as its count. Behavior for counts outside the operation’s defined range, and right shifts of negative signed values, is **documented_only**. No additional count masking is assumed.
