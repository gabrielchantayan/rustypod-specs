3. Invoke **F-D08** with the four-times-scaled result as its sole explicit argument.

### 3. First indirect byte destination

Let the result of F-D12 be the **retained result**, and let its two-bit left shift be the **scaled result**.

Immediately after F-D12:

1. Read one unsigned byte at offset `1` from the incoming address-like value.
2. Add `0x11` to that byte’s numeric value.
3. Write the low byte of the scaled result to the resulting address.

The byte read is used as a small absolute address component, not as a displacement from the original incoming base. Consequently, the write destination lies between `0x11` and `0x110`, inclusive.

The stored value is the low byte of the retained result shifted left by two. Its low two bits are always zero.

Next:

2. Invoke **F-D15** without explicit arguments and retain its represented 32-bit result.
3. Read the byte at offset `1` from the incoming address-like value again.
4. Add `0x11` to this newly read byte.
5. Write the low byte of the following value to that destination: the retained result masked with `0x3FFFFFFF`, then shifted logically right by twenty positions.
6. Invoke **F-D16** with the result of F-D15 shifted left by two as its sole explicit argument.

The second byte read is not replaced by the first read’s value. Dependencies, the preceding write, or external state may have changed the byte between reads.

After truncation to eight bits, the second written value consists of bits 20 through 27 of the retained result. Bits 28 and 29 survive the mask and shift but are discarded by the byte write; bits 30 and 31 are removed by the mask.

4. Write one zero byte to the resulting destination.
5. Invoke **F-D21** with four explicit arguments, in order:
