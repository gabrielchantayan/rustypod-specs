## Behavior

### Dependency naming and ordering

The opaque dependency labels F-D01 through F-D40 identify call sites in execution order. They are local labels for this contract. They do not establish forty distinct functions: computed targets may alias, and no dependency identity beyond the supplied map is available.

A call described as having “no explicit arguments” has none shown in the representation. This does not prove that the callee consumes no registers, memory, or other implicit state.

All following stages are conditional on earlier stages completing.

### 1. Store the entry word using the first result

1. Invoke F-D01 through a target derived from the incoming link-like value plus 0xFFE. Capture its result as Q1.
2. Write E as a four-byte quantity at address **4 × Q1 + 0x14**.
3. Invoke F-D02 with **4 × Q1** as its explicit argument.
4. Invoke F-D03, F-D04, and F-D05 in that order, with no explicit arguments.
5. Invoke F-D06 and capture its result as Q2.

There is no visible range check on Q1 before the store. The store precedes F-D02 and every later call.

### 2. Store a scaled result and capture a word

1. Write **4 × Q2** as a four-byte quantity at address **16 × Q2 + 0x14**.
2. Read a four-byte unsigned value from address **4 × Q2 + 0x14**, capturing it as W.
3. Invoke F-D07 with **4 × Q2**.
4. Invoke F-D08, F-D09, and F-D10 in order, with no explicit arguments.
5. Invoke F-D11 and capture its result as Q3.

The write and read use different scaling factors. They must not be collapsed into an assumed store-and-reload of one field. Their ranges can nevertheless coincide or overlap for particular values or under machine-address wrapping.

W is captured before F-D07. Later use is of that captured value, not an explicit reread of its source address.

### 3. Store the upper portion of the captured word

1. Form the four-byte unsigned scaled value **U = 4 × Q3**.
2. Write **W shifted right logically by 12 bits** as a four-byte quantity at address **U + 0x14**.
3. Invoke F-D12 with U.
4. Invoke F-D13, F-D14, and F-D15 in order, with no explicit arguments.
5. Invoke F-D16 and capture its result as Q4.

The logical shift discards W’s lowest twelve bits. The stored numeric value is between 0 and 0x000FFFFF inclusive.

### 4. Store another scaled result and issue a three-argument call

1. Form **V = 4 × Q4**.
2. Write V as a four-byte quantity at address **16 × Q4 + 0x14**.
3. Invoke F-D17 with these three explicit arguments, in order:
   - **(U masked with 0x3F) shifted left by 14 bits**;
   - V;
   - **Q3 shifted left by 30 bits**.
4. Invoke F-D18, F-D19, and F-D20 in order, with no explicit arguments.
5. Invoke F-D21 and capture its result as Q5.

The first argument is derived from U, not directly from Q3. Under 32-bit wrapping arithmetic, U’s lowest two bits are zero; consequently that argument ranges from zero through 0x000F0000 in increments of 0x00010000.

Under that same arithmetic model, the third argument retains only Q3’s lowest two bits, positioned in bits 30–31. The representation’s use of a signed expression prevents treating that model as an independently proven language-level guarantee.

### 5. Copy the previous scaled result to another computed address

1. Write V as a four-byte quantity at address **4 × Q5 + 0x14**.
2. Invoke F-D22 with **4 × Q5**.
3. Invoke F-D23, F-D24, and F-D25 in order, with no explicit arguments.
4. Invoke F-D26 and capture its unsigned result as Q6.

The value stored here comes from Q4, while the destination address comes from Q5. No explicit memory load refreshes V before this store.

### 6. Perform the first byte-indirected store

1. Form **X = Q6 shifted left by 2 bits**.
2. Read one unsigned byte from address **Q3 × 0x10000000 + 1**, capturing it as B1.
3. Write the low eight bits of X at address **B1 + 0x11**.
4. Invoke F-D27 with:
   - X;
   - X again;
   - **Q6 shifted left by 4 bits**.
5. Invoke F-D28 and capture its four-byte result as T.
6. Invoke F-D29 with T.
7. Invoke F-D30, F-D31, and F-D32 in order, with no explicit arguments.
8. Invoke F-D33 and capture its result as Q7.

F-D27 uses a different visible target-offset form from the surrounding calls: 0xFEC rather than 0xFFE. The evidence does not explain that distinction.

B1 is a byte value used directly in address arithmetic. It is not established as a full pointer. Because B1 is between 0 and 255, the destination of this store is numerically between **0x11 and 0x110**, inclusive.

With 32-bit unsigned shifting, the stored byte is **4 × (Q6 masked with 0x3F)**. Higher bits of Q6 do not affect this first byte’s value.

### 7. Re-read the byte source and perform the second byte store

1. Read one unsigned byte again from address **Q3 × 0x10000000 + 1**, capturing it as B2.
2. Write the low eight bits of **(Q6 masked with 0x3FFFFFFF) shifted right by 20 bits** at address **B2 + 0x11**.
3. Invoke F-D34 with **Q7 shifted left by 2 bits**.
4. Invoke F-D35, F-D36, and F-D37 in order, with no explicit arguments.
5. Invoke F-D38 and capture its result as Q8.
6. Invoke F-D39 with:
   - **Q8 shifted left by 2 bits**;
   - B2, promoted to an unsigned word-sized value.
7. Invoke F-D40 with no explicit arguments.

The second byte-source read is observable and cannot be replaced by reuse of B1. Calls between the two reads may change the source location. The first byte store can also alias that location under some address-arithmetic models.

After truncation to one byte, the second stored value consists of **Q6 bits 20–27**. Bits 28–29 survive the mask and shift but are then discarded by byte truncation; bits 30–31 are masked off earlier.

The second store does not necessarily overwrite the first. It does so only when B1 and B2 select the same destination.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static expectations, not executed tests.

1. **Initial store precedes notification.**  
   If Q1 is 3 and the destination is writable, the first explicit write places E at address 0x20. F-D02 subsequently receives 12. A trace that calls F-D02 before that write would not match the represented order.

2. **Distinct scaled addresses remain distinct.**  
   If Q2 is 2, the second word store writes 8 at address 0x34. The subsequent word read occurs at address 0x1C. Assuming no aliasing, the store does not determine the loaded value.

3. **Logical extraction of W.**  
   If W is 0xABCDEF01 and U is 0x20, the next word store writes 0x000ABCDE at address 0x34. It does not preserve W’s low twelve bits.

4. **Three-argument bit construction.**  
   Assuming 32-bit wrapping arithmetic, if Q3 is 0x13 and Q4 is 2, U is 0x4C and V is 8. F-D17 receives 0x00030000, 8, and the bit pattern 0xC0000000, in that order. The preceding store writes 8 at address 0x34.

5. **Different byte reads select different destinations.**  
   If Q6 is 0x12B00035, B1 is 0x20, and the later read yields B2 = 0x40, the first byte store writes 0xD4 at address 0x31. The second writes 0x2B at address 0x51. This example requires the intervening execution to account for the changed source byte.

6. **Equal byte reads cause an overwrite.**  
   With the same Q6 but B1 = B2 = 0x20, both byte stores target address 0x31. In the absence of other writes, the second leaves 0x2B there.

7. **A fault limits the observed prefix.**  
   If the read producing W faults, the represented path has already performed two word stores. F-D07 and later operations are not reached through this path. No recovery behavior is specified.

8. **Completing the calls does not prove normal completion.**  
   Even if all forty calls and all ten explicit memory operations complete, the evidence establishes only arrival at the invalid-instruction boundary, not a successful return.
