# F-5027506055ce

## Purpose

F-5027506055ce performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, derives addresses and values from their results, mutates memory through mixed-width accesses, and ends with an unresolved indirect transfer.

No application-level purpose is established. Recurring offsets do not establish an object layout, and constants resembling characters do not establish text processing.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented accesses, conversions, value relationships, and ordering.
- **[INFERENCE]:** an interpretation that is not an acceptance requirement.
- **documented_only:** behavior not resolved by the available evidence or this static analysis.

Every effect is conditional on execution reaching it. Earlier dependencies may change memory or implicit state, fail, or not return. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Inputs and implicit context

The nominal interface accepts three opaque 32-bit values. Their encoding, signedness, pointer status, valid ranges, and ownership are unspecified.

The first invocation receives those three values unchanged and in order. Its fourth represented argument is an incoming machine-context value shifted left by five bits. Its target is derived from a separate incoming return-link context value plus 0xffe. No validation precedes this invocation.

## Behavior

### Invocation sequence and arithmetic

The opening region is a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. No explicit controlling loop or conditional branch is represented.

Targets change: later invocations use code-related values displaced by 0xffe. They must not be treated as calls to one established dependency. Arguments include shifted primary results, secondary-channel values, explicit zeros, masked fields, duplicated transformed values, and values retained across intervening invocations. Invocations with no represented arguments may still consume implicit state.

Results are consumed as individual words and 64-bit pairs. Logical pair shifts by 34, 36, and 44 bits, followed by 32-bit extraction, respectively yield the high word logically shifted right by two, four, and twelve bits. Masks select fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, and 17 bits.

Under a **conditional 32-bit bitvector model**, left shifts discard bits beyond bit 31. In that model:

- A word shifted left by 28 bits and then left by another 28 bits becomes zero.
- A word shifted left by eight bits and then logically right by eight bits retains its original low 24 bits.

Some represented arithmetic instead uses signed intermediates. Signed overflow, signed left-shift validity, signed right-shift behavior, and overflowing address arithmetic are not independently resolved. The conditional model does not establish defined arithmetic for every represented operation.

Invocations remain ordered events even when their primary results are unused. No dependency is assumed to preserve globals, secondary channels, ownership, lifetimes, or control flow.

### Address-derived values and compact-range invariant

The direct-memory region uses these descriptive values:

- **Retained field:** an earlier paired-result low word shifted left by 25 bits.
- **Large base:** an earlier primary result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Compact index:** a later primary result shifted left by eight bits, masked to 15 bits, then logically shifted right by six bits.
- **Compact base:** twice the compact index.
- **Payload word:** another retained primary result shifted left by eight bits.

Under the conditional 32-bit model, the compact calculation has a stronger bound than the mask alone suggests. Shifting left by eight clears the low eight bits. The 15-bit mask retains only seven potentially nonzero bits, and shifting right by six leaves two low zero bits.

Therefore:

- The compact index is a multiple of four in the inclusive range 0 through 0x1fc.
- The compact base is a multiple of eight in the inclusive range 0 through 0x3f8.
- Equivalently, the compact index is four times the producing word’s low seven bits, and the compact base is eight times those bits.
- Narrowing the compact base to eight bits can produce only multiples of eight from 0x00 through 0xf8.

These are conditional arithmetic invariants, not evidence that the resulting low addresses are accessible.

### Initial dynamic-memory effects

The direct-memory region begins with a word store of the retained field at the final paired result’s high-word base plus 0x14. A byte at retained-field offset 9 receives the high word’s low eight bits. A word at the final pair’s low word scaled by 256, plus 0x54, receives the large base. The retained-field offset-9 byte is then overwritten with the retained field’s low eight bits.

Large-base offset 0x14 is cleared as a word before an unsigned byte is fetched at large-base offset 5. Compact-base offset 0x14 is then cleared as a word, and the fetched byte is zero-extended into a word at large-base offset 0x34.

The word at large-base offset 0x14 is reread. Intervening accesses may alias it, so its value need not remain zero. After another word clear at compact-base offset 0x14, two separate byte stores place the reread value’s low eight bits at compact-base offset 0x15.

### Literal stores and publication

The numeric value 0x3a is stored as a byte at the literal base, as a halfword at eight times the literal link, and as a word at literal-base offset 0x20. These widths are distinct and explicit.

The **adjusted scale** is four times the saved link minus 0x96. The **working base** is four times the adjusted scale.

Working-base offset 0x20 receives four ordered word values: 0x20, 0x2f, a zero-extended unsigned byte fetched from working-base offset 4, and 0x20 again. The byte fetch follows the 0x2f store.

The **publication base** is fetched from separate fixed storage and retained in 32 bits **before** fixed storage at 0x60 receives 0x20. Both fixed-access widths remain **documented_only**.

Publication consists of a finite sequence of repeated four-store groups. Within each group, the order is:

1. A word at scaled-base offset 0x54 receives the literal base.
2. A byte at publication-base offset 0x19 receives the working base’s low eight bits.
3. A byte at working-base offset 0x11 receives the publication base’s low eight bits.
4. A word at publication-base offset 0x34 receives the scaled base.

The first group uses eight times the saved link as its scaled base. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted scale. The publication base remains the captured value, not a reload.

Each repetition is a separate represented event. Exact multiplicity is **documented_only** in this contract; neither a single-group replacement nor an unbounded loop is authorized.

### Attainable compact base and fixed-storage narrowing

If the compact-index-producing invocation returns 0x0000007f, shifting left eight bits produces 0x00007f00. The 15-bit mask retains that value; the logical right shift by six produces compact index 0x1fc. The compact base is therefore 0x3f8.

The assignment input for fixed storage at 0x1 is consequently 0xf8. The later compact-base assignment to fixed storage at 0x15 likewise has input 0xf8. Neither assignment establishes an eight-bit destination access.

A compact base of 0x123456ab is unreachable under this model: it exceeds 0x3f8 and is not a multiple of eight. An assignment input of 0xab cannot result from narrowing this compact-base calculation.

If the producing word is 0x00000080, the shifted value’s only set bit is removed by the mask, producing compact index and compact base zero. This does not guarantee successful access to the resulting addresses.

The 0x5 reference precedes the 0x1 assignment. The 0x15 fixed assignment follows both four-times-saved-link byte stores at compact-base offset 0x15.

### Retained publication base and final interleaving

If the publication-base reference supplies 0x1000, publication uses retained base 0x1000 even if the subsequent fixed assignment at 0x60 alters related storage. Moving the fetch after that assignment violates the ordering requirement.

If the fixed assignment at 0x3a changes memory subsequently read at secondary-base offset 0x14, that later word fetch observes the resulting state. The literal-base-offset-0x74 word has already been retained.

### Independent references to 0x67
