## Behavior

### Width and arithmetic boundary

For explicit computed-address dereferences, the representation establishes byte, halfword, and word widths of 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Paired intermediate results occupy 64 bits.

A word’s low byte means its low eight value bits. Conversion of an explicitly unsigned byte load to an unsigned word zero-extends that byte. These facts do **not** determine the storage widths of undeclared globals.

Unsigned 32-bit shifts and arithmetic retain their represented width, including truncation before subsequent operations. Masks select low-order portions of several widths, including 11–22 bits and 24 bits. A masked-and-scaled value must not be replaced by scaling an unrestricted value.

For paired results, low and high words mean bits 0–31 and 32–63. Full-result right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 act on the 64-bit value. This bit decomposition does not establish memory byte order.

Many other operations use signed 32-bit values. Negative left operands, signed overflow, right shifts of negative values, and unsigned-to-signed conversions require an environmental interpretation. Universal modulo-\(2^{32}\) behavior is not established for those operations.

In particular, the **packed base** is produced by signed 32-bit multiplication by \(2^{25}\), not by a represented left shift. Address calculations also include factors of two, four, eight, and 256, and subtraction of `0x96`, without range checks.

### Opening computed-memory effects

The following labels describe value provenance, not objects:

- **Final pair:** the last paired result before direct stores.
- **Tag base:** an earlier result shifted left 25 positions.
- **Packed base:** the signed multiplication result described above.
- **Small quantity:** a masked-and-shifted nine-bit quantity.
- **Small base:** twice the small quantity.
- **Saved word:** an earlier result shifted left eight positions.

The opening effects first place the tag base in a word at final-pair high word plus `0x14`. A byte at tag base plus `0x09` then receives the final high word’s low byte. A word at 256 times the final low word plus `0x54` receives the packed base, before the tag-base byte is overwritten with the tag base’s low byte.

Next, the packed-base word at `0x14` is cleared, a byte is read at packed base plus `0x05`, and the small-base word at `0x14` is cleared. The captured byte is zero-extended into a word at packed base plus `0x34`.

The packed-base word at `0x14` is then reloaded. Another small-base clearing store follows, before two separate byte stores of the reloaded value’s low byte at small base plus `0x15`.

Under ordinary nonvolatile numerical-address semantics, without external interference, the reload is zero. The word at packed base plus `0x34` is disjoint from the word at `0x14`; an overlapping small-base clearing store writes only zero bytes. Volatility or device behavior can nevertheless make the reload separately observable.

The byte stores at small base plus `0x15` overlap the word beginning at `0x14`. Their effect on a numerical word depends on byte order.

### Global-derived addressing and linked updates

After those two byte stores, the global reference at `0x05` is evaluated into the saved unsigned 32-bit global-derived base. This precedes the assignment at `0x01`.

The saved base supplies a byte-store address at offset `0x15` and a later word-load address at offset `0x54`. Between these accesses, the body performs packed-base tag writes, a saved-word store through the earlier reload plus `0x54`, and clearing of the packed-base tag.

Subsequent operations traverse explicitly word-sized accesses at offsets `0x74`, `0x64`, and `0x04`, and reuse loaded or scaled values as bases. Some accesses instead use an **explicit unsigned byte load** at computed offset `0x05` as the base for a word load at `0x74`. Unlike the undeclared global at `0x05`, these computed byte-derived bases are established narrow values.

Repeated linked writes include self-values, saved words, scaled links, and low-byte tags. One compound update contains two evaluations of a word at small base plus `0x74`; the representation does not justify treating them as one read or imposing an unsupported order between operand evaluations.

The fixed-global assignment at `0x54` occurs after a small-base word update at `0x14` and before further updates there. Its assigned expression is the captured link shifted left one position; its global write width is unknown.

### Final captures and global evaluations

After repetition, the global reference at `0xA9` is captured into a 32-bit saved value. A computed word at an earlier fourfold-link base plus `0x74` is also captured before the assignment at `0x3A`.

For the `0x3A` assignment, the adjusted quantity is first converted to an eight-bit character value, then multiplied by eight and increased by `0x30` using promoted arithmetic. The character’s signedness and the undeclared destination prevent an unconditional claim that this is a single byte write containing the low byte of an unrestricted arithmetic expression.

The body then captures a word at doubled-link base plus `0x14`, used later as the final transfer’s third argument, and a word at adjusted base plus `0x04`.
