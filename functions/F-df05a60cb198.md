It next writes a byte at fixed address 0x3A representing twice the low byte of A plus 0x30, narrowed to eight bits. This is arithmetic, not an established character-encoding operation. For example, its result is not restricted to decimal digit characters.

The body then captures, in order:

- A word from A + 0x04, called T.

Following those captures, it writes the low byte of A at C + 0x0D, the byte 0x6C at A + 0x08, and the low byte of B at both A + 0x0D and A + 0x09, in that order. It then stores C as a word at C + 0x54.

This copy must not be replaced by an unconditional use of the saved low byte of G: the intervening operations may alias 0x67.

### Word replacement and derived destination capture

The low byte of V is written at A + 0x10, followed by a word containing V at B + 0x54. A byte derived from a signed right shift of V is then written at C + 0x01. The shift count is the low eight bits of A; the result is narrowed to one byte.

The word at B + 0x54 is subsequently replaced with A.

Next, a word is captured from A + 0x40. After that capture, T is stored at B + 0x74, and the captured A + 0x40 word is stored at A + 0x10. Consequently, the earlier byte write at A + 0x10 is not the final write to that location under ordinary, noninterfering storage.

- Copies the current byte at fixed address 0x67 to A + 0x09.

Only after those writes does it read the word at A + 0x54. Call this captured address-like value D. It then copies the current byte at fixed address 0x67 to A + 0x09 again.
