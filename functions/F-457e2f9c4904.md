# F-457e2f9c4904

## Behavior

### Execution model and arithmetic

The supplied body represents a straight-line path without an explicit conditional branch or data-dependent loop. Repeated calls and writes are individually represented.

The principal phases are an indirect-call prefix, initial direct mutation, interdependent traversals and constant stores, a repeated-write region, and final accesses followed by an indirect transfer. Each later phase depends on prior operations completing.

**[INFERENCE: 32-bit machine-word interpretation]** Calculations assigned to 32-bit values and dynamically computed addresses retain the low 32 bits. Multiplication and left shifts therefore wrap rather than saturate. Intermediate narrowing matters: two successive left shifts by 28 positions produce zero at 32-bit width, not a retained wider result.

This interpretation describes bit-pattern consequences, not defined portable semantics for every represented signed expression. Exact signed-overflow and instruction behavior remain **documented_only**.

A represented 64-bit return is commonly divided numerically into low and high 32-bit words. That split does not establish memory byte order.

Byte stores retain eight bits; halfword stores retain 16 bits; word stores have width 32 bits. Explicit unsigned byte reads are zero-extended when used in word stores. Masked subfields are not necessarily bytes: selections include fields substantially wider than eight bits.

### Initial direct-memory group

The following are descriptive mathematical roles, not source identifiers:

| Role | Definition |
|---|---|
| Link base | A retained returned word shifted left by 25 positions. |
| Scaled base | Another retained returned word multiplied by \(2^{25}\). |
| Small base | Twice a value formed by shifting a returned word left by eight, masking with `0x7fff`, then logically shifting right by six. |
| Final pair | The low and high words of the last represented 64-bit result before direct mutation. |
| Retained shifted word | An earlier returned word shifted left by eight and retained across later calls. |

The initial effects have these ordering constraints:

- A word store of the link base at final-pair-high plus `0x14` precedes a byte store of the final pair’s high word at link-base plus `9`.
- A word store of the scaled base at final-pair-low multiplied by 256 plus `0x54` precedes a second byte store at link-base plus `9`, this time taking its value from the link base.
- A zero word store at scaled-base plus `0x14` precedes an unsigned byte read at scaled-base plus `5`.
- A zero word store at small-base plus `0x14` precedes the word store of that zero-extended byte at scaled-base plus `0x34`.
- The word at scaled-base plus `0x14` is then reloaded. Another zero word store at small-base plus `0x14` follows, before two identical byte stores at small-base plus `0x15`, using the reload’s low byte.

**[INFERENCE: 32-bit machine-word interpretation]** The link and scaled bases have their low 25 bits clear. The second byte written at link-base plus `9` is therefore zero. The small base is a multiple of eight in the inclusive range zero through `0x3f8`.

The reload at scaled-base plus `0x14` must not be confused with the byte-derived word stored at scaled-base plus `0x34`. These addresses differ by `0x20`, including under 32-bit wraparound, and their four-byte ranges do not overlap. Aliasing among other bases does not make these two same-base destinations coincide.

**[INFERENCE: ordinary stable RAM]** The reload observes zero. The intervening write at scaled-base plus `0x34` cannot change it, and any overlapping part of the intervening small-base word store also writes zero. A nonzero reload would require additional behavior, such as external mutation, memory-mapped read/write semantics, or address-space mappings not described by ordinary numerical-address aliasing.

### Absolute input and subsequent traversals

This read precedes a byte write at absolute address `0x01`, whose value is the small base’s low byte. Subsequent addressing uses the retained value from `0x05`, not a fresh read of that absolute location.

The next group includes:

- A byte store at retained-absolute-input plus `0x15`, using half the small base.
- A byte store of the retained shifted word at scaled-base plus `0x11`, followed later by a zero byte store at that same location.
- A word store of the retained shifted word at reload-value plus `0x54`.
- A word read at retained-absolute-input plus `0x54`, before replacing that location with the earlier reload value.

Call the word read before that replacement the **retained traversal word**. Its value remains retained even if later stores alter its original source.

Further traversals use word reads at offsets `0x74` of the scaled and small bases. Their results become destinations for word stores at `0x54`. One small-base traversal expression contains two represented reads of the same `0x74` location; their relative operand-evaluation order is not established.

Two self-referential word stores write the retained traversal word to its own offset `0x24`. Byte stores at another loaded base’s offset `9` are also repeated, with intervening word stores.

Absolute address `0x54` receives a **32-bit** store of twice the retained traversal word. This occurs after a word store at small-base plus `0x14` derived from the earlier scale-producing return, and before two consecutive word stores of the retained traversal word at small-base plus `0x14`.

After two further byte stores at small-base plus `0x15`, using the low byte of four times the retained traversal word, absolute address `0x15` receives an **8-bit** store of the small base. That absolute write precedes the next word store at four-times-retained-traversal-word plus `0x54`.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Initial scaling:** With fourth argument `3`, ordinary 32-bit shift behavior supplies `96` to the initial call. With `0x08000000`, it supplies zero.
- **Byte widening:** A byte read of `0xe7` at scaled-base plus `5` yields a word store of `0x000000e7` at scaled-base plus `0x34`.
- **Same-base reload:** Under stable ordinary-RAM semantics, the reload at scaled-base plus `0x14` is zero, even when the byte-derived word at plus `0x34` is nonzero. Ordinary cross-base aliasing does not justify replacing this result with that byte-derived word.
- **Retained absolute input:** If the absolute `0x05` read yields `0x1200`, subsequent accesses use base `0x1200` even if the following absolute `0x01` write changes overlapping source storage.
- **Absolute writes:** For retained traversal word `0x1234`, the absolute 32-bit store at `0x54` receives `0x2468` under the stated arithmetic interpretation. The later absolute `0x15` write remains a separate byte access.
- **Global snapshot ordering:** If the absolute `0x60` store changes storage underlying the global-base input, subsequent repeated writes still use the base loaded before that store.
- **Separate `0x67` reads:** If the three reads observe `0x12`, `0x34`, and `0x56`, their consumers receive those respective bytes: absolute `0x21`, the first relevant adjusted-base-plus-`9` store, and the later such store.
- **Access widths and repetition:** A word store of `0x2f` is not a one-byte character write. Externally observed repeated writes must preserve their represented order and multiplicity.
- **Partial failure:** A successful early store followed by an inaccessible later address does not establish a restoration obligation.
- **Final transfer:** Reaching the last transfer requires first explicit argument `0x72`; normal return from its destination is not an acceptance prerequisite established here.
