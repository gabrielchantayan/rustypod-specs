## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static cases, not executed tests. Each assumes preceding operations complete and required accesses and calls are valid.

1. **Opening destination.** If the opening selector is 0x20 and the incoming quantity ends in 0xab, the first store writes 0xab at address 0x31.

2. **Self-overwriting selector.** Suppose the opening selector address is 0x31, its initial byte is 0x20, and the incoming quantity is 0x012000ab. The first store writes 0xab at 0x31, overwriting the selector. If intervening operations do not modify that byte, the next corresponding selector read obtains 0xab. The second store therefore writes 0x12 at 0xbc, not at 0x31. Separate objects are not a prerequisite.

3. **Dependency-modified selector.** If an intervening dependency instead makes the relevant selector 0x40 before the second read, that store targets 0x51.

4. **Masked byte extraction.** Incoming quantity 0xffffffff, masked to 30 bits and shifted right by 20, yields 0x3ff. The byte store retains 0xff.

5. **Independent paired bases.** If the earlier paired low component is 0x100 and the later one is 0x200, the doubled-base byte read targets 0x200. The later word read also targets 0x200 because its offset is zero. Equal addresses do not imply common provenance.

6. **High-bit offset.** Under 32-bit unsigned address arithmetic, later paired low component 0x00080000 produces word-read address 0x80080000.

7. **Different scales.** Result three produces word value 12 at the sixteen-times-indexed destination 0x44. The four-times-indexed destination for the same index would be 0x20.

8. **Reset interference.** If a dependency changes the resettable location after its zero assignment, later reads use the changed contents rather than an assumed zero.

9. **Terminal selectors.** Let the retained propagation word be 0x018000ab, the subsequent paired low component 0x00400080, and the payload 0x12c34567. Selector addresses are respectively 4 and 3. If the reads yield 0x20 and 0x40, the stores write 0x67 at 0x31 and 0x4b at 0x51.

10. **Captured provenance.** In the preceding case, changing the original propagation-word storage after capture does not redirect the first selector read. Changing the byte at address 4 before that read can change its store destination.

11. **Final composition.** If the final dependency byte is 0x5a and the three-byte component at composition time is 0x123456, the published numeric word is 0x1234565a.

12. **Failure boundary.** If address zero is not callable, successful return is not established. Neither preservation nor reversal of earlier effects is guaranteed.
