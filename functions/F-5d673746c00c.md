## Behavior

The phases below specify the represented access order. They do not imply independent objects, atomic execution, or freedom from external interference.

### Fixed repeated prefix

The prefix consists of a fixed sequence of repeated access groups. Each complete group has this order:

| Position | Destination | Width | Value |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Address B plus `0x19` | 8 bits | A-byte |
| 2 | Address A plus `0x11` | 8 bits | B-byte |
| 3 | Address B plus `0x34` | 32 bits | Secondary input in the first group; Doubled A in subsequent groups |
| 4 | Doubled A plus `0x54` | 32 bits | Primary input |

The prefix ends with a partial group containing positions 1–3, without another position-4 store.

No input-controlled count, condition, or termination check is represented. The exact numerical multiplicity of this fixed repetition is **documented_only** in this contract.

Repeated stores are part of the access history even when their values match. They must not be reduced to a single final-state initialization when observers, faults, aliasing, or device-backed memory make individual accesses significant.

### Initial reads and fixed-address byte update

After the final prefix store, the function obtains, in order:

1. A 32-bit word beginning at fixed address `0xA9`, called the **fixed word**.
2. A 32-bit word at the primary input plus `0x74`, called the **primary-associated word**.

It then writes a byte at fixed address `0x3A`, retaining the low eight bits of \(2 \times \text{A-byte} + 0x30\).

After that byte write, it reads:

- A 32-bit word at Address C plus `0x14`, called the **saved C-word**.
- A 32-bit word at Address A plus `0x04`, called the **saved A-word**.

These reads occur after the prefix. Overlapping locations therefore reflect earlier stores, subject to target behavior and external changes. The byte calculation does not establish decimal formatting or restrict the result to a digit.

### Early field updates and shift-derived byte

The next stores occur in this order:

| Destination | Width | Value |
|---|---:|---|
| Address C plus `0x0D` | 8 bits | A-byte |
| Address A plus `0x08` | 8 bits | `0x6C` |
| Address A plus `0x0D` | 8 bits | B-byte |
| Address A plus `0x09` | 8 bits | B-byte |
| Address C plus `0x54` | 32 bits | Address C |
| Fixed address `0x67` | 8 bits | Low byte of the fixed word |

A signed 32-bit word is then read at Address C plus `0x44`; this is the **shift-source word**. After that read, the byte at fixed address `0x67` is read and copied to fixed address `0x21`.

The following stores are ordered:

- Low byte of the shift-source word to Address A plus `0x10`.
- Full shift-source word to Address B plus `0x54`.
- Low byte of the shift-source word right-shifted by A-byte positions to Address C plus `0x01`.
- Address A as a word to Address B plus `0x54`.

The first word written to Address B plus `0x54` is subsequently replaced, but remains an observable intermediate store.

The shift operates on a represented signed 32-bit value. For negative operands, the right-shift behavior is target-dependent. Counts of 32 or greater are also unresolved; a masked shift count must not be assumed.

### Saved-value propagation and repeated fixed-byte reads

A 32-bit word is read at Address A plus `0x40`. The following effects then occur in order:

| Destination or operation | Width | Value or result |
|---|---:|---|
| Store at Address B plus `0x74` | 32 bits | Saved A-word |
| Store at Address A plus `0x10` | 32 bits | Word just read at Address A plus `0x40` |
| Store at Address B plus `0x44` | 32 bits | Address C |
| Read fixed address `0x67`, then store at Address A plus `0x09` | 8 bits | Freshly read byte |
| Store at Address C plus `0x09` | 8 bits | A-byte |

The word store at Address A plus `0x10` covers offsets `0x10` through `0x13`, replacing the earlier bytes at offsets `0x10` and `0x11` unless another relevant effect intervenes.

A 32-bit value is then read at Address A plus `0x54`; call it **Address D**. After that read, fixed address `0x67` is read again and its byte is again stored at Address A plus `0x09`.

These fixed-byte reads are distinct accesses. Their results need not match if earlier computed stores alias fixed address `0x67` or the environment changes it.

### Updates through Address D and acquisition of Address E

The low byte of the primary-associated word is stored at Address D plus `0x0D`.

The subsequent order is:

1. Store the saved C-word at Address C plus `0x54`.
2. Read a 32-bit word at Address A plus `0x14`, called the **late A-word**.
3. Store the low byte of the primary-associated word at Address C plus `0x11`.
4. Store the low byte of Address D at Address A plus `0x09`.
5. Store Address D as a word at Address A plus `0x44`.
6. Read a 32-bit value at Address C plus `0x04`, called **Address E**.

The store of the saved C-word uses the earlier value from Address C plus `0x14`. It does not restore a saved prior value of Address C plus `0x54`.

## Side effects

### Layout and aliasing

Offsets specify accessed locations, not field meanings, object sizes, allocation boundaries, or independent objects.

All computed and fixed regions may overlap unless constrained by the caller or environment. Consequently:

- Earlier stores may change later read results.
- A byte store may modify part of a previously written word.
- A word store may replace several previously written bytes.
- Stores through loaded addresses may alter locations read later.
- Fixed addresses may coincide with computed destinations.

Final-state claims based on independent regions require an explicit nonaliasing assumption.

The three explicitly written fixed-address byte locations are `0x3A`, `0x67`, and `0x21`. The fixed word read begins at `0xA9` and spans four bytes.

### Arithmetic and byte layout

Address A uses unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. Its doubling and unsigned address additions wrap modulo \(2^{32}\).

Other address expressions use represented signed 32-bit operands, including the primary input and Addresses B, C, D, E, and G, as well as Value F. Overflow behavior for these signed expressions is **documented_only**. This contract does not silently widen them or impose unsigned wrapping.

Byte stores retain low-eight-bit patterns. The byte at `0x3A` is specified modulo 256, independently of any character interpretation.

A halfword store writes two bytes and a word store writes four. Byte order determines which stored component appears at each address and is unresolved here.

### Ownership, synchronization, and failure

This does not constrain the dependency’s behavior. It may affect memory or ownership; preservation cannot be assumed.

No explicit error result, rollback, or cleanup path is represented. A failed access may leave earlier effects in place. Whether a faulting access partially completes, terminates execution, or can be resumed is **documented_only**. The full sequence is not established as atomic.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional consequences of static evidence, not executed tests.

### Ordinary nonoverlapping memory, before invocation

Assume all preceding accesses succeed, address arithmetic has defined target behavior, distinct addressed regions do not overlap except for the explicitly repeated or overlapping accesses within each region, and no intervening external changes occur.

**Immediately after the prefix**, Address B plus `0x34` contains Doubled A, not the secondary input. The initial secondary-input store still belongs to the access history. Address B plus `0x19` contains A-byte.

**Immediately before the indirect invocation**, after the final store at twice Value F:

- Address B plus `0x54` contains Address A, not the earlier shift-source word.
- Address C plus `0x54` contains the saved C-word.

These assertions concern the pre-invocation observation point only. They do not establish either value after the dependency executes or after F-5d673746c00c returns.

### Byte-calculation boundary

If A-byte is `0xFF` and execution reaches the fixed-address byte update, the value written at `0x3A` is `0x2E`: the low eight bits of \(2 \times 255 + 48\).

This example concerns that write, not execution through the later shift, whose count would be outside the ordinary 32-bit shift range.

### Shift boundary

If A-byte is zero and the shift is reached, the byte written at Address C plus `0x01` is the low byte of the shift-source word.

For a count of 32 or greater, this contract does not predict the byte without additional target semantics.

### Unsigned doubling boundary

If Address A is `0x80000000`, Doubled A is zero. Each reached primary-input store in a complete prefix group therefore targets address `0x54`, provided the access is permitted.

No saturation or overflow rejection is represented.

### Overlapping byte and word accesses

Assume no unrelated aliasing or external changes. Immediately after a prefix store at Address A plus `0x11`, that location contains B-byte.

After the later word store at Address A plus `0x10`, the byte at offset `0x11` instead comes from that word, according to target byte order.

### Failure before invocation

If the read at Address E plus `0x74` faults and execution does not resume past it, the earlier halfword store at twice Address E and the word store of `100` at Address D plus `0x54` have already been reached.

The indirect invocation is not reached along that interrupted execution. No rollback is represented.

### Retained dependency argument

If execution reaches the indirect invocation, its third argument is the saved C-word obtained earlier from Address C plus `0x14`, regardless of subsequent changes to that memory.

The dependency’s behavior after receiving this argument remains unknown.
