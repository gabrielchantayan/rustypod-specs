## Behavior

The first indirect invocation receives the first argument unchanged and the fourth argument shifted left by seven bits. Its target is constructed from the incoming return-link value plus 0xffe.

Two invocations with no explicitly listed arguments follow. The next receives the third argument shifted left by four bits. Later early calls also use the fourth argument shifted left by twenty-five bits.

The remainder is a long, visibly straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. Its explicit argument transformations include:

### Widths and arithmetic

A byte is 8 bits, a halfword 16 bits, and a word 32 bits. Represented 64-bit results are often used only to expose their constituent words.

Arithmetic constraints include:

- Shifting a low 32-bit word left by eight does not concatenate it with a high word.
- Unsigned 64-bit right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 bits expose the high word shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 bits, respectively.
- A 24-bit mask followed by a six-bit left shift produces at most 30 significant bits.
- A 22-bit mask followed by an eight-bit left shift likewise produces at most 30 significant bits.
- Successive 32-bit left shifts may discard all original information under a bit-vector interpretation; unbounded multiplication is not equivalent.
- Narrow stores retain only the represented low 8 or 16 bits.

The representation also contains signed left shifts, signed multiplication, and a variable signed right shift. Overflow, negative-operand shifts, address overflow, and out-of-range shift counts remain **documented_only**. Wraparound is not an unconditional executable guarantee.

### Entry into direct memory updates

The first word store places the initial byte base at offset 0x14 from the final dependency result’s high word. A byte store then places that high word’s low byte at offset 9 from the initial byte base.

Next, a word store places the primary base at an address formed from the final result’s low word multiplied by 256, plus 0x54. Only afterward is the initial byte base’s offset-9 byte overwritten with the low byte of the initial byte base.

The following interleaving is mandatory in the represented access order:

- The primary base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared, then its offset-5 byte is read and retained.
- The doubled base’s offset-0x14 word receives its **first** zero store.
- The retained byte, zero-extended to 32 bits, is stored at primary-base offset 0x34.
- The primary base’s offset-0x14 word is then read.
- The doubled base’s offset-0x14 word receives its **second** zero store.
- Two consecutive byte stores place the low byte of the just-read word at doubled-base offset 0x15.

Thus, the offset-0x34 store occurs **between** the two separate-location zero stores. The intervening offset-0x14 read occurs after the offset-0x34 store, not before it.

Aliasing may make every part of this interleaving significant. Neither the retained byte nor the later word read may be replaced by an assumed value merely because a nearby location was cleared.

### Initial shared access and saved-link acquisition

After those two offset-0x15 byte stores, the fixed shared value at 0x05 is read and converted to an unsigned 32-bit value. This read precedes the byte write at fixed address 0x01, whose value is the low byte of the doubled base.

The saved 0x05-derived value is used as an address base. Its offset-0x15 byte receives the low byte of the masked index. The primary base’s offset-0x11 byte receives the retained shifted word’s low byte. A word store through the earlier primary-offset-0x14 read follows, before primary offset 0x11 is explicitly overwritten with zero.

The word at offset 0x54 of the saved 0x05-derived base is then read as the **saved link**. That location is subsequently overwritten with the earlier primary-offset-0x14 read. The saved link is retained independently; later uses are not reloads of that overwritten field.

### Constant-writing and repeated-store region

The constant region includes separate stores of 0x3a:

Define the **adjusted value** as four times the saved link minus 0x96, and the **scaled base** as four times that adjusted value, subject to the arithmetic limitations above.

The scaled base’s offset-0x20 word successively receives 0x20, 0x2f, a zero-extended byte read from scaled-base offset 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read remains an actual intervening read.

### Post-region shared accesses

After the repeated-store region, fixed address 0xa9 is read as a 32-bit word and saved. A word at offset 0x74 from four times the address seed is also read before the byte write at fixed address 0x3a.

The 0x3a byte value is the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30. This is numeric byte arithmetic, not evidence of text formatting.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

### Initial argument

If the first invocation is reached, its first explicit value equals the first input word. With fourth argument 1, its represented second value is 128. No preceding validation is visible.

### Correct zero-store interleaving

If the direct-memory transition is reached, the first doubled-base zero store precedes the primary-offset-0x34 store, which precedes the primary-offset-0x14 read and then the second doubled-base zero store. Moving both zero stores after the offset-0x34 store violates the represented order.

### Aliasing during clear-and-read

If the primary-offset-0x34 store overlaps the primary-offset-0x14 word, the later word read may reflect that overlap rather than the earlier zero. Assuming a zero result without excluding aliasing is unacceptable.

### Saved shared-derived base

If the shared word supplies address A, later repeated groups continue using retained A even if the 0x60 write or a computed store changes the shared word’s memory. Reloading the base is not equivalent.

### Saved word versus fresh byte reads

If the saved 0xa9 word has low byte 0x12, the explicit 0x67 write uses 0x12 even if 0xa9 has since changed. If a later intervening computed write changes 0x67 to 0x34, a subsequent 0x67 read must observe memory according to the applicable memory model, not automatically reuse 0x12.

### Constant widths and failure

The separate 0x3a stores retain their 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. If an earlier store succeeds but a later access faults, no visible recovery restores the earlier state.

### Final transfer

If F-unknown-03 is reached, its first explicit word is 0x72 and the remaining words have the saved/read origins specified above. Normal return is not guaranteed.
