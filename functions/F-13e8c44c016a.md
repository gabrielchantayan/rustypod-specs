# F-13e8c44c016a

## Purpose

F-13e8c44c016a computes three signed integer outputs from two supplied integers and two fields in caller-provided storage. Its result depends on the first supplied integer’s position relative to one field, called the **anchor**, and on additional conditions involving the second supplied integer or another field, called the **bound**.

The outputs are called **start**, **counterpart**, and **count** in this contract. These labels describe their roles in the result; they do not establish application-level meanings.

The default result is **(−1, −1, 0)**, in start, counterpart, count order. A nondefault result is available only when both supplied integers are nonnegative and one of two strict relationships to the anchor is satisfied. Equality with the anchor always produces the default result.

The formulas resemble inclusive-span calculations. That interpretation is inferred intent, not an established requirement that the inputs represent intervals, coordinates, or indexes.

### Arithmetic requirements and boundaries

The arithmetic uses signed integer operations. This contract does not replace them with unsigned arithmetic, saturation, or assumed wraparound.

For both count formulas, subtraction occurs before addition of one. The counterpart formula likewise subtracts count from bound before adding one. Representability must therefore hold for the intermediate subtraction, not merely for the final mathematical value.

Let \(M\) denote the signed integer maximum and \(L\) its minimum.

**Above the anchor, with a stable bound:** eligibility implies that the bound is at least the nonnegative first value. The subtraction used for count is consequently between zero and \(M\). Its following increment can exceed \(M\). In particular, an anchor below zero, first value zero, and bound \(M\) satisfy eligibility but require a mathematical count of \(M+1\).

**Below the anchor:** eligibility implies that the anchor is strictly positive, because the first value is nonnegative and strictly smaller. Since the second value is at least the anchor, the count is between 1 and \(M\), inclusive. Its arithmetic is representable under those constraints.

The below-anchor counterpart remains subject to overflow. A bound of \(L\) and count of one would require an intermediate value of \(L-1\), even though the complete mathematical expression would return to \(L\). A final result that appears representable does not make that intermediate subtraction valid.

No overflow status, recovery result, saturation rule, or wrapping rule is established.

### Stability and repeated bound access

The accepted above-anchor path expresses two bound accesses with different roles: eligibility and count calculation. The stable-field formula assumes that they provide the same value.

If the execution environment permits the bound to change between these accesses, eligibility depends on the earlier value while count depends on the later one. The earlier comparison alone therefore cannot establish that the resulting count is positive.

This observation does not grant defined behavior to unsynchronized concurrent mutation. Whether such mutation is permitted, and whether both accesses remain independently observable in the actual execution model, are unresolved.

### Aliasing

The visible interface imposes no explicit requirement that the three output destinations be distinct.

For destinations that validly identify the same complete integer object, later stores replace earlier stores:

- Shared start and counterpart storage ends with counterpart, unless count also targets it.
- Storage shared with count ends with count.
- If all three destinations identify one object, its final value after normal completion is count.

These rules do not authorize arbitrary partial-byte overlap.

An output destination may also identify a record field if the target’s object-access rules permit it. Such a store changes record storage. Because all record reads precede the output stores, that change does not feed a subsequent record read within this invocation.
