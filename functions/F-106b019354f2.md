## Behavior

### Derived quantity

Let **N** be the unsigned interpretation of the second input’s original 32-bit representation.

The dependency’s second argument, **S**, is determined as follows:

- Add 31 to N with unsigned 32-bit wraparound.
- Take the resulting unsigned value’s logical right shift by five positions, equivalently its integer quotient by 32.
- Multiply that quotient by four.
- Add eight.

Thus, expressed mathematically:

**S = 4 × floor(((N + 31) modulo 2³²) / 32) + 8.**

The modulo operation before the division is essential. This is not an unbounded-integer ceiling calculation for all possible input bit patterns.

The shifted quotient ranges from zero through 134,217,727. Consequently:

- The multiplication by four does not overflow 32 bits.
- The final addition of eight does not overflow 32 bits.
- S ranges from 8 through 536,870,916, inclusive.
- Every possible S is a multiple of four.
- The only wraparound affecting this calculation occurs in the initial addition of 31.

For nonnegative signed inputs, S is eight plus four times the number of 32-unit groups needed to cover the input, rounding a partial group upward. That interpretation also holds for unsigned N through 4,294,967,264. It ceases to hold for the final 31 unsigned values because the initial addition wraps.

Negative signed inputs are not rejected or clamped. Their bit patterns participate in the same unsigned calculation. Some therefore produce very large quantities, while values from −31 through −1 produce S = 8.
