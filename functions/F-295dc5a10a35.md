### Signed doubling and the `0x002F` store

Doubling is representable exactly for operands in this inclusive range:

\[
-0x40000000 \leq x \leq 0x3FFFFFFF.
\]

Within that range, the mathematical signed result is \(2x\). Outside it, signed-overflow behavior is not established. The evidence does **not** establish a destination obtained by multiplication modulo \(2^{32}\).

Even when doubling is representable, converting the result to a usable pointer remains target-dependent. Representable arithmetic does not prove that the destination is mapped, writable, suitably aligned, or otherwise valid. Negative results are not assigned an unsigned address interpretation by this contract.

This store precedes the next external call.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Memory accesses and calls are assumed to complete unless the example addresses their uncertainty.

1. **Initial selector.** A nonnegative signed selector chooses the byte at `0x10`; a negative selector chooses the byte at `0x11`. The byte value is forwarded.

2. **Unsigned subtraction.** A first saved high word of zero yields wrapped difference `0xFFFFFFF9` and argument one. Seven yields zero. `0x80000007` yields one despite being greater than seven as an unsigned value.

3. **Signed byte-selection subtraction.** A second saved high word interpreted as signed six selects `0x11`; seven selects `0x10`. A signed value of \(-2147483648\) does not receive an established selection because subtracting seven overflows.

4. **Representable signed doubling.** Operand `0x3FFFFFFF` doubles to signed `0x7FFFFFFE`. Operand \(-0x40000000\) doubles to \(-0x80000000\). In each case, the attempted halfword store still depends on target-specific pointer conversion and memory validity.

5. **Unestablished doubling overflow.** Operands `0x40000000` and \(-0x40000001\) are outside the representable doubling range. Acceptance must not require a wrapped destination or any particular store address.

6. **Bit 22.** Results `0x00400000` and `0xFFC00000` produce argument one; `0x003FFFFF` produces zero.

7. **Unsigned source-base calculation.** Operand `0x33` gives \(P=0\). Operand `0x32` gives \(P=255\) because subtraction wraps before shifting. Neither result establishes readable or aligned memory.

8. **Overlap.** If \(P=\texttt{0xC3}\), all four source words are captured before fixed-address writes. The value written at `0xCB` is the previously captured source word, not a reread after overwriting.

9. **Rejected bound.** For captured \(N=9\), the store at \(B+0x68\) precedes rejection. The bounded-path calls and stores are not represented as executing.

10. **Accepted upper boundary.** For captured \(N=8\), the bounded path stores byte eight at \(B+0x0F\), attempts its halfword store at `0x30`, and proceeds toward the final call and invalid-instruction endpoint. This is not a successful-return case.
