# F-f1f12655806e

## Purpose

F-f1f12655806e is a sequential dispatcher. It derives a repetition count from an unsigned 32-bit input, invokes dependency F-ad81d5a8fb8d that many times, and advances two integer arguments by eight between successive invocations. A third, opaque argument is forwarded unchanged on every invocation.

The established behavior is repeated delegation at an eight-unit stride. The supplied representation does not establish that the operation copies, transforms, compares, encrypts, or otherwise processes memory. Such interpretations depend on the undocumented behavior of F-ad81d5a8fb8d.

The count is truncated to complete groups of eight. A residual amount smaller than eight produces no additional invocation. The function has no direct return value and performs no explicit validation or error reporting.

## Behavior

### Invocation count

The unsigned extent is shifted right by three bits to obtain the repetition count. For an unsigned 32-bit value, this is equivalent to division by eight with the remainder discarded.

Consequently:

- Extents from zero through seven schedule no invocations.
- Extents from eight through fifteen schedule one invocation.
- Extents from sixteen through twenty-three schedule two invocations.
- The largest possible repetition count is 536,870,911, produced by extents from 4,294,967,288 through 4,294,967,295.

The repetition counter starts at zero and increases by one after each completed iteration. Its maximum value is below the unsigned 32-bit limit, so the counter itself does not overflow.

The low three bits of the extent do not affect the scheduled call sequence. Subject to the same dependency behavior, any two extents with the same quotient after division by eight schedule the same arguments in the same order.

### Dependency argument sequence

The first invocation receives the original context word and the two original positions.

After each invocation returns, the wrapper advances the first position by eight, then advances the second position by eight. It then advances its repetition counter and determines whether another invocation is required.

Where those additions are defined without overflow, the invocation numbered \(k\), counting from zero, receives:

- The original context word.
- The original first position plus \(8k\).
- The original second position plus \(8k\).

The dependency receives three arguments. The extent and repetition counter are not passed to it.

### Ordering and completion

Dependency invocations are sequential. The next scheduled invocation is not reached until the preceding invocation has returned and both local position advances have occurred.

The wrapper performs the two advances even after the final scheduled invocation returns. Those final position values are not passed to another invocation or explicitly returned to the caller. Nevertheless, the final additions matter when determining whether all visible arithmetic is defined.

Normal return occurs after all scheduled invocations have completed and their associated local updates have occurred.
