### Arithmetic semantics

Most arithmetic uses 32-bit intermediates. Under a **32-bit bit-vector model**, left shifts and multiplication discard bits above bit 31. Thus an eight-bit left shift followed by a logical eight-bit right shift retains only the original low 24 bits. Successive left shifts totaling at least 32 bits yield zero when every intermediate is 32 bits and each individual shift is defined.

The representation also contains signed operands. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, address conversion, and signed right-shift behavior require target-specific interpretation; the bit-vector consequences are not unconditional portable-language guarantees.

For the explicitly unsigned 64-bit logical shifts, **narrowing the entire shifted result to 32 bits** after shifts of 34, 36, or 44 bits equals logically shifting the original high 32-bit word by 2, 4, or 12 bits, respectively. This is not extraction of the shifted result’s upper word: that upper word would be zero.

Masks and shifts select fields of several sizes. Their order matters: retaining 24 low bits before shifting right 20 yields at most four bits, not an unrestricted shifted word.

A late variable right shift uses the low eight bits of a computed address-derived word as its count. Neither the treatment of counts above 31 nor the signed operand’s right-shift semantics is established.

### Opening mutation and low fixed storage

The opening cross-location ordering is significant:

- A word at the final pair’s high word plus 0x14 receives the high-scale root.
- A byte at the high-scale root plus 0x09 receives the high word’s low byte.
- A word at 256 times the final pair’s low word plus 0x54 receives the scaled root.
- The same high-scale-root byte is overwritten with the high-scale root’s low byte.

The scaled root’s word at 0x14 is then cleared, followed by a byte read at its offset 0x05. A word at the doubled index plus 0x14 is cleared before the saved byte is stored, zero-extended, at the scaled root plus 0x34. The scaled root’s word at 0x14 is reread afterward.

That reread is not safely replaceable with zero: intervening accesses can overlap. The doubled-index word is cleared again, and two byte writes at its offset 0x15 use the reread word’s low byte.

Next, the byte at fixed address **0x05** is read and zero-extended into a base. The low byte of the doubled index is written to fixed address **0x01** before further computed-address mutations.

The function subsequently reads the loaded base word, overwrites its source field, and performs updates through the scaled root, doubled index, and loaded words. These include accesses through offset 0x74 and stores at offsets 0x24 and 0x54. Some address fields are read separately more than once; treating them as immutable links is unsupported.

Three further fixed-storage writes have explicit placement:

1. A word at the doubled index plus 0x14 receives an earlier result shifted left 26 bits.
2. **The 32-bit word at fixed address 0x54 receives the loaded base word shifted left once.**
3. The doubled-index word at 0x14 receives the loaded base word, and then receives it again.

Thus the fixed 0x54 write lies between successive writes through the doubled-index base. It cannot be moved past them without resolving aliasing.

Afterward, two byte writes at the doubled index plus 0x15 receive the low byte of four times the loaded base word. **The byte at fixed address 0x15 then receives the doubled index’s low byte.** This fixed byte is distinct from the computed offset-0x15 locations unless addresses happen to coincide.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional contract checks, not executed tests.

### Truncation and wide shifts

Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, shifting 0x12345678 left eight yields 0x34567800; a subsequent logical right shift by eight yields 0x00345678.

For a 64-bit result whose high word is 0x12345678, narrowing after logical shifts of 34, 36, and 44 bits yields 0x048D159E, 0x01234567, and 0x00012345. Extracting the shifted upper word instead yields zero and is not equivalent.

### Low-index addressing

Under the same model, the small index cannot exceed 508 and is divisible by four. Its doubled address is divisible by eight and cannot exceed 1016. Treating it as an unrestricted returned pointer would violate the represented transformation.

### Fixed-write ordering

If writes are observable and the loaded base word is 0x12345678, the fixed-address 0x54 word write carries 0x2468ACF0. It must occur between the specified doubled-index word writes. The fixed 0x15 byte write follows the two later doubled-index byte writes.

The fixed 0x60 word write must follow the fixed-derived-base read and precede the repeated-update groups.

### Fresh fixed-byte reads

If storage permits aliasing or externally changing reads, the byte copied from 0x67 to 0x21 need not equal the saved fixed word’s low byte. Each later computed-offset-0x09 copy must likewise use its own read.

A later overwrite of computed offset 0x09 with the late base word’s low byte does not constitute another read of 0x67.

### Address-derived fixed byte

If the adjusted base is 0x101 under bit-vector arithmetic, the byte written to 0x3A is 0x38: the low byte of eight times 0x101 plus 0x30. This example makes no claim about character meaning.

### Overlap and failures

With ordinary little-endian memory, clearing a word at offset 0x14 and then writing 0x7B at offset 0x15 produces 0x00007B00, absent other overlapping effects.

If a loaded word produces an inaccessible address, no visible validation converts that access into a clean status return or rolls back prior mutations.
