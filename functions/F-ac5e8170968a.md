## Behavior

The large prefix consists of indirect invocations interspersed with word transformations. No explicit data-dependent branch or loop is represented.

The visible argument-construction patterns include:

- A paired result’s low word shifted left by eight, alongside its unchanged high word.
- Several differently shifted versions of one result in a single invocation.

- Explicit zero arguments.
- Secondary post-invocation machine-state words supplied independently of the primary result.

Similar argument shapes do not establish interchangeable dependencies. Each invocation remains an ordered effect boundary; unknown dependencies may affect memory or machine state used later.

The transformations select and reposition fields using masks of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit. Some operations shift a full 64-bit result right by 34, 36, or 44 bits before narrowing. Others shift a word, retain the word-width result, and shift it again later.

Intermediate truncation matters. A finite-width left shift followed by a right shift is not generally reversible. Explicitly zero-initialized retained values remain zero under ordinary shift semantics, but this does not remove effects of intervening invocations.

The complete per-invocation argument trace is not enumerated here and remains **documented_only** for replacement purposes.

- **Doubled selector:** twice the selector.
- **Retained shifted word:** an earlier dependency result shifted left by eight.

The opening access order is established:

- A word at terminal-low-word multiplied by 256 plus 0x54 receives the scaled base. The same offset-9 byte destination is then overwritten with the retained scaled value’s low byte.

## Acceptance examples

### Paired-word separation

Assuming a paired result has low word 0x00123456 and high word 0x89ABCDEF, a consumer requiring the low word shifted left by eight and the unchanged high word receives 0x12345600 and 0x89ABCDEF under 32-bit shift semantics. No memory byte order follows from this example.

### Masking and truncation

Selecting the low 24 bits of 0xAB123456 and shifting left by six produces 0x048D1580.

Under modulo-\(2^{32}\) shift semantics, a word shifted left by 28 and then left by another 28 becomes zero. An unbounded intermediate result is not equivalent.

### Overlapping accesses

If a word at base plus 0x14 is cleared and byte 0x7B is subsequently stored at base plus 0x15, the containing word is no longer zero. It becomes 0x00007B00 under little-endian layout or 0x007B0000 under big-endian layout.

### Fixed-store aliasing before repetition

If global-derived-base plus 0x34 equals 0x60, the fixed word store of 0x20 at 0x60 occurs before the repeated groups overwrite that location. Moving the fixed store after the groups changes the represented trace and potentially final memory.

### Retained word versus fresh byte

Assume the 0xA9 read captures 0x123456AB. The explicit 0x67 store writes 0xAB. If an intervening calculated store changes 0x67 to 0xCD before the second fresh read, the corresponding calculated offset-9 byte store receives 0xCD under ordinary RAM semantics.

The retained word remains 0x123456AB and supplies the later calculated offset-0x44 word store.

### Independent rereads

If a calculated write between the second and third 0x67 reads aliases 0x67, those reads may differ. Reusing the second read for the third consumer is not generally acceptable.

### Partial execution

If a calculated destination is unmapped, no validation result or rollback is established. Earlier invocations and stores may already have occurred.
