### Entry into memory mutation

Late dependency results supply several address-like quantities: a result multiplied by \(2^{25}\), a result positioned eight bits higher, another lower-result half positioned 25 bits higher, and a **compact value**.

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, the compact value is obtained by positioning a word eight bits higher, retaining the low 15 bits, and logically shifting right by six. It is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. Its doubled value is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016. These bounds do not establish accessible addresses.

The initial memory trace preserves this order:

- A word store at the final represented result’s upper-half base plus +0x14.
- A byte store at an earlier derived base plus +9, using the upper half’s low byte.
- A word store at the final lower half multiplied by 256, plus +0x54.
- Another byte store to the same +9 destination, now using an earlier captured scalar’s low byte.

The two byte-store values have different derivations, although particular inputs may make them equal. Both events remain part of the represented trace.

### Clearing, capture, and fixed-location effects

A derived region’s +0x14 word is cleared before its unsigned byte at +5 is captured. That byte is subsequently promoted to a 32-bit unsigned value and stored at +0x34. Another +0x14 word, based on the doubled compact value, is cleared twice.

The first cleared word is later loaded and used as a base. Its load must reflect intervening aliasing writes. Without such changes, ordinary memory semantics yield zero; no local null check protects subsequent accesses through it.

The fixed-location value at **0x05** is then obtained and converted to unsigned 32-bit form. After that capture:

- A byte is written at **0x01**, containing the low eight bits of the doubled compact value.
- A computed byte store uses the captured 0x05-derived value as a base plus +0x15.
- Computed stores through other previously captured bases follow.
- A word at the captured 0x05-derived base plus +0x54 is read before that destination is overwritten.

The captured old +0x54 word becomes an important downstream scalar, called the **captured overwritten word** below. The fixed write at 0x01 occurs after the 0x05 capture, so it cannot retroactively change that captured scalar even if the underlying storage overlaps.

Further updates use words loaded through +0x74 as bases, write self-references at +0x24 and +0x54, and repeat byte stores at +9. A word store through the doubled compact base at +0x14 precedes a fixed **32-bit write at 0x54** containing the captured overwritten word positioned one bit higher. Additional +0x14 stores follow that fixed write.

Later, two byte stores at doubled compact base +0x15 contain the low byte of four times the captured overwritten word. They precede a distinct fixed **byte write at 0x15**, whose value is the low byte of the doubled compact value. Computed +0x54 and +0x34 word stores follow.

The fixed addresses 0x01, 0x15, and 0x54 are actual represented destinations, not inferred application state. They may overlap computed destinations and affect subsequent reads.

### Constant stores and repeated region

The linked value determines three ordered constant stores: byte 0x3A at its fourfold base, halfword 0x003A at its eightfold base, and word 0x0000003A at fourfold base +0x20.

An **adjusted scalar** is four times the captured overwritten word minus decimal 150. A **repeated-region base** is four times that adjusted scalar. Arithmetic involving these quantities is conditional on the established machine interpretation.

At repeated-region base +0x20, word stores occur in the order 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended unsigned byte freshly read at repeated-region base +4, and 0x20 again. Earlier writes may affect that byte read through overlap.

After those stores, a later **fixed memory-base location** is read into a 32-bit base value. This capture occurs **before the fixed word write of 0x20 at 0x60**. The captured base is retained for the repeated region; the 0x60 write does not cause an implicit reload.

The repeated region has a four-store shape:

- A word at an eightfold-scaled destination plus +0x54 receives four times the linked value.
- A byte at captured fixed base +0x19 receives the low byte of the repeated-region base.
- A byte at repeated-region base +0x11 receives the low byte of the captured fixed base.
- A word at captured fixed base +0x34 receives an eightfold-scaled scalar.

The initial group uses eight times the captured overwritten word for the first destination’s base and for the last word’s value. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted scalar for those roles. Thus, the initial group differs in both its first destination and its last supplied word value.

These are finite straight-line repetitions, not an input-controlled loop. Exact repetition count remains documented_only in this contract. Distinct store events must not be replaced with final-state assignments when access events are observable.

Next, a fixed **byte write at 0x3A** supplies the low eight bits of:

This is a derived byte, not a constant 0x3A or an established character encoding.

### Live fixed-byte reloads and final mutation

The final phase initially writes bytes at doubled linked value +0x0D and repeated-region base +8, +0x0D, and +9. The +8 byte is 0x6C. A self-reference word is stored at doubled linked value +0x54.

The low byte of the saved 0xA9 word is then written to fixed address **0x67**. This saved word and the live byte at 0x67 are distinct state sources:

- A word is read at doubled linked value +0x44 after the 0x67 store.
- Fixed byte **0x21** is assigned from a **live read of 0x67** after that word read.
- Computed byte and word writes follow, including a byte derived from a signed right shift, overwrites at captured fixed base +0x54, and further word captures.
- A later byte store at repeated-region base +9 obtains its value from another **live read of 0x67**.

Intervening computed writes may overlap 0x67. Therefore, neither later computed-destination byte value is guaranteed to equal the saved 0xA9 word’s original low byte, and the two reloads need not agree. The 0x21 assignment is also a represented read, not substitution of a saved byte.

The variable-count signed shift uses the low eight bits of the repeated-region base as its count; restricting the count to eight bits does not establish that it is below 32.

Remaining updates include restoration of a previously captured +0x14 word into a +0x54 destination, additional +0x14 and +4 captures, a halfword store of 0x0065 through a doubled loaded base, and a word store of decimal 100 at a computed +0x54 destination. ## Side effects

Byte stores retain eight bits, halfword stores affect two bytes, and word stores affect four. Endianness and alignment behavior are unresolved. A +0x15 byte can overlap a +0x14 word. Fixed-location storage must not be assumed disjoint from wider or computed accesses.

No error code or exception interface is represented. Invalid targets and unsupported memory accesses may prevent completion; exact fault behavior is environment-dependent.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Input preservation.** Input 0x89ABCDEF reaches F-U001 as that exact 32-bit pattern.

2. **Width-sensitive positioning.** A 32-bit value 0x12345678 positioned eight bits higher yields 0x34567800 under 32-bit machine arithmetic, not 0x1234567800.

3. **Field extraction.** Selecting bits 10–19 of 0xFFFFFFFF yields 1023. Positioning that field six bits higher yields 0x0000FFC0.

4. **Known byte promotion.** An explicit unsigned +5 byte read yielding 0xE7 supplies 0x000000E7 to its promoted word store.

5. **Unknown fixed load width.** If the 0x05 expression’s conversion yields 0x00001234, its subsequent base uses retain that converted value. Replacing it with 0x34 is unsupported. Whether this result is possible depends on the missing declaration.

6. **Fixed compact-value stores.** If the compact value is 508, its doubled value is 1016, whose low byte is 0xF8. The fixed writes at 0x01 and later 0x15 therefore supply 0xF8, subject to reaching each event.

7. **Adjusted-scalar byte.** If the captured overwritten word is decimal 40, the adjusted scalar is 10 and the repeated-region base is 40. The late fixed byte at 0x3A receives 0x80.

8. **Live 0x67 state.** If the saved 0xA9 word’s low byte is 0xAB, the initial 0x67 store supplies 0xAB. If an intervening computed write changes 0x67 before a later reload, that reload uses the changed byte, not necessarily 0xAB.

9. **Capture ordering.** A supported 0x3A write overlapping a later computed-word source affects that later capture according to memory semantics. It does not change the already saved 0xA9 word.

10. **Observable overwrites.** The four +0x20 word writes remain ordered events. Checking only the final 0x20 is insufficient where accesses are observable.

11. **Failure without recovery.** An inaccessible derived low address need not permit completion. No rollback or local validation may be assumed.

12. **Final transfer.** If preceding operations complete, F-U003 receives 0x72 first and the designated captured words thereafter, not fresh replacements from their original locations.
