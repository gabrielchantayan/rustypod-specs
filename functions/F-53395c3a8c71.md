# F-53395c3a8c71

## Purpose

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated offsets and character-like numeric constants do not establish a string operation, serialization format, or object type.

This contract distinguishes **established static evidence**, conditional **[INFERENCE]**, and **documented_only** uncertainties. Every event is conditional on execution reaching it; an earlier fault or non-returning dependency can prevent subsequent events.

## Behavior

### Dependency interactions and arithmetic

The first indirect target is derived from the entry-time control-location value plus 4094 bytes. It receives the first input, the unchanged second input, and the first input shifted left six bits. Later targets are generally derived from fixed control-location anchors with the same displacement.

A long, explicitly ordered invocation sequence precedes the first direct data-memory store. Invocations with no displayed arguments remain separate events; this notation does not prove that the machine interface consumes no arguments.

Retained results undergo left shifts, unsigned right shifts, low-24-bit masking, narrower masks, scaling, and extraction from paired-result high words. Represented masks include widths from 11 through 22 bits, selected smaller fields, and one-bit extraction. Some arguments are explicit zeroes or transformations of values already set to zero.

**[INFERENCE]:** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, shifts, addition, subtraction, and scaling retain the low 32 bits. Intermediate truncation matters: successive left shifts by 28 bits yield zero; a left shift by eight followed by an unsigned right shift by eight retains only the original low 24 bits.

This interpretation does not establish portable signed-overflow behavior. The later variable signed right shift has separately unresolved machine semantics.

No dependency is assumed to preserve globals, memory, ownership, or secondary result channels. Similar invocation shapes do not establish identical targets.

### Initial mutation and sampling of P

A word store of T at H+20 precedes a byte store of H’s low byte at T+9. Between that store and a second byte store at T+9, now carrying T’s low byte, B is stored as a word at 256R+84.

The function then clears the word at B+20 and samples an unsigned byte at B+5. After clearing the word at D+20, it stores that sampled byte, widened without sign extension, as a word at B+52. It samples B+20 into I, clears D+20 again, and performs two consecutive byte stores of I’s low byte at D+21.

The next explicit global read samples the unsigned byte at absolute address 5 into β. Only afterward does a byte write at absolute address 1 receive D’s low byte.

The interval leading to P is fully ordered:

- β+21 receives K’s low byte.
- B+17 receives S’s low byte.
- I+84 receives S as a word.
- B+17 is cleared with a byte store.
- β+84 is read as a word into P.
- β+84 is overwritten with I as a word.
- D+52 receives K as a word.

Thus P is not an entry-state sample. In particular, the preceding word store at I+84 can affect the location supplying P. P remains retained after β+84 is overwritten. β is not resampled for these accesses.

### Linked updates before the constants

A word sampled at B+116 supplies a retained base, called **the first link** here. Its offset-84 word first receives twice B, using the retained pre-scaling result shifted left 26 bits. A separate nested update contains two reads at D+116: one supplies the destination base, and the other supplies the word stored at that base plus 84. The representation establishes both reads, not one shared sample; their internal operand-evaluation order is **documented_only**.

P+36 receives P, the first link’s offset-9 byte receives P’s low byte, and its offset-84 word receives S. The P+36 word store and first-link offset-9 byte store then repeat.

D+20 next receives twice B. A word write of twice P at absolute address 84 separates this update from two consecutive word stores of P at D+20. Two consecutive byte stores of C’s low byte at D+21 follow. A byte write at absolute address 21 then receives D’s low byte.

The subsequent linked mutations retain the following ordering and provenance:

- C+84 receives S; D+52 receives C. A fresh word read at D+116 supplies a doubled value, **the doubled link**. C+84 then receives C.
- C+21 receives the low byte of eight times P, and P+68 receives the doubled link.
- An unsigned byte read at C+5 supplies a base whose offset-116 word is sampled as **the byte-derived link**. That link’s offset-68 word receives the doubled link.

- A word read at C+4 supplies **the later link**. The doubled link’s offset-9 byte receives the byte-derived link’s low byte, and its offset-68 word receives eight times P.

### Constants and finite repeated-write burst

The numeric value 58 is stored first as a byte at V, then as a halfword at eight times L, then as a word at V+32.

U+32 receives four successive word writes: 32, 47, an unsigned byte sampled at U+4 widened to a word, and 32 again. The byte sample occurs between the second and third writes.

The global slot at **0x087fbbbc is then read once into G**. A word write of 32 at absolute address 96 follows before the burst.

The burst consists of **64 consecutive four-write groups: one initial group followed by 63 subsequent groups**.

| Position within group | Initial group | Each of the 63 subsequent groups |
|---|---|---|
| First | Word V at 8P+84 | Word V at 8M+84 |
| Second | Byte containing U’s low byte at G+25 | Same |
| Third | Byte containing G’s low byte at U+17 | Same |
| Fourth | Word 8P at G+52 | Word 8M at G+52 |

The burst therefore contains **256 represented stores**, including 64 stores at each position in the table. It is finite straight-line repetition, not input-dependent iteration.

G remains the originally sampled value throughout. Overwriting its source slot does not cause a reload. Equal payloads and repeated destinations do not authorize collapsing access events.

The ordering after that last address-103 propagation is:

U+9 next receives E’s low byte, and U+68 receives E as a word. A word read at J+4 supplies **A**. The halfword 101 is stored at twice A, and then the word at A+100 is read into **Q**.

Only after Q has been sampled does the saved final payload supply its low byte to E+17. The word 100 is then stored at E+84.

A word read at A+116 supplies **F**. A subsequent word read at J+100 supplies **O**. After A’s low byte is stored at J+9, Q is stored as a word at O+84, followed by a word store of O at twice Q.

The terminal dispatch receives **114, Q, Y, F**, in that order. These arguments use retained samples, not fresh reads from their original locations.

## Side effects

Recurring offsets establish access patterns, not a proven record layout. No non-aliasing guarantee separates dependency-derived addresses, scaled addresses, byte-derived bases, or explicit globals.

Absolute addresses 1, 5, 21, 33, 58, 84, 96, 103, and 169 are represented locations, not offsets into an established caller object. Access widths matter: the word at address 169 spans addresses 169–172, and the word at address 96 spans addresses 96–99.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Input forwarding:** First input 1 supplies initial arguments 1, the unchanged second input, and 64.

2. **Word-width truncation:** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, first input 0x04000000 produces a zero third argument, not a widened 0x100000000.

3. **Unsigned widening:** A byte value 0xFF sampled at B+5 supplies word payload 255 at B+52, not −1.

4. **Intervening mutation before P:** If I+84 and β+84 denote the same word, the S word store precedes P’s sample. P observes the memory state after that store and the intervening B+17 clear. β+84 is subsequently overwritten with retained I.

5. **Burst multiplicity:** Reaching the entire burst produces one initial four-store group and 63 subsequent four-store groups. One group, 63 total groups, or a single final-state-equivalent update does not preserve the represented access sequence.

6. **Retained global base:** If a burst store overlaps the slot supplying G, later groups still use the original G. The sample precedes the word write at absolute address 96.

7. **Post-burst interleaving:** If the byte write at address 58 changes storage later read at J+20, Y reflects the later read under ordinary memory semantics.

8. **Fresh address-103 sampling:** If an intervening computed-address store changes address 103 from 0x12 to 0x34, a later explicit sample forwards 0x34 to U+9, not the earlier 0x12.

9. **Final read boundary:** If the byte store at J+17 overlaps U+20, the saved final payload nevertheless comes from the preceding word read. Moving the J+17 store before that read changes the contract.

10. **Q before the final payload store:** If E+17 overlaps A+100, Q is sampled before the byte write at E+17. That byte write also precedes the word write of 100 at E+84.

11. **Retained dispatch argument:** Later mutations of J+20 do not replace saved Y in the terminal arguments.

12. **Partial failure:** A fault after completed stores establishes neither success nor rollback. Normal return remains conditional on reaching and returning from the terminal target.
