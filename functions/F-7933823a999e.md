## Behavior

### Character replacement stages

Stages 1–3 share a replacement rule. A signed displacement is acquired from cursor plus selector. A character is then acquired from displacement plus incoming carry. That character, converted to signed 16 bits, replaces the halfword at displacement plus selector.

The previous halfword contents do not contribute. Outgoing carry is freshly acquired from selector plus 0x44 after replacement.

Their additional obligations differ:

| Stage | Additional observations and effects |
|---|---|
| 1 | After character acquisition, the additional ambient byte is written at selector plus 0x1D, before replacement. |
| 2 | After character acquisition, four bytes from first argument plus 0x64 are transferred to displacement plus 0x54. Next, a carry-derived character is written at retained character plus 0x0D. An anchor word is then retained from selector plus 0x64, before replacement. |
| 3 | A four-byte payload from first argument plus 0x64 is retained before displacement and character acquisition. After character acquisition and before replacement, the ordered writes are: selector-derived character at first argument plus 1; carry-derived character at selector plus 0x0D; anchor at selector plus 0x44; selector-derived character at retained character plus 0x0D. |

The anchor is the value acquired in stage 2, not a later reread of its source. Stage 3’s outgoing carry is nevertheless fresh: its value may differ from the anchor just published if the intervening byte write or replacement overlaps the carry source.

Likewise, stage 1’s additional-byte write cannot retroactively change its retained character, although it can affect subsequent accesses.

### Link replacement stages

Stages 4–7 acquire a signed-word link from incoming cursor plus 0x44 and a signed displacement from incoming cursor plus selector. Replacement contents are the link converted to signed 16 bits.

| Stage | Replacement address | Outgoing-carry source | Cursor treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Displacement plus redirection | Redirection plus 0x44 | Replacement read from incoming cursor plus 0x14 |
| 5 | Displacement plus redirection | Redirection plus 0x44 | Unchanged |
| 6 | Displacement plus selector | Selector plus 0x44 | Replacement read from displacement plus 0x14 |
| 7 | Displacement plus selector | Selector plus 0x44 | Replacement read from displacement plus 0x14 |

**Stage 4:** Payload acquisition from first argument plus 0x64 precedes link acquisition, which precedes displacement acquisition. Redirection is then acquired from incoming carry plus 0x74, followed by replacement-cursor acquisition. A redirection-derived character is written at retained link plus 0x0D before replacement.

**Stage 5:** Link acquisition precedes displacement acquisition. Before redirection is acquired from retained link plus 0x74, a selector-derived character is written at incoming carry plus 0x15, followed by publication of incoming carry at selector plus 0x44. Overlap can therefore affect the redirection observation. No new payload or cursor is acquired.

**Stages 6 and 7:** Payload acquisition from first argument plus 0x64 precedes link acquisition, which precedes displacement acquisition. In stage 6, replacement-cursor acquisition immediately follows.

Stage 7 instead has an intervening feedback boundary. Incoming cursor is published at incoming carry plus 0x54; the payload’s explicit low byte is written at incoming cursor plus 0x11; and selector is published at retained link plus 0x44. Replacement-cursor acquisition follows those effects. The extracted byte is retained for stage 8.

After replacement-cursor acquisition, both stages impose the same ordered obligations:

- Link publication at retained link plus 0x54 precedes anchor publication at selector plus incoming carry.
- Anchor publication precedes link publication at selector plus 0x14.
- A fresh four-byte observation from anchor plus 0x44 supplies a character written at selector plus 0x0D.

The anchor-relative character is memory-derived. It is not a character conversion of the anchor’s numeric value.

### Traversal and feedback

Stage 8 continues beyond its opening boundary by acquiring a signed-word link from cursor plus 0x44, then a signed displacement from cursor plus selector. The extended displacement becomes the **factor**.

Before traversal, factor publication at factor plus 0x54 precedes a cursor-derived character write at selector plus 0x11. That ordered pair occurs twice. Neither occurrence may be discarded as redundant: overlap can make the sequence relevant.

Traversal follows four dependent signed-word observations:

| Observation | Address basis |
|---|---|
| First | Factor multiplied by retained link, plus 0x44 |
| Second | First observation plus 0x74 |
| Third | Second observation plus 0x14 |
| Fourth | Third observation plus 0x14 |

The fourth value is retained as the **old word**. Its supplying cell is then replaced with cursor shifted left once. Only afterward is a continuation word acquired from old word plus 0x14. If that source overlaps the replaced cell, continuation acquisition can observe the replacement.

Three arithmetic depths govern later addressing:

- The **continuation product** is factor multiplied by the continuation word.
- The **extended product** is factor multiplied by the continuation product.
- The **final-address product** is factor multiplied by the extended product.

These are separately reached signed multiplications. The final-address product is evaluated later, during final-payload acquisition. No special handling of zero or negative factors is established.

After continuation-product formation, factor publication at factor plus 0x54 precedes an old-word-derived character write at selector plus 0x11. The staging word is then initialized from a signed word at continuation product plus 0x74. Extended-product formation follows that initialization.

Two payloads are then retained in order: four bytes from extended product plus 0x44, followed by four bytes from first argument plus 0x74. Current staging is subsequently published at selector plus 0x64.

Once acquired, the destination is not reread from the first-argument-relative source. ## Acceptance examples

These examples assume suitable storage, valid arithmetic and conversions, and no additional changes beyond those stated.

1. **Replacement rather than accumulation.** A character-replacement stage with displacement 2 and retained character value 7 writes signed-halfword value 7 at selector plus 2. Previous contents do not contribute.

2. **Character-dependent address.** If retained character bits 0xFF represent −1, its publication address at character plus 0x44 is 0x43. If they represent 255, that address is 0x143.

3. **Separate selector observations.** If the applicable execution model permits a selector source to change between observations, addressing uses the retained selector while opening publication uses the separately observed value. This does not prove separate physical transactions.

4. **Anchor retention with fresh carry.** Overwriting stage 2’s anchor source does not replace the retained anchor. In stage 3, an overlapping intervening write can still make outgoing carry differ from the anchor publication.

5. **Displacement-relative cursor.** With displacement −4, stage 6 or 7 reads its replacement cursor at address 0x10, independently of incoming cursor. Stage 7’s intervening writes can affect that observation.

6. **Memory-derived character.** If anchor plus 0x44 identifies selector plus 0x14 in stage 6, the fresh anchor-relative observation can consume the newly published link.

7. **Traversal feedback.** If old word plus 0x14 identifies the replaced traversal cell, continuation acquisition observes the shifted-cursor replacement, not the retained old word.

8. **Product depth.** Factor 4 and continuation word 3 produce continuation, extended, and final-address products of 12, 48, and 192. The final payload source is therefore 0x104.

9. **Retained versus fresh payload.** If selector plus 0x64 overlaps extended product plus 0x44, staging publication can change the later fresh payload observation without changing the retained payload.

10. **Retained destination.** Changing first argument plus 0x14 after destination acquisition does not redirect the final two destination-relative writes.

11. **Return-dependent continuation.** If F-18c745a290e1 does not return, seventh-argument clearing is not reached. If it changes the external source and returns, the later external interaction can receive the changed value.

12. **Shift validity.** Under signed-shift rules requiring representability, first argument 0x01000000 cannot validly be shifted left eight places merely because the destination occupies four bytes.
