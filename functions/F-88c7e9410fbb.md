### Entry into direct mutation

The first direct mutations use several retained dependency results:

- The last carrier’s high word supplies a base for a 32-bit store at offset 0x14.
- A retained value shifted left by 25 bits supplies an address base and stored data.
- The last carrier’s low word, multiplied by 256, supplies a base for a word store at offset 0x54.
- Another retained result, multiplied by 2²⁵, supplies an early base.
- A small value is derived by shifting another result left eight bits, masking to fifteen bits, and shifting right six bits. Doubling it supplies a small base.

Under the conditional model, the small value is four times the originating result’s low seven bits. The doubled small base is therefore **eight times those seven bits**: its attainable values are **0 through 1016, in increments of eight**. Values such as 1022 are impossible.

The opening order is a word store through the last high word, a byte store at offset 0x09 of the retained shifted base, a word store through the scaled last low word, and another byte store to the same offset-0x09 destination.

The two byte stores use **distinct expressions**, not necessarily distinct byte values. The first takes the last high word’s low byte; the second takes the retained 25-bit-shifted value’s low byte, which is zero under the conditional model. If the high word also has a zero low byte, both stores write zero.

### Early zeroing and represented reload

The early base multiplied by 2²⁵ receives a 32-bit zero at offset 0x14. Its byte at offset 0x05 is captured. The small doubled base then receives a word zero at offset 0x14, and the captured byte is zero-extended into a word at offset 0x34 of the early base.

The early base’s offset-0x14 word is subsequently reloaded. Another small-base zero store follows, then two successive byte stores at the small base’s offset 0x15 using the reload’s low byte.

**Under the conditional model with ordinary linear memory and no external modification, this reload yields zero.** Both zero-store destinations are word-aligned. If their four-byte ranges overlap, their starting addresses coincide and the intervening store still writes zero. The early base’s offset-0x34 word is 0x20 bytes away from its offset-0x14 word and cannot overlap it.

Consequently, aliasing among those intervening accesses alone does **not** justify treating the reload as an arbitrary value. The represented read remains part of the access trace. Uncertainty about a nonzero result or special read observability instead rests on unresolved device behavior, nonordinary memory mappings, or concurrent modification.

### Small-base boundaries

Under the conditional model, originating results 1, 127, and 128 produce doubled small bases 8, 1016, and 0, respectively. Every attainable base is divisible by eight; 1022 is unattainable.

### Equal opening byte stores

If the last carrier’s high word has low byte zero, both opening stores at the retained shifted base’s offset 0x09 write zero. Acceptance still retains both stores and the intervening word store; unequal byte values are not required.

### Early reload in ordinary memory

Assume ordinary linear memory, conditional 32-bit arithmetic, and no external modification. The early offset-0x14 reload returns zero even if the small-base clear aliases that word: any overlap is exact and writes zero again. The represented reload remains required in the access trace.

A device or mapping that does not behave as ordinary storage falls outside this numerical conclusion.

If the captured word is decimal 50, the adjusted base is 50, both scaling expressions are 400, and the reciprocal-byte base is 200. The first and subsequent groups can therefore target the same offset-0x54 address and publish the same offset-0x34 value. Their ordered operations remain distinct.

### Equal final-region word payloads
