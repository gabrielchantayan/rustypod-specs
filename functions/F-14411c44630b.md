# F-14411c44630b

## Purpose

F-14411c44630b interleaves dependency calls with unsigned byte reads, byte writes, and 32-bit memory accesses. It extracts fields from retained values and writes values to numerically computed addresses.

No application-level purpose is established. Bit-field distribution is a possible interpretation, not a required behavior. The evidence does not establish serialization, allocation, device control, or a particular object layout.

The supplied static representation ends at an invalid-instruction boundary, without a displayed normal return. Warnings concerning invalid instruction data and overlapping global symbols limit confidence in execution-level interpretation. This contract describes the displayed relationships without asserting that the entire path is executable.

### Widths, layouts, and arithmetic

Byte reads are unsigned 8-bit reads. Byte writes retain eight bits. Word reads and writes are 32 bits wide.

The following address descriptions apply:

- A **four-scaled slot** has numeric address \(4s+20\), where \(s\) is its selector.
- A **sixteen-scaled slot** has numeric address \(16s+20\).
- A **selector write** reads an unsigned byte at a specified selector address, then writes one byte at numeric address equal to that byte plus 17.

Selector-write destinations therefore range from 17 through 272. No separate buffer base is added.

The offset 20 belongs to the slot address, not to the scaled selector itself. In particular, “four times a selector” excludes that offset.

Unsigned 32-bit arithmetic wraps modulo \(2^{32}\). Some displayed expressions instead involve signed 32-bit multiplication or left shifts. Their machine-level overflow behavior is not established. Simplifications involving those expressions are conditional on a 32-bit wrapping interpretation.

The wide dependency result is 64 bits. Its lower and upper halves are numerical bit ranges 0–31 and 32–63, respectively; this does not establish a memory layout for that result.

Endianness, alignment requirements, address mappings, and access-fault semantics are unknown. No aggregate object layout is established.

## Behavior

### Ordering model

The displayed path contains no conditional branch or loop. Calls and accesses can nevertheless fault, fail to return, or otherwise prevent later operations.

The phases below preserve the significant ordering of accesses and argument relationships. Intervening calls remain observable boundaries: they may change memory, global state, registers, or external state. Repeated reads must not be combined merely because their address expressions match.

For separate memory-read expressions used as arguments to one call, the representation does not establish evaluation order.

### 1. Initial selector write and early retained result

The first access reads one unsigned byte at the incoming address-bearing value plus 1. The incoming byte-valued state is then written to the selector’s numeric value plus 17.

After several indirect calls, one call receives, in order:

1. The original selector, zero-extended to 32 bits.
2. The third explicit input shifted left by 26.

Further calls produce an early 32-bit result. Another call receives that result twice, followed by the fourth explicit input shifted left by 10.

After another call, the early result is written to a four-scaled slot selected by a secondary register output. The result is also retained for later extraction.

### 2. Two global evaluations and the first field-output sequence

Only after the preceding word store, the routine evaluates the named global twice in sequence:

1. It retains the first 32-bit observation as an unsigned value.
2. It evaluates the global again and forms four times this second observation.

No dependency call is displayed between these evaluations. That fact does not establish that their returned values are equal.

The earlier secondary output is passed to a dependency. Following additional calls, a dependency supplies a slot selector. The early retained result, logically shifted right by 12, is stored in that selector’s four-scaled slot. Four times the selector is then passed to another dependency.

A later dependency supplies a new 32-bit field value. The routine reads a selector byte at four times the **second** global observation plus 1, then writes the low byte of the field value shifted left by 2.

A subsequent call receives bits 10 through 29 of the **first** global observation: the first observation is masked to its low 30 bits and logically shifted right by 10.

After further calls, the selector byte at four times the second observation plus 1 is read again. The routine writes bits 20 through 27 of the retained field value to the newly selected destination.

This second selector is retained. Before a later call forwards it as the second argument, another call receives four times a dependency result. The later two-argument call receives four times a further dependency result first and the retained selector second.

### 3. Shifted global-derived quantities and repeated word reads

The four-times-scaled **second** global observation is shifted left by 26. The routine also forms 1024 times that shifted quantity.

Two separate 32-bit reads occur at the shifted quantity plus 28. They supply two arguments to one indirect call. Neither read order nor equality of the returned words is established.

Following another call, a selector write stores zero. Its selector is read at the 1024-times-scaled quantity plus 1.

Under 32-bit wrapping arithmetic, the 1024-times-scaled quantity is zero: the combined scaling discards all original bits. This conclusion depends only on the second global evaluation’s derived quantity, not on equality with the first evaluation. Under that interpretation, the selector address is 1.

A subsequent call receives zero, a secondary 32-bit register output, and the 1024-times-scaled quantity, in that order.

After additional calls, the routine rereads a selector at the same derived address and writes the low byte of the secondary output logically shifted right by 22. Four times a dependency result is then passed to another call.

A later call receives the derived quantity shifted left by 26 and four times a further dependency result.

### 7. Lower-half stores and four scaled-slot instances

The retained lower half is multiplied by \(2^{26}\). That value is written at the numeric address equal to the lower half multiplied by \(2^{28}\), plus 20. These multiplications use the displayed unsigned 32-bit lower half.

The \(2^{26}\)-scaled value is passed to a dependency. Later, bits 9 through 28 of the lower half are stored in a dependency-selected four-scaled slot. Four times that slot selector is passed onward.

Four successive instances then share an initial operation: obtain a dependency result, derive four times that result, and store the derivative in the result’s sixteen-scaled slot. Their subsequent behavior differs:

1. **First instance.** Two separate words are read at the derivative plus 20. One contributes a logical-right-shift-by-12 argument; the other contributes a left-shift-by-2 argument. The derivative is the middle argument. After intervening calls, the derivative is written to a dependency-selected four-scaled slot.
2. **Second instance.** A call receives the derivative twice and the retained lower half shifted left by 26. After intervening calls, the derivative is written to a dependency-selected four-scaled slot.
3. **Third instance.** A call receives the derivative alone. After intervening calls, the derivative is written to a dependency-selected four-scaled slot.
4. **Fourth instance.** Immediately after the sixteen-scaled-slot store, a word is read at the derivative plus 20 and retained. A call receives the derivative. After further calls, the **saved word logically shifted right by 12**, not the derivative, is written to a dependency-selected four-scaled slot.

For the first three instances, four times each later destination selector is passed onward. The fourth instance instead leads into the extraction behavior below.

The sixteen-scaled store and derivative-plus-20 read generally address different locations. No read is required to return the stored derivative. Aliasing and dependency effects must remain possible.

### 8. Final extraction, field writes, and termination boundary

Let the **extraction-slot selector** mean the dependency result selecting the fourth instance’s later four-scaled destination.

After storing the saved word shifted right by 12, the routine passes four times the extraction-slot selector to a dependency.

A fifth scaled-slot instance obtains another result, derives four times it, and stores that derivative in the result’s sixteen-scaled slot. A subsequent call receives:

1. The low six bits of **four times the extraction-slot selector**, shifted left by 14. The added slot offset of 20 is excluded.
2. Four times the newly obtained result.
3. The extraction-slot selector shifted left by 30.

After additional calls, the fifth derivative is written to another dependency-selected four-scaled slot. Four times that destination selector is passed onward.

- A call receives that shifted value twice and the final result shifted left by 4.

## Side effects

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes all preceding calls and accesses needed to reach the operation complete.

1. **Initial destination.** An initial selector of 5 and incoming byte value `0xAB` produce a byte write of `0xAB` at numeric address 22.

2. **Independent global provenance.** If the first global evaluation yields `0x00000400` and the second yields 3, the masked right-shift call argument is 1. The early global-derived selector addresses are 13. Deriving both uses from either observation would violate the displayed provenance.

3. **Logical extraction.** An early retained result of `0xABCDEF12` produces a right-shift-by-12 word value of `0x000ABCDE`.

4. **Byte fields.** A left-shift-by-2 selector write driven by `0x123456FF` stores `0xFC`. A bits-20-through-27 write driven by `0x3AB00000` stores `0xAB`.

5. **Selector mutation.** If a reused selector byte changes from 3 to 9 between reads, the corresponding writes target addresses 20 and 26. Reusing the first selector is not equivalent.

6. **Wide-result split.** For upper half 7 and lower half 2, the zero store targets address 48. The unsigned lower-half scaled store targets `0x20000014` and writes `0x08000000`.

7. **Global-derived zero.** Under 32-bit wrapping arithmetic, the second global observation’s four-times-scaled value, shifted left by 26 and then multiplied by 1024, becomes zero. Its selector address is 1 regardless of either global observation’s value or whether the two observations agree.

8. **Fourth-instance distinction.** If its result is 3, the fourth instance stores derivative 12 at address 68 and reads a word at address 32. If that read yields `0xABCDEF12` and the later destination selector is 7, the later word store writes `0x000ABCDE` at address 48—not 12.

9. **Offset excluded from final argument.** With extraction-slot selector 1, four times the selector is 4, and the masked-and-shifted first argument is `0x00010000`. Including the slot offset 20 would incorrectly produce `0x00060000`.

10. **Partial execution.** A dependency that does not return after an earlier store prevents later displayed operations. No restoration of the earlier store is required by this representation.
