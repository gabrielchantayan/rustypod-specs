This contract distinguishes:

### Masked arguments and remaining word writes

The operation writes four times F-D035’s result at sixteen times that result plus `0x14`.

### First selector read and byte write

After F-D045, the operation reads an unsigned byte from the address formed by multiplying F-D030’s result by `0x10000000` and adding one.

The byte is a numeric selector, not a pointer-width value. Adding `0x11` to its unsigned value forms the destination of an 8-bit write. The written byte is the low eight bits of F-D045’s result shifted left by two.

Under 32-bit modular arithmetic:

- The destination lies between `0x11` and `0x110`, inclusive.
- The stored byte is a multiple of four.
- The selector-read address is congruent to one modulo `0x10000000`.

The selector-read address and this byte-write destination **cannot be the same numeric address** under that model. The only selector-read address below `0x10000000` is one; all other possible selector-read addresses exceed the entire destination range.

Distinct numeric addresses could refer to shared storage under an additional address mapping, but no such mapping is established. The first byte write must not be characterized as numerically aliasing the selector address.

F-D046 then receives three arguments:

1. F-D045’s result shifted left by two.
2. That same shifted value again.
3. F-D045’s result shifted left by four.

F-D046’s target uses displacement `0xFEC`, unlike the `0xFFE` displacement used by the other represented calls.

The operation then reads the selector byte again from the address based on F-D030’s retained result: that result multiplied by `0x10000000`, plus one.

This is a fresh memory read. It does not reuse the first selector. Intervening dependencies may have changed the selector’s contents; their behavior is unknown.

At the second selector’s unsigned value plus `0x11`, the operation writes one byte derived from F-D045’s retained result. The derivation masks that result with `0x3FFFFFFF`, logically shifts right by 20, and retains the low eight bits for the byte write.

The stored byte is therefore exactly bits 20 through 27 of F-D045’s result. Bits 28 and 29 survive the shift but not the final byte-width truncation; bits 30 and 31 are removed by the mask.
