## Behavior

### Widths, narrowing, and character interpretation

Byte stores affect 8 bits, halfword stores affect 16 bits, and word stores affect 32 bits. Descriptions such as “low byte” and “low halfword” identify the represented stored bit patterns; they do not imply that a larger surrounding word is replaced.

The Index U load is through a **plain character type**, whose signedness is not established by the supplied material:

- With signed plain characters, bytes 0x80–0xFF are interpreted as negative values before conversion to unsigned 32-bit form.
- With unsigned plain characters, every byte becomes its value in the range 0–255.
- Bytes 0x00–0x7F produce the same Index U under either interpretation.

Thus, sign extension for high-bit bytes is conditional on target-specific plain-character signedness. It is not an unconditional property of this routine.

No visible operation checks addresses, bounds, null values, access permissions, or alignment.

- Index U + 0x44 produces 0x00000043.
