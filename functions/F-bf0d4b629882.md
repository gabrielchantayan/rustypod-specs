# F-bf0d4b629882

## Purpose

F-bf0d4b629882 executes an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect invocations, transforms their results, performs mixed-width memory accesses, and finally transfers control to an unresolved destination with four represented argument words.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. In particular, numeric constants resembling characters, scaled addresses, and self-referential stores do not establish text formatting, allocation, initialization, or container manipulation.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** widths, expressions, accesses, and sequencing supported by the supplied representation.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations such as “record,” “link,” or “payload,” used only as descriptive aids.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior that is not converted into an acceptance requirement by assumption.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Invocation and results

The represented interface declares no parameters and no result. Nevertheless, execution depends on implicit machine context:

- An incoming general-purpose word used in early shifted arguments.

The first destination is the incoming link-related value plus 0xFFE. Later destinations are predominantly represented as code-associated anchors plus the same displacement. This is not evidence of a conventional table of stored function pointers.

Some invocations have no explicit arguments; others have one through four represented words. An invocation without explicit arguments must not be assumed to have no implicit inputs.

No success value, failure value, or externally usable return payload is established. A return is represented after the final transfer, conditional on that transfer returning.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven phase

The opening phase is a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. No data-dependent branch or runtime loop is represented in this phase.

Results may be discarded, retained as words, split from combined results, or consumed through secondary machine-result channels. Discarding a result does not make its producing invocation removable: dependency effects are unknown.

Arguments include shifted low result words, unshifted or shifted high result words, duplicated argument values, explicit zeros, and quantities retained across intervening invocations. Shift counts include values from 4 through 31. These transformations establish numeric relationships, not a serialization format.

Representative unsigned operations have the following meanings:

| Transformation | Established numeric meaning |
|---|---|
| Mask with 0xFFFFF, then shift right 10 | Extract bits 10–19; range 0–1,023 |
| Mask with 0xFFFFFF, then shift right 20 | Extract bits 20–23; range 0–15 |
| Mask with 0x3FFFFF | Retain the low 22 bits |
| Mask with 0x7FFFF, then shift left two | Scale the low 19 bits; maximum 2,097,148 |
| Mask with 0x1FFFF, then shift right four | Extract bits 4–16; range 0–8,191 |
| Unsigned combined result shifted right 34 | High word shifted right two |
| Unsigned combined result shifted right 36 | High word shifted right four |

Some retained quantities are explicitly zeroed and later shifted without an intervening represented assignment. Their represented dataflow remains zero. This does not establish that intervening invocations preserve globals or lack side effects.

A late selector is formed by shifting a result word left eight, retaining its low fifteen bits, and shifting right six. Under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, the selector is four times the original result’s low seven bits: 0 through 508 in steps of four. Twice the selector is 0 through 1,016 in steps of eight and is subsequently used as an address base.

The complete invocation-site inventory and exact argument trace are not enumerated here; full conformance to that unresolved dependency trace is **documented_only**.

### Entry into explicit memory mutation

The following conceptual labels describe retained values without asserting object types:

- **Tag:** a late low result word shifted left 25.
- **Scaled root:** a later result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Shifted payload:** a retained result shifted left eight.
- **Selector base:** twice the bounded selector.
- **Final pair:** the low and high words of the last combined result before explicit stores.

The initial access trace has several order-sensitive boundaries.

First, a word containing the tag is stored at the final pair’s high word plus 0x14. A byte from that high word is then stored at tag plus nine. A word containing the scaled root is stored at 256 times the final pair’s low word plus 0x54, before tag plus nine is overwritten with the tag’s low byte.

Next, the word at scaled root plus 0x14 is cleared, and a byte is read at scaled root plus five. The word at selector base plus 0x14 is cleared before the captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at scaled root plus 0x34. Only then is the word at scaled root plus 0x14 read back.

After that read-back, selector base plus 0x14 is cleared again. Two successive byte stores at selector base plus 0x15 both use the read-back word’s low byte.

These are separate accesses. The read-back cannot generally be replaced with zero: the intervening word store at scaled root plus 0x34 may overlap the field read through another address expression. Likewise, the earlier byte load must observe memory after the preceding clear.

Under the stated 32-bit shift interpretation, the tag’s low byte is zero. That does not erase the earlier nonzero-capable byte store or its position in the access trace.

### Mixed-width updates and address chasing

A byte is read at fixed address 0x05 and zero-extended into another base. A byte containing the selector base’s low byte is then written at fixed address 0x01.

Subsequent updates include:

- A selector byte at the byte-derived base plus 0x15.
- A shifted-payload byte at scaled root plus 0x11, followed later by a zero byte at that same address.
- A shifted-payload word at the read-back-derived base plus 0x54.
- Capture of the existing word at the byte-derived base plus 0x54 before replacing that field with the earlier read-back word.
- A selector word at selector base plus 0x34.
- Offset-0x74 word reads used to address later offset-0x54 stores.

Repeated offset-0x74 reads remain separate reads. One represented store uses two reads through the same offset-0x74 expression: one supplies an address base and another supplies the stored value. Their relative operand-evaluation order is not established, and equality of the two observed values is not guaranteed for externally changing memory.

The captured old offset-0x54 word is subsequently written twice to its own plus-0x24 address, with intervening updates. Its low byte is also repeatedly stored through another loaded base plus nine. Selector base plus 0x14 receives several successive word replacements, including two consecutive stores of that captured word.

Further arithmetic derives bases using factors two, four, and eight. The routine writes twice the captured word at fixed address 0x54 and writes the selector base’s low byte at fixed address 0x15.

Additional behavior includes:

- Self-value word stores at derived offset-0x14 and offset-0x54 addresses.
- Reads through offsets four, 0x64, and 0x74.
- Byte-derived addressing in which a byte read at a derived base plus five becomes the base for a word read at offset 0x74.

These access relationships do not establish disjoint records. Stores through any calculated base may affect later reads through another base.

### Constant stores, root capture, and repeated region

A loaded word supplies scaled destinations for three numeric stores of 0x3A: one byte at four times the word, one halfword at eight times the word, and one word at four times the word plus 0x20.

For the following region, define these conceptual quantities:

- **Captured link:** the earlier old word captured from the byte-derived base plus 0x54.
- **Adjusted quantity:** four times the captured link, minus 150.
- **Derived base:** four times the adjusted quantity.
- **Retained word:** four times the loaded word used for the 0x3A stores.
- **Fixed root:** the word captured from a fixed global location.

These relationships retain the signed-arithmetic qualifications already stated.

At derived base plus 0x20, successive word stores contain 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from derived base plus four, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the 0x2F store and before its resulting word store.

The fixed root is read **after the final derived-offset-0x20 store of 0x20 and before the word store of 0x20 at fixed address 0x60**. Its capture must not be moved after that fixed-address store or deferred until the repeated groups begin. An alias or externally observable access can distinguish those orders.

The repeated region begins after the fixed-address-0x60 store. Its initial group and later groups differ as follows:

| Group | Offset-0x54 destination | Word stored there | Word stored at fixed root plus 0x34 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial | Eight times the captured link, plus 0x54 | Retained word | Eight times the captured link |
| Later | Eight times the adjusted quantity, plus 0x54 | The same retained word | Eight times the adjusted quantity |

Thus, the initial word-link store has a different **destination expression**, not a different stored word. Different expressions do not establish different effective addresses.

Within each group, the offset-0x54 word store precedes a byte store at fixed root plus 0x19, then a byte store at derived base plus 0x11, then the word store at fixed root plus 0x34. The byte values are the low bytes of the derived base and captured fixed root respectively.

The root is retained across these groups; there is no represented root reload within each group. Aliasing writes can change root-relative memory without changing the captured root value.

The repetition is statically expanded, with no runtime loop condition shown. Exact repetition-count conformance is **documented_only** in this contract. Repeated accesses must not be collapsed merely because their expressions and values repeat.

### Closing phase and final transfer

After the repeated region, a word is read at fixed address 0xA9, followed by a retained offset-0x74 word read. A byte is then written at fixed address 0x3A. Under a byte-level bit-vector interpretation, its value is eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30, reduced to eight bits.

Two further words are captured before subsequent byte updates: an offset-0x14 word from a retained derived base and an offset-four word from the derived base.

The closing mutations include a byte value 0x6C at derived base plus eight, updates at offsets nine and 0x0D, and a self-value word store at another base plus 0x54. The low byte of the word captured at fixed address 0xA9 is stored at fixed address 0x67. An offset-0x44 word read intervenes before fixed address 0x67 supplies the byte written at fixed address 0x21.

A later byte store uses a signed-looking right shift whose count is the low byte of the derived base. Negative operands and counts at or above 32 remain **documented_only**.

Additional ordered effects include:

- Capture of the word at derived base plus 0x40 before storing it at derived base plus 0x10.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

### Extracted field

For unsigned operand 0xABCDEFFF, retaining the low twenty bits and shifting right ten produces 0x37B. Acceptance requires that value.

After masking, the 32-bit sign bit is clear. Arithmetic and logical right shifts by ten therefore agree on the masked value; either can satisfy this example.

### Selector boundary

If the selector-producing word’s low seven bits are 0x7F, the selector is 508 and its doubled base is 1,016 under the stated bit-vector interpretation. Higher input bits do not affect this result. Address validity remains a separate precondition.

### Overwritten byte

If tag plus nine addresses writable observable memory, both initial byte stores occur in order. The second contains zero under the stated tag interpretation. Preserving only the final contents does not preserve the represented access trace.

### Root capture order

If the fixed-address-0x60 store can affect the fixed-root location, the captured root reflects the state before that store, subject to preceding effects. Reading the root only after the store is not equivalent in this case.

### Initial and later repeated groups

If the scaled destination expressions evaluate to different addresses, the initial offset-0x54 store targets the captured-link-derived address and later stores target the adjusted-quantity-derived address. All store the same retained word.

If the effective addresses coincide under the applicable arithmetic or mapping, acceptance does not require them to differ. The group order and other stores remain relevant.

### Narrow numeric constants

The 0x3A mutations have widths of eight, sixteen, and thirty-two bits at their respective destinations. They are not a three-byte string write.

### Interrupted dependency

If an unresolved dependency does not return, later direct accesses are not required to occur. No fallback or recovery path is represented.

### Final transfer

If reached, the final transfer’s first argument is 0x72. That constant is not an established return status. Normal completion additionally depends on the destination returning.
