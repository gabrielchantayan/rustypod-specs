### Arithmetic interpretation

The prefix includes left shifts through 31 bits, unsigned right shifts, masks, and successive transformations of retained values. Mask placement matters: masking before a shift is not generally equivalent to masking afterward.

Paired-result unsigned shifts use 64-bit width. A right shift by 36 selects the high word with its four low bits removed; a right shift by 44 removes twelve low bits from that high word.

Under a **32-bit bitvector interpretation**, a required 32-bit arithmetic result retains only its low 32 bits. Successive left shifts can therefore eliminate all information even when each individual count is below 32.

Some operands have signed-looking types. Actual signed overflow, address wraparound, and late variable-shift behavior are not independently established. Numerical simplifications relying on modular arithmetic are conditional on the bitvector interpretation.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static-review examples, not executed tests.

1. **Zero extension:** If packed-base offset 0x05 yields 0xe7, its widened word publication is 0x000000e7, not a sign-extended word.

2. **Small-base bounds:** Under 32-bit bitvector arithmetic, originating values zero and 127 produce small bases zero and 1016. Changing only bits 7–31 leaves this base unchanged.

3. **Fixed byte 0x01:** For originating value 63, the small index is 252 and the small base is 504. If fixed byte 0x05 yields 0x20, fixed byte 0x01 receives 0xf8 before address 0x35 receives byte 0xfc, assuming permitted accesses.

4. **Information loss:** Under 32-bit bitvector arithmetic, a word shifted left 25 bits and subsequently left 11 bits becomes zero. Wider retained intermediates change that result.

5. **Intermediate publications:** Observing only the final 0x20 at computed-base offset 0x20 is insufficient. Its preceding publications are 0x20, 0x2f, and the widened observed byte.

6. **Retained publication base:** If the fixed publication-base observation yields address A, later publications use retained A even if subsequent effects change its original source to B.

7. **Calculated byte 0x3a:** With retained record value 0x40, the adjusted value is 0x6a. The stored byte is 0x80, the low byte of \(8 \times 0x6a + 0x30\), at the specified observation boundary.

8. **Fresh 0x67 observations:** If its three fresh suffix reads yield 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56, fixed byte 0x21 receives 0x12, and the two read-fed computed offset-0x09 publications receive 0x34 and 0x56.

9. **Returned error:** If a prefix dependency returns an application-defined failure indication normally, that indication alone does not select an exit path. Execution continues along the represented continuation, subject to subsequent effects and faults; its result bits may affect later operations.

10. **Missing continuation:** If a prefix dependency does not return, faults, or transfers elsewhere without resuming the continuation, later direct mutations and final dispatch need not occur.

11. **Variable shift:** A count of 40 establishes neither a zero result nor modulo-32 count behavior without instruction semantics.
