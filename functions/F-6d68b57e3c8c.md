# F-6d68b57e3c8c

## Purpose

F-6d68b57e3c8c performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms primary results and secondary register outputs, and then mutates storage through calculated and loaded addresses. Its final observable control-flow operation is an unresolved indirect transfer with four represented arguments.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation exposes invocation order, fixed-width transformations, explicit memory-access widths, retained values, and ordered storage updates. It does not establish an application-level purpose or a complete executable calling convention.

**Inferred intent:** recurring offsets, field masks, and loaded words reused as addresses suggest manipulation of packed values and interconnected storage. They do not establish a particular record format, text encoding, initialization protocol, or resource-management operation.

**Unknowns:** dependency identities, some storage declarations, instruction-level arithmetic semantics, and terminal control-flow semantics remain **documented_only**. This is a static behavioral contract; no runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Invocation phase

A long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations precedes the first explicit data-storage write. The first target is formed from incoming control-link state plus displacement 0xFFE. Later targets are formed from represented code-location values with that displacement.

No data-dependent branch or loop is exposed in this phase. Dependencies may nevertheless branch, mutate storage, block, fault, or fail to return.

Invocation order is observable:

- Producers precede consumers.
- Invocations remain significant when their results are unused.
- Matching argument values do not justify merging invocations.
- A displayed invocation without arguments does not prove absence of implicit inputs.
- Retained results remain distinct from results produced by later invocations.

Argument construction includes low result words shifted left by eight, high words forwarded unchanged or shifted, duplicated transformed arguments, secondary register outputs, and explicit zeros. Some retained intermediates are shifted again after intervening invocations.

Under truncating 32-bit semantics, truncation occurs at intermediate word boundaries. Widening an entire composed calculation can therefore change the result.

### Field transformations

The represented transformations select low fields of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits at different consumers. Subsequent shifts can discard additional information.

Notable relationships include:

- Retaining twenty low bits and logically shifting right by ten, thereby selecting bits 10–19.
- Extracting portions of high result words.
- Logically shifting full unsigned 64-bit results right by 34, 36, or 44 before narrowing.
- Extracting from retained intermediates that have already undergone word-width truncation.

A 64-bit unsigned result shifted right by 44 has at most twenty significant bits. No saturation, rounding, checked-overflow, or general sign-normalization behavior is visible.

Explicitly initialized zero values remain zero through their represented masks and shifts under ordinary fixed-width interpretation. This does not make invocations consuming those zeros removable.

### Initial storage mutation

Immediately before explicit mutation, retained values include a dependency result scaled by \(2^{25}\), another result shifted left by eight, another shifted left by twenty-five, and a bounded index.

The index comes from shifting a result left by eight, retaining fifteen low bits, and logically shifting right by six. Under the fixed-width interpretation, it is a multiple of four between 0 and 508 inclusive. Twice the index is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016 inclusive.

The opening access relationships are:

- A word write at offset 0x14 from the high word of the final combined result.
- A byte write at offset nine from a retained shifted base.
- A word write at offset 0x54 from the final low word scaled by 256.
- A second byte write to that same offset-nine destination, using a different source.

The intervening word write and both byte writes remain part of the access trace even if only the final byte survives.

A word at offset 0x14 from the \(2^{25}\)-scaled base is cleared, followed by an explicit unsigned-byte read at offset five from that base. A small-index-derived word destination is cleared before the loaded byte is widened and stored as a word at offset 0x34.

The earlier cleared offset-0x14 word is subsequently read and retained. Another zero-word write revisits the small-index-derived destination, followed by two consecutive byte writes at its offset 0x15.

Aliasing matters throughout: an earlier clear does not establish that a later read returns zero if an intervening overlapping write changes the location.

### Literal writes and propagation

A later section writes numerical literal 0x3A as a byte through a loaded value scaled by four, as a halfword through that value scaled by eight, and as a word at offset 0x20 from the four-times-scaled address.

Another retained value is reduced by 0x96 and scaled by four to form the **adjusted base**. Its offset-0x20 word destination receives, in order:

- 0x20.
- 0x2F.
- An explicit unsigned byte read from offset four of the adjusted base, widened to a word.
- 0x20.

After these four writes, the fixed-storage propagation-base word is read and retained. **That read precedes the fixed-address word write of 0x20 at 0x60.** Later propagation uses the retained base, not a post-write reload.

Propagation consists of repeated ordered four-write groups: an offset-0x54 word write through a scaled destination, an offset-0x19 byte write through the propagation base, an offset-0x11 byte write through the adjusted base, and an offset-0x34 word write through the propagation base.

The first group differs from later groups in its scaled destination and final word value. It uses eight times an earlier retained linked value; later groups use eight times the adjusted value after subtraction of 0x96. The offset-0x19 byte comes from the low eight bits of the adjusted base; the offset-0x11 byte comes from the low eight bits of the propagation base.

The propagation base is not visibly reloaded between groups. Repeated writes cannot be collapsed for access-trace equivalence. This contract leaves the independently verified numerical repetition count **documented_only**.

### Final mutations and the three reads at 0x67

After propagation, a fixed-address word-valued read associated with 0xA9 precedes a retained offset-0x74 word read. A byte assignment at 0x3A follows. Words read at offset 0x14 from a **doubled linked base** and offset four from the adjusted base are retained across later mutations.

The initial final-phase writes include bytes at the doubled linked base’s offset 0x0D and the adjusted base’s offsets eight, 0x0D, and nine. The offset-eight literal is 0x6C. A self-referential word write then targets offset 0x54 from the doubled linked base.

Fixed byte storage at **0x67 is assigned the low byte of the earlier retained fixed-address word value associated with 0xA9**. The subsequent three reads of 0x67 are separate visible operations:

| Read | Consumer and established ordering |
|---|---|
| First | Supplies the byte assignment at fixed address 0x21. It follows the signed word read at offset 0x44 from the doubled linked base and precedes the adjusted base’s offset-0x10 byte write. |
| Second | Supplies the first of two later byte stores to the adjusted base’s offset-nine destination. It follows the propagation base’s offset-0x44 word write, and precedes the doubled linked base’s offset-nine byte write. |
| Third | Supplies the second of those offset-nine stores. It follows that doubled-linked-base byte write, the adjusted base’s offset-0x44 word write using the retained fixed-address word, and the word read at offset 0x54 from the adjusted base. |

Between the first and second reads, the represented mutations include the adjusted base’s offset-0x10 byte write; two word writes at offset 0x54 from the propagation base; a signed-shift-derived byte write at offset one from the doubled linked base; a word read at offset 0x40 from the adjusted base; and further word writes at offsets 0x74, 0x10, and 0x44.

Between the second and third reads, the doubled linked base receives the offset-nine byte write, and the adjusted base receives the offset-0x44 word write. The offset-0x54 word read occurs before the third read of 0x67.

**Each consumer uses the value read from 0x67 at its own position.** Neither later read may be replaced by the originally assigned byte or by an earlier read result. Intervening derived-address writes can alias 0x67, including through overlapping word storage. No dependency behavior is needed to establish this alias-sensitive requirement.

The signed shift uses the retained signed offset-0x44 word and a count equal to the low eight bits of the adjusted base. Its result is narrowed to a byte. Negative-operand and oversized-count semantics remain unresolved.

The adjusted base’s offset-0x10 byte write precedes a word write to that same offset using the retained offset-0x40 word. The later word write does not eliminate the earlier byte access.

## Side effects

Fixed assignments include byte-valued storage at 0x01, 0x15, 0x21, 0x3A, and 0x67, and word-valued storage at 0x54 and 0x60. Fixed byte storage at 0x67 is read three times after its assignment, with the distinct consumers and ordering specified above.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Field boundary.** Retaining twenty low bits of 0xFFFFFFFF and logically shifting right by ten yields 1023. Further left shifts by six or five yield 65472 or 32736.

2. **Index boundary.** If the relevant result is 0x7F, the bounded index is 508 and its double is 1016 under the stated fixed-width interpretation.

3. **Byte widening.** An explicit unsigned-byte read returning 0xFE supplies word value 254 when widened. This range guarantee does not apply to the unresolved named-storage conversion at 0x05.

4. **Store truncation.** A byte store supplied with 0x12345678 writes numerical byte 0x78; adjacent bytes are outside that explicit store.

5. **Propagation-base retention.** If the propagation-base read yields 0x1000, later groups use that retained value after the 0x60 write. The represented order is base read before fixed-address write.

6. **Literal sequence.** With supported non-aliasing storage, the adjusted offset-0x20 destination receives 0x20, 0x2F, the widened offset-four byte, and 0x20 in that order.

7. **Three separate fixed-byte reads.** An access trace must contain three reads of 0x67 after its assignment: one for 0x21, then one for each specified adjusted-base offset-nine store. The third follows the adjusted-base offset-0x54 word read.

8. **Alias-sensitive fixed-byte values.** If supported aliasing makes the byte at 0x67 equal to 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56 at those three respective reads, the consumers receive 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56 respectively. Reusing the assigned byte or an earlier read fails this criterion.

9. **Stable fixed-byte storage.** If no overlapping write or environmental change affects 0x67 between assignment and the three reads, their values match the assigned byte. Their equality does not remove any read event.

10. **Repeated stores.** Consecutive identical byte writes remain distinct access events even when final storage contents match a collapsed implementation.

11. **Finite-width composition.** Under truncating 32-bit semantics, shifting a retained word left by twenty-eight twice yields zero. Widening the intermediate is not equivalent.

12. **Interrupted execution and terminal values.** A nonreturning dependency prevents later effects and F-U003. If F-U003 is reached, it receives 0x72 and the specified retained word sources, without substituting later reloads.
