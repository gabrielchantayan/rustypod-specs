### Widths and arithmetic

A byte access is exactly 8 bits. A word access is exactly 32 bits. Loaded bytes are unsigned and are zero-extended when used in address calculations. Byte stores retain the low eight bits of their value.

Unsigned 32-bit arithmetic is reduced modulo \(2^{32}\). In particular, define:

- \(S\): the 32-bit unsigned product \(P \times 1024\).
- \(Z\): the 32-bit unsigned product \(P \times 2^{26}\).
- \(M = P \mathbin{\&} 0x003FFFFF\).
- \(Q = M \times 128\).
- \(R = M \times 16\).

The masked quantities do not overflow: \(Q \leq 0x1FFFFF80\) and \(R \leq 0x03FFFFF0\).

Several other shifts, multiplications, and address additions use signed 32-bit intermediates in the representation. The notation below records their arithmetic relationships; it does not impose unsigned wrapping on those signed expressions. Signed overflow, negative left shifts, and conversions of unsigned values outside the signed range remain unresolved. In particular, \(S\) and \(Z\) are retained through signed intermediates before their subsequent address uses.

No byte order, pointer-width contract, or alignment guarantee is established for word accesses.
