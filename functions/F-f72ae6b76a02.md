# F-f72ae6b76a02

Its terminal indirect invocation receives four explicit arguments beginning with 0x72.

### Widths and arithmetic

A byte, halfword, and word mean 8, 16, and 32 bits. Some dependency results are represented as 64-bit quantities, with separately selected low and high words.

Under a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector interpretation**:

- Left shifts discard bits beyond bit 31.
- Widening after a word-width shift does not recover discarded bits.
- Byte and halfword stores retain the low 8 and 16 bits.
- Explicitly unsigned right shifts insert zeros.
- Address formation uses the finite-width result, not an unbounded integer.

Signed left shifts, signed multiplication overflow, address wrapping, and signed right shifts still require target-specific semantics. The conditional model does not resolve them universally.

Visible masks retain fields of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, or 11 bits. Subsequent shifts may narrow the selection further. Retaining 20 low bits and shifting right by 10 selects original bits 10–19; retaining 17 low bits and shifting right by 4 selects bits 4–16.

Unsigned shifts of represented 64-bit results by 34, 36, or 44 bits select upper-result information.

A late address-forming transformation shifts a word left by 8, retains 15 low bits, and shifts right by 6. Under the conditional model, its result is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Doubling it produces a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

A later signed right shift takes its count from the low byte of a computed working base. Counts can exceed 31. Large-count behavior and any count masking remain **documented_only**.

### Opening memory mutation

Direct mutation begins after the last invocation of the initial dependency phase.

The final represented 64-bit result contributes two distinct destinations: its high word supplies a base for an offset-0x14 word store, while its low word, scaled by 256, supplies a base for an offset-0x54 word store. The stored values are retained earlier computations, not necessarily portions of that final result.

An early computed offset-9 byte destination first receives the low byte of the final result’s high word and is then overwritten with the low byte of a retained shifted value.

The opening group also establishes these relationships:

- A computed offset-0x14 word is cleared before a byte read at that base’s offset 5.
- Another offset-0x14 word is cleared before the read byte is zero-extended into an offset-0x34 word store.
- The first cleared word is read later, after intervening effects.
- Further clears and duplicate offset-0x15 byte stores occur.

The later read of the cleared word is not unconditionally zero: intervening accesses may alias it. Duplicate stores and overwritten stores remain separately represented effects.

### Fixed-storage read at 0x05

Fixed storage at 0x05 is read after the opening clears and duplicate stores, but **before** the byte write at fixed address 0x01. Its value is converted to an unsigned 32-bit address-like value and retained.

The 0x01 write receives the low byte of the restricted doubled base described above. The captured value from 0x05 supplies:

### Saved word at 0xA9 and late bases

The 0x3A byte receives the low-byte result of an adjusted value scaled by eight and increased by 0x30.

These roles imply neither ownership nor disjointness.

### Live byte storage at 0x67 and the offset-1 store

The saved word from 0xA9 supplies two different effects: its low byte initializes fixed storage at 0x67, and its full word is later stored at working offset 0x44.

After initialization of 0x67, a word is read at companion offset 0x44. A current byte read at 0x67 then supplies the byte write at fixed address 0x21.

The companion-offset-0x44 word supplies a working-offset-0x10 byte store and a retained-fixed-base offset-0x54 word store. Its signed-right-shift result, using the working base’s low byte as count, supplies the **companion offset-1 byte store**. The retained fixed-derived base’s offset-0x54 word is subsequently overwritten with the working base.

A working-offset-0x40 word read supplies a working-offset-0x10 word store. Other intervening effects include the retained fixed-derived base’s offset-0x74 and offset-0x44 stores.

Two later, separate reads of byte storage at 0x67 each supply a working-offset-9 byte store. Between them are a companion-offset-9 byte store, the full-word working-offset-0x44 store of the saved 0xA9 value, and a working-offset-0x54 word read.

All three post-initialization reads of 0x67 use current storage. Aliased computed writes can change it. Neither the 0x21 update nor the two offset-9 stores unconditionally reuse the original saved low byte.

### Final mutation and dispatch

The remaining effects include storing the saved companion-offset-0x14 word at companion offset 0x54, further byte stores, and replacing working offset 0x44 with a loaded base.

A word read at companion offset 4 supplies a newer base. A halfword constant 0x65 is stored through twice that value. A word read at the newer base’s offset 0x64 supplies the terminal second argument.

A computed offset-0x54 word destination receives decimal 100. A later word read at the newer base’s offset 0x74 supplies the terminal fourth argument. A word read at companion offset 0x64 supplies a destination base.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static obligations, not executed tests.

### Finite-width transformations

Under 32-bit bit-vector shifts, a third argument of 0x00000200 becomes zero after a left shift by 23. Unbounded arithmetic is not equivalent.

For a fourth argument of 1, shifting left by 3 and then by 23 yields 0x04000000. Its separate left shift by 26 yields the same word without proving equivalence of the receiving dependencies.

### Unsigned byte conversion

An explicitly unsigned byte read of 0xFE converts to word value 254, not −2.

### Companion offset 1 is written

Suppose the word read from companion offset 0x44 is 0x12345678, the working base’s low byte is 8, and the target gives the ordinary in-range signed-right-shift result for this positive value. The companion-offset-1 byte store receives 0x56.

The prior byte at that destination is not explicitly read to form the result. Changing only that prior byte does not change the shift operand or count, absent aliasing or other external effects.

For a count exceeding 31, this example establishes no masked-count rule.

### Fixed read before mutation

The value supplying subsequent accesses through fixed storage at 0x05 must be captured before the fixed-address 0x01 byte write. Its conversion does not prove a word-width read.

### Captured repeated-region base

If a repeated-region store aliases the fixed storage that supplied its base, later groups still use the already captured base rather than reloading the changed storage.

### Saved 0xA9 word versus live 0x67 byte

If the saved 0xA9 word has low byte 0xAB, initialization writes 0xAB to 0x67. If an intervening aliased write changes 0x67 to 0xCD before a later read, that read supplies 0xCD.

The two working-offset-9 stores may therefore receive different values.

### Saved terminal argument

Later modification of the original companion-offset-0x14 storage does not replace the saved terminal third argument. This differs from the live rereads at 0x67.

### Incomplete execution

If a loaded base cannot support its next access, no graceful error result or rollback is established. Terminal dispatch is required only when execution reaches it.
