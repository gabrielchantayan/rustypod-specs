# F-99e552cf8e0a

## Purpose

F-99e552cf8e0a performs an ordered sequence of unresolved dependency calls, indirect memory accesses, byte-field updates, 32-bit payload propagation, and global assignments. Its terminal sequence attempts a call through address zero.

Application-level purpose is **documented_only**. Repeated clearing and scaled propagation suggest state preparation or transformation, but do not establish an object model, protocol, initialization guarantee, or successful completion condition.

This contract distinguishes visible static behavior from interpretation. The represented return is reachable only if every preceding access and dependency call completes, including the address-zero call.

## Behavior

### Ordering rules

The visible body has no local branches, loops, validation checks, or early returns. Execution follows one order unless interrupted by an access failure, nonreturning dependency, or environment-defined event.

Calls are observable boundaries. Similar accesses on opposite sides of a call cannot be merged: dependencies are not established to preserve globals or pointed-to storage.

Each ordinary selector update rereads its selector. Explicitly retained bytes and word snapshots are exceptions described below. Repeated memory expressions within one call’s argument list have no established physical read count or relative evaluation order.

### Entry and early selector operations

The first memory operation reads the byte at incoming context address +1. The initial byte is then written at that selector +0x11, before F-unresolved-001 receives the initial forwarded value.

After several calls, the selector-bearing input shifted right by 19 becomes the base of a zero selector update. Its selector-read address is between 1 and 8192 inclusive.

Early call groups forward dependency results shifted left by two, sometimes in duplicated positions or alongside explicit zeros. Other zero selector updates use absolute address 1, apparent secondary results shifted right by nine, and a retained base formed from an unsigned result shifted right by 18 and multiplied by 1024. That retained base ranges from zero through 0x00FFFC00 and is used twice across calls; its selector is reread.

A byte at an apparent secondary-result address +0x0E is supplied in four argument positions. An apparent paired result contributes a duplicated upper half and a lower half shifted right by 19. Later, the 32-bit value at absolute address 0x30 appears in four argument positions; physical read multiplicity is unresolved.

### Early word reads and slot propagation

A later unsigned result supplies a saved **scaled-read seed**, distinct from the earlier repeated selector base.

An apparent secondary result is stored in its own four-scaled slot. Before a later index receives that same payload, a word is read at:

**1024 × (scaled-read seed shifted right by 18) +0x14.**

The word, shifted right by 19, is forwarded alongside the retained secondary-result payload. Calls then produce the later slot index.

A separate chain stores four times a dependency result in its sixteen-scaled slot. It reads a word at the retained four-times value +0x14 and forwards that word shifted right by 19, together with the retained value, zero, and another copy of the value. After calls, another four-scaled slot receives the retained payload.

These two word reads have different address derivations and cannot be substituted for one another.

### Masked fields and mixed-width reads

An unsigned result supplies the low byte of its left shift by two for a selector update. After calls, another update writes the low byte of the original result masked by 0x3FFFFFFF and shifted right by 20.

The same original result supplies fields masked by 0x3FFFFFFF and shifted right by 17 and seven. A dependency receives those fields, four copies of another result, and an execution-position-derived argument.

Later operations include:

- Repeated zero selector updates based on the retained seven-bit-shifted field.
- Forwarding the top four bits of a paired result’s lower half.
- Zero selector updates based on that lower half shifted right by nine.
- Reading a byte at twice that lower half, zero-extending it, and forwarding it four times.

A distinct call consumes an apparent secondary result shifted right by 18. Another word read uses a retained base plus an offset formed by shifting that base right by 19 and then left by 31. Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, only original bit 19 contributes to the offset.

Further selector bases involve a saved signed-looking lower half multiplied by 4096 and an unsigned shifted secondary result multiplied by the represented value negative 0x80000000. Address interpretation remains qualified under Uncertainties.

### Stack address, halfword input, and interleaved field accesses

A dependency receives the same stack address in four positions. A following paired result supplies a retained lower half. Later, a byte at that lower-half address plus an apparent secondary-result offset is zero-extended and forwarded four times.

Another secondary result shifted right by 19 supplies an address component. A 16-bit quantity at that component plus the retained byte is forwarded four times; argument extension is unresolved.

A subsequent paired result supplies an upper-half four-scaled-slot index and a retained lower-half address. The slot receives the shifted address component. After calls, another result-indexed slot receives the same component.

The direct byte writes at the retained lower-half address +0x0D are interleaved with distinct word reads:

- First, that byte receives the low eight bits of the retained address shifted left by one.
- While that first write is in effect, a word is read at **four times a newly saved unsigned result +0x44** and supplied to a dependency.
- Intervening calls execute.
- The byte at the retained address +0x0D is then overwritten with constant 4.
- Only afterward, a word is read at **the saved unsigned result masked by 0x3FFFFFFF, shifted right by 20, then increased by 0x44**. That word is supplied to the next dependency alongside another result shifted left by two.

The second word-read address has no multiplication by four. Dependencies between these effects may observe or modify either byte or word storage. Aliasing can therefore change the read values.

### Exceptional scaling

The next propagation is not an ordinary slot chain:

- A new unsigned result selects a 32-bit destination at **256 times that result +0x14**.
- The destination receives **four times the earlier saved unsigned result**.
- The following call receives **64 times the new result in two positions**, plus a left-by-31 field derived from the earlier result’s masked right-by-17 field.
- After calls, a subsequent result’s four-scaled slot receives **the new result masked by 0x03FFFFFF and shifted right by 13**.

The final masked payload ranges from zero through 8191. The destination scale 256, argument scale 64, and payload scale four are separate operations.

### Middle state propagation and staged payload derivation

Several chains store four-times-result payloads in sixteen-scaled slots, forward scaled or shifted fields, and later copy saved payloads into four-scaled slots. Arguments include left shifts by 24 and 22, with significant duplicated positions.

One chain writes zero at a retained sixteen-scaled base +0x24, then reads a word at that base +0x74. A distinct dependency receives the read value and other saved arguments.

A subsequent unsigned result shifted right by 20 is retained. After calls, it is written at the earlier +0x74 read value’s address +0x24. A fresh word at that address +0x74 is forwarded. Selector updates based on that same address write a masked-field byte and, later, zero.

The retained right-by-20 result is at most 4095, so its later right shift by 21 is zero. Repeated selector updates also use 1024 times that retained result.

Bytes read at apparent secondary-result addresses +0x15 are forwarded. One byte is stored zero-extended in a four-scaled slot; a later slot receives the corresponding apparent secondary index rather than that byte. Another field masks a secondary result by 0x1FFFFFFF and shifts right by 18, yielding at most 2047. Its left shift by 13 is forwarded and subsequently stored.

A separate payload has a known staged derivation that must survive intervening calls:

1. An apparent secondary result is shifted left by **three**, and that intermediate is saved before the secondary result is forwarded.
2. After multiple calls and repeated zero selector updates, an independent dependency-returned payload is written to a four-scaled slot indexed by another secondary result. The saved intermediate is then shifted left by **two** and retained.
3. Further calls and slot propagation occur, including a word read at a result’s four-scaled slot and forwarding that word shifted right by 21.
4. A later result-indexed four-scaled slot receives the retained intermediate shifted left by **two again**.

These are saved-value stages, not fresh secondary-result reads. They are represented as signed-looking 32-bit shifts; an unconditional mathematical multiplication by 128 would discard unresolved overflow semantics.

### Global clear and low-address propagation

The state global is explicitly assigned 32-bit zero. Calls and memory writes continue; this is not an exit or established commit boundary.

Following chains write zero to four-scaled slots, store four-times payloads in sixteen-scaled slots, and forward masked or shifted words from scaled bases +0x14. Another saved result is shifted left by six, forwarded, and later stored.

Ordinary retained values explicitly set to zero reduce several selector-read addresses to absolute address 1. These remain actual reads; dependencies may change that byte between accesses.

This region also retains a byte read at a dependency-produced address +1 and later reads another byte at retained byte +1. A byte from a zero-derived address is stored as a zero-extended word. Further chains propagate zero fields, scaled results, and masked fields. A byte-sized dependency result supplies a left-shift-by-two selector payload, truncated to one byte.

### Late direct fields and first state snapshot

A result shifted left by two supplies the base for a zero byte write at 8192 times that retained base +9. A separate saved result later receives its own low byte at address +9.

Calls consume paired-result components and a word at the separate address +0x34. Slot propagation and nested word reads follow, including a word at a retained word shifted right by 22, plus 0x14.

The first state-global snapshot occurs immediately after a four-scaled-slot write propagating a retained left-by-two dependency result, before the next call.

After several calls, this snapshot is shifted right by 23 and forwarded with duplicated slot-index arguments and a separately shifted word payload. After that call and a following result-producing call, the saved shifted snapshot is shifted right by 23 again. The second shift yields zero because the first produces at most 511. A later slot payload also uses the first-shift value shifted right by 23.

Calls may change the underlying global but do not turn this retained snapshot into a reread.

### Saved late selector and first control assignment

Much later, the byte at absolute address 1 is saved. Zero is written at saved byte +0x11.

Calls continue. A dependency-produced 32-bit value is assigned to the control global, forwarded to a dependency, and followed by another call.

Only then is a word read at **saved byte +0x74**, with its low byte written directly at **saved byte +1**. Both addresses retain the earlier byte; neither uses a fresh selector read.

Subsequent result-derived bases receive similar +0x74-to-+1 copies. One base is reused across calls, but its word is reread for the later copy. Another word is saved and propagated into sixteen-scaled and four-scaled slots.

Further effects include a scaled-result word write at a retained base with its low two bits cleared, plus 0x14, and explicit zero slot writes.

### Second state snapshot and termination

A later paired result supplies a lower half shifted left by two and an upper-half slot index. The scaled lower half is written to the upper half’s four-scaled slot.

Immediately afterward, before the next call, the state global is read again. This second snapshot is independent of the first.

After calls, another four-scaled slot receives the same scaled lower-half payload. A word is then read at **second snapshot +0x14**, before the following call. That retained word supplies a selector payload byte and, later, its right-by-22 selector payload byte.

The explicit earlier clear does not determine either snapshot: dependencies and potentially aliasing writes intervene.

The terminal sequence obtains a byte-sized dependency result, clears one byte through an apparent secondary-result pointer, and then reads the represented 24-bit global fragment. The fragment becomes the upper 24 bits of a 32-bit value whose low byte is the returned byte. This replaces the control global.

A zero 16-bit quantity is then written at a retained paired-result lower half masked by 0x3FFFFFFF and shifted right by seven. Finally, F-unresolved-004 is attempted with three explicit zeros. The represented return follows only if that call returns.
