# F-632563f1b768

## Purpose

F-632563f1b768 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, mixed-width memory accesses, and a concluding unresolved indirect transfer. It has three explicit 32-bit inputs and no established return value.

No business-level purpose is established. The available evidence does not justify identifying the operation as a formatter, allocator, collection update, device operation, or serializer. Repeated offsets and character-like constants do not establish those meanings.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** behavior displayed in the supplied representation.
- **Arithmetic deductions:** consequences of displayed widths and operations, conditional on ordinary fixed-width machine semantics.
- **Inferred intent:** possible application meaning, not an acceptance requirement.
- **documented_only:** unresolved or expressly unspecified behavior.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Execution and arithmetic model

The displayed operation is predominantly straight-line:

1. Indirect invocations and argument preparation.
2. Direct memory reads and mutations.
3. A final unresolved indirect transfer.

No explicit input-validation branch, retry, bounded traversal, or recovery path is displayed.

Access widths are 8, 16, and 32 bits. Some dependency results are represented as 64-bit quantities split into low and high 32-bit portions. Unsigned byte loads used in word stores are zero-extended; byte stores retain the low eight source bits.

Address arithmetic includes scaling by 2, 4, 8, 256, and `0x02000000`, subtraction of `0x96`, and addition of offsets. Arithmetic deductions below assume 32-bit machine-bit operations where applicable. Actual signed overflow, address wrapping, alignment failures, and excessive shift-count behavior remain **documented_only**.

Successive shifts retain their intermediate widths. Bits discarded by one 32-bit shift do not reappear in a subsequent shift.

### Opening memory mutations and the early zero load

The direct mutation stage begins with a word store through an address derived from the high portion of the last represented 64-bit result. A byte store follows through a separately derived address. A further word store uses the low result scaled by 256, and the preceding byte destination is written again.

A dependency-derived base scaled by `0x02000000` then participates in this significant access relationship:

- Its word at offset `0x14` is cleared.
- Its unsigned byte at offset `0x05` is read.
- A word at another derived address is cleared.
- The captured byte is widened and stored at offset `0x34` of the first base.
- The word at offset `0x14` of that first base is loaded.

**Under ordinary fixed-width memory semantics, without external interference or access-triggered effects, this load observes zero.** The intervening zero word store cannot introduce nonzero bytes even if it overlaps the cleared word. The widened-byte store at offset `0x34` does not overlap the word at offset `0x14` of the same base.

The byte read itself does not mutate ordinary memory. Device-memory semantics, read-triggered effects, or another execution agent could invalidate the zero deduction, but no displayed intervening nonzero overlapping store supports doing so.

The loaded value subsequently supplies duplicate byte stores at offset `0x15` of another derived base. Under the same assumptions, those stores write zero. A later word store through the loaded value plus `0x54` consequently addresses fixed location `0x54`.

### Subsequent derived-memory relationships

The operation next reads a byte from fixed address `0x05`. Its zero-extended value becomes an address base, rather than merely stored data. A byte write at fixed address `0x01` follows that read, before a byte write at offset `0x15` of the byte-derived base.

A word loaded from offset `0x54` of that byte-derived base is captured before that location is overwritten. This captured word becomes an important scaling source throughout later mutations.

Further addresses come from word loads at offsets `0x04`, `0x44`, `0x54`, `0x64`, and `0x74`, including a word read at offset `0x74` from a base obtained by loading one byte. These accesses have no displayed null, range, or tag checks.

Several source values remain captured across subsequent writes. A captured word is not automatically reloaded merely because its original location changes. Conversely, two separately displayed loads must not automatically be combined: intervening writes may affect their results.

### Layout and overlap

### Post-repetition captures and fixed-byte dependencies

The fixed-storage reads after the repeated region have material ordering and data-flow consequences.

First, a word is captured from fixed address `0xA9`. Next, a word is captured at offset `0x74` of the repeated-payload base. Only then is fixed byte address `0x3A` written with the low byte of eight times the adjusted scale plus `0x30`.

After that fixed byte write, two further words are captured, in order:

- A word at offset `0x14` of a previously derived, doubled base.
- A word at offset `0x04` of the work base.

Thus the `0x3A` write precedes these two loads and can affect either capture if their storage overlaps it. The earlier captures do not change retrospectively.

Following several derived byte and word writes, fixed byte address `0x67` receives the low byte of the captured word from `0xA9`. The operation then:

- Reads a derived word at offset `0x44`.
- Reads byte `0x67` and copies the observed byte to fixed address `0x21`.

Two additional reads of byte `0x67` occur later, each supplying a byte store at work base plus `0x09`. Derived writes intervene between the initial `0x67` store and these later reads, and also between the later reads themselves. Those writes include a word store at work base plus `0x10`, a word store at shared base plus `0x44`, byte stores, and a word store at work base plus `0x44`.

Consequently, the later reads of `0x67` must not generally be replaced by the originally captured low byte from `0xA9`. Derived addresses may overlap `0x67`; the first later destination may itself overlap it. Each displayed read observes storage at its own position in the sequence.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

### Initial transformation

Under 32-bit machine-bit semantics, third inputs 0, 1, 2, and 3 produce third arguments `0x00000000`, `0x40000000`, `0x80000000`, and `0xC0000000`. The first two arguments remain unchanged; the fourth is zero.

### Early clear remains zero

With ordinary memory, supported accesses, and no external interference, the early load from the cleared offset-`0x14` word yields zero. Allowing the other zero store to overlap that word does not change this result. The widened-byte store at offset `0x34` does not overlap it.

A nonzero result requires assumptions beyond the displayed intervening stores, such as device effects or external modification.

### Unsigned widening

If the relevant byte read observes `0xFE`, its widened word store contains `0x000000FE`, not `0xFFFFFFFE`.

### Captured shared base

If `0x087FBBBC` yields a particular base, the repeated updates use that captured value. A subsequent overlapping write at `0x60` does not retroactively change the capture.

### Re-reading fixed byte storage

If a derived write changes byte `0x67` after its initial store but before a later read, that later read supplies the changed byte. Substituting the original low byte captured from `0xA9` is not accepted under that condition.

### Repetition and partial completion

The first group uses the root-derived scale; later groups use the adjusted scale. Exact total write-count acceptance remains **documented_only**.

If execution faults after a successful write, restoration is not required by the displayed behavior. Reaching the final transfer requires all preceding reached operations to have completed; normal return remains conditional on its target.
