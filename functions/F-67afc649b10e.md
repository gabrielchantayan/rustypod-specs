# F-67afc649b10e

## Purpose

F-67afc649b10e is a parameterless, stateful operation with three visible phases:

- An extended sequence of indirect dependency invocations, forwarding and transforming returned words and additional machine-state outputs.
- Mixed-width memory mutation through computed addresses, loaded address-like words, and absolute storage.
- A final indirect transfer with four displayed arguments.

Its application-level purpose is **documented_only**. The supplied representation does not establish object initialization, text construction, collection management, or a protocol. Character-like constants and recurring offsets do not establish those meanings.

This contract describes static access behavior. It does not certify that the supplied representation faithfully captures every executable detail, nor that source-level ordering constitutes a hardware synchronization guarantee.

## Behavior

### Dependency invocation phase

The first indirect target depends on incoming control state plus 0xffe. Subsequent targets are formed from retained address-like constants or references plus the same displacement.

The phase has no visible conditional branch or loop. Its invocations nevertheless may branch, mutate state, fail, or not return.

Visible argument patterns include:

- Scalar results shifted left by eight.
- A combined result’s low word shifted left by eight alongside its unchanged high word.
- Independently shifted low or high words in additional positions.
- Explicit zeros, including multiargument zero combinations.
- Additional machine-state outputs consumed independently of recovered primary results.
- Values retained across intervening calls and transformed again before reuse.

Calls with no displayed arguments, or with unused primary results, remain dependency boundaries. They cannot be discarded as effect-free. Unknown dependencies are not assumed to preserve globals or to avoid allocation and release.

The visible transformations include masks selecting 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, and 17 bits, together with smaller fields. Unsigned right shifts insert zero bits. Masking before a shift is not interchangeable with unrestricted shifting.

Intermediate widths matter. Under a 32-bit truncating interpretation, two successive left shifts by 28 produce zero, rather than a retained 56-bit result. Signed left shifts, signed multiplication, and conversion of high-bit words to signed quantities require target-specific interpretation.

### Entry into direct mutation

Let the final combined dependency result have a low word and a high word. Before any direct-memory reads, the operation writes:

- The seed as a word at high word + 0x14.
- The high word’s low byte at seed + 9.
- The scaled base as a word at 256 times the low word + 0x54.
- The seed’s low byte over the preceding byte destination.

It then clears the word at scaled base + 0x14, reads the byte at scaled base + 5, clears the word at small base + 0x14, and stores the byte as a zero-extended word at scaled base + 0x34.

The cleared scaled-base word is subsequently read, and the small-base word is cleared again. The read value’s low byte is written twice at small base + 0x15. These are separate accesses; replacing the read with zero requires additional ordinary-memory and non-aliasing assumptions.

Under a 32-bit truncating interpretation, the small quantity ranges over 0, 4, 8, …, 508. The small base ranges over 0, 8, 16, …, 1016. Neither range establishes accessible storage.

| Word write at 0x54 | Later; receives twice the anchor. It follows a write at small base + 0x14 using the scalar underlying the scaled base, shifted left by 26. |
| Byte write at 0x15 | Later still; receives the low byte of the small base, after two byte writes at small base + 0x15 containing the low byte of the fourfold base. |

Between the absolute write at 1 and the absolute word write at 0x54:

- The byte-derived base + 0x15 receives the low byte of the small quantity.
- Scaled base + 0x11 receives the low byte of the retained word.
- The word previously read at scaled base + 0x14 is used as a destination base for storing the retained word at offset 0x54.
- Scaled base + 0x11 is overwritten with zero.
- The anchor is read from byte-derived base + 0x54, before that location is overwritten with the earlier scaled-base read.
- Small base + 0x34 receives the small quantity.

A word read at scaled base + 0x74 becomes another base. Its offset-0x54 word first receives the scaled-base-producing scalar shifted by 26 and later receives the retained word. Repeated writes at anchor + 0x24 publish the anchor itself; repeated byte writes at the loaded base + 9 publish its low byte.

An intervening self-publication uses two displayed word reads at small base + 0x74: one provides the destination base, the other the stored value. Their equality and within-expression evaluation order are not established. They must not be collapsed into one observation.

After the absolute word write at 0x54, small base + 0x14 receives the anchor twice. The two fourfold-derived byte writes then precede the absolute byte write at 0x15. Next, fourfold base + 0x54 receives the retained word, and small base + 0x34 receives a fourfold anchor-derived word.

### Interconnected computed-address changes

The remaining preparatory mutations coordinate offsets 4, 5, 9, 0x0d, 0x14, 0x15, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.

A fresh word at small base + 0x74 is doubled and retained. The fourfold base publishes itself at offset 0x54. Byte fragments of fourfold and eightfold anchor-derived quantities are written into related destinations.

A byte at fourfold base + 5 supplies a base for a word read at offset 0x74. The resulting word becomes a further destination base. Another word at fourfold base + 0x64 supplies a separate destination for self-publication.

Later, a word at fourfold base + 4 supplies a base whose offset-5 byte is read twice, with intervening writes. The second byte observation supplies the offset-0x74 read defining the terminal root. The terminal base is twice that root.

These operations include self-valued word stores, repeated overwrites, byte fragments of address-like values, and eightfold anchor-derived values at offset 0x14. They do not establish list, tree, ownership, or reference-count semantics.

### Constants and publication

Three stores of 0x3a have different widths and destinations:

- A byte at four times the terminal root.
- A halfword at eight times the terminal root.
- A word at four times the terminal root + 0x20.

The first precedes the halfword store, which precedes the word store. Computing the adjusted quantity between these events does not itself access memory.

Working base + 0x20 then receives four successive word writes: 0x20, 0x2f, a zero-extended byte read at working base + 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the second write and before the third.

**Only after all four writes**, the publication-base word is read from absolute address **0x087fbbbc**. The operation next writes the word **0x20 at absolute address 0x60**. Thus, aliasing writes preceding the publication-base read may affect the loaded base; the later absolute write cannot retroactively change that retained observation.

The publication region begins by storing four times the terminal root at eight times the anchor + 0x54. Its first publication group writes:

- The low byte of the working base at publication base + 0x19.
- The low byte of the publication base at working base + 0x11.
- Eight times the anchor as a word at publication base + 0x34.

Subsequent groups are preceded by a word write at eight times the adjusted quantity + 0x54, containing four times the terminal root. Their two byte publications use the same retained byte values; their offset-0x34 word publication contains eight times the adjusted quantity instead.

The repetition is finite and visibly expanded, not input-dependent iteration. Its numerical count remains **documented_only** in this contract. No trace-exact repetition count is claimed.

### Final mutation: interleaved absolute and computed accesses

After the repeated region, the following ordering is established:

1. Read the word at absolute address 0xa9.
2. Read the word at four times the terminal root + 0x74.
3. Write a byte at absolute address 0x3a, containing the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30.
4. Read and save the word at terminal base + 0x14.
5. Read and save the word at working base + 4.

The 0x3a write therefore precedes both saved-word reads. Those reads must not be hoisted ahead of it.

Next, terminal base + 0x0d receives the low byte of the working base. Working base + 8 receives 0x6c; its offsets 0x0d and 9 receive the low byte of the publication base. Terminal base + 0x54 receives the terminal base itself.

The operation then writes the low byte of the saved absolute-0xa9 word at absolute address **0x67**. After that write it reads the word at terminal base + 0x44. Only then does it **read the byte at 0x67 and write that observation at absolute address 0x21**.

The offset-0x44 word supplies a byte store at working base + 0x10 and a word store at publication base + 0x54. A signed right-shifted form supplies a byte store at terminal base + 1; its shift count is the low eight bits of the working base. Publication base + 0x54 is then overwritten with the working base.

A word read at working base + 0x40 is retained. Publication base + 0x74 receives the earlier working-base offset-4 snapshot, then working base + 0x10 receives the offset-0x40 word. This word store overlaps the earlier byte destination.

Publication base + 0x44 next receives the terminal base. A **fresh byte read at absolute 0x67** supplies a store at working base + 9. Terminal base + 9 receives the low byte of the working base, and working base + 0x44 receives the saved absolute-0xa9 word.

After reading the word at working base + 0x54, the operation performs **another fresh byte read at absolute 0x67**, again supplying working base + 9. These two later reads are distinct from the read supplying absolute address 0x21. Intervening stores may alias 0x67.

The newly loaded offset-0x54 word becomes a destination base. Its offset 0x0d receives the low byte of the earlier fourfold-terminal-root offset-0x74 observation. The saved terminal-base offset-0x14 word is restored at terminal base + 0x54.

A fresh word at working base + 0x14 is read before further mutations. Its low byte is later written at offset 0x11 of the newly loaded destination base. Between those events, terminal base + 0x11 receives the earlier terminal-root-related byte; working base + 9 and +0x44 receive byte and word forms of the newly loaded destination base.

### Final publication and dispatch

A word read at terminal base + 4 becomes the final loaded base. The operation writes halfword 0x65 at twice that base, then reads its offset-0x64 word for the second dispatch argument.

The destination loaded from working base + 0x54 receives the preceding offset-0x14 snapshot’s low byte at offset 0x11, then word 100 at offset 0x54. The final loaded base’s offset-0x74 word is subsequently read for the fourth dispatch argument.

Finally, a word read at terminal base + 0x64 supplies another destination base. Terminal base + 9 receives the final loaded base’s low byte. The other destination’s offset 0x54 receives the second dispatch argument, and a word store at twice that argument receives the other destination base.

The final transfer displays four arguments:

2. The saved final-loaded-base offset-0x64 word.
3. The saved terminal-base offset-0x14 word.
4. The later final-loaded-base offset-0x74 word.

These are temporally distinct snapshots, not simultaneous final-state reads.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Combined-result forwarding:** If a forwarding site receives low word 0x12345678 and high word 0x9abcdef0, an eight-bit low-word shift produces 0x34567800 under 32-bit truncation. The high argument remains 0x9abcdef0; discarded bits do not migrate into it.

- **Unsigned promotion:** An offset-5 byte observation of 0xfe becomes word 254, not −2.

- **Small-base arithmetic:** A selected 15-bit field of 0x4000 yields small quantity 256 and small base 512. Accessibility of address 512 is a separate precondition.

- **Publication-base timing:** If an earlier working-base write overlaps 0x087fbbbc, the publication-base read observes storage after that write. The read still precedes the absolute word write at 0x60.

- **Early absolute values:** If the small base is 0x120, its absolute byte publications at 1 and later at 0x15 contain 0x20. If the anchor is 0x1234, the intervening absolute word write at 0x54 contains 0x2468, subject to the stated arithmetic model.

- **Final absolute interleaving:** If terminal base + 0x14 overlaps 0x3a, its saved word is read after the derived byte write at 0x3a. A replacement that reads it first is not equivalent.

- **Fresh 0x67 observations:** If an intervening computed store changes byte 0x67 after the copy to 0x21, a later computed-address byte store uses its fresh observation. Reusing the initially written byte is not equivalent.

- **Ordered overwrite:** With ordinary, non-aliasing memory, the four-write working-base offset-0x20 sequence ends at 0x20. Intermediate writes and the offset-4 byte read remain part of the visible access account.

- **Snapshot preservation:** Later mutation of terminal base + 0x14 does not change the third dispatch argument, which uses the saved word.
