## Behavior

The entry payload is then written as a 32-bit value at the address formed by four times that result plus `0x14`. The next indirect call receives four times the result.

This is a byte-selected address, not an established field access through a pointer.

### 2. First encoded-value sequence

An indirect call receives the entry context logically shifted right by 12 bits. Further calls supply another result.

The function rereads the byte at entry context plus `1`. It writes to the newly selected address the low eight bits of:

\[
(\text{first encoded value}\ \&\ 0x3fffffff) \mathbin{\gg} 20.
\]

After byte truncation, this write contains bits 20–27 of the first encoded value. Bits 28–29 survive the mask and shift but not the byte store.

The new call result, scaled by four, is passed onward. Later, another scaled result is passed together with the previously captured selector byte. This argument uses the saved selector; it is not visibly reread at that call.

A separate expression shifts the entry context left by 26 bits. The sequence then presents two 32-bit reads from that shifted value plus `0x1c` as two arguments to one indirect call. These are two represented reads, not one read guaranteed to supply both arguments.

That shifted value is also multiplied by `0x400`. Under a 32-bit modular interpretation, the combined transformation discards every original bit and produces zero. The signed arithmetic recovery prevents treating this simplification as unconditional language-level evidence.

A byte is then read at that product plus `1`, and zero is written at `0x11` plus the byte.
