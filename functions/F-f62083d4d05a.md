These are conditional contract examples, not observed executions. Each assumes preceding operations complete, required dependencies return, and illustrated accesses are permitted.

1. **Primary-derived stores.**

3. **Earlier result retained as data.**  
   If result 038 is 7 and result 043 is 9, the associated stores write 28 at `0x84`, then 28 at `0x38`. Writing 36 at the latter address violates the represented dependency.

4. **Byte truncation and extraction.**  
   If result 060 is `0x12345678`, the first byte store writes `0xE0`, and the second writes `0x23`. Separate selector reads determine their destinations.

5. **Changing selectors.**  
   If the first two selector reads return 2 and 5, their destination addresses are `0x13` and `0x16`. Reusing the first selector for both stores is not equivalent.

6. **Third selector forwarded.**  
   If the third selector is 7 and result 072 is 3, the third byte store writes 12 at `0x18`. F-unknown-073 receives 7, not `0x18` or 12.

7. **Different final selector source.**  
   With result 072 equal to 3, the final selector read is at address 25, independently of byte-source base. The final stored byte is zero; its destination still depends on the byte read at 25.

8. **Unconditional absolute-address write.**

9. **Unsigned product width.**
