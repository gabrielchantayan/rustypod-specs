# F-b52d861b2020

## Purpose

F-b52d861b2020 performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, transforms dependency outputs, and then modifies memory through calculated addresses and fixed locations. Its last represented action is an unresolved indirect invocation supplied with four values.

No application-level purpose is established. Character-like constants and self-referential stores do not establish text formatting, object initialization, or conventional linkage maintenance.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented accesses, widths, arithmetic relationships, and ordering.
- **Inferred intent:** possible interpretations that impose no acceptance requirement.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior requiring dependency contracts, machine semantics, or environmental information.

All behavioral requirements are conditional on execution reaching the relevant operation and on the supplied representation accurately describing it. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Entry, inputs, and outputs

The represented interface has no explicit parameters and no explicit return value. Its implicit inputs include incoming control state, indirect-invocation results, auxiliary machine-state outputs, fixed-location memory, and storage reached through calculated addresses.

Observable outputs include the dependency-invocation trace, memory-access trace, and memory mutations. No success indicator, returned object, output-buffer contract, or error code is established.

A return is represented after the final invocation. Whether that invocation actually returns is documented_only.

## Behavior

### 1. Indirect-invocation phase

The initial phase consists of a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations interspersed with arithmetic. Many targets are represented as a location-dependent base plus 0x0ffe; the first additionally depends on incoming control state.

No explicit loop or conditional branch is represented in this phase. Progress remains conditional on each invocation returning. Dependencies may themselves branch, mutate global state, invalidate memory, fault, or fail to return.

Represented interactions include:

- Invocations without explicit arguments, including invocations whose ordinary results are unused.
- Forwarding a 32-bit result shifted left by eight.
- Splitting a combined result and forwarding transformed low and high portions.
- Supplying several shifted forms of the same result.
- Combining saved outputs with newer outputs.
- Passing literal zeros.
- Consuming auxiliary outputs whose interfaces are unresolved.

Unused ordinary results do not make their producing invocations optional. No purity or global-preservation guarantee is available.

The first combined-result consumer receives four values: the low portion shifted left by eight, the high portion, the high portion shifted left by five, and another copy of the shifted low portion. Later consumers use shifts including 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, and larger counts, together with masks and logical right shifts.

### 2. Arithmetic constraints

The order of masking, truncation, and shifting matters. Established unsigned transformations include:

| Transformation | Maximum result |
|---|---:|
| Low 24 bits, shifted left by six | 0x3fffffc0 |
| Low 22 bits, shifted left by eight | 0x3fffff00 |
| Low 22 bits, shifted left by seven | 0x1fffff80 |
| Low 20 bits, shifted right by ten, then left by six | 65472 |
| Low 24 bits, shifted right by eight | 65535 |
| Low 19 bits, shifted left by two | 0x001ffffc |

Shifts of unsigned combined results by 34, 36, and 44 operate on 64-bit values before conversion to 32 bits. They correspond to logical right shifts of the high portion by 2, 4, and 12, respectively.

Several saved-value chains begin with an explicit zero and subsequently shift that saved value. Those values remain zero in the represented data flow. This says nothing about globals modified by intervening dependencies.

### 3. Initial direct-memory effects

The following descriptive roles identify values, not source identifiers or object types:

- **Shifted base:** a saved dependency result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Encoded base:** a saved low result shifted left by 25.
- **Small index:** a producer result shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7fff, and shifted logically right by six.
- **Doubled-index base:** twice the small index.
- **Final pair:** the low and high portions of the last combined result before direct mutation.

Under the represented 32-bit extraction, the small index is a multiple of four, from 0 through 508. Its doubled base is a multiple of eight, from 0 through 1016.

The first mutation group stores the encoded base as a word at final-high-portion offset 0x14, writes the low byte of that high portion at encoded-base offset 9, and stores the shifted base at final-low-portion multiplied by 256 plus 0x54. The encoded-base byte at offset 9 is then overwritten with the encoded base’s own low byte.

The shifted-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared before reading its byte at offset 5. The doubled-index-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared before that captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at shifted-base offset 0x34.

A subsequent word read at shifted-base offset 0x14 observes storage at that later point. The doubled-index word is cleared again, followed by two byte stores of the captured word’s low byte at doubled-index offset 0x15. These are separate accesses, not an atomic field update.

### 4. Chased bases and overlapping mutations

The next region combines fixed-location accesses, pointer chasing, scaled bases, and repeated stores.

The byte at absolute address 5 is read and zero-extended for use as a base. Subsequent fixed-location effects include byte stores at addresses 1 and 0x15 and a word store at address 0x54.

Words loaded from computed offsets 0x54 and 0x74 become later bases. Other bases are derived by multiplication by two, four, or eight. A byte read at computed offset 5 can itself become the base for a word read at offset 0x74.

The region includes self-referential word stores at offsets 0x24 and 0x14, repeated byte stores at offsets 9 and 0x15, and word writes at offsets 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54. Repetition does not eliminate the earlier access from the represented trace.

Fresh reads must not be replaced by entry-time values. One represented store uses two reads of an offset-0x74 word: one supplies its destination base, the other its stored value. The representation does not establish an internal evaluation order between those two reads; their multiplicity and occurrence before the store are established.

No disjointness guarantee permits treating byte and word fields as independent. Later reads can reflect earlier stores through apparently unrelated bases.

### 5. Literal stores and repeated linkage

For the remainder of the contract:

- **Chased literal base** means the word obtained through the later byte-derived offset-0x74 lookup.
- **Literal word base** means four times that value.
- **Doubled chased base** means twice that value.
- **Adjusted base** means the earlier four-times-scaled base reduced by 0x96.
- **Calculated region** means four times the adjusted base.
- **Linkage base** means the word captured from the fixed location used by the repeated-linkage region.

These definitions retain the arithmetic caveats above.

The literal-store group writes 0x3a as a byte at the literal word base, as a halfword at eight times the chased literal base, and as a word at literal-word-base offset 0x20.

Calculated-region offset 0x20 then receives successive word stores of 0x20, 0x2f, a zero-extended byte freshly read from calculated-region offset 4, and 0x20 again.

**The linkage-base word is loaded after those stores and before the word store of 0x20 to absolute address 0x60.** The captured linkage base is used afterward; it is not reloaded following the absolute-address store. This read-before-write relationship applies even if the fixed source location aliases address 0x60.

The following straight-line region repeats an ordered four-store pattern: a word at scaled-base offset 0x54, a byte at linkage-base offset 0x19, a byte at calculated-region offset 0x11, and a word at linkage-base offset 0x34.

The first occurrence uses an earlier eight-times-scaled base. Later occurrences use eight times the adjusted base. The offset-0x54 word contains the literal word base; the two byte stores contain the low bytes of the calculated region and linkage base, respectively. The final word in each occurrence records the scaled base used by that occurrence.

Each represented occurrence remains a distinct group of accesses. This contract does not assign a numeric multiplicity to the expanded repetition.

### 6. Final captures and intervening writes

The final region does not capture all its inputs before writing memory. Its opening ordering is:

**word read at absolute address 0xa9 → word read at literal-word-base offset 0x74 → byte store at absolute address 0x3a → word read at doubled-chased-base offset 0x14 → word read at calculated-region offset 4.**

The byte stored at 0x3a is the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted base plus 0x30. Consequently, either of the last two captures can reflect that intervening byte store if its source overlaps address 0x3a. The first two captures precede it.

Those four captured words have different subsequent uses:

- The absolute-address-0xa9 capture supplies a later byte and a later full-word store.
- The literal-word-base offset-0x74 capture supplies later low-byte stores.
- The doubled-chased-base offset-0x14 capture supplies a restoration store and the third final argument.
- The calculated-region offset-4 capture supplies a later offset-0x74 store.

Before the fixed-byte copy region, the doubled chased base receives a byte at offset 0x0d derived from the calculated region. The calculated region receives 0x6c at offset 8 and linkage-base-derived bytes at offsets 0x0d and 9. Doubled-chased-base offset 0x54 receives that base itself as a word.

### 7. Separate fixed-byte reads and subsequent mutations

The low byte of the captured absolute-address-0xa9 word is stored at absolute address 0x67. A word is then read from doubled-chased-base offset 0x44. **Only afterward is address 0x67 read as a byte for the byte store at absolute address 0x21.** The fixed-byte read is a separate event, not merely forwarding the original captured byte.

The offset-0x44 word supplies a byte at calculated-region offset 0x10 and a word at linkage-base offset 0x54. A signed right shift of that word supplies the byte at doubled-chased-base offset 1. Its shift count is the calculated region’s low eight address bits. Counts above 31 and right shifts of negative values require target-specific semantics.

Linkage-base offset 0x54 is subsequently overwritten with the calculated region. A fresh word from calculated-region offset 0x40 is stored at calculated-region offset 0x10, after the earlier byte store there. Between that read and store, linkage-base offset 0x74 receives the previously captured calculated-region offset-4 word. Linkage-base offset 0x44 then receives the doubled chased base.

**Address 0x67 is read again for a byte store at calculated-region offset 9.** This read follows the intervening computed writes; it is not the byte previously copied to address 0x21.

Before a second such offset-9 store, three relevant events intervene, in order:

- Doubled-chased-base offset 9 receives the calculated region’s low byte.
- Calculated-region offset 0x44 receives the full captured absolute-address-0xa9 word.
- Calculated-region offset 0x54 is freshly read as a word.

**Address 0x67 is then read separately once more for the second calculated-region offset-9 store.** The two later reads can therefore differ. Computed destinations are not established disjoint from 0x67, and the intervening offset-0x54 read must retain its position between the two stores.

The captured offset-0x54 word becomes another base. It receives a byte at offset 0x0d from the earlier literal-word-base offset-0x74 capture. Doubled-chased-base offset 0x54 is restored from its captured offset-0x14 word.

A fresh calculated-region offset-0x14 word supplies a later byte at the newly loaded base’s offset 0x11. Additional stores place the earlier offset-0x74 capture’s low byte at doubled-chased-base offset 0x11, and the newly loaded base’s low byte and full word at calculated-region offsets 9 and 0x44.

### 8. Final linkage and transfer

A fresh word from doubled-chased-base offset 4 becomes a subsequent base. A halfword value 0x65 is stored at twice that base, before reading its word at offset 0x64.

The base previously loaded from calculated-region offset 0x54 receives the saved calculated-region offset-0x14 value’s low byte at offset 0x11, followed by a word value of decimal 100 at offset 0x54.

The subsequent base’s word at offset 0x74 is captured before a fresh word read at doubled-chased-base offset 0x64. Final stores write the subsequent base’s low byte at doubled-chased-base offset 9, write the earlier offset-0x64 capture through the later offset-0x64-derived base at offset 0x54, and store that later derived base as a word at twice the earlier capture.

The unresolved final invocation receives, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The word captured from the subsequent base at offset 0x64.
3. The earlier doubled-chased-base offset-0x14 capture.
4. The word captured from the subsequent base at offset 0x74.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional examples, not executed tests.

1. **Combined-result forwarding.** For an early combined result with low portion 0x12 and high portion 3, the identified consumer receives 0x1200, 3, 0x60, and 0x1200.

2. **Small-index boundary.** A relevant producer result of 127 yields a small index of 508 and doubled-index base of 1016. The left shift is within the nonnegative signed 32-bit range for this example.

3. **Unsigned byte extension.** A captured byte of 0xfe from shifted-base offset 5 produces word value 254 at shifted-base offset 0x34, provided preceding accesses complete.

4. **Linkage capture before fixed store.** If the linkage-base source aliases address 0x60, its captured value is the word read before the store of 0x20 there, not an automatic reload of 0x20.

5. **Interleaved final capture.** If doubled-chased-base offset 0x14 overlaps address 0x3a, its capture reflects the intervening byte store according to the target byte order. Moving that capture before the store is not equivalent.

6. **Three fixed-byte reads.** Suppose the read for address 0x21 obtains 0x12, the first later read for calculated-region offset 9 obtains 0x34, and the second obtains 0x56 because intervening aliased writes changed address 0x67. The respective stores use 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56—not three copies of the initial capture’s low byte.

7. **Transient literals.** An access-observing environment distinguishes the four calculated-region offset-0x20 word stores: 0x20, 0x2f, the freshly loaded byte value, and 0x20.

8. **Unresolved shift or fault.** A shift count of 40 has documented_only numerical behavior. An uncallable target or inaccessible address does not require successful completion, a specific error code, or rollback.
