# F-200d10639b83

## Purpose

F-200d10639b83 specifies a represented sequence of narrow memory stores, computed-target calls, and transformations of call results. It repeatedly derives a destination address and a 16-bit value from a represented 64-bit result, writes that value twice, and passes part of the result to another dependency.

No application-level purpose is established. Device initialization, hardware control, decoding, and reset behavior are possible interpretations, not contractual conclusions.

The available evidence is a static representation with warnings about invalid instruction data and overlapping global symbols. This document describes the operations expressed by that representation. It does not establish that the recovered boundary is a valid callable function, that every operation is executable, or that execution terminates normally.

### Width and arithmetic rules

Halfword stores are 16 bits. Word stores are 32 bits. For each represented 64-bit result, its low word comprises bits 0–31 and its high word comprises bits 32–63.

The following operations have explicitly unsigned behavior in the representation:

- Multiplication of W by sixteen produces a 32-bit unsigned result, retaining the low 32 bits.
- Shifting W left by eight produces a 32-bit unsigned result, retaining the low 32 bits.
- The low-word shifts and masks used to derive result-dependent destinations use unsigned 32-bit arithmetic.
- The shift of the full result used for one halfword value is unsigned and 64-bit.
- Narrowing an unsigned expression to a halfword retains its low 16 bits.

These rules do **not** establish modulo arithmetic for every address expression. In particular:

- P is converted to a signed 32-bit integer before doubling.
- B plus 0x14 is a signed addition.
- L plus 0xFFE is a signed addition.
- The unsigned product of W and sixteen is assigned to signed 32-bit storage before 0x14 is added.
- The unsigned value obtained by shifting W left by eight is assigned to signed 32-bit storage before later word stores.
- Extracted high result words are converted to signed 32-bit arguments.
- The final high-word adjustment is signed subtraction after that conversion.

Out-of-range conversions to signed storage and overflowing signed expressions are not established as wrapping. Where such a case occurs, the resulting address, argument, stored value, or continuation is **documented_only**. A low-32-bit machine interpretation may be considered conditionally, but is not an unconditional guarantee of this contract.

Integer-to-pointer conversion, executable-address interpretation, and target endianness are also unresolved. Numeric destinations below identify represented addresses, not proven accessible objects.

## Behavior

### Sequencing model

The supplied representation contains a fixed sequence without visible conditional branches, loops, or retries. Every later event is conditional on earlier accesses and calls completing sufficiently for execution to continue.

Repeated stores are separate represented operations. They are not interchangeable with a single store in this contract’s operation trace. Whether the execution environment exposes both stores externally is unresolved.

### Entry operations

Before invoking any dependency, the function performs these operations in order:

1. Stores the low 16 bits of P at P.
2. Repeats that same halfword store at P.
3. Stores the same value at the address formed by converting P to a signed 32-bit integer and doubling it.
4. Stores W as a 32-bit word at the address represented by signed B plus 0x14.
5. Invokes F-DEP-01 at the address represented by signed L plus 0xFFE, with no explicit arguments shown.

The initial value comes from P’s address representation, not from loading the contents at P.

The third store does not unconditionally target the low 32 bits of twice P. That interpretation requires an appropriate pointer conversion and arithmetic semantics. Even after a signed conversion has been established, doubling must remain within signed 32-bit range for ordinary signed arithmetic to determine the result.

Likewise, the fourth store and initial call do not acquire defined wrapping behavior merely because addresses are represented using 32 bits. For example, B greater than 0x7FFFFFEB or L greater than 0x7FFFF001 makes the respective positive signed addition exceed the signed 32-bit maximum.

F-DEP-01’s return value is not visibly consumed.

### Result-dependent halfword operations

Eleven subsequent dependencies produce represented 64-bit results. Each result is processed using one of two forms.

**Form A: byte-shifted low word**

The destination is the result’s low word shifted logically right by eight. The stored halfword is bits 8–23 of the result. Two consecutive stores write that value to that destination.

The destination ranges from zero through 0x00FFFFFF. Its low 16 bits equal the stored value. Although the value expression shifts a full 64-bit result, narrowing to 16 bits excludes any contribution from the high word.

**Form B: masked low word**

The destination is the result’s low word masked with 0x07FFFFFF and shifted logically right by three.

The stored value comes from shifting the unsigned low word left by five, retaining 32 bits, shifting right by eight, and retaining 16 bits. It therefore equals original low-word bits 3–18, and also equals the destination’s low 16 bits.

Two consecutive halfword stores write that value to that destination. The destination again ranges from zero through 0x00FFFFFF.

Neither form enforces even alignment. A zero or odd destination is possible. The high result word does not affect these halfword destinations or values.

### Form A

### Unsigned multiplication followed by safe signed addition

In contrast, W = 0x08000000 yields unsigned M = 0x80000000. Its assignment to signed storage is unresolved here; the contract does not require D = 0x80000014.
