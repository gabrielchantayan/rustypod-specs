# F-d350614e3326

## Purpose

F-d350614e3326 returns a 64-bit bit pattern derived from four explicit 32-bit words and two implicit values. Its established behavior comprises:

- Selecting transformations according to the signed interpretation of the fourth input.
- Combining retained or discarded input bits into a residual word.
- Invoking F-96fcc6b36b88 when that residual is nonzero.
- Conditionally adjusting an upper result word using the residual, an implicit mode, and the transformed input words.
- Assembling the result with bit 31 of the first input.

The residual inspection, parity check, and mode-dependent adjustment suggest rounding-related intent. That is an **inference**, not an established external numeric format or mathematical operation. In particular, the visible transformations must not be replaced with a conventional multiword shift or rounding algorithm: some control ranges explicitly discard input words.

All behavior below is established from static evidence unless identified as inferred or **documented_only**. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

| Position | Contract name | Role |
|---|---|---|
| First | Sign source | Supplies bit 31 for the final lower result word. Its other bits do not affect the visible computation. |

### Output and layout

## Behavior

### 1. Initialize the result state

The lower-result accumulator initially receives the control word unchanged.

For a nonnegative signed control:

- Both candidates remain unchanged.
- The incoming residual remains unchanged.
- The lower-result accumulator remains the control word.

Processing then proceeds to residual assessment.

For every negative control, the lower-result accumulator becomes zero. The residual and candidates are transformed according to the ranges below.

### 2. Define the residual fold

A **16-bit fold** combines the upper and lower 16-bit halves of a 32-bit word using bitwise OR. The combined value occupies bits 0–15; bits 16–31 are zero.

For example, folding `0x80000001` yields `0x00008001`.

Every negative-control path first folds the incoming residual. This preserves a pattern of bits, not merely a Boolean indication that discarded information was nonzero.

### 3. Transform negative-control inputs

References to original candidates in this subsection mean their values before the negative-control transformation.

#### Controls −1 through −31

Let the shift distance be the positive magnitude of the signed control, from 1 through 31.

The transformed state is:

- Residual: the folded incoming residual ORed with the original lower candidate shifted left by 32 minus the distance.
- Lower candidate: the original lower candidate shifted right by the distance, ORed with the original upper candidate shifted left by 32 minus the distance.
- Upper candidate: the original upper candidate shifted right by the distance.
- Lower-result accumulator: zero.

The representation folds the residual again before incorporating the shifted fragment. Because it has already been folded and not otherwise changed, this additional fold does not change its value.

The transformed lower candidate can equal `0xFFFFFFFF`: its two contributing fragments occupy complementary portions of the word. The transformed upper candidate cannot equal `0xFFFFFFFF`, because a logical right shift by at least one leaves at least one leading zero.

#### Control exactly −32

The residual becomes the folded incoming residual ORed with the original lower candidate.

Both candidates and the lower-result accumulator become zero. There is no further fold or variable shift.

The original upper candidate makes no contribution. In particular, this boundary does not transfer that word into the lower candidate.

#### Controls −33 through −63

The original lower candidate is ORed into the initially folded residual. Both candidates are cleared before the subsequent shifts.

The residual is then folded again. The shifts operate on the already-cleared candidates and therefore contribute no bits.

The resulting state is:

- Residual: the fold of the initially folded incoming residual ORed with the original lower candidate.
- Both candidates: zero.
- Lower-result accumulator: zero.

The original upper candidate makes no contribution.

#### Control exactly −64

The transformation:

1. Combines the initially folded incoming residual with the original lower candidate using OR.
2. Folds that combined value.
3. ORs the original upper candidate into the folded value.

Both candidates and the lower-result accumulator become zero.

There is no final fold after incorporating the original upper candidate. Consequently, any bit of that original word, including bit 31, can remain in the residual.

#### Controls below −64

The transformation matches the −64 case, followed by one additional fold after incorporating the original upper candidate.

Both candidates and the lower-result accumulator are zero. The final residual occupies only bits 0–15, so its bit 31 is necessarily clear.

This range includes the most negative signed 32-bit control. No magnitude-based variable shift is performed on that path.

### 4. Assess the residual

If the transformed residual is zero, the function skips the dependency and all adjustment logic, proceeding directly to return assembly.

Otherwise, it derives an adjustment indicator:

| Transformed residual | Indicator |
|---|---|
| At least one of bits 0–30 is set | Residual bit 31 |
| Exactly `0x80000000` | Current lower candidate’s bit 0 |

The high-bit-only case is therefore distinct from a residual having both bit 31 and any lower bit set. In that special case, parity of the transformed lower candidate determines the indicator.

The dependency is invoked for every nonzero residual, even when the indicator is false or adjustment is impossible for another reason.

### 5. Invoke the dependency and inspect the mode

F-96fcc6b36b88 is invoked once with two explicit integer arguments, both numerically equal to 16. No explicit return value is consumed.

After the call returns, the mode determines adjustment permission:

| Mode at comparison | Permission |
|---|---|
| Negative one | Denied |
| Zero | Equal to the residual-derived indicator |
| Any other value | Granted |

The supplied material does not establish names or external semantics for these mode values.

The residual-derived indicator is computed before the call. The mode comparison occurs afterward. No claim is made that the dependency preserves globals, the mode, or other unspecified environmental state.

### 6. Adjust the result words

Adjustment requires both:

- Permission from the mode rule.
- A current lower candidate equal to `0xFFFFFFFF`.

If either condition is absent, this stage leaves both result words unchanged.

When both conditions hold, the upper candidate is incremented by one. If its value before incrementing was also `0xFFFFFFFF`, the function replaces the ordinary wrapped result with `0x80000000` and increments the lower-result accumulator by one.

The lower candidate is never explicitly incremented. It gates adjustment of the upper candidate and is not independently returned.

The control ranges impose distinct limits:

- **Controls −1 through −31:** adjustment is possible because the transformed lower candidate can be all ones. Overflow replacement and its associated lower-result increment are impossible because the shifted upper candidate cannot be all ones.
- **Controls at most −32:** adjustment is impossible because the lower candidate has been cleared.
- **Nonnegative controls:** both ordinary adjustment and overflow replacement are possible, depending on the unchanged candidates, residual, and post-call mode.

Thus negative control does not, by itself, prohibit upper-word adjustment.

### 7. Assemble the result

The final upper candidate becomes the upper 32-bit word.

The lower word is the final lower-result accumulator ORed with `0x80000000` when the sign source has bit 31 set. Otherwise, that OR contributes zero.

This is an OR operation, not replacement of an existing bit. No external interpretation of that bit as a numeric sign is established.

For negative controls, the lower-result accumulator remains zero: controls −1 through −31 cannot reach overflow replacement, and controls at most −32 cannot adjust at all.

For nonnegative controls, the accumulator is either the original control or that value plus one in the overflow-replacement case. Since a nonnegative signed control is at most `0x7FFFFFFF`, that increment does not wrap the 32-bit accumulator, though it can set bit 31.

- Upper candidate: `0x7FFFFFFF`
- Lower candidate: `0xFFFFFFFF`
- Residual: `0x80000000`

- The dependency is invoked.

- Control −65 produces residual `0x00008000`.
