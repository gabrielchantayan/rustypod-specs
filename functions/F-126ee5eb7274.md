# F-126ee5eb7274

## Purpose

F-126ee5eb7274 invokes an extensive ordered sequence of indirect dependencies, transforms their results, and performs mixed-width memory accesses through dependency-derived addresses, loaded words, and fixed storage. Its last represented external action is an unresolved indirect transfer with four arguments.

No domain-level purpose is established. In particular, the evidence does not establish text formatting, object initialization, collection maintenance, or a conventional numeric algorithm. Character-like constants do not establish a string interface.

This contract distinguishes three evidence levels:

- **Established:** directly supported by the supplied static representation.
- **Conditional interpretation:** consequences that hold under an explicitly stated arithmetic or memory model.
- **documented_only:** semantics, prerequisites, or trace details not resolved by this contract.

The contract preserves observation times and intermediate writes. It does not reduce the operation to its apparent final memory contents.

## Behavior

All represented indirect dependency invocations preceding the memory-mutation phase occur in their displayed order. Results are retained, transformed, and reused across later invocations.

The opening includes three invocations with no displayed arguments, followed by one receiving two copies of the fourth input shifted left by seven bits. Other early argument constructions include:

- Zero paired with the third input shifted left by 24 bits.
- A dependency result shifted left by eight bits paired with the third input’s low byte shifted left by five bits.

No displayed arguments does not mean no inputs. Zero-valued or duplicated arguments do not make an invocation removable.

The phase consumes 32-bit results, paired results represented in 64-bit containers, and secondary result-channel values. Paired results are separated into low and high 32-bit words. Later arguments include shifts, logical right shifts, masked fields, duplicated values, and explicit zeros. Established field operations include selecting low-width portions and extracting bits 10 through 19. Some extractions shift an unsigned 64-bit result right by 34, 36, or 44 bits before narrowing.

No explicit loop or conditional branch is represented in the body. Dependencies may nevertheless contain arbitrary control flow and side effects.

### Arithmetic model

The representation primarily uses 32-bit quantities, including address-bearing values, with selected 64-bit result containers.

Under a **32-bit bitvector interpretation**, arithmetic retains the low 32 bits. Consequently:

- A left shift by 24 depends only on the original low byte.
- Multiplication by 256 and a left shift by eight have the same low-word result.
- Successive left shifts by 25 and seven produce zero.
- Successive left shifts by 28 and 28 produce zero.
- Multiplication by \(2^{25}\) retains only the original low seven bits.

These are conditional width consequences, not proof of portable signed-arithmetic semantics. Signed left shifts, signed multiplication, address overflow, and signed right shifts require target semantics not supplied here.

One terminal right shift has a data-dependent count formed from the low byte of an address-derived word. Its represented count ranges from 0 through 255, not merely 0 through 31. Masking, saturation, or another oversized-count rule must not be assumed.

### Initial explicit memory accesses

The first mutation stores a retained transformed word at offset 0x14 from the high word of the last paired dependency result. A byte publication at a previously derived base’s offset 9 follows. A word publication then targets offset 0x54 from the last result’s low word multiplied by 256. The offset-9 byte destination is subsequently overwritten with another retained low byte.

These are separate ordered writes, even where the later write appears to supersede the earlier one.

A zero word is next stored at offset 0x14 from another scaled base. A byte is read at that base’s offset 5. A small-index-derived destination then receives a zero word at offset 0x14, and the previously read byte is widened without sign extension and stored as a word at the scaled base’s offset 0x34.

The following offset-0x14 readback occurs after these intervening accesses. It is not unconditionally equivalent to the earlier zero. Aliasing between derived destinations is unresolved.

After another zero-word publication and duplicate byte publications at an index-derived offset 0x15, execution reads the byte at fixed address **0x05**. Only afterward does it write the low byte of the doubled index to fixed address **0x01**. The retained value from 0x05 becomes a base for later accesses; changing 0x05 afterward does not refresh that retained value.

### Derived memory and early fixed-storage ordering

Further accesses use loaded words as bases and combine multiplication by two, four, or eight with offsets. Established patterns include:

- Byte accesses at offsets 1, 4, 5, 9, 0x0d, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19.
- Word accesses at offsets 4, 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x40, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.
- Self-referential word publications.
- Repeated byte and word writes, including consecutive identical writes.
- A byte loaded at offset 5 being used as an address component for a later word read.

These offsets establish access locations relative to particular values, not structure layouts or valid object boundaries.

For the remaining description, **retained root** means the word captured from offset 0x54 of the base supplied by the fixed 0x05 byte. Its capture precedes its source destination being overwritten.

A word equal to the retained root shifted left by one is written to fixed address **0x54**. This precedes the later byte write to fixed address **0x15**. Between these fixed writes are additional derived publications, including repeated offset-0x14 word writes and duplicate offset-0x15 byte writes. They cannot be removed from the ordering on the assumption that they do not alias fixed storage.

Later address construction uses four times the retained root, subtracts 0x96, and scales the adjusted value again. Literal publications include:

- An 8-bit 0x3a at a four-times-scaled address.
- A 16-bit 0x3a at the corresponding eight-times-scaled address.
- A 32-bit 0x3a at offset 0x20 from the four-times-scaled address.

At another offset-0x20 destination, the word publications are 0x20, then 0x2f, then a widened byte read from that base’s offset 4, then 0x20 again. The byte observation follows the first two publications.

After the repeated publications, observation order is:

1. Capture the fixed 32-bit word at **0xa9**.
2. Capture the word at **element-base offset 0x74**.
3. Write an arithmetic byte to fixed address **0x3a**.
4. Capture the word at **chain-base offset 0x14**.

The arithmetic byte is the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30. This describes its stored bit pattern, not a decimal-digit conversion.

The 0x3a write precedes both later captures. It may alias either source. In particular, the chain-base offset-0x14 capture eventually supplies the terminal transfer’s third argument; it is not a pre-0x3a snapshot.

Subsequent byte publications place the working base’s retained low byte at chain-base offset 0x0d, literal 0x6c at working-base offset 8, and the publication base’s retained low byte at working-base offsets 0x0d and 9. The chain base is published as a word at its own offset 0x54.

### Terminal publication, shift, and overwrite

The word captured from chain-base offset 0x44 supplies a byte at working-base offset 0x10 and the **first terminal word publication at publication-base offset 0x54**.

Next, that captured word undergoes the data-dependent signed right shift. The count is the working base’s low byte, and the result’s low byte is stored at chain-base offset 1.

Only after this shift-byte publication is publication-base offset 0x54 overwritten with the working-base word. Only afterward is the word at working-base offset 0x40 loaded.

The captured working-base offset-4 word is then published at publication-base offset 0x74, followed by publication of the offset-0x40 load at working-base offset 0x10. Thus the earlier byte write and later word write at offset 0x10 are distinct events. The offset-0x40 read and offset-0x10 word write also have an intervening publication.

This ordering is alias-sensitive. Neither the shift-byte store nor the later load may be moved across the intervening word overwrite without additional evidence.

The retained chain-base offset-0x14 word is restored at chain-base offset 0x54 before a fresh working-base offset-0x14 capture.

## Side effects

Byte stores retain eight bits; halfword stores retain 16 bits; word stores retain 32 bits. Byte-to-word publications described as widened are zero-extended. No explicit 64-bit data-memory access is established merely by the 64-bit dependency-result containers.

Fixed-storage effects include byte writes at 0x01, 0x15, 0x21, 0x3a, and 0x67, and word writes at 0x54 and 0x60. Meaningful fixed reads include the byte at 0x05, the publication-base word, the word at 0xa9, and three separate observations of 0x67.

Endianness, alignment, volatility, atomicity, and concurrency behavior are unresolved. The word read at 0xa9 is not naturally four-byte aligned; an alignment fault is a risk, not an established outcome.

There is no non-aliasing guarantee. Fixed storage may overlap derived accesses. Intermediate writes and repeated reads therefore remain part of the behavioral contract.

No allocation, release, or ownership-transfer primitive is identified. Dependency calls must not be assumed to preserve globals or to acquire, own, or release memory.

No explicit error code, rollback, recovery, or all-or-nothing update is represented. Failure or non-return at any reached boundary leaves subsequent behavior unestablished.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Input-byte equivalence.** Under 32-bit bitvector arithmetic, third-input values 0x000000ab and 0x123400ab yield 0xab000000 and 0x1560 for the two identified low-byte-derived constructions. Whole-execution equivalence does not follow.

2. **Duplicated scaling.** Fourth input 1 produces two displayed arguments of 128 at the early duplicated-argument boundary.

3. **Unsigned widening.** An initial offset-5 observation of 0xfe produces the identified widened word value 254, not a negative value.

4. **Early fixed ordering.** An access observer must encounter the 0x05 byte read before the 0x01 byte write, and the fixed 0x54 word write before the fixed 0x15 byte write.

5. **Retained publication base.** If the 0x60 write changes the publication-base source storage, repeated publications still use the previously captured base.

6. **Post-mutation capture.** If the 0x3a byte write overlaps chain-base offset 0x14, that write precedes the capture eventually used as the terminal third argument. The resulting word depends on the memory model and other contributing bytes.

7. **Shift ordering.** A trace reaching the terminal overwrite region must place the shift-byte store after the first publication-base offset-0x54 word publication and before its working-base overwrite. The working-base offset-0x40 read follows the overwrite.

8. **Fresh fixed-byte observations.** If intervening writes change 0x67, later working-base offset-9 copies use their respective fresh observations, not an automatically retained original byte.

9. **Halfword literal.** The 0x65 publication updates 16 bits. A one-byte character append is not equivalent.

10. **Non-returning dependency.** If an early invocation does not return, later mutations are not guaranteed. No synthetic success or rollback is specified.
