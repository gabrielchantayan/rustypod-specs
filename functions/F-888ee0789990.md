# F-888ee0789990

## Purpose

F-888ee0789990 executes an extended sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms selected results into fields and address-like values, performs ordered mixed-width memory mutations, and ends with an unresolved four-argument indirect handoff.

No domain-level purpose is established. Printable constants and recurring link offsets do not establish formatting, serialization, initialization, allocation, or a conventional object model.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented operations, widths, ordering, and data dependencies.
- **Inferred intent:** possible interpretations that are not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior that cannot be resolved from the supplied material.

The contract is conditional on the supplied static representation accurately describing execution. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven phase

An explicitly ordered chain of indirect invocations precedes the direct mutation phase. Most targets are formed from code-location references plus a displacement of 4,094 address units; the first instead depends on incoming control-flow state.

The visible chain contains no explicit conditional branch, loop, status-based recovery, or early-return check. Dependencies themselves remain unconstrained.

Absent explicit argument expressions do not establish a zero-argument ABI. Selected invocations receive shifted results, secondary results, explicit zeros, or combinations of these. Transformations include:

- Frequent left shifts by eight, with other represented shifts reaching 31.
- Separation of 64-bit results into low and high 32-bit halves.
- Masking and extraction of fields of several widths.

Invocations must not be eliminated or reordered merely because results appear unused or arguments are zero. Unresolved dependencies may modify shared memory, perform I/O, or communicate through implicit state.

### Initial writes and owner acquisition

The mutation phase first distributes dependency results:

- A word at offset 20 of a dependency-provided high-half base receives the packed result base.
- The packed result base’s offset-9 byte receives the low byte of another high-half result.
- A word at offset 84 of a separately scaled dependency-result base receives the shifted result base.
- The packed result base’s offset-9 byte is written again, now with the low byte of the packed result base.
- The shifted result base’s offset-20 word is cleared.

The offset-5 byte of the shifted result base is then read unsigned. After clearing the small base’s offset-20 word, the saved byte is widened to a word and stored at offset 52 of the shifted result base.

A subsequent read of the shifted result base’s offset-20 word is not unconditionally zero: the intervening small-base clear or widened store may alias it. After that read, the small base’s offset-20 word is cleared again, and its offset-21 byte receives the low byte of the saved read result twice.

The following shared-storage ordering is established:

1. The shared byte at address 0x05 is read unsigned and retained as an address base.
2. The shared byte at address 0x01 receives the low eight bits of the small base.
3. Offset 21 of the retained byte-valued base receives the low byte of the small field.

The write to address 0x01 does not cause the already retained address base to be reread.

Next, offset 17 of the shifted result base receives the low byte of the saved shifted value. Offset 84 of the base obtained from the earlier offset-20 read receives the full saved shifted value. The shifted result base’s offset-17 byte is then cleared.

Only afterward is the loaded owner read from offset 84 of the retained byte-valued base; that location is then replaced with the earlier offset-20 read result. These accesses are alias-sensitive.

### Owner-linked and direct shared-storage changes

The small base’s offset-52 word receives the small field. Offset-116 links are read from the shifted result base and the small base, and linked offset-84 fields are updated. The small-base link location appears in two reads within one represented assignment; it is not established as a permanently cached link.

The loaded owner is stored into its own offset-36 word twice, interleaved with writes to a linked base’s offset-9 byte and offset-84 word.

The direct shared word at address 0x54 has a specific position in this sequence:

- The small base’s offset-20 word receives the earlier dependency result shifted left by 26.
- The shared word at address 0x54 receives the loaded owner shifted left once, retaining 32 bits.
- The small base’s offset-20 word receives the loaded owner twice.

The small base’s offset-21 byte then receives the low byte of the owner-scaled base twice. **After those two byte writes**, the shared byte at address 0x15 receives the low eight bits of the small base. Subsequent operations write the saved shifted value to offset 84 of the owner-scaled base and install scaled-owner values in other fields.

These fixed shared writes are not merely annotations on independent globals. Calculated destinations can overlap them, and they can affect later link and byte reads.

The remaining linked-storage portion establishes:

- Self-references in offset-20 and offset-84 fields.
- Owner-derived values scaled by two, four, or eight.

The workspace offset-100 result’s offset-84 word receives the saved downstream offset-100 value. ## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

### Field boundaries and narrowing

With otherwise identical relevant state, third arguments 0 and 1,023 produce zero for the identified early argument; 1,024 produces 32; 1,048,575 produces 32,736. Changing bits 20–31 does not change that calculation.

A small-field dependency result of 127 produces field 508 and small base 1,016. Result 128 produces zero, demonstrating truncation rather than saturation.

An unsigned byte read of 255 widens to word value 255, not a sign-extended negative value.

### Direct shared effects

For small base 1,016, both shared-byte writes derived directly from that base—at addresses 0x01 and 0x15—supply 248, at their separately specified points.

For loaded owner 0x80000001, the shared word at address 0x54 receives bit pattern 2. Acceptance requires its position between the specified small-base offset-20 writes, not merely its eventual value.

### Read-before-write anchor acquisition

If the anchor slot yields a particular value when read, publication uses that saved value after the shared word at address 0x60 receives 32. Acceptance does not permit delaying acquisition until after that write.

### Final boundary aliasing

For adjusted base value 31, the shared byte at address 0x3a receives 40. If either subsequent saved word spans that byte, its read must reflect the resulting bytes according to the environment’s endianness.

The earlier shared-word and published-link offset-116 reads remain earlier observations; they are not refreshed by this write.

### Fresh shared-byte observations

Suppose address 0x67 initially receives 0x12, but an intervening calculated write changes it to 0x34 before the first late calculated-base offset-9 assignment. That assignment must read 0x34.

If a later intervening write changes address 0x67 to 0x56 before the second assignment, that assignment must read 0x56. Unconditionally supplying 0x12 to both is not equivalent.

### Intermediate effects and handoff

For observable calculated-base offset-32 storage, acceptance includes writes of 32, 47, the widened byte, and 32 in order.

If F-UNRESOLVED-03 is reached, its first represented argument is 114; the remaining arguments use the specified saved reads rather than recomputed links.
