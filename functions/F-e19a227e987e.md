# F-e19a227e987e

## Purpose

The supplied static representation describes a procedure that interleaves indirect calls with five 32-bit stores, two byte stores, one 32-bit read, and two byte reads. Selected call results determine stored values, access addresses, and arguments to later calls. The procedure also extracts portions of a captured word and a later call result.

No application-level purpose is established. Interpreting the procedure as serialization, allocation, device access, or object initialization would exceed the evidence.

The representation reports invalid instruction data and truncated control flow. This contract therefore describes the represented behavior, conditional on execution reaching each operation and preceding calls returning. It does not establish that the representation faithfully reconstructs executable behavior.

### Initial write and first result

F-U01 receives P. F-U02, F-U03, and F-U04 follow, with no explicit arguments shown. F-U05 then supplies A.

### First scaled store and word capture

After F-U05 returns, the procedure stores S4(A) as a 32-bit word at S16(A) + 20. It then reads Q from S4(A) + 20.

These are different address formulas. The read is not generally a readback of the preceding store. If the calculated addresses coincide, the earlier store precedes the read; otherwise, Q comes from independently addressed memory.

F-U06 receives S4(A). F-U07 through F-U09 follow without explicit arguments, and F-U10 supplies B.

### Captured-word extraction and second scaled value

The procedure stores Q logically shifted right by 12 as a 32-bit word at S4(B) + 20. It uses the previously captured Q; no reread of Q’s original address is represented.

F-U11 receives S4(B). F-U12 through F-U14 follow, and F-U15 supplies C. The next direct store writes S4(C) as a 32-bit word at S16(C) + 20.

F-U16 then receives three explicit arguments:

| Position | Value |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The low six bits of S4(B), shifted left by 14. |
| 2 | S4(C). |
| 3 | B shifted left by 30, interpreted conditionally under the 32-bit model. |

The first argument is formed from the already scaled value S4(B), not from B’s low six bits before scaling. Under the conditional model, only bits 16 through 19 of this argument can be set: the low two bits of S4(B) are zero. The third argument depends only on B’s low two bits under that model.

### Propagation of the retained scaled value

F-U17 through F-U19 follow F-U16, then F-U20 supplies D.

The procedure stores the previously calculated S4(C) at S4(D) + 20 as a 32-bit word. The stored value is not derived from D; D determines the destination.

F-U21 receives S4(D). F-U22 through F-U24 follow, and F-U25 supplies E.

### First byte lookup and write

**R = W(B × 2²⁸ + 1)**

under the conditional 32-bit model.

The first lookup reads an unsigned byte J₁ from R. The procedure then writes the low byte of S4(E) at J₁ + 17.

J₁ is used as an unsigned numeric address component. It is not established to be a pointer or an offset within a known object. Its derived destination lies from 17 through 272 inclusive. The byte written has its low two bits clear.

F-U26 receives S4(E), S4(E) again, and W(E shifted left by four). Its target expression uses a displacement of 4076, unlike the 4094 displacement used by the other represented target expressions.

F-U27 supplies F, which is passed unchanged as the explicit argument to F-U28. F-U29 through F-U31 follow, then F-U32 supplies G.

### Second byte lookup and write

After F-U32, the procedure reads a fresh unsigned byte J₂ from R. It does not reuse J₁.

At J₂ + 17, it stores the low byte of:

**(E AND 0x3fffffff), logically shifted right by 20.**

The resulting stored byte consists of E’s bits 20 through 27. Bits 28 and 29 survive the mask and shift but are discarded by the final byte conversion.

F-U33 receives W(G shifted left by two). F-U34 through F-U36 follow, and F-U37 supplies I.

F-U38 receives W(I shifted left by two) and the zero-extended J₂. Its second argument is the value captured by the second lookup, not a third read from R after the intervening calls.

F-U39 follows without explicit arguments. The representation then terminates at its invalid-instruction/truncation marker.

### Numeric address relationships and aliasing

There is no general non-aliasing guarantee for the word accesses or dependency effects. Word stores may affect subsequently read memory when their addressed regions overlap.

However, the first byte-store destination **cannot numerically equal R under the conditional 32-bit model**:

- R has its low 28 bits equal to 1.
- Equivalently, R is 1 + k × 2²⁸ for some integer k from 0 through 15.
- J₁ + 17 lies between 17 and 272.

None of these possible byte-store destinations equals any possible R. The same numeric distinction holds for J₂ + 17.

Distinct numeric addresses might refer to shared underlying storage through an unusual or changing memory mapping, but such aliasing is unestablished. It is not evidence of numeric equality and is not required by this contract.

The two byte destinations may equal each other because J₁ and J₂ may be equal. If both stores execute at the same destination, the second direct store is later. It determines that byte’s value immediately after that store, subject to external or concurrent effects.

Dependencies between the two lookups may modify memory at R. Consequently, the numeric separation above does not justify reusing J₁ in place of J₂.

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests. They assume permitted accesses, returning dependencies, and the conditional 32-bit model where applicable.

- S4(C) as its second argument;
- 0xC0000000 as its third argument under the conditional 32-bit model.

Taking B’s low six bits before scaling would produce a different first argument and would not satisfy the represented calculation.

### Fresh byte lookup

With E = 0x12345679, J₁ = 40, and J₂ = 41:

- The first byte store writes 0xE4 at address 57.
- The second byte store writes 0x23 at address 58.
- F-U38 receives 41 as its second explicit argument.

Changes to R after the second lookup do not change that retained second argument in the represented behavior.

### Same destination

With the same E and both lookup results equal to 40, both byte stores target address 57. The later direct store writes 0x23 after the earlier 0xE4. The byte immediately after the second store is 0x23 absent interfering effects.

### Lookup address versus byte destination

With B = 19, R is 0x30000001 under the conditional model. Every possible byte-store destination remains between 17 and 272, so neither byte store numerically targets R.

With B = 0, R is 1, which also lies outside that destination range. More generally, R’s fixed low 28 bits exclude numeric equality for every B.

### Dependency modification between reads

If a dependency between the lookups changes the byte at R from 40 to 41, the procedure uses 41 for the second destination calculation and F-U38’s second argument. Preservation of the first lookup value is not an acceptance condition.
