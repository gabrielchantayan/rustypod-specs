# F-99a258675c9c

## Purpose

F-99a258675c9c conditionally performs a byte modification, submits staged memory contents to opaque dependencies, and publishes six records. Five publications use a captured destination; one uses an area within secondary storage.

The contract concerns observable accesses and their ordering, not merely the final memory image. Replacing intermediate publications with a single final write would omit established behavior.

Admission depends on three separate observations of condition state. Rejected paths and the terminal admitted path reach boundaries whose runtime consequences are unresolved. No ordinary success return, transaction, rollback, or ownership transfer is established.

The requirements below are conditional on execution reaching the relevant operation and on preceding dependencies allowing continuation. They describe static evidence only. **documented_only** identifies behavior that cannot be resolved from the supplied material.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes that the required accesses and interactions remain usable.

### Admission must remain distinguishable

A zero first observation requires F-DEP-02. A nonzero first observation followed by a zero second observation requires F-DEP-15 instead. A zero third observation reaches its unresolved boundary without another dependency shown.

None admits the direct byte modification or record work. Replacing all three alternatives with a common returned error would not preserve the established behavior.

### Overlapping byte modification affects consumption

Suppose the byte-store address falls within primary offset 0 and the payload is 0xA7. If intervening activity neither replaces that byte nor redirects the primary region, F-DEP-05 receives a field containing the modified byte.

The complete field value depends on byte order and the other bytes. It need not equal the number 0xA7.

### Staged reads must observe persistent changes

If F-DEP-06 replaces primary offset 20, and that replacement persists at the address subsequently used, F-DEP-07 receives the replacement.

Preloading every region field before the dependency sequence would violate this requirement. The example does not assume that the primary address is preserved.

### Conversion examples retain their limits

For a represented result of 0x0000000012345678, F-DEP-10 receives 0x12345678 and zero as its first two arguments.

For 0x1122334455667788, a bit-truncating narrowing convention gives 0x55667788 followed by 0x11223344. The first value is conditional: the full 64-bit value exceeds the signed 32-bit range even though its low word does not.

An upper word of 0x80000000 also requires the unresolved signed-conversion convention for the second argument.

### Source replacement does not recapture operands

Suppose the captured payloads are 0x10203040 and 0x50607080. Replacing their source fields afterward does not, by itself, alter later record payloads. Replacing the destination-bearing source field does not, by itself, redirect later captured-destination publications.

This does not guarantee that the captured address remains valid or undisturbed.

### Publication history matters despite overlap

If execution stops immediately after a record’s leading-word store, only that field has been written by that publication. Without other interference, its remaining fields retain their preceding contents.

If the captured destination equals the secondary address used for publication 5 plus 60, publications 5 and 6 use the same fields. Publication 6 replaces the leading word and rewrites the captured operands; publication 5 remains a required earlier event.

For disjoint publication areas undisturbed by dependencies, immediately after publication 6 the captured destination has the F-DEP-14-associated leading word, while the secondary record has the F-DEP-13-associated leading word. Persistence through terminal activity is unresolved.

### Arithmetic boundaries are not validity checks

A byte-address base of 2,147,483,636 produces the representable integer 2,147,483,647. The next larger base exceeds the established addition domain.

A target-selection value of 2,147,479,639 similarly reaches the signed maximum after adding 4,008. Neither calculation proves that its result denotes usable storage or a callable target.
