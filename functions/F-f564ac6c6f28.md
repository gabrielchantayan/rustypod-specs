### Initial memory effects and the zero reread

At the boundary, the shifted-base word is stored at offset 0x14 from the terminal pair’s upper word. A byte derived from that upper word is written at shifted-base offset 9. A word containing the primary base is then stored at the terminal pair’s lower word scaled by 256, plus 0x54. The byte at shifted-base offset 9 is overwritten with the shifted base’s low byte, which is zero under the fixed-width model.

The next group clears the primary-base word at offset 0x14 and reads a byte from primary-base offset 5. Before rereading the cleared word, the represented writes are:

- A zero word at doubled-selector offset 0x14.
- The captured byte, zero-extended to a word, at primary-base offset 0x34.

**Under ordinary memory semantics without external mutation, the reread at primary-base offset 0x14 is zero.** The selector-based store can only write zero, even if it overlaps the cleared word. The primary-base store at offset 0x34 cannot overlap the four-byte word at offset 0x14: their starting addresses differ by 0x20 modulo \(2^{32}\).

Thus, this sequence does not demonstrate an intervening nonzero aliasing write. Device-specific read behavior, concurrent modification, or inaccuracies in the representation could invalidate ordinary-memory reasoning, but those are separate **documented_only** uncertainties.

The reread is followed by another selector-based zero-word store and two consecutive byte stores of the reread’s low byte at doubled-selector offset 0x15.
