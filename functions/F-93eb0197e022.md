# F-93eb0197e022

## Purpose

F-93eb0197e022 coordinates preparation of text in storage associated with a caller-supplied object, then selects between two outcomes using a returned 32-bit quantity and two object fields:

- Invoke a transfer dependency and adjust the object’s current-position field.
- Skip that transfer and adjust a separate accumulation field.

The visible routine establishes the call sequence, address calculations, branch predicate, and final field updates. It does not establish the text produced, the interpretation of the two accumulation fields, or the safety properties of the dependencies.

**Inferred intent:** the object appears to contain an output destination, capacity and position accounting, and inline temporary storage. The separate accumulation field may account for output not transferred. These interpretations are not established field types or dependency contracts.

### 4. Evaluate the branch predicate

After F-1cb585febd84 returns, the routine reads the words at offsets 8 and 12 and subtracts the offset-12 value from the offset-8 value.

The comparison is:

- Left side: the reported quantity plus one, interpreted through unsigned 32-bit arithmetic.
- Right side: the field difference converted to unsigned 32-bit.
- Relation: strict less-than.

Consequences:

- Equality selects the non-transfer branch.
- For ordinary nonnegative values without arithmetic overflow, transfer requires remaining space strictly greater than the reported quantity plus one.
- The condition is therefore stricter than merely requiring space for the reported quantity and one additional unit.
- A negative reported quantity is not automatically rejected.
- A negative field difference is not automatically treated as zero available space.

The offset-8 and offset-12 reads both occur after the preceding dependency returns. Their relative evaluation order is not established by the supplied expression.

The subtraction is presented as signed integer arithmetic before conversion to unsigned. Behavior when that subtraction exceeds the signed 32-bit range is **documented_only** pending target-level evidence. The explicit unsigned addition on the left has modulo-\(2^{32}\) behavior.

### 5. Transfer branch

If the predicate is true, the routine:

1. Reads the destination-base word at offset 4 and the displacement word at offset 12.
2. Forms a destination address by adding those values.
3. Calls F-93c705790509 with that destination and the address at object offset 21.
4. After the dependency returns, reads the word at offset 12 again.
5. Adds the reported quantity to that newly read value.
6. Stores the result at offset 12.
7. Returns.

No explicit length is passed to F-93c705790509. The reported quantity is used for accounting but is not supplied to that dependency. Consequently, the visible routine does not establish that the dependency transfers exactly that many bytes.

The post-call read is important: the update uses the offset-12 value present **after** F-93c705790509 returns, not a preserved value from the predicate or destination calculation. If the dependency changes that field, the final update builds on the changed value.

The routine does not directly update offset 16 on this branch. This does not imply that dependencies leave offset 16 unchanged.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance examples, not executed tests. Unless stated otherwise, assume preceding dependencies return normally, required memory is valid, relevant field values remain as described until their indicated reads, and arithmetic stays within the signed range.

### Ordinary transfer

Suppose the reported quantity is 6 and the field difference is 8.

The unsigned comparison is 7 against 8, so the transfer dependency is called. If offset 12 contains 20 when the destination is calculated, the destination is the offset-4 address plus 20. If offset 12 is still 20 after that call returns, it becomes 26.

The routine makes no direct offset-16 update.

### Equality selects non-transfer

Suppose the reported quantity is 7 and the field difference is 8.

The comparison is 8 against 8, which fails. No transfer call occurs. If offset 16 was 10 at its update read, it becomes 17.

### Zero quantity still requires space

With a reported quantity of zero:

- A field difference of 1 selects non-transfer because 1 is not less than 1.
- A field difference of 2 selects transfer because 1 is less than 2.

On either branch, adding zero leaves the selected accounting word numerically unchanged. The dependency call trace still distinguishes the outcomes.

### Dependency mutation affects accounting

Suppose the transfer predicate succeeds with reported quantity 6. The destination is calculated using an offset-12 value of 20, but F-93c705790509 changes that field to 100 before returning.

The routine then stores 106 at offset 12. It does not restore 20 or store 26.

### Negative reported quantity

Suppose the reported quantity is −1 and the field difference is 8.

The unsigned left side is zero, so the predicate succeeds. After the transfer call, the routine adds −1 to the newly read offset-12 value. If that value is 20, the final value is 19.

This demonstrates the absence of a negative-result rejection; it does not establish that −1 is a valid dependency result.

### Negative remaining-space difference

Suppose the reported quantity is 6 and the signed field difference is −1, with the subtraction itself representable.

Conversion makes the right side \(2^{32}-1\). The left side is 7, so transfer is selected. A replacement that clamps negative remaining space to zero would change the established behavior.
