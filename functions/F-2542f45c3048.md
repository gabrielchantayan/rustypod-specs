### Widths and arithmetic

The represented access widths are 8, 16, and 32 bits. Some dependency results are represented as 64-bit aggregates, with individual 32-bit components subsequently used.

Packed transformations retain fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 20, 19, 18, 17, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. Examples with established numeric bounds include:

- Retaining 20 bits and logically shifting right by ten: at most 1023.
- Retaining 24 bits and logically shifting right by twenty: at most 15.
- Retaining 18 bits and logically shifting right by eleven: at most 127.

These operations are not unbounded integer arithmetic. Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, left shifts and multiplication retain only the low 32 result bits. A subsequent right shift does not recover discarded bits.

Signed left shifts, overflow, signed right shifts, and address wraparound require target semantics not established by the representation. All wraparound-dependent examples below are conditional on a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation.

Byte stores retain only the low eight bits of their source. A late shift uses the low eight bits of an address-like value as its count; counts above 31 are not visibly normalized.

### Initial mutation and early absolute accesses

The direct-memory phase starts with a word store at offset 0x14 from the last aggregate result’s high component. It then writes a byte at shifted-base offset 9, writes the large base as a word at offset 0x54 from the aggregate low component multiplied by 256, and rewrites shifted-base offset 9 from a different source. The first byte uses the aggregate high component; the second uses the shifted base’s low byte.

The large-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared. A byte at large-base offset 5 is captured, the even-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared, and the captured byte is zero-extended into a word at large-base offset 0x34. A later read of large-base offset 0x14 remains a real reload: intervening even-base stores may alias it.

After another even-base clear and two byte writes at even-base offset 0x15, the function reads an unsigned byte from absolute address 0x05. **After that read**, it writes the low byte of the even base to absolute address 0x01.

The captured byte from 0x05 is zero-extended and used as an address base. A byte is written at its offset 0x15. Derived stores then include a byte update at large-base offset 0x11, a word update through the previously reloaded word at offset 0x54, and a zero byte at large-base offset 0x11. The captured link is then read from the byte-valued base at offset 0x54, before that word destination is overwritten.

Further link-like updates use repeated reads at offset 0x74, word stores at offsets 0x24 and 0x54, and byte stores at offset 9. The repeated offset-0x74 reads are distinct; their values are not established as equal.

Two additional absolute writes occur within these derived-address updates:

- Absolute address 0x54 receives a **32-bit word** equal to twice the captured link. This occurs after an even-base offset-0x14 word update and before two stores of the captured link to that field.
- After two byte stores at even-base offset 0x15 using the fourfold link base, absolute address 0x15 receives the **low byte of the even base**. Subsequent stores continue through derived bases, including offsets 0x54 and 0x34.

The absolute destinations must not be treated as independent of calculated destinations.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance examples, not runtime tests.

1. **Input scaling.** For third input 3, the first two argument-bearing invocations each receive numeric 12 in the position associated with that input.

2. **Width truncation.** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, shifting 0x01000001 left by eight yields 0x00000100, not a wider retained result.

3. **Low-address derivation.** Under those semantics, a contributing value of 127 produces even base 1016 and offset-0x14 address 1036. Address validity is not implied.

4. **Early global order.** The byte read at 0x05 precedes the byte write at 0x01. Moving the write before the read fails the represented ordering contract.

5. **Mixed-width offset 0x04.** If the publication-base byte at offset 0x04 is 0x80, the corresponding offset-0x20 word store receives numeric 128. It does not consume the adjacent three bytes or sign-extend to a negative word.

6. **Anchor capture.** The anchor-global read precedes the word write of 0x20 at 0x60. If a derived alias subsequently modifies the anchor-global storage, the already captured anchor is not automatically reloaded.

7. **Final absolute ordering.** The read at 0xa9 precedes the publication-value offset-0x74 read, which precedes the computed byte write at 0x3a. The doubled-node-base offset-0x14 and publication-base offset-0x04 captures follow that write.

8. **Independent 0x67 values.** Suppose 0x67 receives 0x12, its read for 0x21 returns 0x12, and an intervening aliasing store changes it to 0x34. The next publication-base offset-9 write uses 0x34 if its own read observes that value. A further aliasing change may give the third read a different value again.

9. **Partial mutation on failure.** A late fault does not imply unchanged memory. No rollback guarantee applies.

10. **Terminal arguments.** Reaching the terminal destination establishes first visible argument 0x72 and the three captured words described above, not a character command or known API call.
