## Behavior

### Ordering and addressing rules

The represented flow is straight-line, without an explicit loop, conditional branch, recovery path, or early error return. Dependencies can nevertheless fault, alter memory, or fail to return.

A **selector update** reads an unsigned byte from a specified selector address, adds `0x11` to the byte’s numeric value, and stores one byte at that resulting absolute address. Its destination lies in `0x11`–`0x110`. The selector’s own address is not the base of the destination.

A **word slot** here means a 32-bit destination at `4 × index + 0x14`. Other stores use `16 × index + 0x14`, and one uses `256 × index + 0x14`; these forms are distinct.

Calls separate observable phases. Identical-looking loads remain separate reads where represented separately. Conversely, an explicitly captured value remains the operand of its later uses: changes to the original memory do not substitute a fresh value for that capture.

### Initial selector updates

Two calls precede the first direct store. The first target depends on incoming control state.

The initial selector update reads the byte at incoming-address offset `1` and writes the low byte of the incoming 32-bit value. After five intervening calls, the operation rereads that selector and writes the low byte of the incoming value masked by `0x3FFFFFFF` and logically shifted right by 20. Although the masked expression contains ten bits, the stored byte contains original bits 20–27.

Subsequent calls receive dependency results shifted left by two. One receives two equal shifted arguments and zero; another later receives two equal shifted arguments without that third argument.

A call receives four separately represented 32-bit reads from absolute address `0x30`. Their mutual argument-evaluation order is unresolved. Neither one cached read nor an assumed left-to-right order is established.

### Early zeroing and word construction

Two selector updates write zero using a selector address formed from a captured secondary result shifted right by nine, then increased by one. Calls intervene, so the selector is reread for the second update.

A later call receives four zero arguments. Word-slot operations then include:

- Storing a secondary result at the slot indexed by that same result.
- Reading a word at `((earlier unsigned result >> 18) × 0x400) + 0x14` and passing its logical right shift by 19 onward.
- Copying the captured secondary result into a dependency-selected slot.
- Storing `4 × index` at `16 × index + 0x14`.
- Reading a word at `4 × index + 0x14`, shifting it right by 19, and supplying it to a dependency.
- Copying a previously captured scaled value into another slot after intervening calls.

The differently scaled store and read addresses must not be treated as one field without establishing address equality.

### Masked values and paired results

A later unsigned result supplies a left-shift-by-two byte value and masked right shifts by 20, 17, and seven. The first two selector updates in this region reuse the same captured selector-address calculation but perform separate selector reads.

One dependency invocation has seven visible arguments: four repetitions of one result, two masked derivatives, and a control-location-derived value. The unresolved calling convention does not justify reducing this to a familiar smaller prototype.

A following paired result provides a retained low word and a high word passed onward. Zero selector updates intervene before later calls.

Further operations:

- Pass the low word’s top four bits onward.
- Zero destinations selected through `(low word >> 9) + 1`.
- Read a byte at twice the low word, widen it unsigned, and pass four copies to a dependency.
- Zero a destination selected by the byte at absolute address `1`.

One later argument shifts a value left by 31 after it has already been shifted left by two. Under 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, that argument is zero. This simplification does not eliminate intervening calls or stores.

### Direct reads and exposed stack storage

The middle region contains distinct accesses separated by calls and selector updates:

- A 32-bit read at a paired low word plus an offset formed by shifting that low word right by 19 and then left by 31.
- A byte read at a secondary-result address plus `0xE`.
- Four equal arguments containing the address of one stack-local location.
- An unsigned byte read at a retained result plus a signed secondary-result offset.
- A 16-bit read at a right-shift-by-19 result plus an earlier widened byte.

In the first form, the 32-bit offset is either zero or `0x80000000`, determined by one bit. It is not an ordinary element-size scaling. The halfword access has no represented alignment check.

After the halfword is passed four times to a dependency, a paired result supplies a high-word slot index and a low-word address-like value. The operation stores a previously captured shifted word in that slot. It subsequently writes at low-word offset `0xD`, first the low byte of twice the low word, then literal `4`, with calls between the writes.

Two other word reads use different forms: `4 × result + 0x44` and `((result AND 0x3FFFFFFF) >> 20) + 0x44`.

A later store writes `4 × result` at `256 × another result + 0x14`. Another slot receives a result masked with `0x03FFFFFF` and shifted right by 13. These forms do not imply a common object layout.

### Offset-based storage and byte fields

Repeated constructions store `4 × index` at `16 × index + 0x14` and copy captured values into later slots.

Later accesses include a byte read at a dependency-produced base plus `1`, a subsequent byte read through the widened byte plus `1`, and a selector update using a widened byte masked with `0x3FFFFF` and shifted right by 12. That last value is zero because its input is at most `255`.

### Saved absolute-byte base and state word B assignment

A late sequence specifically captures the unsigned byte at absolute address `1`. Call this retained numeric value the **saved byte base**. It is a 32-bit widened value in `0`–`255`.

The operation immediately clears one byte at saved-byte-base plus `0x11`. It then performs seven dependency invocations before assigning state word B. Those calls include a left-shift-by-two argument, intervening calls without explicit arguments, a call with two equal shifted arguments, and a result-producing call.

The resulting 32-bit word is assigned to state word B. Two further calls follow: the first receives that assigned word; the second has no explicit arguments. Only after both does the operation read a 32-bit word at saved-byte-base plus `0x74` and store its low byte at saved-byte-base plus `1`.

Both addresses use the saved byte base. There is no fresh read of absolute address `1` for this field transfer. Intervening calls may change address `1`, state word B, or the word being read; none of those changes replaces the retained base. The word load occurs after the global assignment and both subsequent calls, so its contents are not captured earlier.

Additional sequences then read words at dependency-selected scaled bases plus `0x74` and store their low bytes at those bases plus `1`. A repeated field transfer uses a fresh word load after intervening calls. Another captures the full loaded word for subsequent word-slot stores.

### Later address-bearing state word A snapshot

Near the end, a paired result supplies a low word shifted left by two and a high word used as a slot index. The shifted low word is stored at that slot. Immediately afterward, the operation reads state word A and retains its full 32-bit value as the **saved global base**.

Five dependency calls intervene. The first receives four visible arguments derived from the paired result; the fifth produces a new slot index. The operation then stores the previously captured shifted low word at the new word slot.

Only after this second slot store does it read a 32-bit word at saved-global-base plus `0x14`.

Consequently:

- The address comes from the earlier state word A snapshot, not a reread after the calls.
- Intervening changes to state word A do not replace that address.
- Intervening calls and the second slot store can change the contents subsequently read, including through aliasing.
- The dependent word load cannot be moved before those calls or that store.

The loaded word supplies later selector-write data, including its low byte and its logical right shift by 22. Further calls produce another paired result whose retained low word participates in the terminal halfword address.

### Terminal actions

After the final call group, the operation receives a byte-sized result and:

1. Stores a zero byte through a dependency-supplied pointer.
2. Replaces state word B with a packed 32-bit value: the result byte occupies bits 0–7, and a represented 24-bit read at absolute address `9` supplies bits 8–31.
3. Stores a zero halfword at `(retained unsigned word AND 0x3FFFFFFF) >> 7`.
4. Attempts a call to address zero with three zero arguments.
5. Returns only if that call returns.

The packed assignment does not preserve state word B’s former upper bits. The byte-pointer store precedes the 24-bit read; aliasing may therefore affect that read. The halfword store follows the packed assignment.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Selector destination.** A selector byte of `0x20` selects absolute address `0x31`, irrespective of where the selector was read.

2. **Byte truncation.** Incoming value `0x12345678` supplies initial byte `0x78` and later masked-shifted byte `0x23`.

3. **Separate selector reads.** If an intervening call changes a selector from `0x20` to `0x30`, separate selector updates target `0x31` and `0x41`. Caching fails this requirement.

4. **Distinct slot scales.** Index three gives addresses `0x20` for the fourfold form and `0x44` for the sixteenfold form.

5. **Saved byte base.** Suppose the late absolute-address-`1` capture yields `0x20`. The immediate clear targets `0x31`. Even if a subsequent call changes address `1` to `0x40`, the field transfer after state word B’s assignment and two further calls reads a word at `0x94` and writes its low byte at `0x21`. Reading at `0xB4` instead would incorrectly refresh the base.

6. **Late field contents.** In that sequence, if the second call after state word B’s assignment changes the word at `0x94` to `0x123456AB`, the subsequent transfer stores `0xAB` at `0x21`, subject to no intervening aliasing effect.

7. **Saved global address.** Suppose the later state word A snapshot is `0x1000`. The five intervening calls may change state word A to `0x2000`; the dependent read still targets `0x1014`, after the second word-slot store. If that store aliases `0x1014`, the read observes its resulting contents rather than an earlier snapshot.

8. **Range-derived zero.** Any 32-bit word shifted right by 20 is at most `4095`; shifting that captured value right by 21 yields zero.

9. **Terminal packing.** Result byte `0xAB` and 24-bit read value `0x123456` produce state word B value `0x123456AB`, independently of its former contents.

10. **Non-returning terminal call.** If the address-zero target does not return, normal completion is not required and earlier stores are not rolled back.
