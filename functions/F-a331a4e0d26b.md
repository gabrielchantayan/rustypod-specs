# F-a331a4e0d26b

## Purpose

F-a331a4e0d26b performs a straight-line sequence of indirect calls, integer transformations, memory reads, and memory writes.

Its directly represented effects include:

- Writing 32-bit values at calculated addresses.
- Reading bytes that select destinations for subsequent byte writes.
- Reading complete 32-bit fields and copying their low bytes to other locations.
- Assigning a global control word twice.
- Reading a global root word and a separately represented 24-bit global fragment.
- Clearing a byte through a dependency-produced pointer and clearing a calculated 16-bit location.

No domain-level purpose is established. Initialization, allocation, dispatch, serialization, and cleanup would be inferred interpretations, not supported functional descriptions.

### 1. Initial extraction and word stores

Three indirect calls precede calculation of the primary and secondary extracts. The first target is derived from incoming link state.

The initial direct store writes the primary extract as a 32-bit value at four times a secondary-register result, plus 20. The next call receives that secondary-register result twice, followed by the secondary extract and primary extract.

A subsequent dependency result is forwarded to another call. After further calls, a newly produced integer selects another destination at four times that integer, plus 20. This destination also receives the primary extract. The scaled integer is then passed to a dependency.

After another call sequence, a different secondary-register output selects a third four-times-plus-20 destination. A 32-bit zero is written there, and the unscaled selector is passed to a dependency.

Further calls forward a result and produce another integer. Its four-times-plus-20 destination receives the secondary extract shifted right by 23. That value is necessarily zero because the secondary extract is at most 511.

Before the following call receives this last scaled integer, the function reads a 32-bit word at:

**20 plus the third destination’s secondary-register selector, masked to 29 bits and logically shifted right by 19.**

This read address ranges from 20 through 1043. It need not be word-aligned. The read occurs after the fourth word store, so any aliasing between them affects the observed value.

### 2. First selector-mediated writes and independent call results

After intervening calls, the word just read is logically shifted right by 22. Denote this retained value as the **shifted word**; it ranges from 0 through 1023.

The function reads a byte at absolute address 1, adds 17 to that byte’s unsigned value, and stores the low byte of the shifted word there. The read address reduces to 1 because its other contribution is the secondary extract shifted right by 23, which is zero.

A following call receives the same secondary-register output in its first three displayed argument positions and zero in its fourth. Another call later receives an explicit zero.

After more calls, the function reads a byte at four times the shifted word, plus 1. It writes zero at the resulting byte value plus 17.

The byte-read address ranges from 1 through 4093. The destination ranges from 17 through 272.

The surrounding call-result provenance is significant:
