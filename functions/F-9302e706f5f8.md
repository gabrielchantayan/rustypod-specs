# F-9302e706f5f8

## Purpose

F-9302e706f5f8 performs an extended, ordered sequence of indirect calls and integer transformations, followed by direct mutation of memory and a final unresolved indirect transfer.

The established behavior is a **stateful operation**, not a pure calculation. Its observable results include dependency invocations, byte-, halfword-, and word-sized memory accesses, repeated writes, accesses to fixed low addresses, and arguments supplied to the final transfer. No application-level purpose—such as parsing, rendering, initialization, or serialization—is established.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Static evidence:** operations and ordering represented in the supplied material.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations requiring assumptions about execution widths, calling convention, or intended data structures.
- **documented_only:** behavior whose meaning or exact executable semantics cannot be resolved from the supplied material.

The supplied material is evidence, not an authoritative statement of intent. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Ordered dependency interaction

The opening region contains a long straight-line sequence of indirect calls. It repeatedly:

- Consumes a 32-bit result or the two halves of a 64-bit result.
- Passes transformed results to later calls.
- Passes the same transformed value in multiple argument positions.
- Invokes intervening targets without represented explicit arguments.
- Supplies literal zero arguments, including zeros obtained through arithmetic.

No visible conditional branch selects among these calls. This establishes an unconditional sequence in the supplied representation, conditional on each preceding operation returning and completing.

Calls with identical arguments are not interchangeable. Their targets and positions differ, and their side effects are unknown. A call whose result is unused may still be observable. Nothing establishes that these calls preserve globals, leave memory unchanged, or preserve secondary machine-result values.

The complete dependency-level trace is documented_only because target identities and interfaces are unresolved. It must not be replaced by an invented higher-level operation.

### Integer transformation requirements

Many dependency arguments are formed by left shifts of 32-bit values. Shift distances range up to 31 bits. Other arguments use masked fields, logical right shifts, or multiplication by powers of two.

For unsigned 32-bit expressions, truncation to the represented width is material. A field shifted out of the word does not reappear after a later right shift.

Examples of established field selections include:

- Bits 10–19, selected by masking with 0xFFFFF and shifting right by 10.
- The low 24 bits, selected with 0xFFFFFF.
- The low 22 bits, selected with 0x3FFFFF.
- The low 19 bits, selected with 0x7FFFF.
- Smaller fields selected with 0x3FFF, 0x1FFF, 0x7FF, and 0x1FFFF.

Some 64-bit results are shifted right by 34, 36, or 44 bits before conversion to a 32-bit unsigned value. These operations respectively expose the upper word shifted right by 2, 4, or 12 bits.

**[INFERENCE]:** treating every signed-looking shift and multiplication as wrapping 32-bit machine arithmetic is a plausible execution model, but it is not established solely by the supplied representation. Conclusions relying on overflow or signed right-shift behavior remain conditional.

### Entry into direct memory mutation

To describe the memory effects without implying recovered object types, this contract names several quantities by their provenance:

- **Shifted base:** a dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Packed base:** a different dependency result shifted left by 25.
- **Index field:** a later result shifted left by 8, masked with 0x7FFF, and logically shifted right by 6.
- **Small base:** twice the index field.
- **Retained shifted value:** another earlier result shifted left by 8.
- **Final result pair:** the last combined result before direct stores begin.

Under unsigned 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, the index field contains only bits 2–8. Its range is therefore 0–508 in multiples of four. The small base ranges from 0–1016 in multiples of eight. These are potentially low addresses, not proven heap pointers.

The initial mutation region establishes this ordering:

- A word derived from the packed base is stored at upper-result-relative offset 0x14.
- A byte derived from that upper result is stored at packed-base-relative offset 0x09.
- The shifted base is stored at offset 0x54 from a base obtained by scaling the lower result by 256.
- The byte at packed-base-relative offset 0x09 is overwritten with the low byte of the packed base.
- The word at shifted-base-relative offset 0x14 is cleared.
- A byte is read from shifted-base-relative offset 0x05.
- A word at small-base-relative offset 0x14 is cleared.
- The previously read byte is zero-extended into a word at shifted-base-relative offset 0x34.
- The word at shifted-base-relative offset 0x14 is read.
- The word at small-base-relative offset 0x14 is cleared again.
- The low byte of the just-read word is written twice to small-base-relative offset 0x15.

This order matters even when some values appear redundant. The supplied evidence does not establish that the underlying storage is ordinary, nonvolatile, or disjoint.

### Low-address state and linked mutations

The next region reads a byte from fixed address 0x05 and treats its zero-extended value as another base. It also writes:

- A byte at fixed address 0x01.
- A byte at fixed address 0x15.
- A word at fixed address 0x54.

These locations must not be silently replaced with private temporary storage.

The function performs additional word reads and writes at offsets 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, and 0x74. Among the observable relationships:

- A retained shifted value is written as a byte and then overwritten by zero at shifted-base-relative offset 0x11.
- A word read from offset 0x54 of the byte-derived base is retained and then replaced.
- A word read from shifted-base-relative offset 0x74 becomes a base for writes at offsets 0x09 and 0x54.
- A word at small-base-relative offset 0x74 is read twice within a subsequent write expression. A single cached read is not established as equivalent.
- Self-valued words are written at offset 0x24 of a retained base.
- The small-base-relative word at 0x14 is overwritten repeatedly.
- A retained value is scaled by four and eight to form further addresses and stored values.

Further accesses use a byte read at offset 0x05 as a zero-extended address base for a word read at offset 0x74. This is byte-to-address conversion, not an established lookup through a pointer table.

**[INFERENCE]:** repeated offsets and self-valued stores may resemble linked state. No ownership, list invariant, or object type is established.

### Mixed-width constants and repeated writes

A later region writes the numeric value 0x3A using three widths at calculated destinations: one byte, one halfword, and one word. Equal numeric values do not make these accesses equivalent.

Another calculated destination at offset 0x20 receives, in order:

1. A word containing 0x20.
2. A word containing 0x2F.
3. A word containing a zero-extended byte read from offset 0x04 of the same calculated base.
4. A word containing 0x20.

The byte read therefore occurs after two writes and before the final write. Its result cannot be inferred from initial memory without resolving aliasing.

A word containing 0x20 is also written to fixed address 0x60.

The following region repeatedly writes a stable pattern involving a stored base and a calculated base:

- A byte at stored-base-relative offset 0x19 receives the low byte of the calculated base.
- A byte at calculated-base-relative offset 0x11 receives the low byte of the stored base.
- A word at stored-base-relative offset 0x34 receives an eightfold-scaled value.
- Between successive groups, a calculated destination at offset 0x54 receives a retained fourfold-scaled value.

The first group uses a different eightfold-scaled value from the later groups. The repetition is finite and explicitly represented; it is not evidence of an unbounded loop.

The exact repetition count is documented_only in this contract. Removing duplicates or retaining only final memory contents is not established as equivalent.

### Final mutation and transfer

After the repeated region, the function reads a word at fixed address 0xA9 and several base-relative words. It writes a byte at fixed address 0x3A using an eightfold-scaled quantity plus 0x30, truncated to eight bits.

Subsequent effects include:

- Writing 0x6C as a byte at calculated-base-relative offset 0x08.
- Writing low bytes at offsets 0x09, 0x0D, and 0x10.
- Writing a self-valued word at offset 0x54 of another retained base.
- Writing the low byte of the word read from 0xA9 to fixed address 0x67.
- Copying a later read of address 0x67 to fixed address 0x21.
- Overwriting a word at offset 0x54 first with a retained value, then with the calculated base.
- Copying a word read at calculated-base-relative offset 0x40 into offset 0x10.
- Writing previously retained words to offsets 0x44, 0x54, and 0x74.

One byte value comes from a signed-looking right shift whose shift count is the low eight bits of the calculated base. Large shift counts and negative operands require target semantics; they are documented_only.

The final region reads a word at offset 0x04 of a retained base, doubles it to form a halfword destination, and stores 0x65 there. It then follows words at offsets 0x64 and 0x74, performs further byte and word writes, stores the word 100 at offset 0x54 of a selected base, and writes another word at a doubled address.

The final unresolved target receives four represented arguments:

| Position | Value |
|---|---|
| First | 0x72 |
| Second | A word obtained through the final offset-0x64 access chain |
| Third | An earlier retained word read at offset 0x14 |
| Fourth | A late word read at offset 0x74 |

No additional direct cleanup is represented after this transfer.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional review examples, not executed tests.

### Truncation before extraction

Given an unsigned 32-bit result of 0x12345678, shifting it left by eight produces 0x34567800. Logically shifting that retained word right by eight produces 0x00345678, not the original result.

Acceptance requires preserving the loss of the upper byte.

### Sparse index field

Given a result whose low seven bits are all set, the index-field calculation produces 508 and the small base is 1016.

Acceptance requires respecting the preceding left shift. Treating the masked intermediate as an unrestricted 15-bit field would admit values not produced by this calculation.

### Clear followed by read

Assume ordinary stable memory, no aliasing with intervening stores, and successful accesses. The read from shifted-base-relative offset 0x14 after its clear obtains zero.

Without those assumptions, acceptance must retain the actual read rather than replace it unconditionally with zero.

### Distinct write widths

Given valid calculated destinations, the 0x3A byte, halfword, and word stores affect one, two, and four bytes respectively.

Acceptance does not permit replacing all three with byte writes.

### Repeated write observability

Assume a destination records every write. The two identical byte writes at small-base-relative offset 0x15 must remain two events.

Matching only the final byte is insufficient.

### Failure and final transfer

If an early indirect target does not return, no later direct-write postcondition is required.

If execution reaches the final target, its first represented argument must be 0x72, with the other three values retained from their specified access provenance. Replacing that transfer with an immediate return is not equivalent.
