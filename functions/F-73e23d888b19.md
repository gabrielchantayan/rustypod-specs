# F-73e23d888b19

## Purpose

F-73e23d888b19 performs a read-modify-write of one 32-bit word reached through an indirect address. Its apparent intent is to replace an inclusive range of bits with low-order bits from a supplied value, preserving bits outside that range.

The supplied representation establishes the masking expression and an indirect assignment. It does not establish portable behavior for every input: some shift counts reach or exceed the operand width, and some left-shift operands are represented as signed values. Consequently, the ordinary bit-field interpretation below is conditional on the execution environment providing the corresponding 32-bit bit-vector shift behavior.

No validation, status reporting, allocation, or explicit synchronization appears in the supplied body.

## Behavior

### Established arithmetic structure

Let:

- \(W\) denote the destination word read for the calculation.
- \(H\) denote the upper-position argument.
- \(L\) denote the lower-position argument.
- \(V\) denote the replacement value.
- \(\operatorname{low8}(x)\) denote the least significant eight bits of an integer expression.

The expression uses four shift counts:

| Use | Count |
|---|---|
| Right shift of an unsigned all-ones 32-bit word | \(\operatorname{low8}(32-L)\) |
| Left shift of an all-ones value | \(\operatorname{low8}(H+1)\) |
| Left shift of the value one, before subtracting one | \(\operatorname{low8}(H-L+1)\) |
| Left shift positioning the replacement | \(\operatorname{low8}(L)\) |

The relevant additions and subtractions have unsigned 32-bit behavior in the supplied expression. In particular, the signed representation of \(H\) does not imply signed comparison, range validation, or signed saturation. Where it participates in these mixed expressions, its 32-bit bit pattern enters unsigned arithmetic.

The count expressions retain only eight bits. This is a modulo-256 reduction, **not** an explicit modulo-32 reduction.

The calculated word combines two components:

1. The old word, filtered by the union of a lower preservation mask and an upper preservation mask.
2. A masked portion of the replacement value, shifted to its destination position.

There is no branch for an invalid range, an empty range, a full-width range, or a replacement value larger than the intended field.

### Ordinary inclusive-field interpretation

Under 32-bit bit-vector shift semantics, a straightforward interior range is:

\[
1 \leq L \leq H \leq 30.
\]

For this range, all four shift counts lie between 1 and 31, and the expression implements an inclusive bit-field replacement:

- Bit zero is the least significant bit.
- Bits below \(L\) retain their values from \(W\).
- Bits above \(H\) retain their values from \(W\).
- Bits \(L\) through \(H\), inclusive, receive the lowest \(H-L+1\) bits of \(V\).
- Replacement bits above that width have no effect.

The replacement does not merely set bits. A zero in the selected portion of \(V\) clears the corresponding destination bit.

This is an interpretation of the represented bit operations, not a claim that the supplied expression is portable ISO C. The all-ones left-shift operand is represented as signed negative one; treating its shift as an ordinary bit-vector operation requires execution semantics beyond a strict source-language reading.

### Boundaries and unusual ranges

**Lower position zero.** The lower preservation-mask calculation requests a right shift by 32. The expression does not contain a special case defining that shift.

**Upper position 31.** The upper preservation-mask calculation requests a left shift by 32. The expression does not contain a special case defining that shift.

**Full 32-bit field.** Selecting positions zero through 31 also requests a shift by 32 in the replacement-width calculation. An unconditional “replace the entire word” contract is therefore not established.

**Adjacent reversed positions.** When \(1 \leq L \leq 31\) and \(H=L-1\), the replacement-width shift count is zero. Under bit-vector semantics, the replacement mask is zero and the two preservation masks cover the entire word. The calculated result is \(W\). This is an arithmetic consequence, not an explicit empty-range policy.

**Other reversed or out-of-range positions.** There is no rejection path. Unsigned arithmetic and eight-bit count reduction still apply, but their result must not be described as a conventional field replacement without establishing the execution environment’s shift behavior.

**Negative upper positions.** These are not explicitly rejected. Their 32-bit representations participate in the unsigned count calculations. They are not independently clamped to zero.
