## Behavior

### Initial captures and first writes

The first two global evaluations capture the initial comparison word and initial arithmetic word, in that order. Both occur before any visible store.

The first store writes a halfword at the incoming base plus hexadecimal `44`. Its value is the low sixteen bits of the incoming base.

An adjusted third-argument value is then formed from the third argument, decimal 65, and the converted carry-like input. The next store writes the low eight bits of the third argument at the address obtained by adding the third argument to the captured initial arithmetic word.

Only after those two stores is the word-store base read. If either earlier write overlaps that global’s storage, the subsequent read can observe the modified contents.

The captured initial arithmetic word is squared. Subsequent square-dependent operations use this retained result, not another global read.

### Remaining early stores

Four stores follow, in this order:

1. A halfword containing the low sixteen bits of the square is written at the adjusted third-argument value plus the square.
2. A word containing the adjusted third-argument value is written at the captured word-store base plus that adjusted value.
3. A halfword containing the low sixteen bits of the incoming base is written at the third argument plus the square.
4. A halfword containing the low sixteen bits of the square is written at the square plus the incoming base.

Together with the preceding two writes, these are the first six stores. Their destinations may overlap one another, global storage, or other memory. No non-aliasing guarantee is established.

### Late arithmetic capture and separate address evaluation

After the first six stores, three global evaluations occur in order:

1. Read and retain the word-store payload.
2. Read and retain the halfword-store index.
3. Read and retain the late arithmetic word.

The late arithmetic global is then **read a second time** to calculate the next byte-store destination. That store writes the low eight bits of the initially captured comparison word at twice the **reread** late arithmetic value.

The captured late arithmetic value and this reread value have different roles:

- The reread value supplies this byte-store address.
- The captured value supplies the later displacement, later word-store address, and final halfword-store address and value.

A halfword containing hexadecimal `004E`, decimal 78, is then written at twice the captured halfword-store index.

These are stores seven and eight.

### Final address capture, displacement, and reread

The final address global is read for the first time after those two stores. This first value is retained for the eventual indirect argument.

The displacement is formed from the captured late arithmetic value plus decimal 78, with one added exactly when the incoming unsigned comparison value is less than or equal to the initially captured comparison word. The comparison is unsigned and includes equality. Its result changes the numerical displacement; the visible representation does not establish a separate control-flow branch.

After the displacement is calculated, the final address global is **read again**. Store nine writes the low eight bits of the displacement at the unsigned 32-bit sum of:

- The second evaluation of the final address global.
- The displacement.

The retained first evaluation is not substituted for this second evaluation.

### Last stores and indirect invocation

Store ten writes the captured 32-bit payload at twice the **captured** late arithmetic value.

Store eleven writes a halfword at the fourth argument plus the captured late arithmetic value. Its value is the low sixteen bits of that captured late arithmetic value.

The unresolved target is then invoked with one explicit 32-bit argument: the bitwise OR of the displacement and the **first**, retained evaluation of the final address global.

The final byte-store address and indirect argument therefore differ in two independent respects:

- The address uses addition; the argument uses bitwise OR.
- The address uses the second final-address evaluation; the argument uses the first.

### Capture, reread, and overlap rules

Captured values are not implicitly refreshed after writes. However, both explicitly repeated global evaluations remain independent reads.

The early byte store at twice the reread late arithmetic value and the later payload word store have the same start address **only if their relevant address calculations yield the same result**. Equality of both late-arithmetic evaluations is sufficient when the arithmetic is defined. It is not an unconditional property of the function.

When those addresses coincide in ordinary writable memory, the later word store replaces the byte at their common start. The earlier byte store nevertheless remains part of the required access sequence and may be observable through a fault, device semantics, or another observer.

A change to the final address global between its evaluations can move store nine without changing the retained component of the indirect argument. Changes after the second evaluation do not retroactively change that store’s calculated address.

The established ordering concerns the supplied representation. It does not independently establish concurrency synchronization, atomicity, volatile-access guarantees, or hardware memory ordering.
