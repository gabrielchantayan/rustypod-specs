### Arithmetic interpretation

The representation predominantly uses 32-bit words for shifts, scaling, and address construction. Under a machine-word interpretation, each word operation is narrowed to 32 bits; arithmetic expressions must not silently acquire unlimited-precision intermediates.

Successive left shifts can erase every original bit when their cumulative displacement reaches thirty-two, even though each individual count is below thirty-two. A word shifted left by twenty-five and subsequently by seven is zero under this model.

Unsigned right shifts are zero-filling. Shifting a represented unsigned 64-bit result right by 34, 36, or 44 and narrowing selects, respectively, its upper thirty, twenty-eight, or twenty bits.

Signed overflow, signed left shifts, signed right-shift interpretation, and correspondence to actual machine instructions remain **documented_only**. Examples using modular arithmetic are conditional on the 32-bit machine-word model.
