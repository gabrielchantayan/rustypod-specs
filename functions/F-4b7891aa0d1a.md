# F-4b7891aa0d1a

## Purpose

The established behavior is an ordered sequence of memory accesses, global assignments, address calculations, and conditional termination markers. The supplied static representation does not establish a reliable application-level purpose.

This contract describes effects only when execution reaches them. It does not promise valid addresses, successful completion, recovery from faults, or a normal return.

Three limitations materially constrain interpretation:

- Some inputs come from ambient register or stack state rather than the apparent argument list.
- Two address calculations contain right shifts by the apparent operand width.
- The representation warns of invalid instruction data and ends both represented paths at invalid-instruction markers.

An initialization or architectural-vector interpretation is **inferred intent**, not required behavior.

## Behavior

The following explicit memory accesses occur in order:

1. Write B’s low 16 bits at S + B.
2. Read a 32-bit word at `(R OR A) + J`, obtaining **L**.
3. Read an unsigned byte at `S + (R OR A)`, obtaining **E**.
4. Write L as a word at D + 8.
5. Write D’s 32-bit representation at A + `0x40`.
6. Write L as a word at K + `0x74`.
7. Read a signed 32-bit word at D + `0x28`, obtaining **M**.
8. Read an opaque word at K + `0x5c`, obtaining **N**.
9. Write K’s low byte at L + 3.
10. Write E as a byte at K + `0x10`.
11. Write N’s low byte at E + `0x1c`.

E is zero-extended when used as an address component. The last destination therefore has numerical value between `0x1c` and `0x11b`.

No validation precedes these accesses. Aliasing can make their order observable.

### Wrapped arithmetic and halfword region

Define the following unsigned 32-bit values:

- **U:** A − `0x38`.
- **V:** A − `0xe0`.
- **W:** V with bit 31 cleared.
- **Q:** W shifted logically right by one.
- **P:** twice V, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\), then interpreted as an address.

W is at most `0x7fffffff`; Q is at most `0x3fffffff`.

Another address, **Z**, comes from shifting signed M left by two and then right by 32. The apparent operand width is 32 bits. Z’s executable meaning is **documented_only**; neither zero, a masked shift count, nor widening is established.

Subject to that uncertainty, effects continue in this order:

1. Write Q’s low byte at M + `0x0897e8b8`.
2. Assign `0xb8` to a global.
3. Write U’s low byte at A − `0x32`.
4. Write U as a word at Z.
5. Write halfword `0xffff` at P + 4.
6. Write halfword `0xffff` at P + 6.
7. Write byte `0xb8` at Z + 4.
8. Write halfword `0xffff` at P + 2.
9. Assign the low 16 bits of P’s represented address to a global.
10. Read an unsigned halfword at P.
11. Zero-extend that halfword and use it as the address for a word store containing `U AND 0x897e8b80`.

The global assignments in steps 2 and 9 have unestablished destination widths. In particular, extracting 16 bits from P does not prove a halfword store.

The final word destination is numerically between zero and `0xffff`. It is not automatically P. The three explicit halfword stores do not directly initialize the halfword at P, although other aliasing may affect it.

### First dependency address and branch

Let **F** be the 32-bit numerical address representation of F-e47d7b2e9c88. No call to that dependency is represented.

Define:

- **Y:** signed F shifted right by 32.
- **Sign-halfword:** the low 16 bits of signed F shifted right by 31.

Y remains **documented_only** because the shift count equals the apparent operand width. Under conventional arithmetic-right-shift semantics, Sign-halfword is zero for nonnegative F and `0xffff` for negative F. The 16-bit truncation occurs after the shift; this is not a shift of F’s low halfword.

The routine writes Sign-halfword at Y + 4, then tests whether signed F is negative.

If negative, the represented path reaches an invalid-instruction marker. The preceding halfword store remains part of that path.

Otherwise, effects continue:

1. Write Sign-halfword at Y + `0x12`.
2. Write F as a word at numerical address F.
3. Read a signed halfword at Y + Q.
4. Repeat the halfword write at Y + `0x12`.
5. Repeat the word write of F at address F.
6. Sign-extend the loaded halfword to 32 bits, shift it right by 16, and write the result as a word at Y + 8.

Under conventional arithmetic shifts, the final word is zero for a nonnegative loaded halfword and `0xffffffff` for a negative one.

The repeated stores are separately represented effects. Intervening reads, overlapping storage, or device-backed memory can distinguish them.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consistency examples, not executed tests. Each assumes execution reaches the relevant point and the target permits the preceding operations.

1. **Initial truncation.** If B has bit pattern `0x12345678`, the first explicit halfword store contains `0x5678`. Byte order remains unspecified.

2. **Unsigned boundary.** If A is `0xe0`, V, W, Q, and P are zero. The three explicit `0xffff` halfword stores target addresses 4, 6, and 2, in that order.

3. **Unsigned wraparound.** If A is `0xdf`, V is `0xffffffff`, W is `0x7fffffff`, Q is `0x3fffffff`, and P is `0xfffffffe`. Subsequent concrete address-offset behavior still requires target semantics.

4. **Byte-derived address.** If E is `0xff`, the N-derived byte store targets `0x11b`, not an offset from K, D, or L.

5. **Loaded destination.** If the halfword read at P yields `0x1234`, the masked-U word targets numerical address `0x1234`.

6. **Early termination.** If signed F is negative, the explicit halfword store at Y + 4 precedes the early marker. This condition does not establish Y’s numerical value.

7. **Signed-halfword propagation.** Under conventional arithmetic shifts, a loaded halfword of `0x8000` produces `0xffffffff` at Y + 8; `0x7fff` produces zero.

8. **Conversion without store-width proof.** The global assignment derived from P supplies P’s low 16 bits. Acceptance must not require that only two destination bytes change unless a separate storage declaration establishes that width.

9. **Zero without word-store proof.** The continuing path assigns zero to the designated global. The supplied evidence cannot distinguish a two-byte, four-byte, or other destination access solely from that assignment.

10. **Small literals without byte-store proof.** The late assignments supply `0x1a` and `0x5c`. A requirement that adjacent bytes remain unchanged is unsupported without destination-width evidence.

11. **Stack-slot distinction.** If T differs from H, `4 × T + 0x18` and `32 × H + 0x18` remain distinct formulas, even if particular values could make their results coincide.

12. **Late component reread.** Suppose step 3 of the final sequence assigns value `0x1234`, but intervening effects leave the current upper component as `0xabcd`. Step 12 constructs `0xabcdea70`, not `0x1234ea70`.

13. **Unchanged component.** If I is `0x12345678` and the upper component remains unchanged, the constructed value is `0x1234ea70`. Equality with the earlier assignment is conditional.

14. **No success result.** Reaching the final marker does not establish a successful return.
