# F-804de8948f01

## Purpose

F-804de8948f01 invokes an ordered series of indirect dependencies, derives address-like and packed values from their results and implicit entry state, performs mixed-width memory mutations, and finishes with another indirect invocation.

**Established static evidence:** the visible interface has no return value. Explicit effects include 8-bit, 16-bit and 32-bit memory accesses, repeated stores, overlapping fields and absolute low-address accesses. Calls precede the first explicit data-memory store and may themselves have effects.

**Inferred intent:** recurring offsets and scaled values suggest manipulation of interconnected state. They do not establish application-level records, strings, a publication protocol or a meaningful interpretation of the character-like constants.

This is a static behavioral contract, not a portable execution specification. Unknown calling conventions, dependencies and address-space semantics remain **documented_only**. Completion of the visible path is not established.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven preparation

Before the first explicit data-memory store, the operation executes a long ordered chain of indirect invocations. The first receives the first two arguments unchanged and the first implicit unsigned register shifted right by twelve. Its target depends on the incoming control link.

Early argument formation includes:

- Two copies of the fourth argument shifted left by seven.
- An invocation with three explicit zero arguments.
- Low result halves shifted left by eight, paired with high result halves.
- Both implicit unsigned registers shifted right by eleven.
- Two copies of the first implicit register shifted right by twelve and then left by seven.

Later calls consume masked fields, shifted primary and secondary results, and explicit zeros. Calls with no recovered arguments remain observable dependency invocations: missing explicit arguments do not establish absence of machine-level inputs or effects.

There is no visible conditional branch, retry or bounded loop in this preparation. Later actions depend on earlier dependencies returning. Unused results do not justify removing calls; dependencies may modify memory, globals or other machine state.

### Widths and derived-value vocabulary

A **byte**, **halfword** and **word** mean 8, 16 and 32 bits respectively. Byte-to-word conversions described below are unsigned promotions. Byte stores retain only the low eight bits.

Supported field ranges include:

| Extraction | Result range |
|---|---:|
| Low eleven bits shifted left by ten | 0–0x1FFC00 |
| Low twenty-four bits shifted right by eight | 0–0xFFFF |
| Low twenty-four bits shifted right by nineteen | 0–31 |
| Low eighteen bits of a high result half shifted right by eleven | 0–127 |
| Low seventeen bits shifted right by four | 0–0x1FFF |
| Low twenty-four bits shifted right by twelve | 0–0xFFF |

The following contract labels describe dataflow, not recovered object types:

- **Packed base:** a dependency-result low word shifted left by twenty-five.
- **Scaled base:** a separate dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Small index:** another result shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, then shifted right by six.
- **Index base:** twice the small index.
- **Shifted word:** a retained dependency result shifted left by eight.
- **Final pair:** the last two-word dependency result before explicit stores.

Under wrapping 32-bit arithmetic, the packed and scaled bases have twenty-five zero low bits. The small index ranges from 0 through 508 in steps of four; the index base ranges from 0 through 1016 in steps of eight.

Address use does not widen prior arithmetic. Left-shifted high bits are not recovered by subsequent multiplication or pointer interpretation. Explicitly introduced zeros remain zero through the displayed shifts and masks; earlier occupants of the same working storage are not substitutes.

Several expressions have signed 32-bit operands. Negative left shifts, signed overflow and oversized shifts require target semantics; unbounded arithmetic is not an established replacement.

### Initial direct-memory effects

The initial effects are ordered as follows. A word at the final pair’s high-half value plus 0x14 receives the packed base. The packed-base byte at 0x09 receives that high half’s low byte. A word at 256 times the final pair’s low half plus 0x54 receives the scaled base. The packed-base byte at 0x09 is then overwritten with the packed base’s low byte, which is zero under wrapping 32-bit shifts.

The scaled-base word at 0x14 is cleared before reading its byte at 0x05. The index-base word at 0x14 is cleared before the captured byte is promoted into the scaled-base word at 0x34. The scaled-base word at 0x14 is then read, and the index-base word at 0x14 is cleared again. The fetched word’s low byte is written twice to index-base offset 0x15.

These are individual accesses, not merely final-state assignments. In particular, the scaled-base read at 0x14 cannot unconditionally be replaced with zero: intervening writes may alias it, and access behavior may be externally observable.

### Absolute low-address stores and linked updates

Next, the operation reads the byte at absolute 0x05 and promotes it to a **Byte-derived base**. Immediately afterward, absolute byte 0x01 receives the low byte of the index base. The byte-derived-base byte at 0x15 then receives the small index’s low byte.

The scaled-base byte at 0x11 receives the shifted word’s low byte, a word selected through the previously fetched scaled-base-offset-0x14 value receives the shifted word at offset 0x54, and the scaled-base byte at 0x11 is cleared.

The word at byte-derived-base offset 0x54 is read before replacement with the earlier scaled-base-offset-0x14 value. Call this original fetched word the **Link value**. The index-base word at 0x34 receives the small index.

Further effects use words obtained through scaled-base and index-base offsets 0x74. The former selects a destination whose word at 0x54 first receives the scaling result shifted by twenty-six. The latter participates in a separate self-linking store at offset 0x54; the representation contains separate reads of index-base offset 0x74, not a guaranteed single captured read.

The link-value destination receives its own base at offset 0x24 twice. Between those stores, another selected destination receives the link value’s low byte at offset 0x09 and the shifted word at offset 0x54. Another byte store follows the second self-reference.

The index-base word at 0x14 receives the twenty-six-bit-shifted scaling result. **Absolute word 0x54 then receives twice the link value.** Afterward, index-base offset 0x14 receives the link value twice. Its byte at 0x15 receives the low byte of four times the link value twice, followed by **absolute byte 0x15 receiving the low byte of the index base**.

These absolute stores have fixed values and positions in the visible effect order. Unknown address-space semantics do not make them unspecified operations.

### Scaling, selected fields and constant stores

Define the **Quadrupled base** as four times the link value. Subsequent updates use that value, the original link value, eight times the link value, and twice a newly read index-base-offset-0x74 word. These derived quantities are not interchangeable pointers.

This portion includes:

- Repeated words at quadrupled-base offset 0x54, including replacement of the shifted word with the quadrupled base itself.
- Writes through offsets 0x0D, 0x14 and 0x44 using both selected bases and their low bytes.
- A word selected through a byte at quadrupled-base offset 0x05 followed by offset 0x74.
- A separate word read at quadrupled-base offset 0x04, whose offset-0x05 byte is read twice with intervening stores.

The second of those byte reads selects another word through offset 0x74. Call this fetched word the **Terminal seed**, and twice it the **Terminal base**.

Before the publication phase, the operation writes 0x3A as a byte at four times the terminal seed, as a halfword at eight times the terminal seed, and as a word at four times the terminal seed plus 0x20. The subtraction used below occurs between the first and second of these stores.

Define the **Adjusted quantity** as the quadrupled base minus 0x96, and the **Adjusted base** as four times that quantity. At adjusted-base offset 0x20, the ordered word values are 0x20, 0x2F, a freshly read unsigned byte from adjusted-base offset 0x04, and 0x20 again. That byte read follows the first two stores.

### Repeated publication effects

The operation next reads the word-sized global supplying the **Publication base**. **After that read and before the first publication, absolute word 0x60 receives 0x20.**

The publication value is four times the terminal seed. Each publication has four ordered stores:

- A word at an eightfold-derived destination plus 0x54 receives the publication value.
- Publication-base byte 0x19 receives the adjusted base’s low byte.
- Adjusted-base byte 0x11 receives the publication base’s low byte.
- Publication-base word 0x34 receives the eightfold-derived destination.

The first destination is eight times the original link value. Subsequent explicitly repeated publications use eight times the adjusted quantity.

The global-derived base and the two byte values are captured values; the global is not visibly reread for each publication. No dependency is assumed to have preserved the global before its actual read.

The repetitions do not establish traversal, index advancement or a termination condition. Their exact numeric multiplicity is not enumerated in this contract and remains **documented_only**; no reduction to one final-state update is authorized.

### Final mutation: capture boundaries

The final phase begins by reading absolute word 0xA9, then the word at publication-value offset 0x74. These are the **Absolute capture** and **Publication capture**.

Only then does absolute byte 0x3A receive the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30.

**After that byte store**, the operation reads terminal-base word 0x14 and adjusted-base word 0x04. These are the **Saved terminal word** and **Saved adjusted word**. They must not be hoisted before the absolute 0x3A store: that store may alias either read.

Preparation for the remaining mutation writes the adjusted base’s low byte at terminal-base offset 0x0D, 0x6C at adjusted-base offset 0x08, and the publication base’s low byte at adjusted-base offsets 0x0D and 0x09. Terminal-base word 0x54 receives the terminal base itself. Absolute byte 0x67 is then initialized from the absolute capture’s low byte.

### Final mutation: independent reads of absolute 0x67

Three later reads of absolute byte 0x67 are distinct memory events.

**Copy read.** After initialization, terminal-base word 0x44 is read as a signed word. Absolute byte 0x67 is then read for the store to absolute byte 0x21. The intervening computed-address word read is not evidence that the byte can be replaced with its initialization value.

The signed word’s low byte is written at adjusted-base offset 0x10, and the whole word at publication-base offset 0x54. A byte at terminal-base offset 0x01 receives the shifted word’s low byte, using the adjusted base’s low eight bits as the shift count. Publication-base word 0x54 is then overwritten with the adjusted base.

Adjusted-base word 0x40 is read before publication-base word 0x74 receives the saved adjusted word. The newly read word is stored at adjusted-base offset 0x10, and publication-base word 0x44 receives the terminal base.

**First adjusted-destination read.** Absolute byte 0x67 is now read again for a store to adjusted-base byte 0x09. This read follows all mutations just described. Next, terminal-base byte 0x09 receives the adjusted base’s low byte, and adjusted-base word 0x44 receives the absolute capture.

The operation then reads adjusted-base word 0x54, obtaining the **Selected base**.

**Second adjusted-destination read.** After that word read, absolute byte 0x67 is read a third time for another store to adjusted-base byte 0x09. It is not a reuse of either previous byte read.

Computed-address stores between these reads can alias absolute 0x67. Consequently, the copied byte and the two later adjusted-base bytes need not match, even without concurrency.

### Final stores and invocation

The selected-base byte at 0x0D receives the publication capture’s low byte. Terminal-base word 0x54 is restored from the saved terminal word. Adjusted-base word 0x14 is then read; its low byte is retained for a later selected-base store.

Terminal-base byte 0x11 receives the publication capture’s low byte. Adjusted-base byte 0x09 and word 0x44 receive, respectively, the selected base’s low byte and full word.

A word read at terminal-base offset 0x04 supplies a **Late base**. A halfword 0x65 is written at twice that value before reading late-base word 0x64. The selected-base byte at 0x11 receives the retained adjusted-base-offset-0x14 low byte, and selected-base word 0x54 receives decimal 100.

Late-base word 0x74 is read, followed by terminal-base word 0x64. Terminal-base byte 0x09 receives the late base’s low byte. The word fetched from terminal-base offset 0x64 selects a destination at offset 0x54, which receives the earlier late-base-offset-0x64 word. The final explicit word store writes that destination base at twice the earlier late-base-offset-0x64 word.

The final indirect invocation receives four visible arguments, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The late-base-offset-0x64 word.
3. The saved terminal word.
4. The late-base-offset-0x74 word.

An apparent return follows. Ordinary return, dispatch, tail transfer and non-returning behavior are not distinguishable from the supplied evidence.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional review examples, not executed tests.

- **Entry shift:** an implicit unsigned value of 0xABCDEF01 supplies 0x000ABCDE as the first invocation’s third visible argument.
- **Field extraction:** masking 0xAB123456 to twenty-four bits and shifting right by eight produces 0x1234.
- **Index boundary:** a small-index source of 0x7F produces 508; 0x80 produces zero under 32-bit shifts.
- **Absolute index stores:** a small index of 508 produces index base 1016, so the explicit stores to absolute bytes 0x01 and 0x15 each carry 0xF8 at their respective positions.
- **Doubled word:** a link value of 0x12345678 produces the explicit absolute-word-0x54 value 0x2468ACF0 under wrapping arithmetic.
- **Capture ordering:** if the absolute 0x3A store aliases the later terminal-offset-0x14 read, the saved terminal word reflects that store’s actual effects. The two preceding captures do not.
- **Fresh byte reads:** if an intervening computed store changes absolute byte 0x67, the next read for adjusted-base offset 0x09 observes the changed memory, not the initialization byte.
- **Publication ordering:** the global read precedes absolute word 0x60 receiving 0x20, which precedes the first publication.
- **Partial failure:** if a dependency does not return, later explicit stores are not promised, and earlier effects are not promised to be undone.
- **Oversized shift:** a final shift count of at least 32 has no portable expected result without target-machine evidence.
