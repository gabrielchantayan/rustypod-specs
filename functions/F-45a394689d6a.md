# F-45a394689d6a

## Purpose

F-45a394689d6a performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms returned and implicit machine-state values, mutates memory through mixed-width accesses, and finally transfers control indirectly with four represented arguments.

The evidence establishes low-level interactions, not an application-level purpose. It does not establish text formatting, device initialization, collection maintenance, or object construction. Character-like constants and recurring offsets do not justify those interpretations.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** accesses, transformations, and ordering visible in the supplied representation.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations that are not behavioral requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior not resolved by this contract’s evidence.

All acceptance examples are conditional static examples. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Dependency interactions and arithmetic

The dependency phase is represented as a long straight-line sequence without an explicit local loop or conditional branch. The first target depends on incoming control-flow state plus a constant displacement. Later targets are derived from embedded references and displacements.

Invocation order is behaviorally significant. Calls with unused results, no represented arguments, repeated argument values, or zero arguments cannot be omitted on that basis. Unknown callees may have observable effects, change globals, invalidate storage, fail, or not return.

Represented argument transformations include:

- Word values shifted left by eight bits.
- Independent forwarding or shifting of the high word of a combined result.
- Duplication of transformed values across argument positions.
- Literal zeros interleaved with dependency-derived values.
- Masking, logical right shifts, and extraction from combined 64-bit results.
- Forwarding additional machine-state values whose complete result conventions are unresolved.

Masks select widths including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits, with smaller masks also present.

Masking, shifting, and narrowing are not interchangeable. A logical right shift after a word-width left shift cannot recover discarded upper bits. Extraction from a 64-bit result must not be replaced with a signed shift of a 32-bit value.

Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, left-shifted-out bits are discarded. A value shifted left by 25 bits and then by seven more bits consequently becomes zero. This is a conditional arithmetic conclusion, not a claim that all represented signed expressions have portable overflow semantics.

Values explicitly captured as zero remain zero through transformations using only those captures. That does not imply that globals remain unchanged across dependencies.

### Direct-memory boundary

The following descriptive roles identify address relationships without assigning object types:

- **Shifted base:** a dependency-derived word shifted left by 25 bits.
- **Scaled anchor:** another returned word multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Compact base:** twice a value obtained by shifting a returned word left eight bits, masking to 15 bits, and logically shifting right six bits.
- **Saved shifted value:** a separate returned word shifted left eight bits.
- **Final low word** and **final high word:** portions of the last combined dependency result.

The direct-memory phase starts with these ordered effects:

1. A word store at the final high word plus 0x14 receives the shifted base.
2. A byte store at the shifted base plus nine receives the final high word’s low byte.
3. A word store at 256 times the final low word plus 0x54 receives the scaled anchor.
4. The shifted-base byte destination is overwritten with the shifted base’s low byte.
5. The scaled-anchor word at offset 0x14 is cleared, followed by an unsigned byte read at offset five.
6. The compact-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared.
7. The captured byte is zero-extended into the scaled-anchor word at offset 0x34.
8. The scaled-anchor word at offset 0x14 is read.
9. The compact-base word is cleared again, followed by two byte stores at compact base plus 0x15, both using the readback word’s low byte.

The readback is a memory dependency, not an unconditional zero constant. Aliasing or memory-specific behavior can distinguish the clear from the later read.

### Memory-derived addressing

An unsigned byte read from fixed address 0x05 supplies another address base. A byte store to fixed address 0x01 follows, using the compact base’s low byte.

Subsequent updates use offsets nine, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x14, 0x15, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. Important relationships include:

- The word at the byte-derived base plus 0x54 is captured before that location is overwritten. Call this captured word the **retained link**.
- Words reached at offset 0x74 become destination bases.
- One nested update separately reads the compact-base offset-0x74 word for its stored value and its offset-0x54 destination. The evidence does not establish stability between those reads or their relative evaluation order.
- Self-address word stores occur, including repeated stores at retained link plus 0x24.
- Repeated byte stores remain distinct effects.
- A word store to fixed address 0x54 receives twice the retained link.
- A byte store to fixed address 0x15 receives the compact base’s low byte.

Further relationships use fourfold and eightfold forms of the retained link. A word read through an unsigned byte plus 0x74 supplies another base. A word read at offset four subsequently supplies a byte-read base; the byte at that base plus five is read twice, with intervening mutations. These are distinct reads, not one stable field lookup.

No linked-list or record-layout interpretation is established.

### Constant stores and captured destination base

For the remaining description:

- **Quadrupled link** means four times the retained link.
- **Adjusted base** means the quadrupled link minus 0x96.
- **Publication base** means four times the adjusted base.
- **Late link** means the word obtained through the second of the offset-five byte reads described above, followed by an offset-0x74 word read.
- **Doubled late base** means twice the late link.
- **Stored link value** means four times the late link.
- **Captured global base** means the word read from a separate global location immediately before the fixed-address 0x60 store.

The function performs three distinct constant stores: byte 0x3A at four times the late link, halfword 0x3A at eight times the late link, and word 0x3A at four times the late link plus 0x20.

The word destination at publication base plus 0x20 then receives, in order:

- 0x20.
- 0x2F.
- The zero-extended byte read from publication base plus four.
- 0x20 again.

**The separate global word is read next, before the word store of 0x20 to fixed address 0x60.** The captured global base remains the destination base for the following repeated-store phase. It is not reloaded after the 0x60 store, even if that store affects the global’s backing storage through the actual memory environment.

The repeated phase has four ordered effects per group:

| Effect | Destination | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Word store | Eightfold destination plus 0x54 | Stored link value |
| Byte store | Captured global base plus 0x19 | Publication base’s low byte |
| Byte store | Publication base plus 0x11 | Captured global base’s low byte |
| Word store | Captured global base plus 0x34 | Eightfold destination |

The first group uses eight times the retained link. Subsequent explicit repetitions use eight times the adjusted base. These stores do not establish synchronization or ownership publication.

This contract leaves the total multiplicity of that extended repetition **documented_only**; it does not supply a complete bus-access trace.

### Final-phase entry and fixed address 0x3A

After the repeated phase, the required memory-access order begins with:

1. A 32-bit capture from fixed address 0xA9.
2. A 32-bit capture from stored link value plus 0x74.
3. A byte store to fixed address 0x3A.
4. A 32-bit capture from doubled late base plus 0x14.
5. A 32-bit capture from publication base plus four.

The byte stored to 0x3A is formed from the **adjusted base**, not the publication base: narrow the adjusted base to an eight-bit character value, multiply that character value by eight, add 0x30, and retain the resulting low byte.

With conventional eight-bit character narrowing and ordinary promoted arithmetic, either signed or unsigned character interpretation produces the same final low eight bits. This does not establish general character signedness elsewhere.

The 0x3A store therefore precedes both later computed word captures. If its destination overlaps either capture, its effect must be included in that read.

### Final updates and all fixed-address 0x67 reloads

The function next writes the publication base’s low byte at doubled late base plus 0x0D; writes byte 0x6C at publication base plus eight; writes the captured global base’s low byte at publication-base offsets 0x0D and nine; and writes a self-address word at doubled late base plus 0x54.

Fixed address 0x67 then receives the low byte of the word captured from 0xA9. **Three subsequent byte reads of 0x67 are represented:**

- **First reload:** after the word read from doubled late base plus 0x44, it supplies the byte store to fixed address 0x21.
- **Second reload:** later supplies a byte store to publication base plus nine.
- **Third reload:** separately supplies another byte store to that same publication-base offset-nine destination.

Between the second and third reloads, the function performs a byte store at doubled late base plus nine, a word store of the captured 0xA9 value at publication base plus 0x44, and a word read at publication base plus 0x54. The third reload follows that word read.

None of these reloads may be replaced unconditionally with the originally written byte or a prior reload. Intervening accesses may alias 0x67.

Other effects between the first and second reloads include:

- Byte and word stores derived from the captured offset-0x44 word.
- A signed right shift of that captured word, with count taken from the publication base’s low eight bits.
- Two word stores to captured global base plus 0x54, first the captured offset-0x44 word and then the publication base.
- A word read at publication base plus 0x40, whose captured value is written at publication base plus 0x10.
- Stores at captured-global-base offsets 0x74 and 0x44.

The shift count can exceed 31. Excessive-count behavior is **documented_only**; no modulo reduction or clamping is specified.

### Closing memory chain and transfer

The word read from publication base plus 0x54 supplies a **closing destination base**. Following the third 0x67 reload:

- Its offset 0x0D receives the low byte captured earlier from stored link value plus 0x74.
- Doubled late base plus 0x54 receives the earlier capture from doubled late base plus 0x14.
- Publication base plus 0x14 is read.
- Doubled late base plus 0x11 receives the same captured offset-0x74 low byte.
- Publication-base offset nine receives the closing destination base’s low byte, and offset 0x44 receives that base as a word.

A word read from doubled late base plus four supplies a **terminal base**. The closing sequence then:

- Stores halfword 0x65 at twice the terminal base.
- Captures a word from terminal base plus 0x64.
- Writes the publication-base offset-0x14 capture’s low byte at closing destination base plus 0x11.
- Writes word 100 at closing destination base plus 0x54.
- Captures a word from terminal base plus 0x74.
- Reads another destination base from doubled late base plus 0x64.
- Writes the terminal base’s low byte at doubled late base plus nine.
- Stores the captured terminal offset-0x64 word at the newly read destination base plus 0x54.
- Stores that newly read destination base as a word at twice the captured terminal offset-0x64 word.

Only afterward does the final indirect transfer receive 0x72, the captured terminal offset-0x64 word, the earlier doubled-late-base offset-0x14 capture, and the captured terminal offset-0x74 word.

## Acceptance examples

These examples assume execution reaches the relevant accesses with suitable memory and arithmetic semantics.

### Capture before fixed-address mutation

If the separate global read yields base 0x1000, subsequent publication stores use 0x1000 as their captured base. If the later 0x60 store changes what a fresh global read would return, switching to that new value is not equivalent.

### Computed byte at 0x3A

If the adjusted base’s low byte is 0x12, the byte written to 0x3A is 0xC0 under conventional eight-bit narrowing. That store follows the 0xA9 and stored-link offset-0x74 word captures, but precedes the doubled-late-base offset-0x14 and publication-base offset-four captures.

### Independent 0x67 reloads

If the second 0x67 reload returns 0x41 and an intervening access changes 0x67 to 0x9E, the two corresponding publication-base offset-nine stores receive 0x41 and 0x9E respectively. Reusing 0x41 for both loses a represented dependency.

### Width and promotion

A byte store receiving 0x123456AB stores 0xAB. An unsigned byte read yielding 0xE2 subsequently promoted into a word supplies 0x000000E2, not 0xFFFFFFE2.

### Intermediate values

An observer of publication base plus 0x20 sees word stores of 0x20, 0x2F, the promoted byte, and 0x20. Keeping only the final store is insufficient when individual accesses are observable.

### Completion and partial failure

Completion requires the final indirect transfer with the designated captures. Failure after earlier mutation supplies no requirement to undo those effects or return an error code.
