# F-a2d200a7e6f2

## Purpose

F-a2d200a7e6f2 invokes an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect dependencies, transforms returned bit fields, and then performs direct memory mutations through computed and fixed addresses. Its final visible action is a four-argument indirect control transfer.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Recurring offsets do not establish an object layout, and constants resembling character codes do not establish text formatting or an output stream.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** displayed operations, widths, dependencies, and ordering.
- **[INFERENCE]:** conclusions requiring an interpretation of the target machine.
- **documented_only:** behavior whose details remain unresolved or are not fully enumerated here.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Explicit and implicit inputs

The displayed interface has no explicit parameters and no explicit return value. Execution nevertheless consumes:

- An incoming control-link value used to construct the first indirect target.
- An incoming unsigned 32-bit register value used in early argument transformations.
- Primary and secondary results from indirect dependencies.
- Values read from computed memory locations.
- Fixed-address inputs beginning at 0x05, 0x087FBBBC, and 0xA9.
- Later observations of byte storage at 0x67.

The fixed-address input at 0x05 supplies an address-bearing value during the early direct-memory phase. The word at 0x087FBBBC supplies a separately sampled base for the repeated publication region and subsequent updates. Neither input is interchangeable with an arbitrary preexisting object pointer.

A conventional, caller-independent no-argument interface is not established.

## Behavior

### Ordered indirect-dependency phase

The initial phase is a long straight-line sequence of indirect invocations. The first target depends on the incoming control-link value plus 0xFFE. Subsequent targets are generally constructed from embedded control-location values plus the same displacement.

The sequence is observable even when a primary result appears unused. An invocation may modify memory, globals, secondary result registers, or control state. No dependency is assumed to preserve any of those.

Visible argument relationships include:

- The incoming unsigned word shifted left by 13 and right by 11.
- Returned words shifted left by 8.
- A 64-bit result supplying its low word shifted left by 8 and its high word separately.
- Arguments derived from masks, further shifts, literal zeros, and secondary result-register contents.
- Reuse of saved values across intervening invocations.

These relationships establish dataflow, not the meaning of the receiving dependency.

### Field extraction

An early returned word supplies a low-24-bit selection. From that selection, the function derives a field by discarding four low bits and shifting left by 13, and a five-bit field corresponding to original bits 19 through 23.

The five-bit field ranges from 0 through 31. Its left shift by 31 retains only its original low bit under unsigned 32-bit semantics. That low bit is also positioned at bit 16 for later arguments.

A subsequent argument shifts the bit-31-positioned intermediate left by another 30 positions. Under 32-bit machine-word semantics, this yields zero, not a surviving wider field.

Other visible selections include:

- Bits 8 through 23 of a word: range 0 through 65,535.
- Bits 11 through 17 of a saved high result word: range 0 through 127.
- Bits 4 through 16 of another word: a 13-bit field.
- Bits 12 through 23 of a low-24-bit selection: a 12-bit field.

Repeated shifts by 14, 15, 16, 22, 23, 24, and 25 positions occur. Several values are explicitly cleared and later shifted; those derived arguments remain zero. This does not eliminate intervening dependency effects.

### Entry into direct memory mutation

The following descriptive labels identify data relationships without asserting object types:

- **Terminal result:** the last 64-bit result before direct memory mutation.
- **Saved shifted value:** an earlier result shifted left by 25.
- **Shifted address:** a different earlier result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Saved shifted word:** another earlier result shifted left by 8.
- **Scaled index:** a returned word shifted left by 8, masked with 0x7FFF, and shifted right by 6.
- **Doubled index:** twice the scaled index.

The opening direct accesses preserve this ordering:

- A word containing the saved shifted value is written at terminal-high-word plus 0x14.
- Its plus-9 byte destination first receives the low byte of the terminal high word.
- The shifted address is written as a word at terminal-low-word multiplied by 256 plus 0x54.
- The same plus-9 byte destination then receives the low byte of the saved shifted value.
- The word at shifted-address plus 0x14 is cleared before the byte at shifted-address plus 5 is read.
- The word at doubled-index plus 0x14 is cleared.
- The loaded byte is zero-extended into a word at shifted-address plus 0x34.
- The word at shifted-address plus 0x14 is read and saved.
- Doubled-index plus 0x14 is cleared again, followed by two byte stores of the saved word’s low byte at doubled-index plus 0x15.

These accesses are not an atomic initialization. Aliasing can affect later reads.

Under 32-bit word semantics, the scaled index is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. The doubled index is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1,016. Consequently, its plus-0x14 word locations range from 0x14 through 0x40C. No independent allocation base is visibly added.

### Fixed-address input at 0x05

The read beginning at fixed address 0x05 occurs **after both byte stores at doubled-index plus 0x15 and before the byte write at fixed address 0x01**.

Its converted unsigned word becomes the **early sampled base**. Subsequent operations use this saved base, rather than rereading 0x05:

- Fixed address 0x01 receives the low byte of the doubled index.
- Early-sampled-base plus 0x15 receives the low byte of the scaled index.
- Shifted-address plus 0x11 receives the low byte of the saved shifted word.
- A word at the previously saved plus-0x14 value plus 0x54 receives the saved shifted word.
- Shifted-address plus 0x11 is then cleared.
- The old word at early-sampled-base plus 0x54 is read and saved.
- That same plus-0x54 word location is overwritten with the previously saved plus-0x14 value.

The saved old word is called the **saved link** below. It continues to affect addresses and values after its original storage has been overwritten.

The 0x05 sample is not necessarily the invocation-entry value: preceding computed writes may overlap it. Likewise, intervening writes after the sample can affect the later plus-0x54 read. No nonaliasing assumption is established.

### Linked and scaled updates

Subsequent operations load and update words at offsets including 0x04, 0x14, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74, and bytes at offset 0x05. Byte destinations include offsets 0x01, 0x09, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19.

The saved link is stored into its own plus-0x24 word location twice, with other writes between those stores. Its low byte is also written twice to a loaded destination’s plus-9 location.

The doubled-index plus-0x14 word receives a shifted value and then the saved link twice. Fixed address 0x54 receives twice the saved link. Fixed address 0x15 receives the low byte of the doubled index.

Four and eight times the saved link become additional address-bearing quantities. Loaded links are doubled, published into plus-0x44 fields, and reused as addresses. A byte loaded at a computed plus-5 location becomes the base for a word read at that byte value plus 0x74; no separate table base is visibly added.

Repeated loads remain significant. In particular, two reads from a computed plus-5 byte location are separated by writes. Their results are not necessarily equal.

Later, a loaded word determines destinations receiving 0x3A at three different widths and addresses: a byte at four times that word, a halfword at eight times it, and a word at four times it plus 0x20.

### Adjusted destination and fixed-address publication base

An address-bearing quantity equal to four times the saved link is reduced by 0x96. Call that result the **adjusted root**, and four times it the **adjusted base**.

The word at adjusted-base plus 0x20 receives, in order:

1. 0x20.
2. 0x2F.
3. The zero-extended byte freshly read from adjusted-base plus 4.
4. 0x20 again.

The byte read occurs between the second and third stores.

**After the final 0x20 store, the function reads the 32-bit word beginning at fixed address 0x087FBBBC. Before the next publication writes, it writes the 32-bit value 0x20 at fixed address 0x60.**

The word sampled at 0x087FBBBC becomes the **publication base**. This is an established global-memory input, sampled at this point—not an unspecified separately supplied pointer.

Earlier computed writes may have modified its storage. After sampling, subsequent changes to 0x087FBBBC do not replace the saved publication base in the displayed dataflow.

### Repeated publication region

The publication region repeatedly performs an ordered four-write pattern:

- A word publication to a scaled destination plus 0x54.
- A byte publication of the adjusted base’s low byte at publication-base plus 0x19.
- A byte publication of the publication base’s low byte at adjusted-base plus 0x11.
- A word publication of the relevant scaled destination at publication-base plus 0x34.

The first group uses eight times the saved link for its scaled destination. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted root. Thus, both the first word destination and the final word’s value differ between the first group and later groups.

The other saved operands remain unchanged throughout this region. Repeated groups do not reload 0x087FBBBC.

No runtime loop condition or variable repetition count is displayed. Exact access multiplicity is **documented_only** in this contract; the source’s fixed repetitions must not be collapsed to one group where write multiplicity is observable.

### Final reads, publications, and fresh reads of 0x67

After the repeated groups, the function reads a word beginning at 0xA9 and a word at a saved computed address plus 0x74. It then writes a derived byte at fixed address 0x3A, using the low-byte result of eight times the adjusted root plus 0x30.

It saves words from a previously derived doubled base plus 0x14 and from adjusted-base plus 4 before further mutations. Subsequent writes include 0x6C at adjusted-base plus 8 and low-byte publications at plus-0x0D and plus-9 locations.

The low byte of the saved 0xA9 word is published to fixed address 0x67. A computed plus-0x44 word is then read, after which byte storage at 0x67 supplies the write at fixed address 0x21.

Further computed writes follow, including:

- A byte at adjusted-base plus 0x10.
- Two different word values at publication-base plus 0x54.
- A shift-derived byte at the doubled base plus 1.
- A saved word at publication-base plus 0x74.
- A word at adjusted-base plus 0x10, overlapping the earlier byte.
- The doubled base at publication-base plus 0x44.

Only after those operations does a **fresh read of byte storage at 0x67** supply a byte store at adjusted-base plus 9.

Then the function writes the adjusted base’s low byte at doubled-base plus 9, stores the saved 0xA9 word at adjusted-base plus 0x44, and reads the word at adjusted-base plus 0x54. A **second fresh read of byte storage at 0x67** supplies another byte store at adjusted-base plus 9.

These two later values are memory observations at their respective read points. They are not specified as cached copies of the initial publication, nor as necessarily equal to each other. Computed writes between these observations may alias 0x67. The plus-9 destination is subsequently overwritten again with a loaded link’s low byte.

The shift-derived byte uses a signed right shift whose count comes from the adjusted base’s low byte. Its exact target semantics remain unresolved.

### Final link updates and control transfer

The remaining operations include restoring a saved word to a computed plus-0x54 location, reading another plus-0x14 word, and loading a link through the doubled base plus 4.

A 16-bit store places 0x65 at twice that loaded link. A further word is loaded through its plus-0x64 location. A derived plus-0x54 destination receives the 32-bit value 100. Another word is loaded through the link’s plus-0x74 location.

The function then updates another derived plus-0x54 link and stores a word at twice the previously loaded plus-0x64 word.

The final unresolved target receives, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The word loaded through the late plus-0x64 access.
3. The word saved earlier from the doubled base plus 0x14.
4. The word loaded through the late plus-0x74 access.

These are not established as text, buffer, length, or object arguments.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

### Width-sensitive extraction

For an early returned word of 0x00F80000, bits 19 through 23 yield 31. Its low-bit-derived bit-16 value is 0x00010000. Under 32-bit machine-word semantics, positioning the field at bit 31 yields 0x80000000; shifting that intermediate left by 30 yields zero.

### Early fixed-address sample

If the read at 0x05 converts to 0x80, the subsequent base-relative byte destination is 0x95 and the saved-old-word location is 0xD4. The read at 0x05 follows both initial doubled-index plus-0x15 byte stores and precedes the write at 0x01.

The old word at 0xD4 is sampled before its overwrite. It must not be replaced by the overwrite value in later saved-link calculations.

### Publication-base sampling

If the word at 0x087FBBBC is 0x1000 when sampled, publication-base plus 0x19 is 0x1019 and publication-base plus 0x34 is 0x1034.

The sample follows the adjusted destination’s final 0x20 store and precedes the word write at 0x60. Subsequent publication groups use the saved 0x1000 even if later writes change the original global storage.

### Fresh 0x67 observations

If the initial publication to 0x67 is 0xA1, but aliasing writes leave 0xB2 there before the first later read and 0xC3 before the second, the corresponding adjusted-base plus-9 stores receive 0xB2 and 0xC3. Reusing 0xA1 for both violates the read dependencies.

### Low-address and width boundaries

For an index-producing word of 1, the scaled index is 4 and the doubled index is 8. Its plus-0x14 location is address 0x1C, not an implicit heap-relative address.

A byte value of 0xE3 published as a word becomes 0x000000E3. The halfword store of 0x65 affects two bytes; the word store of 100 affects four, subject to environmental access support.

### Partial execution and final transfer

If a later access faults, acceptance must not require rollback of preceding completed writes.

If the final target is reached under a compatible calling convention, its first argument is 0x72 and its other arguments have the saved and late-loaded origins specified above.
