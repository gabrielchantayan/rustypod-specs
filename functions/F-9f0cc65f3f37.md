### Incoming-payload writes and forwarding

Ordinary incoming-payload writes use destinations eight times a fresh primary result. The associated following interaction receives two equal arguments, each scaled from that result by a factor of 16,384.

Three boundary types differ:

- **Exceptional producer:** receives four times a preceding primary result. Its own result supplies a destination at twice its value, followed by two equal arguments scaled by 4,096.
- **Four-argument interaction:** the byte destination remains eightfold. Argument positions one, two, and four receive the producer’s 16,384-fold value; position three receives the incoming 32-bit word scaled by 1,024.
- **Later fourfold-argument boundary:** the destination remains eightfold, but both following arguments are four times the producer result.

Immediately after the four-argument interaction, another interaction receives eight times its secondary output. Neither the primary result nor the earlier destination-producing result is the source of this argument.

The surrounding forwarding interactions include fourfold scaling of primary results and unchanged forwarding of opaque words. Some equal-argument interactions produce a new primary result that is then forwarded at fourfold scale. Such forwarding uses the returned value, not the arguments that produced it.

Before the first wide-result boundary, one incoming-payload destination producer contributes a retained historical word. That word already includes a factor of 16,384. Later historical relationships apply to this scaled word, not directly to the original producer result.

### Literal-zero and address-derived writes
