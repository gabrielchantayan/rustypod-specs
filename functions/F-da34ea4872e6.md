# F-da34ea4872e6

## Purpose

F-da34ea4872e6 updates a position, an inclusive endpoint, and a copy of the position in a supplied mutable record.

The position advances by a supplied step. Reaching or exceeding the supplied bound resets the advanced position to zero; excess advancement is discarded rather than reduced modulo the bound. The function then derives an endpoint. If that endpoint reaches or exceeds the bound, the endpoint is limited to one below the bound and the position is shifted backward accordingly.

This operation does not enforce a nonnegative position, a positive step, a positive bound, or a meaningful interval. Its arithmetic resembles movement of a fixed-size inclusive interval within an upper boundary, but that application interpretation is inferred intent.

## Behavior

### Advancement and reset

The advancement decision uses the initially read Position and Step. Their signed sum is written to Position before Bound is read.

If the captured Bound is less than or equal to that sum, Position is overwritten with zero. Otherwise the advanced position remains. Equality therefore belongs to the reset case.

This is a threshold reset, not circular arithmetic. An overshoot does not preserve a remainder. A negative advance does not receive separate handling, and there is no lower-bound correction.

For example, with Step 3 and Bound 10, an initial Position of 20 advances to 23 and then resets to zero. It does not become 3.

### Endpoint and backward adjustment

Endpoint derivation uses a new Position read after the possible reset. The candidate endpoint is mathematically:

\[
\text{candidate endpoint}
=
\text{post-advancement Position}+\text{Step}-1.
\]

If the candidate is strictly below the captured Bound, it becomes the final Endpoint and Position receives no further adjustment.

If the candidate is greater than or equal to Bound, the final values are mathematically:

\[
\text{Endpoint}=\text{Bound}-1,
\qquad
\text{Position}=\text{Bound}-\text{Step}.
\]

The second equation describes the resulting value, not a guarantee that only that simplified arithmetic is evaluated. The backward adjustment forms the decremented bound, subtracts Step from it, and subsequently adds one. These intermediate values must also be representable.

Clipping does not ensure a nonnegative Position. In particular, a Step larger than Bound can produce a negative position even after an advancement reset. Neither clipping nor resetting guarantees that the result is a valid interval for an external collection.
