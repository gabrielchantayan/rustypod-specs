# F-c4b34006a13c

## Purpose

F-c4b34006a13c transforms four 32-bit input words into a 64-bit result. Its arithmetic path constructs an unsigned wide product, combines it with an exponent-like quantity, normalizes and rounds a candidate, and either returns that candidate or selects a fallback result. Other paths return special bit patterns or transfer control to an unresolved dependency.

**Inferred intent:** the inputs resemble two binary64 operands supplied as low/high word pairs, and the main arithmetic resembles floating-point multiplication.

**Established limitation:** complete IEEE 754 behavior is not established. External masks and constants are unavailable, repeated external references are distinct reads, and the exceptional address expression and indirect-transfer convention remain unresolved. Conventional floating-point semantics must not replace the literal behavior described below.

### Arithmetic conventions

Word additions, subtractions, left shifts, multiplication by word constants, and complements retain 32 bits. Unsigned right shifts are logical. Partial products of two unsigned 32-bit words have 64-bit results; carries are determined by unsigned overflow.

The exponent-like quantity is compared as signed 32-bit and is right-shifted by 16. The supplied representation indicates a signed right shift; arithmetic sign extension is the working interpretation, with original-machine semantics retained as an uncertainty.

## Behavior

### Classification and field combination

The arithmetic family requires both operands to satisfy:

\[
G1\ \mathbin{\&}\ \neg(\text{high word}\gg4)\ne0.
\]

The first operand is tested first. The second operand’s test, including its separate G1 read, occurs only if the first test succeeds. Either failure selects the special-value family.

Within the arithmetic family, another G1 read selects a field from the first high word. The **first compound field** is:

\[
\bigl(G1\mathbin{\&}(\text{first high word}\gg4)\bigr)
\mathbin{|}
\bigl((\text{first high word}\mathbin{\oplus}\text{second high word})\gg31\bigr).
\]

The sign exclusive-OR is incorporated by **bitwise OR**, not addition. A subsequent G1 read supplies the **second field**:

\[
G1\mathbin{\&}(\text{second high word}\gg4).
\]

These fields affect zero-like sign selection and the later exponent and sign contributions.

### Zero-like path

Multiplication is bypassed when the second field is zero. Otherwise, a further G1 read tests whether the first high word intersects \(G1\ll4\); a zero intersection also bypasses multiplication. That further read is skipped when the second field is zero.

On this path, each high word is modified locally by setting `0x00080000` when either its corresponding low word is nonzero or its low 20 high-word bits are nonzero.

The modified first high word, logically shifted right by 19, is compared with one G3 read. Equality toggles bit 0 of the first compound field. The corresponding comparison for the modified second high word then uses another G3 read and can toggle that bit again.

The result low word is zero. Bit 0 of the final compound field becomes bit 31 of the result high word; all other result bits are zero. The result is therefore exactly one of:

- `0x0000000000000000`;
- `0x8000000000000000`.

These bit patterns do not establish a general IEEE signed-zero policy.

### Product construction

When multiplication proceeds, the **field sum** is the first compound field plus the second field, modulo \(2^{32}\). The **exponent-like quantity** is that sum minus `0x03fc0000`, retained at word width and subsequently interpreted as signed.

Product preparation reads G1 for the second operand first, then independently for the first operand. For each operand, its significand-like high word is formed by clearing the original high-word bits selected by \(G1\ll5\), then setting `0x00100000`. The original low word remains unchanged.

The resulting high/low pairs define unsigned 64-bit quantities. Their full unsigned product is reduced to its upper 96 bits. Carries from the cross-products and the upper half of the low-by-low product are included. If the discarded lowest 32 product bits are nonzero, bit 0 of the lowest retained word is set.

Thus the retained value preserves the discarded word’s nonzero status, not its exact value.

### Normalization and candidate construction

Call the three retained words the top, middle, and bottom words, in decreasing significance.

| Top retained word | Right-shift width | Complementary left-shift width | Normalization adjustment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| At most `0x000001ff` | 20 | 12 | 0 |
| Greater than `0x000001ff` | 21 | 11 | 1 |

The unrounded candidate is assembled as follows:

- Its high-word product portion combines the top word shifted left by the complementary width with the middle word shifted right by the right-shift width, using bitwise OR.
- Its low word combines the middle word shifted left by the complementary width with the bottom word shifted right by the right-shift width, using bitwise OR.
- Its remainder word is the bottom word shifted left by the complementary width, truncated to 32 bits.

The unrounded high word additionally receives, by word-width addition, the exponent contribution:

\[
\left(
(\text{signed exponent-like quantity}\gg16)-4
+\text{normalization adjustment}
\right)\times\texttt{0x00100000}.
\]

A sign contribution is then exclusive-ORed into the candidate high word. That contribution is:

\[
\left(
(\text{first compound field}\mathbin{\oplus}\text{second field})
\mathbin{\&}1
\right)\ll31.
\]

Equivalently, it is bit 0 of the field sum placed in bit 31. Addition carries cannot affect bit 0 and therefore cannot change this sign contribution. The earlier OR that constructs the first compound field can affect it; carries into higher field-sum bits cannot.

### Rounding

A rounding increment occurs exactly when bit 31 of the remainder word is set. A nonzero remainder with bit 31 clear causes no increment.

When incrementing:

- The candidate low word increases by one, modulo \(2^{32}\).
- The candidate high word increases by one exactly when the pre-increment low word is `0xffffffff`.
- If the remainder is exactly `0x80000000`, bit 31 of the top retained product word is set.
- Bit 0 of the incremented candidate low word is cleared if bit 31 of that top retained product word is set.

The last condition includes a top-word bit 31 that was already set before rounding. It is not restricted to exact-half remainders.

**Inferred intent:** with conventional masks and bounded significands, this resembles round-to-nearest with ties to even. The literal conditions govern when those assumptions are unavailable.

### Candidate acceptance and arithmetic fallback

Acceptance uses three short-circuited conditions, in order:

1. Exclusive-OR the sign contribution out of the rounded candidate high word; the signed 32-bit result must be nonnegative.
2. Read G2 and require a nonzero intersection with the unrounded candidate high word **before sign insertion**.
3. Read G2 again and require a nonzero intersection with the complement of the rounded candidate high word **after sign insertion**.

No G2 read occurs if the first condition fails. The second G2 read occurs only if the first mask condition succeeds. Acceptance returns the rounded high and low words.

On rejection:

- If the signed exponent-like quantity exceeds `0x03ffffff`, the low word is zero. The high word comes from an unresolved B1-relative address expression indexed by the rounded high word, converted to a 32-bit word, ORed with `0x7fffffff`, and then exclusive-ORed with `0x000fffff`. These final operations fix bits 0–30 of the high word to `0x7ff00000`; bit 31 remains dependent on the unresolved expression.
- Otherwise, the complete result is zero.

The latter result follows because every signed value not greater than `0x03ffffff` is less than `0x04000000`. The alternative nonzero assignment associated with that second comparison is unreachable under the visible signed comparisons.

### Special-value family

Double the first high word at 32-bit width. A G1 read multiplied by 32, also at word width, supplies its threshold.

Second-operand examination occurs only when the first doubled high word is below that threshold, or equals it while the first low word is zero. Otherwise, execution proceeds to indirect dispatch without the second-operand threshold reads.

When the second operand is examined, two separate G1 reads supply thresholds:

1. One tests whether the second doubled high word is at or above its threshold.
2. The next tests equality with its independently obtained threshold.

A successful equality test forces the at-or-above flag true, even if the preceding comparison was false.

A direct special result is selected if the resulting at-or-above flag is false, or if the equality flag is true and the second low word is zero. All other cases dispatch indirectly.

For a direct special result, select the first doubled high word unless it exceeds `0x001fffff`; then select the second doubled high word.

- If the selected word exceeds `0x001fffff`, return a zero low word and a high word of `0x7ff00000` OR the input sign exclusive-OR in bit 31.
- Otherwise, read G4 for the high word and return low word 1.

**Inferred intent:** these patterns resemble infinity and an invalid-operation or NaN-like result. G4’s unavailable value prevents definitive classification of the latter.

### Indirect dispatch

Every indirect path uses **F-unknown-01**, targeting R1 with its lowest two bits cleared.

One G5 read supplies a cached selector consisting of its low four bits. Every subsequently reached G5 control test uses a fresh read.

| Cached selector | Subsequent controls | Explicit argument words |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Test `0x40` | One word: zero if clear, `0x80000000` if set |
| Other than 8, 9, 10 | None | No explicit arguments shown |
| 8 | `0x40` set | `0`, `0` |
| 8 | `0x40` clear; later `0x10` clear | G7, G6 |
| 8 | `0x40` clear; later `0x10` set | `0`, G8 |
| 9 | `0x100000` clear | `0`, G6 |
| 9 | `0x100000` set; `0x70000` nonzero | `8`, complement payload |
| 9 | `0x100000` set; `0x70000` zero | `0x30000000`, G6, `0x30000000` OR Q1 bit 0 placed in bit 27 |

For selector 8 with `0x40` clear, G7 is read first, then G6, then G5 for the `0x10` test. If that test is set, G8 is subsequently read and replaces the second argument; the first argument becomes zero. The earlier G7 and G6 reads still occur.

The selector-9 complement payload uses another fresh G5 read after its control tests. Its value is obtained by shifting that word left by 15 at word width, complementing the resulting 32 bits, then shifting left once at word width. Complementing before the first shift is not equivalent.

On normal return from the dependency, its reported 64-bit result is returned unchanged.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **OR affects the field, not through a carry.** With first selected field 1, input sign exclusive-OR 1, and second field 2, the first compound field is 1 and the field sum is 3. The sign contribution is `0x80000000`. Treating the initial OR as addition would produce a different field and sign.

2. **Higher carries do not determine sign.** First compound field 1 plus second field 1 produces field sum 2 and sign contribution zero. First compound field 1 plus second field 3 produces sum 4 and the same zero contribution. Both follow directly from the fields’ bit-0 exclusive-OR, irrespective of higher carries.

3. **Zero-like toggles cancel.** If both G3 comparisons succeed, the two bit-0 toggles cancel. The final result depends on the original compound field’s bit 0.

4. **Classification short-circuits.** Failure of the first initial classification test prevents the second initial G1 read. A zero second field prevents the additional zero-like mask read.

5. **Discarded product information is sticky.** A nonzero discarded lowest product word sets bit 0 of the bottom retained word even when that bit was previously clear.

6. **Rounding boundary.** Remainder `0x80000000` causes an increment and clearing of the resulting low bit. A pre-increment low word of `0xffffffff` also increments the high word. Remainder `0x7fffffff` causes no increment.

7. **Acceptance reads are conditional.** A failed signed check causes no G2 read. A passing signed check followed by a failed first mask condition causes exactly one G2 read.

8. **Fallback threshold is signed.** Following rejection, exponent-like value `0x03ffffff` returns zero; `0x04000000` selects the B1-dependent result.

9. **Changing special thresholds matter.** If second-operand examination occurs, its doubled high word is `0x40`, the first threshold is `0x60`, and the equality threshold is `0x40`, equality forces the at-or-above flag true. A nonzero second low word then selects dispatch.

10. **First-operand equality is asymmetric.** Equality with the initial threshold and a nonzero first low word selects dispatch without the second-operand threshold reads.

11. **Complement order matters.** A fresh payload G5 value of zero in the complement-payload branch produces second argument `0xfffffffe`.

12. **Overwritten reads remain observable.** Selector 8 with `0x40` clear and the later `0x10` set reads G7 and G6 before forwarding zero and the subsequently read G8.

## Evidence

Established behavior derives solely from the supplied static representation: branch conditions, word-width operations, partial products and carries, sticky reduction, normalization, rounding, conditional external references, result construction, and indirect transfers.

The sign contribution follows from word-width multiplication by a value having only bit 31 set: only bit 0 of the field sum survives. That bit equals bit 0 of the exclusive-OR of the two addends. Carries cannot affect it; the earlier OR used to construct the first compound field can.

The floating-point interpretation is inferred intent, not an independent behavioral guarantee. No runtime tests have been performed.
