## Behavior

### Arithmetic and address terminology

A **word** is 32 bits; a **byte** is eight bits. Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Right shifts of represented unsigned values are logical.

For a selected value \(s\), this contract distinguishes:

- **Fourfold base:** \(4s\).
- **Sixteenfold base:** \(16s\).
- **Fourfold word location:** \(4s+0x14\).
- **Sixteenfold word location:** \(16s+0x14\).

This distinction matters: several dependencies receive the scaled base, **without** the `0x14` storage displacement.

A **selector-directed store** reads an unsigned byte at a specified selector address, adds `0x11` to its numeric value, and stores a byte at that resulting address. Its destination lies between `0x11` and `0x110`, inclusive. The selector is not a pointer-sized load.

Some shifts and products are represented using signed integers. Portable signed-overflow behavior is not established. Examples using truncation to 32 bits are expressly conditional on that arithmetic model. Address width, byte order, alignment requirements, and fault behavior remain unresolved.
