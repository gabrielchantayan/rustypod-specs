# F-1972727bc9f1

## Purpose

F-1972727bc9f1 performs an ordered series of unresolved indirect invocations, transforms invocation results and explicit inputs, and then mutates memory through computed and fixed addresses before a final unresolved invocation.

No domain-level purpose is established. The constants and recurring offsets do not establish formatting, serialization, object construction, collection traversal, or a device protocol.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** accesses, transformations, and ordering visible in the supplied representation.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations not required for acceptance.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which this document supplies no executable guarantee.

The contract remains partial at unresolved invocation boundaries and where the representation does not establish execution semantics. It does not treat unknown dependencies as pure, successful, or side-effect-free.

## Behavior

The invocation-dominated region is straight-line in the supplied representation: no explicit conditional branch or iteration construct appears.

Three invocations with no displayed arguments precede an invocation receiving two copies of the fourth argument shifted left by 7. Later boundaries receive the third argument shifted left by 4 and the fourth argument shifted left by 25, together with values derived from earlier invocation results.

### Arithmetic and widths

The representation uses 8-, 16-, 32-, and 64-bit quantities. Most transformations and address calculations operate on represented 32-bit values.

A logical shift of an unsigned 64-bit result container precedes narrowing in several extractions. Shifts by 34, 36, and 44 therefore correspond respectively to the upper 32-bit portion shifted logically right by 2, 4, and 12. They are not oversized shifts of a previously narrowed word.

Masks select fields of several widths. Retaining the low 20 bits and shifting logically right by 10 gives a range of 0–1023; retaining 19 bits before that shift gives 0–511. Mask placement matters: masking before a shift is not generally interchangeable with masking afterward.

Signed representations appear in left shifts and multiplication. A portable overflow model is not established. Examples using modulo-2³² arithmetic are conditional on that model, not assertions that the execution environment implements it.

Byte and halfword stores retain the low 8 and 16 bits respectively. Explicit unsigned-byte loads subsequently stored as words are zero-extended. Signed right-shift behavior and variable-count shift handling remain unresolved.

### Direct-memory entry

The following descriptive labels identify values, not recovered object types:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Large base | An earlier invocation result multiplied by 0x02000000. |
| Packed base | Another invocation result shifted left by 25. |
| Small base | Twice an unsigned quantity obtained by retaining 15 bits of a shifted result and shifting logically right by 6. |
| Final pair | The low and high 32-bit portions of the last represented 64-bit result before direct mutation. |

The initial write ordering is established: a word carrying the packed base goes to the final pair’s high portion plus 0x14; a byte carrying that high portion goes to packed base plus 9; a word carrying the large base goes to 256 times the low portion plus 0x54; a second byte write at packed base plus 9 carries the packed base; then a word at large base plus 0x14 is cleared.

Both writes at packed base plus 9 remain events. Matching only the final byte is insufficient where write observation matters.

An unsigned byte is then captured from large base plus 5. A word at small base plus 0x14 is cleared before that captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at large base plus 0x34. The word at large base plus 0x14 is subsequently reread. It cannot automatically be replaced by the earlier zero because intervening computed stores may alias it.

### Fixed-address input at 0x05 and linked mutation

After the next clearing of small base plus 0x14 and two byte stores at small base plus 0x15, execution reads storage at fixed address 0x05 and converts the result to an unsigned 32-bit value. This captured value becomes a base for later accesses.

**The 0x05 read precedes the byte write at address 0x01.** That write carries the low byte of the small base. There is no replacement read of 0x05 after the write at 0x01.

The captured base subsequently supplies:

- A byte destination at offset 0x15.
- A word source at offset 0x54.
- A word destination at offset 0x54, after the old word there has been saved.

Thus 0x05 is an input dependency, not merely a nearby constant or implied zero. If the write at 0x01 overlaps its storage, subsequent accesses still use the already captured base. The original load width and extension rules remain **documented_only**.

The saved word from captured base plus 0x54 becomes another address-bearing value. Its scaled forms and values reached through offsets 4, 0x44, 0x64, and 0x74 participate in further mutation.

This region includes repeated self-valued stores, duplicated byte stores, and replacement of recently written fields. A byte loaded at offset 5 can itself become the base for a word read at offset 0x74. A byte-derived address must not be widened into an assumed pointer obtained from a word field.

### Constants and repeated writes

For the following description:

- **Anchor** is the saved word previously read from the base captured at 0x05 plus 0x54.
- **Adjusted value** is four times the anchor minus 0x96.
- **Computed base** is four times the adjusted value.
- **Colon base** is four times a value obtained through a byte-based offset-0x74 word read.
- **Captured global base** is the global word read immediately before the fixed-address word write at 0x60.

Three stores carry 0x3A: a byte at colon base, a halfword at twice colon base, and a word at colon base plus 0x20.

At computed base plus 0x20, four word writes occur in order: 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from computed base plus 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the 0x2F write and before its own resulting word write.

**The global-base read follows those four writes and precedes the word write of 0x20 at address 0x60.** Repeated writes use the captured global value; no fresh global read after the 0x60 write is represented. If the fixed-address write overlaps global storage, it does not retroactively change the captured base.

The repeated region cross-links scaled addresses through word stores at offset 0x54, byte stores at captured global base plus 0x19 and computed base plus 0x11, and word stores at captured global base plus 0x34.

The first group uses eight times the anchor for its scaled destination base and final word value. Later groups instead use eight times the adjusted value. Within each group, the offset-0x54 word write precedes the two byte writes, which precede the offset-0x34 word write.

The repetition is straight-line, not established traversal or buffer advancement. Its exact count remains **documented_only** in this contract.

### Final captures and fixed-address reads at 0x67

The final region first captures a word at 0xA9 and a word at colon base plus 0x74. It then writes a byte at 0x3A before capturing additional words from a doubled, linked base plus 0x14 and computed base plus 4.

These captures serve different purposes: later writes do not replace the saved offset-0x14 value or cause it to be reread for the final invocation.

After several computed byte and word writes, the low byte of the captured 0xA9 word is stored at 0x67. **Three later byte-read boundaries at 0x67 are established:**

| Read boundary | Ordering and use |
|---|---|
| Copy to 0x21 | After the write to 0x67 and a word read from the doubled linked base plus 0x44, the byte currently at 0x67 is copied to fixed address 0x21. |
| First later computed-base byte store | After intervening computed stores, a word read at computed base plus 0x40, and further word stores, 0x67 is reread for a byte write at computed base plus 9. |
| Second later computed-base byte store | After another byte store, a word store at computed base plus 0x44, and a word read at computed base plus 0x54, 0x67 is reread for another byte write at computed base plus 9. |

The intervening stores include word destinations derived from the captured global base and computed base. They may overlap 0x67. Consequently, neither later byte read may be replaced by the earlier value written to 0x67, nor may the two later reads be merged.

The first copy is also specified as a read, not as forwarding a saved byte. No external-mutation or memory-mapped-access behavior is established.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

### Explicit-input shifts

Assuming low-word 32-bit shift semantics and normal return from preceding dependencies, fourth argument 1 supplies two values of 128 at the early duplicated-argument boundary. Third argument 1 supplies 16 at its early shifted-input boundary.

### Extraction and narrowing

Retaining the low 20 bits of a word whose selected bits are all set, then shifting logically right by 10, produces 1023.

An explicit unsigned-byte load of 0xE3 followed by its word store carries 0x000000E3. A byte store of an address-bearing value ending in 0xA7 carries only 0xA7.

### Capture at 0x05

If the 0x05 read converts to 0x120, later accesses through that captured base include locations 0x135 and 0x174. A change to the source storage caused by the subsequent write at 0x01 does not select a new base.

This example requires the unresolved source-width and extension semantics to produce 0x120.

### Global capture before 0x60

If the global read produces base 0x200, repeated writes use that captured value, including destinations 0x219 and 0x234. This remains true if the following word write at 0x60 changes the global’s backing storage.

Reading the global only after writing 0x60 is not equivalent.

### Independent reads at 0x67

Suppose the initial byte written to 0x67 is 0xA4, but an intervening computed store leaves 0xB7 there before the first later read. That read supplies 0xB7 to computed base plus 9.

If another intervening effect leaves 0xC2 before the second later read, that boundary supplies 0xC2. Reusing 0xA4 or the first later read is not accepted under these conditions.

### Observable write ordering

At computed base plus 0x20, a write-sensitive observer must distinguish 0x20, 0x2F, the zero-extended loaded byte, and 0x20, in that order. Final-state equality alone does not satisfy this trace requirement.

### Arithmetic and completion

Under truncation to 32 bits after each shift, a left shift by 25 followed by a left shift by 7 produces zero.

If all preceding boundaries complete and the final invocation is reached, its first displayed argument is 0x72. No particular return status is required without resolving that target.
