### Integer widths and arithmetic

Most arithmetic and address-forming quantities are represented at 32 bits. Some dependency results are represented at 64 bits. Direct accesses include 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, and 32-bit words.

Under a **conditional 32-bit truncating interpretation**, additions, subtractions, multiplications, and left shifts retain the low 32 result bits. Byte and halfword stores retain the low eight and low 16 bits respectively. Unsigned right shifts introduce zero bits.

Consequences under that interpretation include:

- Shifting the fourth argument left by 28 retains only its original low four bits.
- Shifting that already-truncated intermediate left by another four produces zero.
- Restricting a word to its low 20 bits and shifting right by ten extracts bits 10 through 19, giving a result from 0 through 1023.
- Restricting a word to its low 24 bits before shifting is not generally equivalent to shifting the unrestricted word.
- Right-shifting a full unsigned 64-bit result by 34, 36, or 44 extracts its upper 30, 28, or 20 bits respectively.

These deductions are not a claim that all represented signed expressions have defined language-level overflow or shift behavior. Exact target arithmetic semantics remain unresolved where the representation alone is insufficient.

A late signed-word right shift uses a count restricted to eight bits. That restriction permits counts above 31; it does not establish how such counts execute. Right-shift behavior for negative operands is also not resolved.

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Entry-argument truncation.** Under 32-bit truncating shifts, a fourth argument of 1 contributes `0x10000000` to the early invocation. Its later additional four-position shift yields zero, not a 64-bit retained value.

2. **Full-result extraction.** If a represented 64-bit result has upper word `0x12345678`, shifting the full unsigned result right by 36 yields `0x01234567`, independently of its low word.

3. **Unsigned widening.** An offset-5 byte read returning `0xE2` contributes `0x000000E2` to a widened word store, not a sign-extended value.

4. **Intermediate writes.** Tracing memory must expose the offset-`0x20` word writes of `0x20`, `0x2F`, the byte-derived value, and `0x20` in that order.

5. **Repeated-group distinction.** If the earlier quantity is `0x100`, the adjusted quantity is `0x36A`. Under truncating arithmetic, the first group uses `0x800` for both its scaled destination base and its offset-`0x34` payload. Subsequent groups use `0x1B50` for those two roles.

6. **Captured base ordering.** If the fixed-state base load returns one address and the later write at `0x60` changes that state through overlap, the repeated run still uses the saved address. Moving the load after the write changes the represented behavior.

7. **Saved value versus reload.** If the saved `0xA9` word has low byte `0x12`, the explicit write at `0x67` receives `0x12`. If an intervening computed store changes `0x67` to `0x34`, a subsequent reload for an offset-9 write may use `0x34`. Reusing `0x12` unconditionally would lose the reload behavior.
