### Width-sensitive transformations

The representation distinguishes 8-, 16-, 32-, and 64-bit quantities.

For a represented 64-bit result, the low word contains bits 0–31 and the high word bits 32–63. Some extractions instead shift the entire unsigned 64-bit result by 34, 36, or 44 bits before narrowing. These are not shifts of the low word.

Masks select low-order fields of multiple widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. A separate one-bit selection also occurs. Masking and shifting must preserve their represented order: discarded bits cannot subsequently contribute.

Unsigned right shifts are zero-filling. Unsigned 32-bit transformations retain their word width. Byte stores retain the low eight bits; halfword stores retain the low sixteen bits. Unsigned byte reads widen to values from 0 through 255.

Many other left shifts, multiplications, additions, and subtractions are represented as signed 32-bit expressions. Their overflow and negative-operand semantics are not independently established. Therefore:

- Unlimited-precision mathematical arithmetic is not an established replacement.
- Portable signed-arithmetic behavior is not established.
- Truncating modulo-\(2^{32}\) behavior is conditional on the actual machine-word interpretation.

Successive word shifts must not silently become wider arithmetic. Under truncating 32-bit semantics, shifting a word left by 28 and subsequently by another 28 produces zero; it is not a 64-bit shift by 56.

### Opening memory mutations

After the indirect-call sequence, explicit memory mutation begins. Addresses depend on the last results and retained earlier values.

The opening group establishes the following ordering:

- A word store at offset 0x14 from a high-result-derived base precedes a byte store at offset 0x09 from another computed base.
- A word store at offset 0x54 from a scaled low-result base precedes a second byte store to that same offset-0x09 location.

The first update group writes a word at offset 0x54 from eight times the retained word, then bytes at stored-state-base-plus-0x19 and adjusted-base-plus-0x11, then a word containing eight times the retained word at stored-state-base-plus-0x34.

| First | Supplies the byte write to 0x21. A computed word read at offset 0x44 intervenes between the assignment to 0x67 and this read. |

For stable ordinary memory with no intervening overlapping writes or external changes, all three reads can yield the assigned byte. That equality is conditional, not an invariant.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial forwarding.** With a fourth input of 3, F-U001 receives 96 in its fourth explicit position. Its first three positions retain their input bit patterns. This example avoids signed-shift overflow.

2. **Word separation.** At a boundary using a 64-bit result’s low word shifted by eight, high word unchanged, and high word shifted by six, low word 1 and high word 2 produce 256, 2, and 128.

3. **Masking.** At a boundary selecting 24 low bits before an unsigned left shift by six, changing only the upper eight input bits does not change that derived argument.

4. **Byte widening.** A represented unsigned byte read yielding 0xFE supplies word value 254, not a sign-extended negative value.

5. **Ordered constant writes.** An observable destination receiving 0x20, 0x2F, a freshly read byte, and 0x20 must expose all four writes in that order. A final value of 0x20 alone is insufficient.

6. **Aliasing-sensitive reload.** If an intervening store overlaps a word previously cleared at offset 0x14, its subsequent read depends on memory after that store, not solely on the earlier clear.

7. **Equal repeated-update expressions.** For retained word 50, the adjusted word is \(4 \times 50 - 150 = 50\). The initial and subsequent offset-0x34 expressions both yield 400. Acceptance must not require unequal values or distinct leading word-store addresses.

8. **Unequal repeated-update expressions.** For retained word 51, the adjusted word is 54. Without overflow, the initial offset-0x34 value is 408 and subsequent values are 432.

9. **Stable fixed-byte reads.** If the 0xA9 word has low byte 0xA5 and 0x67 remains unchanged after assignment, the read supplying 0x21 and the two later reads supplying adjusted-base-plus-0x09 each yield 0xA5.

10. **Changed fixed-byte reads.** If a valid intervening computed-address write changes 0x67 before the second read, that read supplies the changed byte. If another intervening write changes it again before the third read, the third consumer receives the newer byte. Neither consumer is required to use the cached low byte of the 0xA9 word.

11. **Partial failure.** If execution cannot continue at a later access, earlier writes are not required to disappear; no rollback is established.

12. **Final boundary.** On reaching F-U003, its first explicit argument is 0x72. Its other arguments are retained load results, not established functions of the four entry inputs alone.
