# F-77b67f3be594

## Purpose

F-77b67f3be594 invokes an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect dependencies, derives numeric fields and address-like values from their results, reads and modifies fixed and computed storage, and finishes with a four-argument indirect invocation.

No domain-level purpose is established. Constants resembling printable characters, self-reference stores, and repeated publications do not independently establish formatting, initialization, serialization, or object destruction.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented invocations, explicit access widths, value conversions, data dependencies, and ordering between separate accesses.
- **Conditional interpretation:** consequences requiring an arithmetic model, storage declarations, valid mappings, or specified dependency behavior.
- **documented_only:** unresolved semantics or documentary limits for which this contract supplies no executable requirement.

“Base,” “reference,” and “publication” describe numeric relationships and access patterns. They do not imply valid objects, ownership, or synchronization.

## Behavior

The represented phase is straight-line: no local loop or conditional branch selects its invocations. Dependencies themselves may branch, fail, or not return.

### Arithmetic model

Under a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector model**, arithmetic retains the low thirty-two bits:

- A twenty-five-bit left shift retains only seven original bits.
- A thirty-one-bit left shift retains only one original bit.
- Successive left shifts totaling at least thirty-two discard the original word.
- Left shift by eight followed by logical right shift by eight retains only the original low twenty-four bits.

A combined-result logical shift beyond thirty-one bits selects part of its high word, not its low word.

Signed multiplication, subtraction, left shifts, overflow, and right shifts require additional execution-model information. Wrapping interpretations remain conditional. Address arithmetic must not silently be widened to avoid truncation.

Character conversion also requires care. Conversion to a character value occurs before some later arithmetic or storage conversion. Character signedness and out-of-range conversion behavior are unresolved; explicit unsigned byte loads are not subject to that signedness uncertainty.

### Opening direct-memory effects

The dependency phase leaves several retained quantities:

- A result shifted left twenty-five bits, used as an address-like word.
- A separate **large base**, formed by multiplication by `2²⁵`.
- An earlier result already shifted left eight bits.
- A **selector**, formed by an eight-bit left shift, a fifteen-bit mask, and a logical right shift by six.
- A final combined result, consumed as separate high and low words.

Under the conditional word model, the selector is a multiple of four from `0` through `508`; twice it is a multiple of eight from `0` through `1016`.

The opening effects have these ordering requirements:

- A word publication at offset `0x54` from the final low word multiplied by `256` precedes another character-width store to that same offset-`9` destination.

Later, fixed `0x15` receives the doubled-selector base after explicit one-byte conversion, again with unresolved destination width.

The access graph contains important distinctions:

- An unsigned byte read at computed offset `5` becomes a numeric base for a word read at offset `0x74`; it is not a pointer-width load.
- A word read at offset `0x64` supplies a new base for subsequent stores.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional acceptance requirements, not executed tests.

1. **Unsigned widening.** An explicit unsigned-byte capture of `0xFE` is published as word `0x000000FE`, not a sign-extended word.

2. **Selector boundary.** Under the 32-bit model, all seven low input bits set produce selector `508` and doubled selector `1016`.

3. **Fixed-access width discipline.** A one-byte-converted operand assigned to fixed `0x01`, `0x15`, or `0x67` does not justify requiring an eight-bit write. Likewise, fixed `0xA9` captured into a 32-bit quantity does not justify requiring a 32-bit read.

4. **Promoted character arithmetic.** If the adjusted quantity is `254`, unsigned eight-bit character conversion gives promoted result `2080`. Under signed eight-bit conversion yielding `−2`, the promoted result is `32`. An applicable eight-bit destination conversion gives byte `0x20` in both cases; a suitable wider destination can distinguish the results.

5. **Conditional byte overlap.** If fixed `0x3A` has an eight-bit destination and overlaps a later doubled-reference offset-`0x14` capture, that capture reflects the intervening byte according to byte order. Without that declaration, the mutation’s extent is unresolved. The two preceding captures remain retained.

6. **Publication-base retention.** If the publication-base capture yields P and the subsequent fixed-`0x60` assignment would make a reread yield Q, later publications still use P.

7. **Word–character–word order.** If the shift is supported and its destination overlaps publication-base offset `0x54`, the adjusted-base word publication follows and may overwrite the intervening character-width store.

8. **Intermediate visibility.** Under a device-backed interpretation honoring represented accesses, the four adjusted-base offset-`0x20` word publications remain distinct.

9. **Terminal capture timing.** If intervening writes alter late-base offset `0x74`, the fourth argument uses its later capture. The second retains the earlier offset-`0x64` value.

10. **Partial progress.** A later fault does not establish reversal of earlier effects. Local normal completion requires F-U03 to return.
