## Behavior

### Indirect invocations and arithmetic

The prefix is a long, straight-line sequence of indirect invocations without an explicit loop or conditional branch. The initial destination depends on incoming control-link state plus displacement 0xFFE. Later destinations predominantly derive from embedded control-location values using the same displacement.

Invocation order and designated result provenance are significant. Discarding a result does not permit discarding its invocation. An invocation without represented arguments is not established to have no implicit inputs.

Argument transformations include:

- Left shifts of word values, particularly by 8 and 15 bits, with other counts through 31.
- Masks selecting low-bit portions before additional shifts.
- Logical right shifts of unsigned quantities.
- Separate consumption of low and high result components.
- Higher-position slices of combined results.
- Repeated argument values and explicit zeros.

Masks discard bits rather than validate them. Captured values can survive many subsequent invocations; a later calculation must not substitute a newer result.

**[INFERENCE] Under a 32-bit wrapping machine-word interpretation**, shifted and multiplied word values retain their low 32 bits. For example, a left shift by 25 followed by a left shift by 7 eliminates all bits. A left shift by 8 followed by a logical right shift by 8 retains only the original low 24 bits.

This interpretation is conditional. Signed-looking shifts and multiplications do not independently establish overflow behavior, and address arithmetic is not proven to wrap.

### Opening updates and the first explicit globals

The opening phase writes the shifted base as a word at final-result-high offset 0x14. It then writes the low byte of that high component at shifted-base offset 0x09, stores the primary base at the address formed by scaling final-result-low by 256 and adding 0x54, and overwrites the earlier byte with the shifted base’s low byte.

Primary-base offset 0x14 is cleared before the byte at primary-base offset 0x05 is sampled. Compact-base offset 0x14 is then cleared before the sampled byte is stored, zero-extended, at primary-base offset 0x34.

The word at primary-base offset 0x14 is subsequently captured. Another clear of compact-base offset 0x14 precedes two separate byte stores of the captured word’s low byte at compact-base offset 0x15. Aliasing prevents universally replacing the captured word with zero.

The next explicit low-address sequence is:

- Read the byte at absolute address **0x05**, extending it to a word used as a small base.
- Write the compact base’s low byte at absolute address **0x01**.
- Write the compact index’s low byte at small-base offset 0x15.

The read at 0x05 therefore precedes the write at 0x01. Neither operation can be subsumed into an assumed independent object update.

The saved shifted word’s low byte is written at primary-base offset 0x11. A word store through the captured primary-offset-0x14 value intervenes before that byte is cleared. The small-base offset-0x54 word is captured and then replaced with the captured primary-offset-0x14 value. Call the displaced word the **link seed**.

### Linking and scaled updates

Subsequent accesses use the link seed and words reached through offsets 0x74 as further addresses. They include self-valued stores at offset 0x24, repeated byte writes at offset 0x09, and repeated updates of compact-base offset 0x14.

Two represented word reads occur through compact-base offset 0x74 in a store that places a read value at offset 0x54 from a read-derived base. They are not specified as one cached read; their operand evaluation order is unresolved.

An explicit word write at absolute address **0x54** stores the link seed shifted left by one. It occurs:

- After a compact-base offset-0x14 store of the earlier primary-result-derived value shifted left by 26.
- Before two stores of the link seed itself at compact-base offset 0x14.

After those two stores, two byte writes at compact-base offset 0x15 supply the low byte of four times the link seed. The byte at absolute address **0x15** is then written with the compact base’s low byte.

Later updates operate through fourfold and eightfold link-seed values. They include reads through offsets 0x04, 0x05, 0x64, and 0x74, plus writes through offsets 0x09, 0x0D, 0x14, 0x15, 0x44, and 0x54.

Byte reads at offset 0x05 are unsigned and become address inputs. Separate reads remain fresh reads, even when they use the same represented address. In particular, a later offset-0x05 sample selects an offset-0x74 word used below as the **payload origin**. Its doubled value remains an address used in the final region.

Define the **adjusted unit** as four times the link seed minus 0x96, and the **adjusted base** as four times that adjusted unit. Arithmetic-width qualifications still apply.

At adjusted-base offset 0x20, the represented word-store order is 0x20, 0x2F, the zero-extended byte freshly sampled from adjusted-base offset 0x04, and 0x20 again. Sequence preservation is required conservatively; external visibility of the intermediate values is not established.

The representation then reads the word at doubled-payload-origin offset **0x44**, and only afterward copies a fresh read of byte **0x67** to absolute byte **0x21**. Thus:

- The computed self-valued word store precedes the 0x67 write and the 0x21 copy.
- A computed word **read**, not an additional computed word store, lies between the explicit 0x67 write and the 0x21 copy.

The word read at offset 0x44 drives a byte write at adjusted-base offset 0x10 and a word write at publication-base offset 0x54. Its shifted form supplies a byte at doubled-payload-origin offset 0x01; the shift count is the adjusted base’s low byte. Signed right-shift and large-count behavior remain unresolved.

Publication-base offset 0x54 is then overwritten with the adjusted base. A fresh word at adjusted-base offset 0x40 is captured, the retained adjusted-offset-0x04 word is stored at publication-base offset 0x74, and the offset-0x40 capture is stored at adjusted-base offset 0x10. This last word store overlaps the earlier byte at offset 0x10.

After another computed word store, adjusted-base offset 0x09 receives a **fresh read of byte 0x67**. More computed byte and word writes follow, including a store of the captured 0xA9 word at adjusted-base offset 0x44. A new base is read from adjusted-base offset 0x54. Adjusted-base offset 0x09 then receives **another fresh read of byte 0x67**.

These reads do not necessarily reuse the low byte originally written to 0x67. Intervening computed writes may overlap that byte.

### Completion and final transfer

The new base is updated at offset 0x0D with the low byte of the earlier payload-offset-0x74 capture. The retained doubled-payload-origin offset-0x14 word is stored at doubled-payload-origin offset 0x54.

A fresh adjusted-base offset-0x14 word supplies a later truncated byte. Additional updates replace adjusted-base offset 0x09 and offset 0x44 with values derived from the new base.

A word read from doubled-payload-origin offset 0x04 becomes the **terminal base**. Halfword 0x0065 is stored at twice this value. Reads through terminal-base offsets 0x64 and 0x74 supply later linking data. The new base receives a truncated byte at offset 0x11 and word value 100 at offset 0x54.

A final word read through doubled-payload-origin offset 0x64 supplies another destination. Linking stores connect that destination and the terminal-offset-0x64 value before the unresolved transfer.

2. The captured terminal-base offset-0x64 word.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

### Width conversion

Given a valid byte destination and word value 0x123456AB, truncation writes 0xAB. Given an unsigned byte read of 0xE1, its word extension is 0x000000E1.

### Fixed-width loss

Under 32-bit wrapping shifts, shifting 0xAB123456 left by eight and logically right by eight yields 0x00123456. Unlimited-precision recovery of the original value is not acceptable.

### Early globals

Given successful preceding accesses, the absolute-byte read at 0x05 precedes the byte write at 0x01. The later absolute-word write at 0x54 precedes the two link-seed stores at compact-base offset 0x14; the absolute-byte write at 0x15 follows the subsequent duplicate offset-0x15 writes.

### Publication capture

Given a shared-base value available at the capture point, publication uses that captured word. The required order is shared-base capture, word 0x20 to absolute 0x60, then the first publication store. A later computed write to the shared-base location does not itself change the captured address.

### Intermediate stores

An access-sequence comparison must retain the four adjusted-offset-0x20 stores and intervening byte sample. Equal final contents alone do not satisfy this conservative criterion. This example does not claim an external observer sees all four values.

### Final capture aliasing

If absolute byte 0x3A overlaps a subsequently captured word, that capture occurs after the byte write. Moving the retained offset-0x14 or offset-0x04 capture before the write is not equivalent under this contract.

### Fresh 0x67 reads

If an intervening computed write changes byte 0x67, a later adjusted-offset-0x09 store consumes the newly read byte, not necessarily the original low byte from the 0xA9 capture. The two later 0x67 reads remain distinct.

### Invalid access or destination

An inaccessible computed address or invalid indirect destination has no specified graceful outcome. Acceptance must not invent validation, rollback, or a success return.
