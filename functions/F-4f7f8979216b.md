## Side effects

Every reached store and dependency invocation is conditionally observable. No bounds validation, null validation, alignment validation, rollback, transactional commit, or status-code error path is visible.

Calculated addresses may wrap under the stated arithmetic model. Selector sources, selector destinations, global storage, and word-access locations may alias. Earlier stores can therefore influence later reads even without a dependency modifying memory.

Repeated selector reads are genuine reads, not permission to reuse a previously captured selector. In contrast, explicit reuse of a captured secondary datum must remain reuse rather than an invented reread.

A memory or call failure may interrupt the prefix after earlier writes have occurred. No restoration guarantee is established.

No allocation or release operation is independently identified in the visible body. Dependencies may nevertheless allocate, release, retain references, transfer ownership, or modify globals. No borrowed-versus-owned contract is established for values supplied to them.

1. **Byte truncation:** With initial quantity 65 and first selector 7, the first selector update writes 4 at address 24.

2. **Selector range:** A selector of 255 selects address 272, without an unstated base pointer.

3. **Captured secondary reuse:** If the first secondary datum is \(0xabc00000\), its earlier call receives that value twice and zero. Its later shifted-byte update writes 175. Intervening calls do not establish a different represented datum.

7. **Selector mutation:** If a selector source changes between its repeated reads, the later write uses the later selector. This also applies to address 1.

8. **Paired result:** A paired high half of 3 causes the first paired-result store to write zero at address 32. A later store writes 3 into a separately supplied slot.

9. **Masked extraction:** A final quantity of \(0xffffffff\) gives a masked-right-shift value of \(0x3ff\), whose stored low byte is 255.

10. **Separate global observations:** Different global observations affect different paths: the first supplies the masked call argument; the second supplies selector addressing.

11. **Failure:** If a required address is inaccessible, graceful recovery is not established, and earlier completed writes need not be undone.
