## Behavior

### Width and arithmetic conventions

A byte access occupies one byte, a halfword access two bytes, and a word access four bytes. The halfword constant is `0x0065`; word constants and captured words are not narrowed unless a byte store is explicitly described.

Byte extraction retains the low eight bits. Signed and unsigned interpretations of the extracted byte do not change the stored eight-bit pattern.

**Primary-base arithmetic is unsigned 32-bit arithmetic.** Both multiplication by two and addition of offsets wrap modulo \(2^{32}\) before conversion to an address:

- Every primary-base-plus-offset address uses the low 32 bits of that sum.
- The doubled primary base is the low 32 bits of twice the primary base.
- The repeated word-store address is the low 32 bits of twice the primary base plus `0x54`. The addition wraps as well as the multiplication.

These rules cover primary-relative offsets including `0x04`, `0x08`, `0x09`, `0x0D`, `0x10`, `0x11`, `0x14`, `0x40`, `0x44`, and `0x54`.

They do **not** establish equivalent wrapping rules for additions involving the represented signed inputs, secondary base, tertiary base, or signed derived bases. The two late doubled-address expressions also involve represented signed values. Overflow semantics for those signed additions and doublings remain **documented_only**.

The byte stored at absolute address `0x3A` is:

\[
\bigl(2 \times (\text{primary base} \bmod 256) + 48\bigr) \bmod 256.
\]

This specifies the stored byte, not a widened arithmetic result.
