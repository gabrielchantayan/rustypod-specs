## Behavior

### Widths and arithmetic

A byte is 8 bits, a halfword is 16 bits, and a word is 32 bits in this contract. Some call results are represented as 64-bit quantities.

Established width-sensitive behavior includes:

- The third-input field shifted left by 8 or 16 has respective maxima `0x0003FF00` and `0x03FF0000`.
- Low and high halves of 64-bit results are consumed separately.
- Logical right shifts of 64-bit results by 34, 36, and 44 select the high 32-bit half shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 respectively.
- Unsigned byte loads widen to values in 0–255.
- Byte stores retain only the low 8 bits; halfword stores retain 16 bits.
- Unsigned 32-bit shifts discard bits outside that width. They are not unbounded multiplication.
- Signed left shifts, signed multiplication, and the late variable-count signed right shift require execution-environment semantics where overflow, negative values, or excessive counts matter.

The variable shift count is selected from an address-derived word’s low byte and can therefore range from 0 through 255. The representation does not establish that this count is restricted to 0–31.

A late masked calculation yields a value in 0–508, always divisible by four, under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation. Its doubled value is in 0–1016. These bounds constrain those particular scalars, not the validity of later memory accesses.

### Opening direct-memory behavior

The direct-memory region begins with a word write at offset `0x14` from the final 64-bit result’s high-half-derived base. A byte at offset `9` from another result-derived base receives the high half’s low byte. A word write through the final result’s low half scaled by 256 follows, at offset `0x54`. The offset-`9` byte is then overwritten with a different result-derived low byte.

A separately scaled base has its offset-`0x14` word cleared before an unsigned byte is read at offset `5`. A clear through the doubled masked-value base intervenes before that byte is widened and written as a word at offset `0x34` of the separately scaled base. The latter base’s offset-`0x14` word is then read.

That read is not unconditionally zero: intervening calculated writes may alias the cleared location. Repeated clears and duplicate byte writes are retained as separate accesses.
