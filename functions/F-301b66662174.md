### Widths and arithmetic

### Entry into direct-memory mutation

The following descriptive labels identify value roles, not object types:

- **Packed base:** a dependency-produced low word shifted left 25 bits.
- **Scaled base:** another dependency-produced word multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Compact field:** a word shifted left eight bits, masked to its low 15 bits, then logically shifted right six.
- **Compact base:** twice the compact field.
- **Terminal pair:** the low and high words of the last represented 64-bit dependency result before direct stores.

The compact field ranges from zero to 508 in multiples of four. The compact base ranges from zero to 1016 in multiples of eight. These ranges do not establish address validity.

The opening mutations preserve these ordering relationships:

- A 32-bit store at terminal high word plus 0x14 receives the packed base.
- A byte at packed base plus nine receives the terminal high word’s low byte.
- A 32-bit store at 256 times the terminal low word, plus 0x54, receives the scaled base.
- The packed-base byte is overwritten with the packed base’s low byte.
- Scaled base plus 0x14 is cleared as a 32-bit word, then the byte at scaled base plus five is read.
- Compact base plus 0x14 is cleared before the captured byte is zero-extended into the word at scaled base plus 0x34.

The last read is a memory readback, not necessarily the earlier zero. The intervening compact-base store can alias it. Another compact-base clear follows, followed by two byte writes at compact base plus 0x15 using the readback’s low byte.

Under 32-bit truncating arithmetic, the packed base’s low byte is zero, so its second byte write supplies zero.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Input forwarding.** Inputs 0x12345678 and 0xABCDEF01 reach F-D001 unchanged in its first two represented positions, if that invocation is reached.

2. **Word truncation.** An unsigned 32-bit value 0x12345678 shifted left eight bits becomes 0x34567800. Keeping a 40-bit result would change the represented width.

3. **Split result.** For high word 0x11223344 and low word 0x55667788, a relationship forwarding shifted low word and unchanged high word supplies 0x66778800 and 0x11223344.

4. **Byte widening.** A captured unsigned byte 0xFE produces the word 0x000000FE, not a sign-extended value.

5. **Early absolute read.** If preceding stores change storage at 0x05, the later address derivation uses the value read after those stores. Using its entry value is not equivalent.

6. **Anchor capture.** If an earlier +0x20 store overlaps the word at 0x087FBBBC, the anchor read reflects that store. A later mutation of the global does not itself replace the already retained anchor base.

7. **Distinct 0x67 reads.** Suppose the initial byte write supplies 0x12, an intervening aliased store changes it to 0x34 before the second read, and another changes it to 0x56 before the third. Subject to no other mutations, the three reads supply 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56 respectively.

8. **Transient writes.** Where individual accesses are observable, the +0x20 sequence includes all four stores. Matching only its final 0x20 value is insufficient.

9. **Retained final argument.** If a later store changes the original terminal +0x14 location, the final third argument still uses the earlier captured word.

10. **No invented fallback.** A zero dependency output has no established local null rejection or fallback. Further behavior depends on the resulting accesses and environment.
