## Behavior

The call phase is represented without a governing loop or conditional branch. Reached calls occur in the displayed order, provided preceding callees return normally. Calls whose ordinary results are unused remain behaviorally significant because their effects are unknown.

Displayed argument patterns include:

- No explicit argument words.
- One or several zero words.
- Results shifted left by eight bits.
- Shifted low portions of paired results alongside unchanged or transformed high portions.
- The same retained value in multiple argument positions.
- Values retained across intervening calls.
- Secondary machine-word outputs not expressed as ordinary declared results.

A secondary output must not automatically be identified with a paired result’s high word. Likewise, a call displaying no arguments is not established to have no implicit inputs.

Early cross-call dependencies include retaining the first returned word after an eight-bit shift and subsequently shifting that retained value further. Later calls combine newly returned values with these retained transformations. Argument order matters: shifted-low/high/retained-word, zero/high/shifted-low, and shifted-low/high/zero/zero are distinct boundaries.

The phase selects fields using masks of multiple widths, including 11 through 15 bits, 16 through 22 bits, and 24 bits. Some transformations extract bits above bit 31 from a paired result. These are not equivalent to shifts of its low word. A left shift followed by a right shift may lose information.

Several retained intermediates are explicitly zeroed. Under ordinary fixed-width interpretation, their subsequent masks and shifts remain zero; this does not remove the associated calls.

### Direct-memory terminology and initial boundary

The following descriptive labels identify data provenance, not recovered objects:

| Label | Provenance |
|---|---|
| Link base | An earlier paired result’s low word shifted left by 25 |
| Scaled base | A later ordinary call result multiplied by \(2^{25}\) |
| Compact index | Another result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, then shifted right by six |
| Index base | Twice the compact index |
| Retained shifted word | Another earlier ordinary result shifted left by eight |
| Final pair | The last paired result obtained before direct memory manipulation |

Under 32-bit modular arithmetic, the compact index ranges from 0 to 508 in steps of four; the index base ranges from 0 to 1016 in steps of eight.

The initial publication writes the link base as a word at the final pair’s high-word-derived address plus 0x14. A byte publication at link base plus 9 initially carries the high word’s low byte. A word publication at 256 times the final pair’s low word plus 0x54 carries the scaled base. The link-base-plus-9 byte is then overwritten with the link base’s low byte.

Next, the scaled-base-plus-0x14 word is cleared. An unsigned byte is read at scaled base plus 5 before the index-base-plus-0x14 word is cleared. The sampled byte is zero-extended into the scaled-base-plus-0x34 word; only afterward is the scaled-base-plus-0x14 word read and retained.

A further index-base-plus-0x14 clear precedes two byte publications of the retained word’s low byte at index base plus 0x15.

These calculated regions are not established to be disjoint. A clear does not unconditionally determine a later read when intervening stores can overlap it.

### Early fixed-location accesses and dependent updates

After those duplicate offset-0x15 byte publications, the function samples the unsigned byte at fixed address **0x00000005**. It then writes the index base’s low byte to fixed address **0x00000001**. Both accesses are 8-bit.

The sampled byte becomes an address base. Its offset-0x15 byte receives the compact index’s low byte. Subsequent calculated accesses publish the retained shifted word at scaled base plus 0x11 and at offset 0x54 from the previously retained scaled-base-plus-0x14 word. The scaled-base-plus-0x11 byte is then cleared.

The word at offset 0x54 from the sampled-byte base is read **before** being overwritten with that previously retained word. Call this saved read the **payload word**. Its retention matters: later updates use it even after its source location changes.

Further calculated updates include:

- Publishing the compact index at index base plus 0x34.
- Reading scaled base plus 0x74 to select another offset-0x54 destination.
- Loading index-base-plus-0x74 words for a dependent offset-0x54 publication.
- Repeatedly storing the payload word at its own calculated offset-0x24 destination and publishing its low byte elsewhere.

A word derived from the ordinary result underlying the scaled base, shifted left by 26, is published at index base plus 0x14. **After this publication**, fixed address **0x00000054** receives a 32-bit word equal to twice the payload word under the applicable arithmetic rules.

The index-base-plus-0x14 word is then overwritten with the payload word twice. Two byte publications at index base plus 0x15 carry the low byte of four times the payload word. **After both**, fixed address **0x00000015** receives the index base’s low byte as an 8-bit store. Subsequent calculated publications use four times the payload word as both data and an address base.

These fixed accesses have established widths, provenance, and relative ordering. Their application meaning, permission requirements, and memory-map interpretation remain **documented_only**.

### Address-linked updates and layout constraints

The middle memory region uses loaded words and unsigned bytes to select further destinations. Define:

- **Quadrupled payload:** four times the payload word.
- **Octupled payload:** eight times the payload word.
- **Doubled link value:** twice a word sampled at index base plus 0x74.

Calculated updates publish these values at offsets 0x14, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54; publish low bytes at offsets 9, 0x0D, and 0x15; and sometimes store a calculated base into its own offset-0x14 destination.

An unsigned byte at quadrupled payload plus 5 selects a word read at offset 0x74. Another path loads a word at quadrupled payload plus 4, samples its offset-5 byte more than once, and uses a later sample to select another offset-0x74 word. The later sample is not an entry-time snapshot.

### Constants, shared-base sampling, and repeated publication

The marker base receives byte constant 0x3A. Twice the marker base receives halfword constant 0x3A. Marker base plus 0x20 receives word constant 0x3A, in that order.

Define the **adjusted payload** as quadrupled payload minus decimal 150, and the **publication base** as four times that adjusted value.

The publication-base-plus-0x20 word receives, successively, 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte freshly read at publication base plus 4, and 0x20 again.

2. A byte carrying the publication base’s low byte at shared base plus 0x19.

### Final fixed accesses and retained snapshots

Following the repeated region, the function reads a 32-bit word at fixed address **0x000000A9**, retaining the full word. It next retains the 32-bit word at marker base plus 0x74.

**Both reads precede** the 8-bit write at fixed address **0x0000003A**. That write carries the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted payload plus 0x30.

Only **after** this fixed byte write are the auxiliary-base-plus-0x14 word and publication-base-plus-4 word read and retained, in that order. The latter snapshots therefore can reflect any aliasing effect of the fixed 0x3A write.

Calculated byte publications then affect auxiliary base plus 0x0D and publication base plus 8, 0x0D, and 9. The offset-8 constant is 0x6C. Auxiliary base plus 0x54 receives the auxiliary base as a word.

Fixed address **0x00000067** now receives the low byte of the retained fixed-address-0xA9 word. This publication does not turn the mutable byte location into a retained value.

### Fresh reads of the mutable fixed byte

There are three distinct subsequent 8-bit reads of fixed address **0x00000067**:

- After reading the auxiliary-base-plus-0x44 word, a fresh read supplies the byte written to fixed address **0x00000021**.
- Later, another fresh read supplies a byte written at publication base plus 9.
- Still later, a third fresh read independently supplies another byte written at publication base plus 9.

The intervening accesses are significant. Before the first calculated offset-9 publication, the function performs calculated writes at publication base plus 0x10, shared base plus 0x54, and auxiliary base plus 1; overwrites shared base plus 0x54 with the publication base; reads publication base plus 0x40; and performs word publications at shared base plus 0x74, publication base plus 0x10, and shared base plus 0x44.

The auxiliary-base-plus-1 byte depends on a signed-looking right shift of the sampled auxiliary-base-plus-0x44 word, using the publication base’s low byte as shift count. Large counts and signed-negative behavior remain **documented_only**.

Between the two calculated offset-9 publications sourced from fresh fixed-byte reads, the function writes a retained byte at auxiliary base plus 9, writes the **full retained fixed-address-0xA9 word** at publication base plus 0x44, and reads publication base plus 0x54.

### Completion and terminal transfer

The later publication-base-plus-0x54 read selects additional byte and word destinations. The earlier auxiliary-base-plus-0x14 snapshot is published at auxiliary base plus 0x54.

A word read at publication base plus 0x14 supplies later byte data. A word loaded at auxiliary base plus 4 selects a halfword store of 0x65 at twice that loaded value. Its offset-0x64 word is then loaded and retained. A selected offset-0x54 destination receives decimal 100.

The word at offset 0x74 from the auxiliary-base-plus-4 loaded base is sampled before the remaining address-linked writes. Those remaining writes include an auxiliary-base-plus-9 byte publication and dependent word stores selected through auxiliary base plus 0x64.

The terminal arguments, in order, are:

2. The retained offset-0x64 word from the base loaded at auxiliary base plus 4.

4. The retained offset-0x74 word sampled near completion.

The fourth argument is not required to be reloaded after the final writes.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Word truncation:** Assuming modular 32-bit arithmetic, shifting 0x12345678 left by eight contributes 0x34567800, not a widened value.
- **Paired separation:** Low word 0x00123456 and high word 0x89ABCDEF produce arguments 0x12345600 and 0x89ABCDEF at a shifted-low/unchanged-high boundary. No carry enters the high argument.
- **Compact index:** A source result of 1 produces compact index 4 and index base 8. A result of 0x80 produces zero for both.
- **Fixed-byte sampling:** If address 0x05 yields 0xFE, subsequent dependent accesses use base 254. The read precedes the byte write at address 0x01.
- **Final snapshot order:** If writing address 0x3A aliases the auxiliary-base-plus-0x14 word, that later word snapshot may reflect the write. The earlier 0xA9 snapshot does not retroactively change.
- **Mutable-byte resampling:** If an intervening calculated write changes address 0x67, a later publication-base-plus-9 store must use the byte available at its own fresh read, not substitute the original low byte of the 0xA9 snapshot.
- **Repeated publications:** Where accesses themselves are observable, final-memory equality does not justify eliminating duplicate publication groups.
- **Terminal retention:** If final calculated writes change the source of the fourth argument after its represented read, the transfer still receives the retained sample.
