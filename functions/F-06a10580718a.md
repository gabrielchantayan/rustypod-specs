## Behavior

### Widths and arithmetic

The explicitly sized stores use:

- One byte: low eight bits of the computed value.
- One halfword: low sixteen bits.
- One word: all 32 bits.

Unsigned 32-bit arithmetic wraps modulo \(2^{32}\). Carry predicates identify overflow separately from the wrapped result. The comparison involving the comparison input is unsigned.

Signed-only address expressions do not have independently established overflow behavior. This affects, in particular, addresses formed from the first and second arguments, the doubled second argument, and expressions combining the auxiliary input with signed arguments or constants.

The shift operation interprets the shift input as signed 32-bit and takes the shift count from its low eight bits. The supplied evidence does not establish the result for counts of 32 through 255. For negative signed operands, the precise signed-right-shift rule also remains **documented_only**.

Byte order is not established. Numeric halfword and word values can therefore be specified without assigning their component bytes to increasing addresses.

### Ordered access sequence

The following ordering is established between the visible operations. It does not establish an evaluation order between operands within a single expression.

1. **Capture the initial word.**  
   Read the four-byte initial value before the first visible write. Its low byte is retained for a later store.

2. **Write a halfword through the first two arguments.**  
   At the address formed by adding the first and second arguments, store the low sixteen bits of the auxiliary input.

3. **Write a byte through the doubled second argument.**  
   At twice the second argument, store the low eight bits of the captured initial word.

4. **Write a byte relative to the auxiliary input.**  
   At the auxiliary input plus `0x52`, store the low eight bits of the third argument.

5. **Write a halfword through the third argument and auxiliary input.**  
   At the third argument plus the auxiliary input, store the low sixteen bits of the third argument.

6. **Assign a constant at a fixed location.**  
   Assign numeric value `0xFFBB` to the memory-resident symbol at address `0xFFFFFF76`.

   The symbol’s storage width is not supplied. This is not a proven halfword store merely because the constant fits in sixteen bits. The supplied overlapping-symbol warning also prevents assuming an independent object boundary.

7. **Write through the doubling word.**  
   Use twice the doubling word as the destination, and store its low sixteen bits.

   Destination and data expressions contain separate references to that memory-resident word. Their relative evaluation order and any volatile-access semantics are not established.

8. **Capture the first primary value.**  
   Read the primary word into a retained unsigned 32-bit value. This capture occurs after the preceding writes.

9. **Perform the shift-derived halfword write.**  
   Select an increment of one when the unsigned shift input exceeds `0x53`, otherwise zero.

   The destination is twice the sum of the shift input, third argument, and selected increment. Because the expression contains unsigned 32-bit operands, this calculation is modulo \(2^{32}\).

   The stored halfword is the low sixteen bits of the sum of:

   - The signed right-shift result of the shift input, with count equal to its low eight bits.
   - The low sixteen bits of the shift input.

   The narrowing around the shifted result does not justify inventing a rule for out-of-range shift counts.

10. **Capture the embedded word.**  
    Read four bytes from the embedded-data region as a single value. Its textual presentation does not make this a string operation or establish its numeric value.

11. **Capture the secondary value.**  
    Read the secondary word into a retained unsigned 32-bit value.

12. **Capture the second primary value.**  
    Read the primary word again into a separate retained unsigned 32-bit value.

    The shift-derived write can overlap primary-word storage, so this value need not equal the first primary capture.

13. **Perform a directly referenced primary-word halfword write.**  
    Form a destination from the directly referenced primary word plus `0x2C`, and store the low sixteen bits of the directly referenced primary word.

    These are fresh references, not established uses of either retained primary capture. Their within-expression evaluation order is unspecified by the supplied evidence.

14. **Write the first primary capture through the later captured bases.**  
    At the modulo-\(2^{32}\) sum of the second primary capture and captured secondary value, store the low sixteen bits of the first primary capture.

15. **Write the captured secondary value in full.**  
    At the captured secondary value plus `0x52`, modulo \(2^{32}\), store that captured 32-bit value.

16. **Write a primary-derived byte.**  
    At twice the second primary capture, modulo \(2^{32}\), store the low eight bits of the first primary capture.

17. **Determine the final comparison and carry terms.**  
    Three Boolean terms contribute to the final store:

    - **Comparison bit:** one when the comparison input is less than or equal to the captured secondary value, otherwise zero.
    - **Doubling carry bit:** one if unsigned doubling of the second primary capture carries, or if adding the comparison bit to the wrapped doubled value carries.
    - **Adjustment carry bit:** one if adding `0x4C` to the captured embedded word carries, or if adding the doubling carry bit to that wrapped sum carries.

    The first embedded-word carry condition is equivalently that the embedded value exceeds `0xFFFFFFB3`. The second condition handles the boundary where the intermediate sum is `0xFFFFFFFF` and the doubling carry bit is one.

18. **Perform the final byte write.**  
    Read the final base word for this operation.

    The destination is the modulo-\(2^{32}\) sum of:

    - The final base value.
    - The captured secondary value.
    - Twice the second primary capture.
    - The comparison bit.
    - The adjustment carry bit.

    The stored byte is the low eight bits of the captured embedded value plus `0x4C` plus the doubling carry bit.

    The adjustment carry bit changes the destination, not the stored byte. The exact evaluation order between the destination expression and data expression is not established.

19. **Perform the indirect transfer.**  
    Invoke the unresolved target with first argument `0x53` and second argument obtained from the transfer argument word.

    This operation follows the visible writes. The transfer argument is not an entry-time snapshot: overlapping preceding stores can affect the value supplied.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional consequences of static evidence, not executed tests. Unless stated otherwise, assume accessible storage, permitted alignment, stable ordinary memory, and no unintended overlap.

1. **Early narrowing**  
   If the auxiliary input’s low sixteen bits are `0xABCD`, the first halfword store writes numeric value `0xABCD`. If the third argument’s low sixteen bits are `0x5678`, the auxiliary-relative byte store writes `0x78`, and the third-argument-derived halfword store writes `0x5678`.

2. **Explicit byte-store count**  
   A complete traversal of the visible store sequence reaches two early explicit byte stores and two later explicit byte stores. The fixed-location assignment cannot be counted as another byte store without additional width evidence.

3. **Shift-address comparison boundary**  
   With third argument zero, shift input `0x53` produces destination `0xA6`; shift input `0x54` produces destination `0xAA`. These address results do not resolve the corresponding out-of-range shift results used for stored data.

4. **Defined-range shift**  
   With shift input one and third argument zero, the shift-derived destination is two. The signed shift of positive one by one produces zero, so the stored halfword is one.

5. **Unsigned comparison equality**  
   If the comparison input equals the captured secondary value, the comparison bit is one. Equality is included.

6. **Unsigned doubling carry**  
   If the second primary capture is `0x80000000`, its doubled address contribution wraps to zero and the doubling carry bit is one.

7. **Embedded boundary with incoming carry**  
   If the embedded word is `0xFFFFFFB3`, a doubling carry bit of zero produces stored byte `0xFF` and adjustment carry bit zero. A doubling carry bit of one produces stored byte zero and adjustment carry bit one.

8. **Direct embedded overflow**  
   With embedded word `0xFFFFFFB4` and doubling carry bit zero, adding `0x4C` wraps to zero. The final stored byte is zero and the adjustment carry bit is one.

9. **Primary-storage overlap**  
   If the shift-derived halfword store changes primary-word storage, the second primary capture reflects the resulting memory state. The first capture retains its earlier value for subsequent data stores.

10. **Late transfer argument**  
    If a preceding store changes the transfer argument word, the indirect transfer receives its later-read value, not an assumed original value.

11. **Unresolved shift count**  
    A low-eight-bit shift count of 32 has no unconditional accepted stored value under this contract. Its result remains **documented_only**.
