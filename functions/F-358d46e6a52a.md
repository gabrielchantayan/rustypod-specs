# F-358d46e6a52a

## Purpose

F-358d46e6a52a produces a quarter-scale measure of the directional separation between two adjacent signed-integer elements. The supplied pointer selects the first element; its immediate successor supplies the second.

For a representable nonnegative separation, the result counts complete groups of four. A separation smaller than four therefore produces zero. Scaling applies to the separation, not independently to the endpoints.

For a representable negative separation, the exact result depends on the governing rules for right shifting negative signed integers. Those rules are not established by the supplied evidence.

No application-specific meaning, unit of measurement, ordering requirement, or interpretation of the result as a status is established.

## Behavior

### Numerical result for nonnegative separations

For a representable separation of zero or greater, the result is the number of complete groups of four in that separation:

| Separation | Required result |
|---:|---:|
| 0 through 3 | 0 |
| 4 through 7 | 1 |
| 8 through 11 | 2 |
| 12 through 15 | 3 |
| 16 through 19 | 4 |

This grouping continues throughout the representable nonnegative range.

A remainder is discarded rather than rounded to the nearest integer. Crossing a multiple-of-four boundary between endpoints has no independent significance. For example, seven followed by eight produces zero because their separation is one.

Zero is an ordinary numerical result. It does not distinguish equality from a positive separation smaller than four, and it is not an established success or failure indicator.

### Numerical result for negative separations

A decreasing pair is not explicitly rejected or converted to an absolute separation. Its representable negative separation is subject to signed right-shift semantics with a distance of two bit positions.

The supplied evidence does not establish the language version or implementation contract needed to prescribe the exact result for every negative separation.

If the governing shift rule is equivalent to division by four rounded toward negative infinity, these results follow:

| Separation | Conditional result |
|---:|---:|
| −1 through −4 | −1 |
| −5 through −8 | −2 |
| −9 through −12 | −3 |

These are conditional requirements, not unconditional guarantees.

Under that rule, reversing the endpoints need not negate the result. A positive separation of one produces zero, while a negative separation of one produces negative one.

There is no explicit rounding adjustment, zero clamp, sign normalization, or decreasing-order error path. These absences do not establish which negative-shift rule applies.
