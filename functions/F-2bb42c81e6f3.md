# F-2bb42c81e6f3

## Purpose

F-2bb42c81e6f3 performs an ordered chain of indirect invocations, derives numeric arguments and addresses from their results, mutates computed-address and shared storage, and transfers control to an unresolved terminal target.

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated offsets do not establish records, strings, allocation, serialization, or an object model.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** explicitly represented arithmetic, conversions, invocation relationships, typed indirect accesses, and access ordering.
- **Inferred intent:** descriptive terms such as “base,” “linked,” and “publication.” These names do not imply valid objects or ownership.
- **documented_only:** behavior requiring absent global declarations, dependency contracts, platform rules, or executable evidence.

All operations are conditional on execution reaching them. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Invocation chain and arithmetic

Two indirect invocations precede a third producing a word result. That result shifted left by eight is passed alongside the third input shifted left by 24. Later invocations consume other results shifted left by eight. One retained shifted result is shifted another seven positions and supplied twice. Other early pairs combine result-derived and input-derived arguments, including the reversed order.

The longer chain contains invocations with no recovered explicit arguments and invocations with one through four represented arguments. Primary results, secondary result state, and portions of wide results feed later arguments and addresses. An unused result does not establish an effect-free invocation.

Transformations include fixed shifts, masks, explicit zeros, duplicated arguments, and unsigned extraction from wide results beginning at bit positions including 32, 34, 36, and 44. Similar argument patterns do not establish identical targets.

For conditional numeric relationships, **bit-vector arithmetic** means retaining the low 32 bits at each word-width addition, subtraction, multiplication, or left shift. Unsigned right shifts introduce zeros. Explicit byte and halfword stores retain eight and 16 bits; explicitly unsigned byte loads yield values from 0 through 255. Successive word shifts by eight, 18, and eight therefore leave no surviving bits.

This interpretation does not resolve represented signed arithmetic. Signed overflow, invalid signed left shifts, negative signed right shifts, and excessive variable shift counts remain **documented_only**.

### Initial mutation and capture

The initial publications occur in this order: packed base as a word at final-high-word plus 20; the final high word’s low byte at packed base plus 9; scaled base as a word at 256 times final-low-word plus 84; then packed base’s low byte at packed base plus 9.

Scaled base plus 20 is cleared as a word. An unsigned byte is read at scaled base plus 5. A word clear at small base plus 20 intervenes before that byte is published, zero-extended, as a word at scaled base plus 52. The word at scaled base plus 20 is then captured as the **initial capture**.

**Under bit-vector arithmetic and ordinary stable-memory semantics, the initial capture is zero.** The scaled source starts at an address congruent to 20 modulo \(2^{25}\); the potentially overlapping small-base clear starts at an address congruent to 20 modulo eight. Both accesses are four bytes wide. They can overlap only at the same starting address, where both write zero. The scaled-base-plus-52 publication is disjoint from scaled base plus 20. These writes therefore provide no aliasing mechanism for a nonzero capture.

Externally sensitive storage, concurrent modification, or uncertain executable correspondence can invalidate ordinary-memory reasoning; those are separate uncertainties.

Another word clear at small base plus 20 precedes two distinct byte stores at small base plus 21, both carrying the initial capture’s low byte.

The global associated with address 5 is evaluated and converted to an unsigned 32-bit value, called the **global-derived base**. Its range is not established as 0–255. A signed narrow source, for example, can convert to a large unsigned word.

Next, the global at address 1 receives an explicitly eight-bit-converted small base, subject to its unknown destination declaration. A definite byte store at global-derived base plus 21 carries the small index’s low byte.
