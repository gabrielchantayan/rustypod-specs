# F-fae2c22212e7

## Purpose

F-fae2c22212e7 performs an ordered sequence of dependency invocations, computed memory accesses, bit-field extractions, and shared-word publications.

No application-level purpose is established. Address-like results, repeated offsets, and shared storage do not establish initialization, decoding, allocation, dispatch, object types, or ownership relationships.

This contract describes represented local behavior, conditional on execution reaching each action. It does not establish that every dependency returns, every computed address is accessible, or normal completion is reachable.

## Interface

The operation accepts one opaque 32-bit argument. The first indirect invocation receives it unchanged. No subsequent direct use of that argument is represented.

No explicit return value, status code, or success indicator is represented. An apparent normal return follows an attempted invocation at address zero; reaching the return requires that invocation to return.

### Widths and vocabulary

A byte is 8 bits, a halfword 16 bits, and a word 32 bits. A represented paired result has low and high 32-bit portions; whether it is an actual 64-bit return is unknown.

The following contract-local terms describe accesses, not recovered structures:

- **Selector-mediated store:** read an unsigned byte at the stated selector address, then write a byte at address 0x11 plus that selector. The destination ranges from 0x11 through 0x110.
- **Indexed slot:** a word at 0x14 plus four times an index.
- **Expanded slot:** a word at 0x14 plus sixteen times an index.
- **G0** and **G1:** two represented shared words.
- **Three-byte component:** the shared 24-bit component beginning at address 0x09.

Unsigned right shifts are logical. Narrow stores retain the low eight or sixteen bits. Explicit unsigned-byte loads widen to 0–255. Narrow values without an established signed interpretation are not assigned an invented extension rule.

Arithmetic identities involving overflow or address wraparound are conditional on 32-bit modular machine arithmetic. Signed shifts, signed overflow, pointer conversion, and actual address-width rules remain separately unresolved.

## Behavior

### Ordering model

The represented local path has no explicit conditional branch or loop. Dependencies interrupt it throughout and may mutate shared or addressed storage, trap, or fail to return.

Observable behavior is an ordered access trace, not merely a final memory image. Reads separated by calls remain distinct. No dependency is assumed to preserve globals, selectors, memory mappings, or pointed-to contents. Where several memory reads appear as arguments to one invocation, their mutual evaluation order is not established.

### Entry and initial publications

F-000000000001 receives the explicit argument. A subsequent invocation receives no explicit arguments.

The operation reads a selector at the incoming address-like value plus one, then publishes the low byte of the incoming unsigned word through that selector.

After intervening invocations, it rereads the same selector address and publishes the incoming word masked with 0x3fffffff and shifted right by twenty, truncated to a byte. This publishes original bits 20–27; bits 28–29 of the extract are discarded by truncation.

Further invocations receive scaled results, duplicate arguments, and an explicit zero. One invocation receives four represented word reads from address 0x30. These are not collapsed into one read.

Two selector-mediated zero-byte stores follow. Their selector address is a retained secondary result shifted right by nine, plus one. Calls intervene, and each store uses a fresh selector read.

### Early word publications and extracted fields

After an invocation with four zero arguments, the operation publishes a secondary result into the indexed slot selected by that result.

A subsequent invocation receives that result and a word shifted right by nineteen. The word is read at 0x14 plus 0x400 times a prior unsigned result shifted right by eighteen.

Later calls obtain destinations for:

- Copying the retained secondary result into another indexed slot.
- Publishing four times a result into its expanded slot.
- Copying that scaled value into another indexed slot.

A separate word read at four times a result plus 0x14 is shifted right by nineteen and passed onward.

A later unsigned result supplies a four-times value for selector-mediated byte publication. After calls, a freshly read selector controls publication of its masked-right-shift-by-twenty byte. Further extracts mask with 0x3fffffff and shift right by seventeen and seven.

An invocation receives four identical arguments, those two extracts, and an address-like argument. A subsequent paired result is split into low and high words. A selector-mediated zero store precedes forwarding the high word. More calls and another selector-mediated zero store follow.

The low word later supplies a right-shift-by-twenty-eight argument. Another argument shifts an already four-times-scaled quantity left by thirty-one. Under 32-bit modular arithmetic that argument is zero; this does not settle signed-overflow semantics.

### Offset fields and retained bases

A retained low result serves as an address-like base. The operation writes the low byte of twice that base at offset 0x0d, then forwards a word read at four times another unsigned result plus 0x44.

After calls, the same offset-0x0d byte is overwritten with 4. Another forwarded word is read at 0x44 plus that unsigned result masked with 0x3fffffff and shifted right by twenty. This address need not be word-aligned.

Subsequent publications use address multipliers of 0x100, four, and sixteen. One published extract masks with 0x03ffffff and shifts right by thirteen. Expanded slots receive four-times results; later indexed slots receive retained copies.

A word at sixteen times a retained result plus 0x24 is cleared. The operation then reads a word at the corresponding scaled base plus 0x74 and retains it as a new base.

After calls, another result shifted right by twenty is written at the new base plus 0x24. A word at the new base plus 0x74 is forwarded. Selector-mediated extracted-byte and zero-byte stores follow, separated by calls.

### Repeated bytes, G0 clearing, and low-address effects

Three separate unsigned-byte reads occur at secondary-result bases plus 0x15. Calls forward their values; calls and selector-mediated zero stores separate the reads. The third byte is additionally published, zero-extended, into an indexed slot.

A retained extract masks with 0x1fffffff and shifts right by eighteen. Its range is 0–2047. Its later left shift by thirteen produces a bounded field, not recovery of discarded original bits.

Further indexed and expanded stores publish secondary results, scaled results, and extracts. A retained secondary result is published with a net factor of 128 under modular arithmetic. G0 is then explicitly assigned zero.

Substantial activity follows before G0 is read. Those later reads cannot be replaced with zero: dependencies may change G0.

Separate retained-value relationships do establish low-address behavior:

- A retained unsigned zero supplies subsequent shifts and scaled addresses.
- An unsigned byte read at a retained result plus one later serves as a base for another byte read at that byte plus one, an address from 1 through 256.
- A bounded retained extract becomes zero after another mask and right shift by nineteen; the ensuing selector read is therefore at address one.
- Additional selector reads at address one follow from retained zeros independently of G0.

Word publications continue throughout these accesses.

### Selector publication before the late offset-nine effects

Two distinct retained unsigned quantities control the selector addresses in this phase:

- A four-times-scaled result, retained from a preceding expanded-slot publication and copied into a later indexed slot.
- An eight-times-scaled result retained from an earlier result.

After calls produce a result represented as a character, the operation freshly reads the selector at **one plus the retained four-times-scaled quantity shifted right by one**. It publishes the character result shifted left by two, narrowed to a byte.

The character’s signedness and the arithmetic interpretation of its shift are unresolved. This is not unconditionally specified as an unsigned-word multiplication.

Subsequent calls receive the retained eight-times-scaled quantity and its right-shift-by-twenty-two extract. After further calls, a selector-mediated zero store freshly reads its selector at **one plus that right-shift-by-twenty-two extract**.

These selector addresses arise from different retained quantities and different shifts. They are not interchangeable.

More calls precede clearing a byte at 0x2000 times a newly four-times-scaled result plus nine. Later, the low byte of another retained base is written at that base plus nine. A word at that base plus 0x34 is forwarded with paired-result-derived arguments.

### Late offset-0x14 read chain before the first G0 capture

The following relationships are established direct memory operations, not unknown dependency semantics.

First, an indexed slot selected by a secondary result receives the retained offset-nine base. Immediately afterward, a word is read at **that secondary result plus 0x14**. This direct-base read is distinct from the preceding four-times-indexed store, although aliasing is possible.

After intervening calls, another indexed destination receives the retained offset-nine base. The operation then reads a second word at **0x14 plus the first loaded word shifted right by twenty-two**. The shifted word is an address displacement, not an index multiplied by four.

After another sequence of calls produces a new result:

1. The second loaded word shifted right by one selects an indexed slot.
2. That slot receives four times the new result.
3. A third word is read at **the unshifted second loaded word plus 0x14**.

The indexed store precedes this third read and may affect it if their storage overlaps.

Calls follow. Another indexed destination receives the same retained four-times result. **Only then is G0 captured for the first time in this late sequence.** The third loaded word remains retained.

After more calls, the retained third word is shifted right by twenty-two and published into an indexed slot selected by a new secondary result. The captured G0 word shifted right by twenty-three is passed onward. After further calls, another indexed slot receives the same retained third-word extract.

These publications use the retained third word, not a reread of its address.

### Separate masked offset-0x14 read and first G1 publication

The first captured G0 word, already shifted right by twenty-three, is shifted right by twenty-three again. For an unsigned 32-bit original, this second extract is zero.

A later secondary result selects an indexed slot that is cleared. After intervening calls, another indexed slot receives that zero extract. Immediately afterward, the operation reads a word at:

**0x14 plus the secondary result masked with 0x1fffffff and shifted right by nineteen.**

The secondary result here is the one that selected the earlier cleared slot. This read address ranges from 0x14 through 0x413 and need not be word-aligned.

After further calls, the loaded word is shifted right by twenty-two and narrowed for selector-mediated byte publication. Its selector is freshly read at one plus the twice-shifted captured-G0 extract: address one.

Calls then precede a selector-mediated zero store whose selector address is one plus four times this retained loaded-word extract. The extract ranges from 0–1023; its low-byte publication does not replace the full extract used for this selector address.

Further calls, selector-mediated stores, and fresh reads at address one occur before a dependency result is assigned to G1. This first G1 publication is not completion.

### Effects after the first G1 publication

After calls, a word read at a retained unsigned-byte base plus 0x74 supplies the low byte written at that base plus one.

A later four-times-result base receives the same form of update. Calls intervene before a fresh offset-0x74 word read repeats the offset-one byte update.

Another offset-0x74 word is retained in full while its low byte is written at offset one. The full word is later copied into expanded and indexed slots.

Further actions publish a scaled result at 0x14 plus a retained value with its bottom two bits cleared, clear an indexed slot, and split another paired result. Four times that pair’s low word is stored in the indexed slot selected by its high word.

**Immediately after that paired-result-derived store, G0 is captured again.** This is a distinct read after the first G1 publication.

Several calls follow, then another indexed slot receives the retained scaled low result. Only afterward is a word read at **the captured G0 value plus 0x14**. The address uses the captured value, not a fresh G0 read. Intervening dependencies may change either G0 or the addressed memory.

A later paired result supplies further arguments and the retained low word used for the terminal halfword address. Selector-mediated stores publish the low byte and a right-shift-by-twenty-two portion of the word read through the captured G0 base.

### Terminal effects

After the final call sequence produces a byte result, local effects occur in this order:

1. Store a **constant zero byte** through an unresolved secondary-result pointer.
2. Read the three-byte component and assign G1 a word containing that component in bits 8–31 and the retained returned byte in bits 0–7.
3. Store a zero halfword at the retained low word masked with 0x3fffffff and shifted right by seven.
4. Attempt F-000000000004 at address zero with three zero arguments.

The returned byte participates in G1 assembly; it is not written through the unresolved pointer.

The pointer store precedes the component read. Overlap may therefore change the component used for assembly. The halfword address ranges from zero through 0x007fffff and may be odd.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static expectations, not executed tests.

1. **Entry bytes.** With incoming word 0x12345678 and first selector 5, the first publication writes 0x78 at 0x16. The later extracted byte is 0x23; its destination uses a fresh selector.

If the separate eight-times quantity is 0x00800000, the later zero store reads its selector at 3, after intervening calls.

The second word selects an indexed destination at 0x4014 and an unshifted-base read at 0x2014. 4. **Masked offset read.** A secondary value 0x00800000 selects the separate masked read at 0x24. If it yields 0x80400000, the right-shift-by-twenty-two extract is 0x201. The selector-mediated byte publication writes 0x01, while the subsequent zero store reads its selector at 0x805.

5. **Post-publication G0 capture.** If the second G0 capture yields 0x1000 and a dependency later changes G0 to 0x2000, the captured-base word read remains at 0x1014. Its contents may nevertheless change before the read.

6. **Terminal ordering.** With returned byte 0xab and subsequently read component 0x123456, the unresolved pointer receives zero and G1 becomes 0x123456ab. Overlap with the component must be evaluated after the zero-byte store.

7. **Interrupted execution.** If a dependency does not return, later actions need not occur. Earlier writes have no visible automatic rollback.
