# F-08426f0ea83d

## Purpose

F-08426f0ea83d returns a numeric candidate derived from a selection and stored configuration, provided that the candidate is strictly below a stored unsigned limit. Selections zero and one return zero without consulting that configuration.

Zero is not a distinct error indicator. It can represent an ineligible selection, a rejected candidate, or an accepted candidate whose value is zero.

**Established static evidence:** Eligibility depends only on the selection. Eligible calculations combine a stored starting quantity with an unsigned, power-of-two-scaled displacement, then apply an exclusive unsigned upper bound.

**Inferred intent:** The operation may select a position in a regularly spaced sequence. Neither that interpretation nor numerical acceptance establishes that the result identifies valid memory, an existing object, or an allocated element.

## Behavior

### Eligibility

Selections zero and one return zero without entering the configuration-dependent calculation.

Every larger representable unsigned selection is eligible. There is no separate maximum-selection check before candidate formation. Large selections can therefore reach the arithmetic and bound comparison.

### Candidate semantics

Selection two is the displacement origin: its unscaled displacement is zero. Each subsequent selection increases that displacement by one.

The lowest eight bits of the stored shift control determine the effective shift count. For a valid count, the displacement is scaled by the corresponding power of two within the width of the unsigned operand.

Under the conditional model, the scaled displacement and the final candidate are 32-bit unsigned quantities. High bits lost during scaling are discarded. The signed starting quantity contributes through its unsigned 32-bit representation, and addition can wrap again.

Consequently:

- The function does not reject mathematical overflow.
- Increasing the selection does not guarantee an increasing candidate.
- A large selection can produce a small candidate.
- A negative starting quantity is not independently rejected.
- An accepted candidate can be numerically below the unsigned representation of the starting quantity.

Selection two does not bypass the shift-control read. Its zero displacement also does not make an otherwise invalid shift count valid.

### Shift-control boundaries

Under the conditional model, the retained eight bits can represent counts from zero through 255. Extracting those bits is not a check that the count is valid for a 32-bit operand.

A complete shift-control value of 256 selects effective count zero. Values two and 43,778 both select effective count two. Differences outside the lowest eight bits do not change the effective count, although the complete 16-bit storage must still be readable.

Effective counts of 32 or greater fall outside this contract’s defined numeric behavior under the conditional model. The supplied evidence does not establish count reduction modulo 32, saturation, a zero result, or an ordinary bound-rejection result. This remains true when the displacement is zero.

### Acceptance boundary

For a defined eligible calculation, the candidate is compared with the stored limit as an unsigned quantity.

A candidate strictly below the limit is returned unchanged. A candidate equal to or greater than the limit produces zero.

The comparison supplies only an exclusive unsigned upper bound. It does not validate an address, enforce alignment, establish a lower bound relative to the starting quantity, or detect wrapping.

Under the conditional model:

- Limit zero accepts no candidate.
- Every positive limit accepts candidate zero.
- Candidate 4,294,967,295 cannot be accepted, because no representable limit is larger.
- Acceptance of zero is indistinguishable in the return value from ordinary rejection.

Invalid memory access, invalid address formation, and excessive shift counts are not additional documented zero-return cases. No recovery result is established for those conditions.

### Observable read ordering

Eligibility is resolved before the configuration-dependent calculation. On the eligible path, both the starting quantity and shift control contribute to candidate formation. The evidence does not establish which of those two values is read first.

Candidate formation precedes the exclusive-limit read. Every defined eligible calculation consults the limit, including calculations whose candidate is zero. No intermediate sign or overflow rejection avoids that read.

The external address component is relevant only on the eligible path. Its storage mechanism and the details of obtaining it remain unresolved.

These are abstract evaluation-order guarantees, not guarantees about physical bus transactions, compiler-generated instruction order, atomic access, or a coherent snapshot. The evidence does not establish volatile semantics.
