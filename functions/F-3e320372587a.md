## Behavior

### Arithmetic and access model

Represented accesses have widths of 8, 16, and 32 bits; some dependency results have represented width 64 bits.

Under a **32-bit bit-vector interpretation**, word arithmetic retains the low 32 bits, byte and halfword stores retain the low eight and sixteen bits, and unsigned right shifts insert zeros. Byte-derived bases are zero-extended.

Some arithmetic and shifts instead have signed representations. Executable signed-overflow behavior, negative-value shifts, and out-of-range shift behavior are **documented_only**. Conclusions requiring modular arithmetic are conditional on the bit-vector interpretation, not assertions about executable signed-language semantics.

Established extraction patterns include:

- Low twenty-four bits followed by right shift eight: a sixteen-bit result.
- Low twenty-four bits followed by right shift nineteen: a five-bit result.
- Low eighteen bits of a high result word followed by right shift eleven: a seven-bit result.
- Low seventeen bits followed by right shift four: a thirteen-bit result.
- Low twenty-four bits followed by right shift twelve: a twelve-bit result.

A later bounded extraction shifts a result left eight, masks with 0x7FFF, and shifts right six. Its possible bit-vector values are **multiples of four from 0 through 508**. Twice that extraction is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

Under the same interpretation, shifting a word left thirty-one and subsequently left thirty erases all original bits. A word shifted left sixteen has a zero low halfword; selecting that halfword and shifting right five therefore produces zero. Such conclusions constrain argument values, not dependency side effects.

### Initial mutation and cleared-word invariant

The first explicit store writes the shifted base as a word at the final pair’s high word plus 0x14. The byte at shifted base plus 9 then receives the high word’s low byte. Next, the word at 256 times the final pair’s low word plus 0x54 receives the scaled base. The offset-9 byte is then overwritten with the shifted base’s low byte, zero under bit-vector semantics.

The scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared, and its offset-5 byte is captured. Before that cleared word is reread, the only intervening stores are a zero word at small base plus 0x14 and the zero-extended captured byte as a word at scaled base plus 0x34.

**In ordinary, nonconcurrent, byte-addressed memory, the cleared-word reread is zero.** The same-base offsets 0x14 and 0x34 are disjoint, including under modular 32-bit addressing. Overlap with the small-base store cannot introduce nonzero bits because that store writes zero.

Device behavior, concurrent mutation, and exceptional access semantics are separate uncertainties. Ordinary aliasing alone does not invalidate this zero invariant.

The small base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared again. Two successive byte stores at small base plus 0x15 use the reread word’s low byte.

### Fixed storage and root transformation

The byte at fixed address 0x05 is captured and zero-extended as a base. Fixed address 0x01 then receives the small base’s low byte. The bounded extraction’s low byte is stored at the captured byte-derived base plus 0x15.

The shifted payload’s low byte is written at scaled base plus 0x11. A word carrying the shifted payload is then written at the cleared-word reread plus 0x54. Under the ordinary-memory invariant, this is a **word store at fixed address 0x54**. The scaled base’s offset-0x11 byte is subsequently cleared.

The root word is then captured from the earlier byte-derived base plus 0x54, and its source word is overwritten with the cleared-word value.

Further transformations include:

- A fresh word from scaled base plus 0x74 supplying a destination base for payload and shifted-result stores.
- A word obtained through small base plus 0x74 being stored at its own offset 0x54.

The subsequent fixed-storage ordering is significant:
