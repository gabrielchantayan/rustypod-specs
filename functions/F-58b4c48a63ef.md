# F-58b4c48a63ef

## Purpose

F-58b4c48a63ef accepts three represented 32-bit arguments, performs an extensive sequence of unresolved indirect invocations, mutates fixed-address and indirectly addressed memory, and ends with another unresolved indirect invocation.

**Established static evidence:** Observable behavior includes dependency calls, transformations of dependency results, mixed-width memory accesses, retained snapshots, fresh reads after potentially aliasing writes, and repeated stores to unchanged destinations. No recognizable application-level operation is established.

**Inferred intent:** Some arithmetic resembles packed-field extraction or placement; some memory updates resemble record linkage. These interpretations do not establish a format, ownership model, or business purpose.

This is a static behavioral contract. It does not establish that the represented function boundary, call signatures, or low-address accesses are executable in an ordinary protected-memory environment. Unresolved behavior is marked **documented_only**.

### Indirect-call phase

Early dependency results are shifted left eight positions before forwarding. The third explicit input, shifted left five positions, appears in two early argument lists.

Across the call phase:

- Results are represented as either 32-bit words or 64-bit values.
- Represented 64-bit values are split into low and high 32-bit portions.

### Width and arithmetic boundaries

Unless separately qualified:

- Explicit byte accesses cover eight bits.
- Byte stores retain the low eight bits of their source.
- Explicit halfword stores cover 16 bits.
- Explicit word accesses cover 32 bits.
- Unsigned byte-to-word widening produces a value from 0 through 255.
- Unsigned right shifts introduce zero bits.
- Splitting a 64-bit result into words does not establish in-memory byte order.

The fixed global at `0x05` is an exception to any byte-width assumption: its value is converted to an unsigned 32-bit value, but its storage type and read width are not supplied. Conversion width is not evidence of access width.

Conditional arithmetic examples below assume truncation to 32 bits at each represented word operation. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, signed right-shift behavior, address wrapping, and exceptional shift counts remain **documented_only**.

Successive word shifts must not be replaced by unlimited-precision arithmetic. Under truncating word semantics, shifting a word left 28 positions and then left another 28 positions produces zero; discarded bits do not reappear.

### Entry into direct mutation

Late call results supply:

- A base formed by shifting a low result word left 25 positions.
- A second large base formed by multiplying a returned word by `0x02000000`.
- A small address component obtained by an eight-position left shift, a `0x7FFF` mask, and a six-position unsigned right shift.
- A retained word formed by an eight-position left shift.
- A final represented 64-bit result supplying low and high words.

Under truncating 32-bit semantics, the small component is between 0 and 508, in multiples of four. Its double is between 0 and 1016, in multiples of eight. These are unchecked address components, not validated array indices.

The initial direct effects have significant ordering: a word store at high-word base plus `0x14` precedes a byte store at the 25-bit-shifted base plus `9`; a word store at the final low word scaled by 256 plus `0x54` intervenes before that byte destination is overwritten. A word at the second large base plus `0x14` is then cleared before a byte is read at that base plus `5`.

The first byte write remains an event even though a later write targets the same address.

### Late snapshots and fixed-global ordering

For this phase, **working region** means the region receiving byte writes at offsets `8`, `9`, and `0x0D`. **Doubled-record region** means the separately retained region used for accesses at `0x14`, `0x44`, and `0x54`. These are descriptive roles, not established object types.

The fixed word at `0xA9` is captured before the byte write at `0x3A`. A word at offset `0x74` of the earlier four-times-scaled region is captured between those two fixed-address events.

The byte written at `0x3A` is derived from eight times an arithmetic-derived quantity plus `0x30`, narrowed to eight bits. Only after that write are the doubled-record word at `0x14` and working-region word at `4` captured. Consequently, aliasing with `0x3A` may affect these later captures; it cannot retroactively change the retained `0xA9` value.

Following several byte writes and a self-address word store at doubled-record offset `0x54`, the low byte of the **captured** `0xA9` word is written to `0x67`. This is not a new read of `0xA9`.

The assignment to fixed byte `0x21` has a separate ordering constraint: an indirect word load from doubled-record offset `0x44` occurs after the write to `0x67` and before the read of `0x67` used for `0x21`. The assignment therefore reads the global at that point; it does not directly use the retained low byte of `0xA9`.

Later, working-region offset `9` receives two further values from **fresh reads** of fixed byte `0x67`:

- The first occurs after intervening byte and word stores, a working-region word load at `0x40`, and further record updates.
- Before the second, the body writes a retained byte at doubled-record offset `9`, writes the full captured `0xA9` word at working-region offset `0x44`, and loads a word from working-region offset `0x54`.

Those intervening stores may alias `0x67`. Neither fresh read may be replaced by the byte originally written there or by the value read for `0x21`. The second fresh read likewise cannot be replaced by the first.

The captured `0xA9` word thus has distinct uses: its low byte supplies the earlier fixed store to `0x67`, while its full 32 bits supply the later working-region store at `0x44`. Subsequent reads of `0x67` remain live memory observations.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static obligations, not executed tests.

1. **Third-input forwarding.** With third input 3 and ordinary defined word-shift behavior, its two early shifted argument positions contain 96.

2. **Narrowing.** A byte store sourced from `0x123456AB` stores `0xAB`; a word store from that source retains all 32 bits.

3. **Small address component.** A contributing word of `0x7F` produces 508; doubling produces 1016. A contributing word of `0x80` produces zero under truncating 32-bit semantics. No range rejection is represented.

4. **Unknown-width fixed read.** The read at `0x05` cannot be accepted as necessarily eight bits or restricted to values through 255 merely because its result is converted to unsigned 32 bits.

5. **Ordered constant events.** An observer of the designated word destination sees `0x20`, `0x2F`, the widened byte value read afterward, and `0x20`. Final memory alone cannot verify that sequence.

6. **Snapshot independence.** If `0xA9` yields `0x123456AB` at capture, its later designated uses receive low byte `0xAB` and full word `0x123456AB`, even if an intervening aliasing write changes memory at `0xA9`.

7. **Fresh-global observations.** If intervening supported effects make the three reads of `0x67` yield `0xAB`, `0x42`, and `0x7E`, then `0x21` receives `0xAB`, and the two designated working-region byte stores receive `0x42` and `0x7E`. Reusing one cached byte fails this condition.

8. **Cleared-word reload.** A cleared word reloads as zero only if no intervening overlapping effect changes it.

9. **Repeated access events.** Where each store triggers a device event, repeated groups require repeated events despite identical values.

10. **Final invocation.** If F-00000003 is reached, its first explicit argument is `0x72`; its third is the retained earlier word, and the final direct store has already been attempted. Normal return is not implied.
