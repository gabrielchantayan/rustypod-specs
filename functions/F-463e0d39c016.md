# F-463e0d39c016

## Purpose

F-463e0d39c016 executes a long sequence of indirect invocations, transforms values obtained from those invocations, performs ordered mixed-width memory updates, and makes a final four-argument indirect invocation.

No application-level purpose is established. Character-valued constants do not establish text output; repeated offsets do not establish a particular container, allocator, or object layout.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** widths, operations, dependencies, and ordering represented in the supplied material.
- **Inferred intent:** possible interpretations, not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior not resolved sufficiently for an executable equivalence requirement.

The function is not established as an ordinary portable three-argument API. Implicit entry state, unresolved callees, computed addresses, and fixed-address storage materially affect its behavior.

## Interface

### Explicit inputs and completion

There are three explicit inputs, each **32 bits** wide. Their signedness and semantic types are unspecified.

The first indirect invocation receives these inputs unchanged, in their original order, followed by a fourth 32-bit argument formed by shifting an implicit incoming value left by five bits.

The visible body does not directly dereference an explicit input. The first dependency may nevertheless interpret one as an address.

There is no represented explicit return value. A return follows the final indirect invocation, but normal return from that invocation is not established. No particular result register or completion status is promised.

## Behavior

### Indirect invocation and transformation phase

Before its first direct data-memory store, the function performs a statically ordered sequence of indirect invocations. No explicit loop or conditional branch appears in that sequence.

The initial target depends on incoming continuation-related state plus **0xFFE**. Later targets generally use embedded continuation-like bases plus the same displacement. Some bases are represented as code-associated addresses. This does not establish that the targets share an implementation.

Invocations include combinations of:

- No represented explicit arguments.
- A primary result shifted left by eight bits.
- Both 32-bit parts of a paired result.
- Shifted secondary results.
- Explicit zero arguments.
- Values retained across intervening invocations.
- Duplicate arguments derived from one transformed value.

An invocation without represented explicit arguments may consume other machine state. An unused represented result does not establish absence of effects. Invocation order is therefore part of the observable behavior.

Transformations include masking, left shifts, logical right shifts of unsigned values, and power-of-two scaling. Represented patterns include:

- Forwarding a low result shifted left by eight while separately forwarding the high part.
- Shifting secondary outputs by six bits and other counts.
- Extracting bits 10–19 of an already formed 32-bit value, then shifting that ten-bit result by five or six.
- Restricting a result to its low 24 bits before additional transformation.
- Other restrictions to 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 14, 13, 12, or 11 bits.
- Extracting portions of 64-bit results by logical right shifts of 34, 36, or 44 bits.

Discarded bits cannot be recovered by later shifts. Values explicitly initialized to zero and subsequently shifted remain arithmetically zero, without making the receiving invocations removable.

These transformations do not establish lengths, indexes, capacities, tags, or pointer encodings. The complete counted invocation trace is not enumerated here; a counted trace oracle is **documented_only**.

### Initial direct memory updates

The transition into direct mutation retains a final paired result, large scaled values, a value previously shifted left by eight, and a small extracted quantity.

The small quantity is obtained from a value shifted left by eight, masked with **0x7FFF**, and shifted right by six. Under 32-bit bitvector semantics it is a multiple of four between zero and **508**. Its doubled value is at most **1016**.

The initial access ordering includes:

- A word store at the final paired result’s high-part address plus **0x14**, followed by a byte store at a retained base plus **9**.
- A word store at the low-part-derived base plus **0x54**, between two byte stores at that retained base plus **9**.
- Clearing a word at a large scaled base plus **0x14**, then reading a byte at that base plus **5**.
- Clearing a word at the doubled-small base plus **0x14**, then storing the zero-extended byte at the large base plus **0x34**.
- Reading back the large-base word at **0x14**, clearing the doubled-small word again, and performing two byte stores at the doubled-small base plus **0x15**, both using the read-back value’s low byte.

The read-back word is not unconditionally zero. Intervening accesses may alias it, and ordinary memory behavior has not been established.

A subsequent byte read at fixed address **0x05** supplies a zero-extended address base. A byte write at **0x01** follows. The function updates byte metadata through the byte-derived base and the earlier large base, including writing and clearing the large-base byte at **0x11**. It reads the word at the byte-derived base plus **0x54** before replacing that word.

### Dependent and scaled updates

The retained word from that last read supplies several scaled addresses and values. This contract calls it the **seed word**. This is a descriptive role, not an assertion of object type.

The mutation region:

- Reads words at computed offsets **0x74** and follows loaded values to offset **0x54**.
- Twice stores the seed word at the seed word’s address plus **0x24**.
- Repeats related byte stores at offset **9**.
- Replaces the doubled-small-base word at **0x14** several times.
- Writes a word at fixed address **0x54** using a doubled seed-derived value.
- Writes a byte at fixed address **0x15**.
- Uses twofold, fourfold, and eightfold seed scaling.
- Reads bytes at offset **5**, zero-extends them as bases, and reads words at those bases plus **0x74**.
- Follows a word at offset **0x64**, updates byte and word fields relative to it, and follows another word at offset **4**.

These are dependent accesses, not evidence of nonoverlapping fields. Repeated reads, including reads through the same represented address expression, must not be assumed to return the same value.

For the later regions, the following semantic roles identify retained numerical values:

| Role | Derivation |
|---|---|
| Adjusted scale | Four times the seed word, minus **0x96** |
| Adjusted destination | Four times the adjusted scale |
| Constant-write base | Four times a later word loaded through a byte-derived base plus **0x74** |
| Doubled-link base | Twice that same later loaded word |

Scaling and subtraction retain the arithmetic qualifications stated in the interface. These names establish neither allocation boundaries nor ownership.

### Constant-valued writes and repeated publication

Before publication, the function writes **0x3A** as:

- A byte at the constant-write base.
- A halfword at twice that base.
- A word at that base plus **0x20**.

The adjusted destination’s word at **0x20** receives successive stores of **0x20**, **0x2F**, a zero-extended byte read from the adjusted destination plus **4**, and **0x20** again. The byte read occurs after the **0x2F** store and before the byte-derived word store.

**After these computed accesses, the function reads the separate fixed-address word supplying the publication base. It then writes the word value 0x20 at fixed address 0x60.** The publication-base read precedes the **0x60** assignment, not the reverse.

The preceding computed stores may alias the publication-base storage. Its sampled value is therefore the value at that point in the access sequence, not necessarily its entry value. The subsequent **0x60** write does not replace the retained publication-base value.

Publication consists of repeated four-write groups, with this order within each group:

- A word store at an eightfold-scaled destination plus **0x54**, carrying the constant-write base.
- A byte store at the publication base plus **0x19**, carrying the adjusted destination’s low byte.
- A byte store at the adjusted destination plus **0x11**, carrying the publication base’s low byte.
- A word store at the publication base plus **0x34**, carrying the group’s eightfold-scaled destination.

The first group uses eight times the seed word. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted scale. The publication base and byte values are retained rather than reloaded between groups.

The groups remain distinct ordered effects. This document does not supply an exact group-count oracle; that aspect is **documented_only**, not permission to collapse publication into one final state assignment.

### Final snapshots and fixed-address dependencies

After the repeated publication region, the function reads the word at fixed address **0xA9**, then reads a word at the constant-write base plus **0x74**. Both snapshots precede the byte write at fixed address **0x3A**.

That **0x3A** byte receives the low eight bits of **eight times the adjusted scale plus 0x30**. It is not a constant **0x30**, nor is it derived from the publication-base snapshot.

Only after that byte write does the function snapshot the word at the doubled-link base plus **0x14**, followed by the word at the adjusted destination plus **4**. Consequently, either snapshot may observe effects of the **0x3A** write if its word access overlaps that address.

The next updates include a byte at the doubled-link base plus **0x0D** carrying the adjusted destination’s low byte, **0x6C** at the adjusted destination plus **8**, and the publication base’s low byte at adjusted-destination offsets **0x0D** and **9**. A self-address-like word is stored at the doubled-link base plus **0x54**.

The function then assigns the low byte of the retained **0xA9** word snapshot to fixed address **0x67**. It reads a word at the doubled-link base plus **0x44**, and only afterward reads **0x67** for a byte assignment to fixed address **0x21**.

The **0x21** assignment uses that read of **0x67**. It is not specified as a direct copy of the original **0xA9** snapshot. The intervening word read may matter for memory with read effects.

### Final computed updates and separate rereads

The word obtained from doubled-link offset **0x44** supplies a byte at adjusted-destination offset **0x10**, a word at publication-base offset **0x54**, and a shifted byte at doubled-link offset **1**. The publication-base word at **0x54** is subsequently replaced with the adjusted destination.

The variable right shift operates on a represented signed 32-bit value; its count is the adjusted destination’s low eight bits. Negative-operand right-shift behavior and counts outside **0–31** remain **documented_only**. Only the resulting low byte is stored.

A word read at adjusted-destination offset **0x40** is retained for a word store at offset **0x10**. Between that read and store, the earlier offset-4 snapshot is written at publication-base offset **0x74**. A word store of the doubled-link base at publication-base offset **0x44** follows.

There are then **two separate later reads of fixed byte 0x67**, each supplying a byte store at the adjusted destination plus **9**:

- The first occurs after the publication-base **0x44** store and before a byte store at doubled-link offset **9** and a word store at adjusted-destination offset **0x44** carrying the retained **0xA9** snapshot.
- The second occurs after those intervening stores and after a word read at adjusted-destination offset **0x54**.

Those reads cannot be replaced by the byte originally assigned to **0x67**, or by each other. Intervening computed stores may overlap **0x67**; fixed-address read behavior may also be significant.

The word obtained from adjusted-destination offset **0x54** becomes another linked base. Subsequent effects include:

- Copying the low byte of the earlier constant-write-base **0x74** snapshot to linked-base offset **0x0D** and doubled-link offset **0x11**.
- Restoring the earlier doubled-link **0x14** snapshot into doubled-link offset **0x54**.
- Reading a word at adjusted-destination offset **0x14** before using its low byte later.
- Replacing adjusted-destination byte **9** and word **0x44** with linked-base-derived values.

The function then follows the word at doubled-link offset **4**. It stores the halfword **0x65** at twice that loaded value and reads a word at its **0x64** offset. Between that read and a subsequent read at its **0x74** offset, it updates linked-base byte **0x11** and stores the word **100** at linked-base offset **0x54**.

A later word read at doubled-link offset **0x64** supplies a destination for another **0x54** word update and the value stored through twice the earlier **0x64**-derived word.

2. The retained word read at offset **0x64** of the value loaded from doubled-link offset **4**.
3. The earlier doubled-link-base **0x14** snapshot, taken after the fixed **0x3A** byte write.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional requirements, not executed tests.

1. **Input forwarding.** If the initial invocation is reached, its first three arguments preserve all 32 bits of the explicit inputs. Its fourth argument derives from implicit entry state.

2. **Finite-width shifts.** Under 32-bit bitvector semantics, shifting **0x01000000** left by eight produces zero. A subsequent right shift cannot restore the lost bit.

3. **Extraction boundary.** A ten-bit extraction of **1023**, subsequently shifted left by six, produces **65472**. Bits outside the extracted positions do not contribute.

4. **Low addresses.** Low masked bits of **0x7F00** in the small-extraction calculation produce **508**, giving doubled base **1016**. Substituting a heap address is not equivalent.

5. **Conditional cleared-word read.** The cleared large-base word reads as zero only if ordinary memory semantics apply and no intervening effect changes overlapping bytes.

6. **Publication-base timing.** If the computed constant-write sequence changes storage supplying the publication base, the later read observes the changed storage. That read still precedes the word write of **0x20** at **0x60**.

7. **Final snapshot timing.** The fixed **0xA9** snapshot and constant-write-base **0x74** snapshot precede the **0x3A** byte write. The doubled-link **0x14** and adjusted-destination **4** snapshots follow it. Moving all snapshots before the byte write is unacceptable when accesses overlap.

8. **Derived fixed byte.** If the adjusted scale’s low byte is **3**, the byte written at **0x3A** is **0x48** under the stated finite-width interpretation.

9. **Separate global rereads.** If an intervening computed store changes **0x67** from **0x12** to **0x34**, a subsequent read supplying adjusted-destination byte **9** uses **0x34**, not the initially assigned byte. The two later reads may supply different values.

10. **Final retained arguments.** Changes to the original snapshot locations after their reads do not alter the retained third and fourth arguments of the final invocation.
