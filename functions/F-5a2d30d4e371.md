### Arithmetic model

**[INFERENCE]** Under 32-bit bitvector arithmetic, left shifts discard bits above bit 31, and scaled address components retain their low 32 bits. The representation contains signed-looking shifts and multiplications, so these consequences are conditional rather than portable language-level guarantees.

Under that model:

- Left shift by eight followed by logical right shift by eight retains only the original low 24 bits.
- Selecting the low 20 bits and shifting right ten yields at most 1023.
- An eight-bit-left-shifted word, masked with 0x7fff and shifted right six, is a multiple of four between 0 and 508.
- Doubling that last value produces a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016.
- Two successive left shifts by 28 yield zero.
- Zero-derived arguments do not eliminate their associated invocations.

A late byte-store value involves a signed-looking right shift whose count is selected from an address-like value’s low byte. That count can exceed 31. Target rules for negative operands and large counts are documented_only; masking the count to five bits is not established.

### Opening memory updates and the zero-load invariant

The following descriptive roles identify retained values without asserting object types:

- **Terminal low word** and **terminal high word:** the last combined dependency result before direct updates.
- **Shift-derived base:** a retained result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Small derived base:** twice the masked-and-shifted value whose range is described above.
- **Retained shifted word:** an earlier dependency result shifted left eight bits.
- **Retained high-shift value:** an earlier low word shifted left 25 bits.

The opening stores publish the retained high-shift value at terminal-high-word plus 0x14. Another destination, retained-high-shift-value plus 9, first receives the terminal high word’s low byte and then the retained high-shift value’s low byte. Between those byte stores, a word store places the shift-derived base at terminal-low-word times 256 plus 0x54.

The opening clearing-and-widening group has an important load invariant. A zero word is stored at shift-derived-base plus 0x14. A byte is then read at shift-derived-base plus 5. Before the word at plus 0x14 is loaded, the displayed stores are:

- A zero word at small-derived-base plus 0x14.
- The previously read byte, unsigned-widened, as a word at shift-derived-base plus 0x34.

**[INFERENCE]** With ordinary 32-bit byte-addressed memory, successful accesses, and no external mutation, the subsequent load at shift-derived-base plus 0x14 returns zero. The plus-0x34 word is disjoint from the plus-0x14 word, including under modulo-2³² addressing. Any overlap from the other zero store can only write more zero bytes; it cannot make the cleared word nonzero. Endianness does not change this conclusion.

Device-memory effects, address mappings with nonordinary aliasing, or external mutation are separate documented_only possibilities. They are not ordinary aliasing counterexamples to this invariant.

The loaded word is subsequently used in addresses and low-byte stores. The representation still displays an actual load; the ordinary-memory value conclusion does not establish that a device-facing read may be removed.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

### Truncation and widening

With 32-bit bitvector shifts, a low word of 0x12345678 shifted left eight and logically right eight produces 0x00345678. An unsigned byte load of 0xfe widens to 254, not −2.

### Opening cleared word

With ordinary successful 32-bit memory accesses and no external mutation, the opening load at shift-derived-base plus 0x14 returns zero. This remains true if the intervening small-derived-base zero store partially or completely overlaps it. The widened-byte store at plus 0x34 is disjoint.

A nonzero result requires conditions outside that ordinary-memory example, such as external mutation or nonordinary device behavior; ordinary overlapping zero stores are insufficient.

### Captured global base

If the captured global word denotes one base and the subsequent fixed-0x60 store changes its original storage, publication still uses the captured base. Reloading the global after that fixed store changes the specified dependency.

### Final capture boundary

If the fixed-0x3a byte write overlaps either subsequently captured word, acceptance uses the word observed after that write. Capturing the saved offset-0x14 word or publication-base offset-4 word beforehand is not equivalent.

### Fresh fixed-byte observations

If the three reads at fixed address 0x67 observe 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56 because intervening writes alter that storage, the fixed-0x21 write receives 0x12 and the two later publication-base offset-9 stores receive 0x34 and 0x56 respectively. Reusing 0x12 for all three fails this conditional trace.

### Repeated destination writes

In a trace-observable environment, publication-base plus 0x20 exposes 0x20, 0x2f, the widened byte-load result, and 0x20 in that order. Checking only the final word is insufficient.
