### Early updates and first fixed-address observation

The final dependency result contributes both halves to direct memory behavior. Its high word is used as a base for an offset-0x14 word store. Its low word participates in a separately scaled offset-0x54 destination.

Another destination receives two byte stores at offset 9. The first uses the low byte of that final high word; the second uses a retained dependency-derived word. The separately scaled word store occurs between them.

The early scaled base has its offset-0x14 word cleared before its offset-5 unsigned byte is captured. A compact-base word clear and a word store of that captured byte at early-scaled-base offset 0x34 intervene before the early offset-0x14 word is reloaded.

That reload is an observation, not a constant-zero substitution point. The intervening accesses can alias the cleared storage. Its captured result also participates in later address formation and overwrites.

A further compact-base offset-0x14 clear precedes two distinct byte writes to compact-base offset 0x15, both using the low byte of that reload.

Only afterward is the byte at fixed address **0x05** captured and zero-extended. Fixed address **0x01** then receives the low byte of the compact base. Subsequent use of the captured byte as an address component does not refresh address 0x05.

The seed capture occurs later, after additional computed byte and word writes. Its source is then overwritten with the earlier offset-0x14 reload. Later scaling uses the captured seed, not the overwritten source.

### Early fixed stores and alias-sensitive state

Between the seed capture and the next fixed stores, loaded offset-0x74 words supply further destinations. Updates include self-valued word stores, offset-9 byte stores, and repeated stores involving the seed capture. A compact-base offset-0x34 word receives the compact component.

The following ordering constraints are established:

- A dependency-derived shifted word reaches compact-base offset 0x14 before fixed address **0x54** receives twice the seed capture as a 32-bit word.
- Two compact-base offset-0x14 word stores of the seed capture follow that fixed store.
- Two compact-base offset-0x15 byte stores then use the low byte of four times the seed capture.
- Fixed address **0x15** subsequently receives the low byte of the compact base.

These are interleaved effects, not an isolated global-update phase. Computed accesses may overlap fixed storage before or after its named observation point.

Byte stores at offset 0x15 overlap a word beginning at offset 0x14 when their bases coincide. Offset 0x11 similarly overlaps an offset-0x10 word. Endianness changes the resulting word interpretation, but not the fact of overlap.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Truncation:** A 32-bit value 0x12345678 shifted left by 8 contributes 0x34567800. Retaining upper bits in a wider argument is not equivalent.

2. **Unsigned capture:** A byte value 0xff observed at 0x05 contributes address component 255, not −1. Later modification of 0x05 does not change the saved component.

3. **Aliasing reload:** If an intervening write changes the cleared early offset-0x14 word, its reload observes the changed storage. Substitution with zero fails.

4. **Fixed-store placement:** The word write at 0x54 remains before the two seed-valued compact-base offset-0x14 writes and the following offset-0x15 byte writes. Deferring it changes the trace.

5. **Saved full word:** If working-base offset 4 yields 0x11223344, the later separate-base offset-0x74 word store uses that saved value despite subsequent source changes.

6. **Saved versus fresh storage:** If 0xa9 yields 0x123456ab, the initial 0x67 byte is 0xab and the later saved-word store uses 0x123456ab. If an intervening computed write changes 0x67, later fresh reads use its changed byte.

7. **Mixed widths:** The terminal 0x0065 store updates two bytes according to target byte order. A one-byte 0x65 store is insufficient.

8. **Observable overwrites:** The four working-base offset-0x20 writes preserve their order and intervening byte observation, even though the final word is 0x20.

9. **Failure boundary:** An invalid reached address does not authorize skipping its access or require a clean error result.

10. **Final provenance:** Reaching F-U003 requires the four stated argument positions to use their respective saved observations, not later reloads from matching addresses.
