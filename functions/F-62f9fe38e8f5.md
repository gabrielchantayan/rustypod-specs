# F-62f9fe38e8f5

## Purpose

F-62f9fe38e8f5 performs an ordered sequence of memory reads and mutations, traverses links obtained from that memory, forms additional destinations arithmetically, and invokes an unresolved execution dependency.

The established behavior includes 8-, 16-, and 32-bit accesses; repeated reads and writes; self-address stores; truncation of words and addresses into bytes; and use of captured values after intervening mutations. Access order is material because no non-aliasing guarantee is established.

No application-level purpose is established. Graph rearrangement is a possible interpretation, not a required semantic model.

## Interface

| Second | 32 bits | No visible use |
| Third, designated **P** | 32 bits | Supplies an initial destination and stored address-like value |
| Fourth, designated **V** | 8 bits | Supplies an early byte store |

Three additional incoming address-like values, designated **A**, **B**, and **C**, are used without a complete account of their calling convention. Their use is consistent with 32-bit addresses.

Three external 32-bit storage locations are designated **E1**, **E2**, and **E3**. These names identify locations, not immutable values. Each contributes a value when its read occurs.

All other labels below designate values captured by particular reads. They are documentary names, not additional arguments or established object types.

### Outputs and completion

The observable outputs are memory mutations and the final invocation. No explicit return-value contract is established. If the final dependency returns normally, the supplied representation reaches a return without visibly consuming its result.

There is no visible allocation, deallocation, validation, error result, recovery handler, or rollback mechanism. A failed access can occur after earlier mutations have completed.

### Preconditions and memory model

Every reached address must support the access attempted there. Required storage includes fields reached through loaded words, sums of address-like values, doubled values, and an address derived from a byte.

No object extent, ownership rule, non-aliasing condition, or synchronization guarantee is established. A loaded word used later as an address need not denote the same region as a similarly named earlier capture.

The contract concerns the access sequence exposed by the supplied static representation. Whether an implementation makes every ordinary-memory access externally distinguishable is unresolved.

## Behavior

### Widths, arithmetic, and ordering

A byte is 8 bits, a halfword 16 bits, and a word 32 bits. Unless stated otherwise, every read below is a word read. Every store explicitly identifies its width.

“Low byte” and “low halfword” mean the low eight and sixteen bits respectively. No range check precedes narrowing. Pointer-like words and stored self-addresses are represented as 32-bit quantities, not portable host pointers.

Address arithmetic must follow the actual execution target. The representation does not establish portable signed-overflow semantics or fully specify address wrapping. Byte order and alignment requirements remain unresolved.

Within each numbered item, accesses occur in the stated textual order; items and stages follow consecutively. Captures retain their read-time values. A later access is fresh only where expressly described as another read. These ordering rules apply even when destinations overlap links, external storage, or other fields.

### 1. Initial publication and first linked region

1. A word containing **P − 0x74** is stored at **P − 0x20**. The word at **A + 0x14** is then captured as **InitialLink**, followed by the byte store of **V** at **C + 0x11**.

2. The word at **C + 0x14** is captured as **InitialValue**. Its low byte is stored at **InitialLink + 0x15**. A separate read of **C + 0x14** supplies **FirstRegion**.

3. The word at **B + 0x64** is captured as **EarlyValue** and written back there unchanged. Byte **0x73** is stored at **FirstRegion + 0x0C**, then EarlyValue’s low byte at **B + 0x15**, then InitialValue as a word at **C + 0x34**.

4. The word at **FirstRegion + 0x74** is captured as **EarlyLink**. Only afterward is FirstRegion’s own address stored as a word at **FirstRegion + 0x54**. B’s low byte is stored at **C + 0x0D**; InitialValue is stored as a word at **C + 0x54**; FirstRegion is stored as a word at **C + 0x14**.

5. A fresh read of **B + 0x64** supplies **LaterValue**. Byte **0x74** is stored at **FirstRegion + 0x10**; word **0x00000074** at **B + 0x54**; then word **0x00000074** at **FirstRegion + 0x10**. Only then is **FirstRegion + 0x74** read again, yielding **SelfLinkedRegion**.

6. C’s address is stored as a word at **C + 0x54**, followed by word **0x00000074** at **FirstRegion + 0x30**. EarlyValue’s low byte is stored at **B + 1**. LaterValue’s low byte is stored at **EarlyValue + 9**, and LaterValue is stored as a word at **B + 0x74**.

7. Byte **0x74** is stored at **B + 0x11**. A read of **FirstRegion + 0x60** supplies **FirstData**. EarlyValue’s low byte then replaces **B + 0x11**; LaterValue is stored as a word at **B + 0x34**; FirstData’s low byte replaces **FirstRegion + 0x10**.

The two FirstRegion offset-0x74 reads are distinct and lie on opposite sides of its offset-0x54 self-address store and offset-0x10 word store.

### 2. Transition through B’s links

1. A read of **B + 0x14** supplies **SecondRegion**. EarlyLink’s low byte is stored at **C + 0x11**, C’s address as a word at **B + 0x44**, and LaterValue’s low byte at **B + 0x11**.

2. A read of **B + 0x74** supplies **BeforeReplacement**. C’s low byte is stored at **B + 0x0D**. Word **0x00000066** is stored at **SecondRegion + 0x10**; C’s address is again stored as a word at **B + 0x44**; byte **0x66** is stored at **SecondRegion + 0x10**. Word **0x00000066** is then stored at **B + 0x54**, followed by the same word at **SecondRegion + 0x60**.

3. Another read of **B + 0x74** supplies **ThirdRegion**, after which **B + 0x74** receives C’s address as a word. Word **0x00000066** is stored at **ThirdRegion + 0x40**, followed by BeforeReplacement’s low byte at **B + 0x0D**.

4. A read of **ThirdRegion + 0x44** supplies **ThirdData**. B’s address is stored as a word at **B + 0x54**, followed by SelfLinkedRegion’s own address at **SelfLinkedRegion + 0x54**.

5. A read of **ThirdRegion + 0x14** supplies **TaggedRegion**. ThirdData’s low byte is stored at **C + 0x11**. A fresh read of **C + 0x14** then supplies the low byte stored at **TaggedRegion + 0x15**.

### 3. Deeper traversal and publication

1. Another read of **C + 0x14** supplies **FourthRegion**. A read of **B + 0x64** captures **BeforeTag**, immediately before word **0x00000073** is stored at **FourthRegion + 0x20**.

2. A separate read of **B + 0x64** captures **AfterTag**. Its low byte is stored at **FourthRegion + 9**. The word at **FourthRegion + 0x74** is then read as **FifthRegion**.

3. B’s low byte is stored at **FifthRegion + 0x0D**. The word at **FifthRegion + 0x54** is read and written back unchanged. Byte **0x74** is stored at **FifthRegion + 9**; BeforeTag is stored as a word at **B + 0x44**; B’s address is stored as a word at **FifthRegion + 0x34**.

4. A read of **FifthRegion + 4** supplies **SixthRegion**. AfterTag is stored as a word at **SixthRegion + 0x24**. **BeforeTag’s low byte is then stored at B + 0x19.** Only afterward are **SixthRegion + 0x74** and **SixthRegion + 4** read, in that order, yielding **RetainedLink** and **ReplacementValue**.

5. A read of **B + 0x74** supplies **TraversalRoot**. Its offset-0x14 word is read to obtain a destination base; RetainedLink is stored as a word at that base plus **0x54**. The word at **TraversalRoot + 4** is then captured as **BranchRegion**.

6. AfterTag’s low byte is stored at **B + 0x0D** twice consecutively. ReplacementValue is stored as a word at **B + 0x64**, followed by word **0x00000074** at **BranchRegion + 0x30**.

7. **TraversalRoot + 4** is read again; that newly obtained base’s offset-0x74 word is read as **IntermediateRegion**. A fresh read of **B + 0x64** supplies a retained low byte, **PublishedByte**, which is stored at **B + 0x1D**.

8. The word at **B + 0x44** is read, followed by that result’s offset-0x14 word, yielding **PublishedRegion**. RetainedLink is stored as a word at **IntermediateRegion + 0x34**, followed by PublishedRegion as a word at **B + 0x14**.

The B + 0x19 store is not an incidental final-state annotation: it precedes both SixthRegion reads and can affect their results through aliasing.

### 4. Byte tagging and late link updates

1. A read of **B + 0x54** captures **SavedTag**. B’s low byte is stored at **PublishedRegion + 9**. The word at **IntermediateRegion + 4** is read, followed by that result’s offset-0x74 word, yielding **DeepRegion**.

2. RetainedLink’s low byte is stored at **DeepRegion + 0x15**. B’s address is stored as a word at **B + 0x54**; IntermediateRegion’s low byte at **B + 0x11**; and SavedTag’s low byte at **DeepRegion + 0x11**.

3. A read of **DeepRegion + 0x64** captures **DeepValue**. PublishedByte is stored at **B + 9**. A fresh read of **IntermediateRegion + 4** then selects **SelectedRegion**.

4. B’s low byte is stored at **SelectedRegion + 9**. The word at **SelectedRegion + 0x50** is captured as **SelectedData**. DeepValue’s low byte is stored at **SelectedRegion + 0x11**, followed by a second store of B’s low byte at **SelectedRegion + 9**.

5. DeepValue is stored as a word at **B + 0x14**. SelectedRegion’s low byte is stored at **B + 0x0D**. SelectedData’s low byte is then stored at **SelectedRegion + 0**.

6. The word at **IntermediateRegion + 0x74** is captured as **R**. The word at **IntermediateRegion + 0x14** is then captured as **FinalParent**. Only afterward is SelectedRegion’s own address stored as a word at **SelectedRegion + 0x54**.

7. A read of **FinalParent + 4** supplies **S**. Byte **0x64** is stored at **S + 0x10**; word **0x00000064** at **B + 0x54**; then word **0x00000064** at **S + 0x40**.

8. DeepValue’s low byte is stored at **B + 0x0D**; S’s low byte at **FinalParent + 9**; DeepValue as a word at **B + 0x24**; and B’s low byte at **FinalParent + 0x11**.

**R is the captured word loaded from IntermediateRegion + 0x74, not IntermediateRegion’s address.** Its read precedes the FinalParent read, SelectedRegion’s self-address store, and the read selecting S.

### 5. Arithmetic-address tail and final invocation

The external reads and ordinary-memory accesses have this order:

1. **E1** is read, capturing **ExternalOne**.
2. **B + 0x74** is read, capturing **Q**.
3. Q’s low halfword is stored at **B + Q**.
4. ExternalOne’s low halfword is stored at **2 × S**.
5. A byte is read at **B + R**. Its plain-character interpretation is converted to a 32-bit unsigned value, **U**.
6. Q’s low halfword is stored at **ExternalOne + Q**.
7. Q’s low halfword is stored again at **B + Q**.
8. Word **0x00000045** is stored at **U + 0x45**.
9. **E2** is read, capturing **ExternalTwo**.
10. **E3** is read, capturing **ExternalThree**.
11. **B + 0x44** is read, capturing **FinalArgument**.
12. Byte **0x44** is stored at **U + 0x44**.
13. ExternalThree is stored as a word at **2 × ExternalTwo**.
14. ExternalTwo’s low halfword is stored at **RetainedLink + ExternalTwo**.
15. The unresolved dependency is invoked with three visible arguments, in order: **U bitwise-OR 0x44**, **ExternalTwo**, and **FinalArgument**.

The two stores at B + Q use captured Q; neither reloads B + 0x74. ExternalOne is likewise retained across intervening writes. The first argument uses bitwise OR, not addition.

## Side effects

All preceding mutations occur before the unresolved invocation. A fault, nonreturning dependency, or other abnormal completion may leave a partially modified memory graph. No restoration behavior is established.

Self-address stores specify word contents only. They do not establish allocation, ownership transfer, reference counting, lifetime extension, or responsibility for release.

Aliasing may alter later traversal and external reads. In particular, byte stores may modify later-read words, word stores may redirect links, and arithmetic destinations may overlap external storage. Same-value write-backs and repeated stores remain distinct accesses in this static contract.

No assumption is made that the final dependency preserves memory, globals, or ownership relationships.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional analytical criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial publication.** With P equal to **0x00001000**, and assuming the relevant arithmetic and write are supported, the initial word store targets **0x00000FE0** and carries **0x00000F8C**.

2. **Distinct early reads.** If FirstRegion’s first and second offset-0x74 reads yield different words, EarlyLink must come from the first and SelfLinkedRegion from the second. Combining those reads changes the contract.

3. **Restored byte store.** If BeforeTag is **0x123456AB**, the B + 0x19 store carries **0xAB**, irrespective of AfterTag. It occurs after SixthRegion’s offset-0x24 word store and before its offset-0x74 and offset-4 reads. Any resulting overlap must affect those later reads according to the target memory model.

4. **Late interleaving.** If SelectedRegion’s first offset-9 store overlaps its offset-0x50 word, SelectedData reflects the intervening read after that first store. Moving the read before the store, or moving both offset-9 stores together, is not equivalent.

5. **Correct R operand.** If B is **0x1000**, IntermediateRegion is **0x2000**, and its offset-0x74 read captures **0x3000**, the tail byte-read address is **0x4000**, not **0x3000**, assuming supported arithmetic.

6. **External-read ordering.** An access trace must place the E1 read before the Q read from B + 0x74, and both before the first halfword store at B + Q. The second such store follows the byte read at B + R and the halfword store at ExternalOne + Q.

7. **Byte-order boundary.** A word store of **0x00000074**, followed by a byte store of **0xAB** at the same address, leaves bytes **AB 00 00 00** under little-endian execution or **AB 00 00 74** under big-endian execution, absent other overlapping writes.

8. **Signed-character boundary.** For tail byte **0x80**, signed plain-character interpretation produces U equal to **0xFFFFFF80**; unsigned interpretation produces **0x00000080**. Target resolution is required to choose between them.

9. **OR and failure behavior.** With U equal to **0x44**, the first visible argument remains **0x44**, not **0x88**. If an earlier tail store faults instead, rollback and subsequent invocation are not established.
