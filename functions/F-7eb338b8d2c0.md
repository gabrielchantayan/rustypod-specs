## Acceptance examples

These are conditional acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial narrowing.** An initial word `0x12345678` produces word `0x12345678` at A + `0x54`, followed by byte `0x78` at C + `0x11`.

2. **Early descendant-byte observation.** Suppose the root’s G store has completed and R₂ + `5` contains `0x21`. If the subsequent R₂ + `0x54` store aliases that byte and changes it to `0x37`, the saved low-address base remains `0x21`. Its read occurred before that mutation.

3. **Distinct external observations.** If saved B₀ is `0x100`, Bᵥ is `0x1234`, and B𝒹 is `0x200`, the first byte store writes `0x34` at `0x400`. The later saved-byte restoration writes `0x34` at twice saved B₀, namely `0x200`.

4. **Unsequenced N references.** If N’s source can change between its two references, no requirement selects which reference observes the earlier value. Once their actual results are fixed, the byte payload comes from Nᵥ and the destination from twice N𝒹. Subsequent uses of saved N remain independent.

5. **Pre-shift partial overwrite.** P’s low halfword is stored at Y + O before the shift, but the shift consumes saved W₀ rather than a reloaded partially modified word.

6. **Graph-entry aliasing.** If Z₁ + `0x34` overlaps Dₑ + `0x74`, storing the saved Y-relative anchor can affect the following owner read. Reading owner before that store would not satisfy the contract.

7. **Absolute snapshot.** A later change at `0xdb` alone does not alter Δ’s subsequent uses at anchor + `0x54`, owner + `0x11`, and owner + `0x54`.

8. **Mixed-width region writes.** Region + `0x14` receives byte `0x73`; region + `0x20` later receives word `0x6e`. These are separate writes, not inferred consequences of stores at region + `0x10`.

9. **Independent comparisons.** H ≤ Y and H ≤ the fresh Q-source observation can differ. They control different contributions and must not share a cached Boolean.

10. **Final alias timing.** The halfword store at twice Dₗ can affect the subsequent signed-byte read through aliasing. The later L₁ + W store cannot retroactively change saved Bₛ.

11. **Negative final byte.** A final signed byte of −1 yields `0xffffffff`; OR with `0x44` leaves it unchanged. The Bₛ-derived stores still require their reached addresses to be accessible.
