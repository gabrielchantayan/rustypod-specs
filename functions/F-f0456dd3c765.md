# F-f0456dd3c765

## Purpose

F-f0456dd3c765 performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, derives packed values and addresses from their results, modifies memory through computed and fixed addresses, and invokes an unresolved final continuation.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation exposes argument transformations, access widths, dependent address formation, repeated stores, fixed-global captures and reloads, and four final arguments. It does not establish an application-level purpose.

**Inferred intent:** some accesses resemble updates to linked records or packed fields. No particular container, protocol, device, initialization task, or text-processing purpose is established.

This contract preserves observable dataflow without repairing suspicious addresses or treating repeated writes as redundant. Behavior not established by the supplied material is marked **documented_only**.

## Behavior

### Ordering model

The visible body has three broad phases: indirect invocations, direct memory mutation, and a final indirect invocation. No explicit conditional branch or loop appears in the supplied representation. Dependencies may branch, fail, or not return.

The ordering described here is the ordering exposed by that representation. It is not an independently verified hardware bus trace. Where a single expression contains multiple reads, their relative machine order remains **documented_only**.

Capturing a value and reading its storage again are different events. Computed storage is not established to be disjoint from fixed globals or other computed destinations.

### Indirect-invocation phase

The first target is selected by adding 4094 bytes to incoming control-link state. Subsequent targets are selected relative to successive embedded control locations using the same displacement. This is an ordered family of indirect calls, not evidence of one reusable helper.

The invocations have zero through four represented arguments. Their transformations include:

- Primary results shifted left by eight bits.
- Repeated copies of a captured value in different argument positions.
- High result words and secondary results shifted by varying counts.
- Explicit zero arguments.
- Logical right shifts and masked low-width fields.
- Values retained across intervening invocations.

Early calls combine an eight-bit-shifted primary result with the incoming register value shifted left by four. A later call repeats the former value, shifted by another seven, in two argument positions and supplies the same incoming-derived third argument.

Later regions combine shifted low words, unshifted or shifted high words, secondary results, and captured fields. Similar argument shapes do not make distinct invocations interchangeable. Calls receiving only zero-valued represented arguments remain required events; neither purity nor lack of effects is established.

Masks retain fields of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit. Operation order affects the result. For example:

- Retaining 24 bits and shifting right by 20 yields 0 through 15.
- A truncating 32-bit left shift by eight followed by a logical right shift by eight retains only the original low 24 bits.
- Retaining 20 bits, shifting right by ten, then left by six positions yields a maximum of 65,472. A final shift by five instead yields a maximum of 32,736.

Some captured values are explicitly zero and remain zero through subsequent masks and shifts. Some nonzero-origin chains also discard every original bit under truncating 32-bit semantics, including successive left shifts by 28. These observations do not resolve whether every represented signed expression faithfully specifies the underlying machine behavior.

### Initial direct accesses and fixed-global capture

The first word store places the packed base at offset 0x14 from the final pair’s high word. A byte store then places the low byte of that high word at packed-base offset 9. The primary base is stored at offset 0x54 from 256 times the final pair’s low word, before packed-base offset 9 is overwritten with the packed base’s own low byte.

Next, primary-base offset 0x14 receives a zero word. The byte at primary-base offset 5 is captured, compact-base offset 0x14 receives a zero word, and the captured byte is zero-expanded into a word at primary-base offset 0x34.

The initial link is then read from primary-base offset 0x14. Compact-base offset 0x14 receives another zero word, followed by two byte writes of the initial link’s low byte at compact-base offset 0x15.

**Only after those accesses is the datum at fixed address 0x05 captured.** The subsequent byte write to fixed address 0x01 receives the compact base’s low byte. Later computed addresses use the previously captured datum base, not an implicit reread of 0x05.

After that fixed write:

- Datum-base offset 0x15 receives the compact index’s low byte.
- Primary-base offset 0x11 receives the captured argument word’s low byte.
- Initial-link offset 0x54 receives the captured argument word as a word.
- Primary-base offset 0x11 is overwritten with zero.

The root word is then captured from datum-base offset 0x54 before that location receives the initial link. Compact-base offset 0x34 receives the compact index.

These orderings remain significant if the datum, fixed destinations, and computed accesses overlap.

### Linked updates before the repeated tail

Words read at offsets 0x04, 0x14, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74 participate in further address formation. Byte reads at offset 5 sometimes become unscaled bases for word reads at offset 0x74.

A word captured at primary-base offset 0x74 becomes a destination base. Its offset 0x54 receives an earlier call-derived value shifted left 26, and later the captured argument word. Root-word offset 0x24 receives the root word twice; the dependent destination’s offset 9 receives the root word’s low byte twice, with intervening writes.

The compact-base offset-0x14 sequence has an important fixed-global interruption:

1. It receives the earlier call-derived value shifted left 26.
2. Fixed address 0x54 receives a **32-bit word equal to the root word shifted left once**.
3. Compact-base offset 0x14 receives the root word twice.
4. Compact-base offset 0x15 receives the scaled root’s low byte twice.
5. Fixed address 0x15 receives the **compact base’s low byte**.

Thus, neither fixed write is merely an unspecified effect at a listed address. Both have established payloads and positions among potentially aliasing computed writes.

Subsequent updates use the scaled root, eight times the root word, and twice a word read from compact-base offset 0x74. They modify word fields at offsets 0x14, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54 and byte fields at offsets 9, 0x0D, and 0x15. Some destinations receive self-address values. Dependent reads remain interspersed with these writes; captured values cannot generally be replaced by later reloads.

For the tail and finalization, define the **source word** as the later word obtained at offset 0x74 from a newly read unsigned byte. Define **tail payload** as four times that source word and **half-payload base** as twice it.

Before the repeated region, the body stores 0x3A as:

- A byte at four times the source word.
- A halfword at eight times the source word.
- A word at four times the source word plus 0x20.

The subtraction producing the adjusted value occurs after the first of these stores and before the latter two.

Work-base offset 0x20 then receives four word writes in order: 0x20, 0x2F, the zero-expanded byte read at work-base offset 4, and 0x20 again.

The tail base is captured **after that four-write sequence and before** the 32-bit store of 0x20 at fixed address 0x60. All tail accesses use that captured address word. Even if the 0x60 store aliases its original storage, no replacement reload is represented.

### Finalization: captures, arithmetic, and fixed-byte reloads

Finalization begins by capturing the **32-bit word at fixed address 0xA9**. This occurs before the word read at tail-payload offset 0x74 and before the byte write to fixed address 0x3A.

The 0x3A write is arithmetic: its payload is the low eight bits of **eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30**. It is not a transfer of an existing byte.

Next, a word at half-payload-base offset 0x14 is captured for later restoration and the final continuation. A word at work-base offset 4 is also captured before later related writes.

The ensuing computed writes place the work base’s low byte at half-payload-base offset 0x0D, 0x6C at work-base offset 8, and the tail base’s low byte at work-base offsets 0x0D and 9. Half-payload-base offset 0x54 receives the half-payload base itself.

The fixed-byte chain then has distinct capture and reload semantics:

- Fixed address 0x67 receives the low byte of the **captured 0xA9 word**.
- A word is read from half-payload-base offset 0x44.
- Fixed address 0x67 is read afresh to supply the byte written to fixed address 0x21.

That dependent word read is between the 0x67 write and reload. The 0x21 payload is therefore not specified as an unconditional reuse of the captured 0xA9 low byte.

The offset-0x44 word supplies a byte at work-base offset 0x10 and a word at tail-base offset 0x54. Its signed right shift supplies a byte at half-payload-base offset 1, using the work base’s low eight bits as the shift count. Large-count and negative-value behavior remain **documented_only**. Tail-base offset 0x54 is subsequently overwritten with the work base.

A word read from work-base offset 0x40 supplies the word written at work-base offset 0x10, after the earlier byte write there. The previously captured work-base offset-4 word is stored at tail-base offset 0x74; tail-base offset 0x44 receives the half-payload base.

There are then **two further, separate reads of fixed address 0x67**, each supplying a byte write at work-base offset 9:

- The first occurs after those computed word updates.
- Between it and the second, half-payload-base offset 9 receives the work base’s low byte, work-base offset 0x44 receives the captured 0xA9 word, and a dependent word is read from work-base offset 0x54.

The second reload follows that dependent read. Neither reload is specified as using a stable cached byte. Intervening computed writes may alias fixed address 0x67.

### Final dependent writes and continuation

The word captured at work-base offset 0x54 becomes another destination base. Its offset 0x0D receives the low byte of the earlier tail-payload offset-0x74 capture. Half-payload-base offset 0x54 is restored from its earlier offset-0x14 capture.

A later word captured at work-base offset 0x14 supplies a byte at the dependent destination’s offset 0x11. Meanwhile, half-payload-base offset 0x11 receives the tail-payload capture’s low byte; work-base offset 9 and offset 0x44 receive the dependent destination as byte and word respectively.

A word read at half-payload-base offset 4 becomes the **final root**. Twice the final root receives a halfword 0x65. The word at final-root offset 0x64 is captured as the **continuation value** before the dependent destination receives a word of decimal 100 at offset 0x54.

The word at final-root offset 0x74 is captured for the fourth final argument. A separate word read at half-payload-base offset 0x64 becomes the last destination base. Half-payload-base offset 9 receives the final root’s low byte; last-destination offset 0x54 receives the continuation value; twice the continuation value receives the last destination as a word.

The final indirect invocation receives, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The continuation value.
3. The earlier half-payload-base offset-0x14 capture.
4. The captured final-root offset-0x74 word.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional obligations, not executed tests.

1. **Unsigned expansion:** a captured byte of 0xE7 produces a word payload of 0x000000E7, not a sign-extended value.

2. **Fixed-global ordering:** if the write to 0x01 overlaps the datum at 0x05, subsequent datum-derived addresses still use the value captured before that write.

3. **Fixed payloads:** under truncating 32-bit shifts, a root word of 0x12345678 produces 0x2468ACF0 at fixed word address 0x54. A compact base of 0x318 produces byte 0x18 at fixed address 0x15.

4. **Tail-address capture:** if the fixed 0x60 store changes the original global address word, tail accesses still use its earlier captured value.

5. **Arithmetic byte:** an adjusted value of 5 produces byte 0x58 at fixed address 0x3A.

6. **Reload sensitivity:** if an intervening computed write changes 0x67, the next represented read of 0x67 supplies the changed byte. The 0x21 write and the two later work-base writes need not share a payload.

7. **Repeated effects:** an observable access trace preserves the four writes at work-base offset 0x20 and the repeated tail stores, rather than only their final values.

8. **Partial failure:** an inaccessible dependent destination has no represented rollback or status-code conversion.
