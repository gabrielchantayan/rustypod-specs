## Behavior

### Arithmetic and representation model

The supplied representation uses 32-bit integers for the principal calculations and 32-bit words for the pointer-based stores.

For describing concrete examples below, a **conditional 32-bit model** means:

- Integer and pointer representations are 32 bits.
- Left shifts and multiplication retain the low 32 bits.
- A right shift of a signed value propagates its sign bit.
- Conversion to an unsigned 32-bit value followed by right shift is logical.
- A byte store retains the low eight bits.

This model is useful for expressing the apparent machine-level operations. It is not an unconditional promise derived from portable language semantics: signed overflow and some signed shifts in the supplied representation do not establish portable behavior.

Multiplication by four and multiplication by eight therefore must not be treated as unbounded mathematical multiplication when calculating addresses.
