### 2. Bit selection and the two byte reads

The direct masked selections yield exactly one bit:

- Bit 24 of the explicit input and first returned word.
- Bit 29 of the second returned word.
- Bit 22 of the final single-bit phase’s word.

Each selected result is zero or one.

The **first byte read** uses bit 29 of a separately captured lookup word as an offset from numeric address 0x10. Its candidate addresses are therefore exactly 0x10 and 0x11. The read occurs after that word has been forwarded and the intervening separator call has returned.

For the first wide result, the high 32-bit half participates in unsigned subtraction of seven modulo \(2^{32}\). The forwarded value is bit 31 of the resulting word. It is not generally equivalent to an unsigned comparison against seven.

The **second byte read** uses the high 32-bit half of a different 64-bit result. The representation interprets that half as signed, subtracts seven, shifts right by 31, and subtracts the shifted result from 0x10.

Under arithmetic right shift and defined signed subtraction:

- A nonnegative subtraction result selects 0x10.
- A negative subtraction result selects 0x11.

Signed-overflow behavior is unresolved. These conditions must not be extended to overflowing inputs by silently replacing the expression with a comparison or unsigned arithmetic.

The two later top-bit phases independently select bit 63 of their respective 64-bit results. Their outputs are zero or one. Forwarding a low half preserves its 32-bit bit pattern; it does not establish an unsigned application-level interpretation.
