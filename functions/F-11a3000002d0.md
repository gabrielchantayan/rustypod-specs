# F-11a3000002d0

## Purpose

F-11a3000002d0 performs an extended sequence of unresolved indirect dependency invocations, transforms dependency-provided bit fields, and then performs ordered mixed-width memory accesses before a final unresolved indirect transfer.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation describes four 32-bit arguments, no explicit return value, dependency results used as both data and addresses, byte/halfword/word stores, computed memory reads, fixed-location state accesses, and four visible arguments to the final transfer.

**Inferred intent:** some memory relationships resemble linked records, and some literal values coincide with text characters. Neither observation establishes a record-management operation, formatting interface, or output stream.

This is a partial behavioral contract for the supplied static representation, not a guarantee that the routine is independently executable or safe for arbitrary inputs. Dependency conventions, executable arithmetic details, memory accessibility, and final continuation remain **documented_only** where unresolved.

## Behavior

### Dependency interactions and arithmetic

The routine begins with indirect invocations before the explicit argument transformations. The first target depends on incoming control state; later targets follow computed control-address relationships.

The visible phase is a long, predominantly straight-line sequence. No explicit validation branch, retry loop, or early error return is shown. Its interactions include:

- Duplicated shifted arguments.
- Forwarding lower 32-bit result halves after left shifts.
- Forwarding upper 32-bit result halves separately.
- Masking selected fields before scaling.
- Explicit zero-valued argument positions.
- Reusing captured results after intervening invocations.
- Using additional result channels whose definitions are incomplete.

Invocations without displayed arguments are not established to have no machine-level inputs. Ignored results do not make an invocation dispensable. Combining similar interactions, caching dependency results, or omitting calls whose visible arguments are zero is unsupported.

The represented widths are 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, 32-bit scalar/address-bearing quantities, and selected 64-bit result containers.

Unsigned 32-bit left shifts retain the low 32 bits; unsigned right shifts are logical. Selecting bits after a 64-bit right shift by 34, 36, or 44 is not equivalent to a 32-bit shift with a reduced count. A later right shift does not restore bits discarded by an earlier narrowing or left shift.

Byte stores retain only the low eight payload bits. Byte reads assigned to unsigned words are zero-extended. Signed left shifts, signed multiplication overflow, and address calculations require executable semantics before overflowing cases can receive exact numerical results.

A concluding signed right shift takes its count from the low byte of a computed address-bearing value. Counts of 32 or greater are not excluded. Count handling and negative-operand right-shift semantics remain **documented_only**.

### Initial memory effects and cleared-word reread

The first direct effects are, in order:

1. A result-derived word store at offset 0x14 from the final dependency result’s upper half.
2. A byte store at offset 9 from another computed base, carrying the low byte of that upper half.
3. A word store at offset 0x54 from a base formed by multiplying the lower result half by 256.
4. A replacement byte store at the earlier offset-9 destination, carrying a different captured value.

The intervening word store may alias either byte destination. Both byte writes remain distinct effects.

Next, the routine clears a word at offset 0x14 from an initial scaled base, reads a byte at offset 5 from that base, clears a word at offset 0x14 from a separately computed doubled-field base, stores the zero-extended byte at offset 0x34 from the initial base, and rereads the initial base’s offset-0x14 word.

**Ordinary-memory consequence:** if the stores succeed, memory is stable except for these accesses, and reads/writes have ordinary byte-storage semantics, the reread yields zero. The separately computed store also writes zero; even partial overlap cannot introduce nonzero bytes. The byte-derived word store is at offset 0x34, not at the cleared offset 0x14, and its four-byte range is disjoint from that cleared word under ordinary effective-address interpretation.

A nonzero reread requires some other explanation, such as external mutation, device-specific behavior, or unsupported memory semantics. Overlap with the second zero store alone is not such an explanation.

The separate doubled-field destination is cleared again after the reread. Two subsequent byte stores to its offset 0x15 carry the reread’s low byte. These later stores do not change what the preceding reread observed.

### Early fixed state and captured address bases

For the following relationships, **doubled field** means the computed field multiplied by two. **Captured link** means the word subsequently read at offset 0x54 from a base obtained from fixed byte state. These are documentary roles, not recovered object types.

The byte at fixed address 0x05 is read and captured **before** the byte store at 0x01. The store at 0x01 receives the doubled field’s low byte. The captured byte from 0x05 is then used as an address base for computed accesses, including a byte store at offset 0x15 and a word read at offset 0x54.

The captured byte remains a value from that earlier read even if later writes alias 0x05. It is not reread for these uses, nor is it established to be a pointer to an allocated object.

Surrounding effects include a byte update at offset 0x11 of the initial scaled base, a word store based on the earlier cleared-word reread, and replacement of that offset-0x11 byte with zero. The captured link is read from the byte-derived base’s offset 0x54 before that word location is replaced.

Subsequent accesses use words loaded from offset 0x74 as further bases. The routine repeatedly stores the captured link into its own offset-0x24 field and publishes its low byte at computed offset-9 destinations.

Two fixed-location writes have specific placement:

- **Word at 0x54:** receives the captured link shifted left by one. This occurs after a computed offset-0x14 store carrying a dependency-derived shifted value and before that computed location is replaced by the captured link.
- **Byte at 0x15:** receives the doubled field’s low byte. This occurs after two computed offset-0x15 byte stores carrying the low byte of four times the captured link, and before subsequent word updates based on that fourfold quantity.

These fixed destinations may alias other computed accesses. Their payloads and order are not merely an access inventory.

Let **adjusted quantity** denote four times the captured link minus 0x96. Let **working base** denote four times that adjusted quantity, after the represented arithmetic and conversion. Overflowing signed derivations remain subject to the arithmetic uncertainties above.

Publication consists of ordered stores carrying:

- The publication base’s low byte to working-base offset 0x11.

The publication base is a captured value, not an unconditional reload from its separate storage. Aliasing writes to that storage therefore do not by themselves change the captured base.

### Concluding fixed-state captures and fresh byte reads

The concluding region first captures the **32-bit word at 0xA9**. It then reads a computed word at offset 0x74 from the four-times-scaled loaded base used in the literal/publication region. Only after those reads does it write the byte at 0x3A.

The byte at 0x3A receives the low-eight-bit arithmetic result of **eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30**. This is a numeric byte payload, not an established character-output operation.

Next, a word at offset 0x14 from a previously derived doubled loaded base is captured for later restoration and the final transfer. A word at working-base offset 4 is also captured. Computed updates then write bytes at offsets 0x0D, 8, and 9, including 0x6C at working-base offset 8, and write a self-reference at the doubled loaded base’s offset 0x54.

The fixed-state sequence is significant:

- The byte at **0x67** receives the low byte of the captured 0xA9 word.
- A computed word is then read at offset 0x44 from the doubled loaded base.
- The byte at **0x67 is read again**, and that read supplies the byte store at **0x21**.

The intervening computed word read is not itself an ordinary-memory write, but may have nonordinary effects. No unconditional global-preservation assumption applies.

After further computed updates—including an offset-0x10 byte store, temporary and replacement offset-0x54 word stores, a variable-shift-derived byte store, and copying a word from working-base offset 0x40 to offset 0x10—the routine reads **0x67 again** for a byte store at working-base offset 9.

Additional computed writes follow, including a byte at another offset-9 destination and a word at working-base offset 0x44. A word is read at working-base offset 0x54. The routine then reads **0x67 once more** for another byte store at working-base offset 9.

These two computed offset-9 stores use fresh reads of 0x67. They are not unconditional reuse of the byte originally stored there: intervening computed writes can alias 0x67, and the first offset-9 store can itself alias that address.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not runtime results.

### Explicit field boundaries

A third argument of 0x000FFC00 contributes 1023 to the extracted field and 0x001FF800 to both relevant shifted argument positions. Changing bits outside 10–19 leaves this particular contribution unchanged, not necessarily the complete execution.

A fourth argument of 1 contributes 128 to both early duplicated positions. Negative and overflowing signed cases require executable arithmetic semantics.

### Cleared-word reread

In stable ordinary memory, successful initial zero stores produce a zero reread at the initial scaled base’s offset 0x14. Partial or complete overlap by the separately computed **zero** store does not permit a nonzero result. The offset-0x34 word store is not a write to offset 0x14.

A device-backed read or external mutation may invalidate this ordinary-memory expectation; that is a separate condition.

### Captured byte-derived base

If the fixed byte at 0x05 yields 0xFE, its captured address-base value is 254. Later mutation of 0x05 does not change that capture. A directly byte-derived word payload is zero-extended, not −2.

### Fixed payloads

If the doubled field is 0x108, its fixed byte stores at 0x01 and 0x15 carry 0x08 at their respective positions.

If the captured link is 3, the fixed word at 0x54 receives 6, assuming the represented signed shift is supported.

If the adjusted quantity is 5, the concluding byte at 0x3A receives 0x58. The fixed word at 0x60 receives 0x20 independently of this example.

### Capture versus reread

If the captured 0xA9 word has low byte 0x78, the initial store at 0x67 carries 0x78. A subsequent computed write that changes 0x67 to 0xAB before an offset-9 publication makes that later publication read 0xAB. Reusing 0x78 would violate the fresh-read relationship.

### Ordered overwrite and failure

An observable offset-0x20 destination receives 0x20, 0x2F, the intervening byte-read payload, and 0x20 in that order. Final-value inspection alone is insufficient.

An invalid computed address has no promised validation or recovery path. Earlier calls and stores may already have occurred.
