## Behavior

### Result contract

Within the representable arithmetic domain, three is the sole input whose result differs from its mathematical successor. Its required result is zero. Every other input returns its successor.

The exceptional case is based on reaching exactly four through incrementing. It is not a threshold rule. Inputs already equal to four, or larger than four, are not reset merely because they lie outside the apparent four-state range.

This distinction gives several externally relevant properties:

- Four returns five.
- Five returns six rather than a modulo-four result.
- Negative one returns zero by ordinary succession.
- Values below negative one advance toward zero without rejection or normalization.

Zero therefore has two distinct predecessors in the defined input domain: negative one and three. The result alone does not distinguish those inputs.

### Repeated calls

The same cyclic property does not hold for arbitrary inputs. Starting at four produces successive larger integers for as long as the arithmetic remains representable. Starting below zero and feeding results back eventually reaches zero and then the four-state cycle.
