## Behavior

### Descriptive conventions

A **descriptor update for a selector** means a 32-bit store of four times that selector at sixteen times that selector plus `0x14`. This term carries no application-level meaning.

A **selected-byte write through a base** means:

- Read an unsigned byte at that base plus one.
- Write a byte at the newly read value plus `0x11`.

Every selected-byte write performs a fresh selector read, including repeated clears.

Opaque dependency identities below identify invocation paths at behavioral boundaries. A path may contain multiple unresolved calls; it is not asserted to be one function. Its completion result is the final result captured by the routine, not necessarily the first callee’s result. Intermediate calls remain ordered and potentially observable even where no explicit argument or result use is described.

## Side effects

### Ordering and aliasing

Explicit accesses occur in the order described. Dependency calls may observe or modify the same locations between accesses.

A selected-byte destination lies between `0x11` and `0x110`, inclusive. The selector is an unsigned byte, not a pointer-sized destination.

Calculated addresses may alias descriptor locations, selector storage, the global reference, or callee-accessed memory. For a descriptor update followed by a word read, the store precedes the read even if their addresses coincide. Subject to ordinary sequential memory semantics and no external interference, the read then observes the stored value.

Repeated stores, selector reads, and clears cannot be merged merely because their formulas match. Calls, aliasing, or memory-mapped effects may distinguish them. The direct late byte store may itself affect a subsequent selector read if addresses alias.
