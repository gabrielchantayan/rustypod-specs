### Dependency selection and argument relationships

One exceptional destination uses an anchor rounded down to a four-byte boundary and a displacement of 1,536 bytes.

The most frequent ordinary-result relationship forwards a word after scaling it by four. Other boundaries forward a word unchanged, scale it by two or eight, duplicate it into multiple ordered argument positions, or pair a transformed current result with a much earlier retained word.

Secondary outputs have independent significance. They supply an early scaled argument, a later pointer, a long-lived saved word, and several unchanged arguments. They must not be substituted with the ordinary result of the corresponding invocation.

### Early byte-write phase

The first explicit byte write occurs after the fourth dependency invocation. It writes the incoming byte to an address derived from that invocation’s ordinary result.

The common early boundary writes one byte at eight times an address-producing result. The following invocation receives two equal arguments, each scaled from that same result by 16,384. Only the addressed byte is written; neighboring storage is not initialized by this event.

The following exceptions are material:

- The exceptional destination produces a result whose byte-write address is twice that result. The next invocation receives two equal arguments scaled by 4,096.
- One boundary has four ordered arguments. The first, second, and fourth contain the address-producing result scaled by 16,384. The third contains the incoming word scaled by 1,024. A following invocation consumes a secondary output of this four-argument call scaled by eight.
- Near the end of the incoming-byte phase, one eight-times-address write is followed by equal arguments scaled by four rather than 16,384.
- At several boundaries, the invocation receiving the equal arguments produces a result subsequently consumed after scaling by four. Those results are not disposable.

Each byte write precedes its associated following invocation.

### Combined results

At the first two consuming boundaries, the ordered arguments are the low 32-bit portion, the high 32-bit portion, and a second copy of the high portion. At the third consuming boundary, only the low and high portions are supplied, in that order.

### Zero writes and captured-byte transition

After the incoming-byte phase, six explicit writes store zero bytes. Their addresses are independently derived from results scaled by eight. They do not establish a contiguous cleared region.

A subsequent boundary writes the low eight bits of its calculated address to that address. The following invocation receives two equal words scaled by 16,384 and a third argument equal to the address-producing result scaled by eight.

Later, an explicitly unsigned 32-bit dependency result supplies a captured byte: the low eight bits of eight times that unsigned result. Its first destination is sixteen times the unsigned result. The next invocation receives two equal arguments scaled by 32,768.

This captured byte is reused across many calls and writes. It is not recalculated from each later destination.

Subsequent boundaries are not uniform. Their address scales include 65,536, 8,192, 4,096, and eight. Associated equal-argument scales include powers of two with exponents 27, 24, 23, and 14. A later boundary again combines an eight-times address with equal arguments scaled by four.

The retained unsigned result also supplies a different byte consisting of its original bits 19 through 26. Higher contributions are discarded by masking and byte narrowing. This separate byte is written at an address derived from a newer ordinary result, followed by an invocation receiving two copies of four times that newer result.

### Long-lived values

Two retained word lineages must remain distinct from current results.

**Earlier ordinary-result lineage.** An ordinary result from the incoming-byte phase is scaled by 16,384 and retained. During the captured-byte phase, this retained word determines a write address scaled by 65,536 and equal arguments scaled by the power of two with exponent 27.

The retained word also supplies a further saved word through multiplication by 32,768. Much later, that saved word determines a captured-byte destination scaled by 4,096 and equal arguments scaled by the power of two with exponent 23. Its unsigned top byte is then captured after the associated invocation returns.

Under an explicit low-32-bit wrapping assumption, the further saved word equals the original ordinary result multiplied by the power of two with exponent 29, reduced to 32 bits. Consequently, its top byte has five low zero bits. Shifting that byte right by five yields the original result’s lowest three bits. This simplification is conditional, not a universal guarantee of the represented signed-looking arithmetic.

**Later secondary-output lineage.** A secondary output is saved as an ordinary word and survives many intervening invocations. It determines a three-bit-byte write address scaled by 2,048 and two equal arguments scaled by the power of two with exponent 22.

Four later invocations receive a current result scaled by four as their first argument and this saved secondary-output word unchanged as their second argument. Later ordinary results must not replace the saved word at these boundaries.

These are conditional static acceptance cases, not executed tests. Each assumes the relevant boundary is reached, required addresses are valid, and arithmetic has the explicitly stated interpretation.

3. **Four-argument ordering.** An address-producing result of 2 and incoming word of 3 require ordered arguments 32,768; 32,768; 3,072; 32,768. The third position is not another copy of the scaled result.

4. **Smaller equal arguments.** At the special boundary near the end of the incoming-byte phase, result 3 gives address 24 but equal arguments of 12.

5. **Captured-byte narrowing.** Unsigned source result 35 supplies captured byte 24. Its initial destination is 560, and the following equal arguments are 1,146,880. Later captured-byte writes retain byte 24 despite unrelated current results.

6. **Combined-result partitioning.** Low word 17 and high word 29 require arguments 17, 29, 29 at either earlier combined-result boundary, but only 17, 29 at the third.

7. **First halfword boundary.** Current result 2 requires the preceding byte write at 16,384 and a halfword read at 8,248. The following arguments are base 8,192 and the halfword observed there.

8. **Scaled pointer read.** Retained pointer numeric value 64 gives second-read address 8,224, not 96. Intervening memory changes must be reflected in the observed halfword.

9. **Conditional retained-bit reduction.** Under low-32-bit wrapping, original retained-lineage result 7 gives further saved word 3,758,096,384, top byte 224, and propagated byte 7. Without that assumption, the lineage cannot be simplified this way.

10. **Separate unsigned extraction.** A retained unsigned result with original bit 19 set and all other bits clear supplies extracted byte 1 at the separate bits-19-through-26 boundary. That byte is not the captured low byte of eight times the result, which is zero in this case.

11. **Aliasing and interruption.** If the byte destination overlaps the direct word destination, the byte write occurs first. Failure during the word write does not authorize erasing the prior byte event or claiming full word completion.

12. **Zero and unresolved behavior.** A zero result may produce numeric address zero without a status branch. The unresolved full-width shift cannot be assigned a fixed result from this evidence, and a dependency that prevents continuation prevents all later events, including termination.
