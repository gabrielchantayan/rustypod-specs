# F-7789da112c5d

## Purpose

F-7789da112c5d performs an ordered series of mixed-width memory mutations, partially prepares a stack-relative argument area, and invokes an inherited indirect dependency. Return from that dependency permits further preparation and a call to F-cdec09f5d432. Return from F-cdec09f5d432 leads to a terminal-operation boundary and a further indirect invocation.

The evidence establishes memory and control-flow behavior, not an application-level purpose. It does not establish that the accessed regions are objects, that the transfers implement a copy operation, or that the prepared argument area has a complete conventional record format.

This is a conditional contract: each obligation applies if execution reaches the relevant operation. Two byte writes have no established payload definition. The evidence therefore does not demonstrate a fully defined execution reaching the calls or terminal sequence.

## Behavior

### Interpretation of observations

Address-bearing quantities and integer calculations are represented at 32-bit width. Calculations include addition, multiplication, doubling, and left shifts by one, four, or eight positions. Wider intermediates and portable wraparound semantics are not established.

Halfword observations are signed. Their range is therefore −32768 through 32767 when widened. Such a value sometimes supplies an address base directly; it is not always a displacement within another region.

Plain-byte signedness is unresolved. A byte with pattern 0xFF may contribute either −1 or 255 when widened. This uncertainty affects both data and addresses.

A word observation remains a 32-bit access even when only its low byte is subsequently written. Narrow writes use the corresponding low-width payload in the represented model; target-specific conversion behavior is not independently established.

Byte order, alignment requirements, signed overflow, negative shifts, and address wraparound are unresolved. Numerical examples below are conditional on the relevant arithmetic being admitted.

### Ordering and interference

The function exposes successive mutations rather than one atomic update. A failure or non-returning dependency can leave an observable completed prefix. No compensating action is established.

No disjointness is established between:

- Argument-derived and inherited regions.
- Selected regions and external selector storage.
- Addresses obtained from signed halfwords or bytes.
- Product-derived addresses.
- Stack-relative storage.

Consequently, an earlier write can alter a later payload, selector, pointer, or stack-relative value. Retaining a value and rereading its original address are different behaviors. Likewise, repeated writes remain separate effects even when their addresses and payloads appear equal.

The stack-relative assignments described below record what is assigned at particular points. They do not guarantee that the assigned contents survive intervening dynamic writes.

### Opening obligations

The first memory effect is a halfword write. Its destination combines the third argument with the inherited opening base, and its payload is the low halfword of the other inherited address-bearing value.

The opening base’s offset-68 word is observed only after that write. Overlap therefore affects the captured word according to the target’s byte order. That capture later provides both a full-word transfer to the first selected region’s offset 20 and the byte written at the first argument’s offset 9.

Before the first selected-region transfer, the initial mutation base is written as a word to its own offset 84. The first external selection and its separate publication observation follow.

Early accesses also establish these obligations:

- A doubled-base byte write at the initial mutation base’s offset 17 precedes its associated signed-halfword observation.
- The first argument’s offset-100 word is observed three times, not captured once for all uses.
- One of those observations is retained for a later byte write; the other two independently supply byte writes at the mutation base’s offset 17.
- The inherited word payload is written to the first argument’s offset 84.
- A selected region receives its own low address byte at offset 1.
- Other early byte destinations at offsets 9 and 13 receive low bytes from captured words, selected addresses, or inherited data.

These offsets describe accesses, not established field meanings.

### External selection and propagation

There are sixteen ordered selection points. They are word observations, not calls.

At each point, an external word is captured to select a region. A separate observation of that designated external source supplies a publication write through another address-bearing base at offset 68. The selected address and the published value therefore need not match.

The recurring transfer relationship carries a previously observed offset-68 word into a newly selected region’s offset 20. Self-valued offset-84 writes and mixed-width mutations intervene. This recurrence does not make the entire function a uniform record transfer: publication bases, active mutation bases, and payload provenance change.

The principal offset roles are:

| 20 | Word source, propagation destination, source of replacement addresses, and closing overwrite destination. |

In the middle portion, byte observations are addressed using a captured signed halfword together with a carried word. The resulting byte may supply both a halfword payload and a subsequent address base. Plain-byte signedness therefore changes the possible address set.

This portion includes publication through byte-derived bases at offset 68 and byte writes through such bases at offset 13. It also includes:

- An offset-29 byte write derived from the earlier carried offset-116 value.

Two word writes use destinations formed by adding dynamic address-bearing quantities. Both receive that same separate offset-100 payload. Their surrounding effects include offset-20 replacement writes, offset-84 mutations, and offset-13 bytes obtained from fresh word observations.

Between these activities, an offset-17 byte write consumes a payload with no established preceding definition. A later known low-byte assignment does not define that earlier effect retroactively.

### Product-dependent closing behavior

The final selection supplies a signed halfword that serves both as an address base and as a multiplication operand. Repeated self-valued writes at its offset 84 and repeated byte writes at the selected region’s offset 17 remain distinct observations.

A product-derived offset-68 word begins a reference chain involving offsets 116 and 20. At the reached region, the offset-20 word is captured before that location is overwritten with a doubled address-bearing value. The captured word then determines another offset-20 source.

A multiplication involving the signed base determines an offset-116 source. Its observed word is the **tail value**, assigned to stack-relative offset 0x108. A further multiplication determines the product used in subsequent closing accesses.

The tail value serves as:

- The payload of a word write at the selected region’s offset 100.
- The source of a low-byte write at twice the current product plus offset 17.
- The address base of the closing offset-1 byte write.

A second offset-17 byte write has no established preceding payload definition. Its later low-byte definition does not repair the earlier unknown.

After this unresolved write, the same product-derived offset-68 source is observed twice. Between those observations, the function observes the long-lived reference’s offset-116 word, writes the tail value to the selected region’s offset 100, and writes the first observation’s low byte at that region’s offset 9. The second observation supplies a byte write through the product-derived base at offset 17.

A self-valued offset-84 write precedes the doubled-product byte destination. Another multiplication-derived offset-68 observation supplies a further low-byte payload.

The final destination region comes from a fresh offset-20 observation through the long-lived reference. The closing offset-1 write uses the tail value as its base and the retained closing reference-offset-116 observation as its byte source. It precedes the final region’s offset-17 and offset-9 writes. The zero assignments occur in the order 0xE4, 0x60, 0xA8, 0x1B8. The subsequent copy destinations occur in the order 0x24, 0x4C, 0x128, 0x13C, 0x17C, 0x188.

The highest explicit word assignment covers offsets 0x1B8 through 0x1BB. This does not establish a complete object extent or initialization of the gaps.
