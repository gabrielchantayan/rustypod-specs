### Width and arithmetic rules

The representation contains 8-, 16-, 32-, and 64-bit quantities. Unless an unresolved machine rule applies, a narrow store retains only the destination width; unsigned byte loads used as words are zero-extended.

For a represented 64-bit result, the lower word contains bits 0–31 and the upper word contains bits 32–63. Logical shifts by 34, 36, and 44 followed by 32-bit extraction correspond to shifting the upper word right by 2, 4, and 12. This describes bit positions, not memory byte order.

Many results are shifted left by eight. Other shifts reach 31 positions, and masks retain fields of differing widths. Intermediate truncation matters:

- Two successive 32-bit left shifts by 28 discard every original bit.
- A 32-bit left shift by 31 followed by one by 30 likewise produces zero under truncating word semantics.
- A left shift followed by a right shift need not recover the original value.
- Values explicitly reduced to zero remain zero through further shifts until replaced.

Address calculations include multiplication by powers of two, multiplication by 0x02000000, and subtraction of 0x96. Unbounded arithmetic is not an equivalent model.

**documented_only:** executable semantics of represented signed overflow, signed left shifts outside their ordinary range, and the late signed right shift whose count is masked to eight bits. Masking a count to eight bits does not establish its treatment when it is at least 32.

### Entry into direct memory modification

The transition derives:

- A primary base from a dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- A compact unsigned quantity obtained by shifting another result left by eight, retaining the low 15 bits, then logically shifting right by six.
- A secondary base equal to twice that compact quantity.
- A shifted-result address obtained with a 25-position left shift.
- A saved word obtained with an eight-position left shift.
- Upper and lower words from the last represented 64-bit dependency result.

Under truncating 32-bit semantics, the compact quantity is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. The secondary base is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016. These are genuine low-address calculations; no heap-base adjustment is established.

The first memory effects have a defined order. A word is stored at upper-result address plus 0x14, followed by a byte at shifted-result address plus 9. The primary base is then stored at offset 0x54 from the lower-result word scaled by 256. The byte at shifted-result address plus 9 is overwritten with the low byte of that shifted address.

The primary-base word at 0x14 is cleared before a byte is read at primary-base offset 5. The secondary-base word at 0x14 is then cleared. The captured byte is zero-extended into primary-base offset 0x34, after which primary-base offset 0x14 is read as a word. Another secondary-base clear follows.

The clear does not unconditionally make the later primary-base read zero: intervening computed stores can overlap its bytes.

If the byte destination at 0x3A overlaps the later linked-base-offset-0x14 source, the saved dispatch word reflects that write. The earlier fixed-word capture from 0xA9 and the intervening computed-word capture precede it and are not retroactively changed.

### Three distinct reads of 0x67

Assume the low byte published to 0x67 is 0x12, the first read supplies 0x12 to 0x21, and an intervening computed store changes 0x67 to 0x34 before the second read. The second read supplies 0x34 to computed-base offset 9.

If another intervening store changes 0x67 to 0x56 before the third read, that read supplies 0x56. Neither computed store is required to use 0x12.

### Saved final argument

If the saved dispatch-word capture obtains 0x11223344 and later writes change its original source, final argument three remains 0x11223344, provided execution reaches dispatch.

### Ordered constant stores

An observer capable of distinguishing the late word stores must see 0x20, 0x2F, the byte-derived word, and 0x20 in that order. Checking only the final value is insufficient.

### Clear and aliasing

A primary-base-offset-0x14 read after its clear returns zero if no intervening effect changes those bytes. If an intervening destination overlaps them, the read reflects the applicable writes instead.
