# F-4754d4041d16

## Purpose

F-4754d4041d16 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, byte reads and writes, and 32-bit word reads and writes. Several early calculations depend on bits 25–31 of the fourth explicit input. Later calculations depend on dependency results, retained byte selectors, and incompletely specified register-derived values.

An initialization or reset role is **inferred intent** only. The evidence establishes neither an object model nor a device, protocol, allocator, or ownership scheme.

This contract covers the **visible execution prefix**. The supplied representation ends at an invalid-instruction boundary, not an ordinary return. Every later operation is conditional on earlier calls and memory accesses completing.

### Widths and notation

Bytes are 8 bits; words and represented addresses are 32 bits. Two dependency results are represented as 64-bit quantities. Their low and high words are bits 0–31 and 32–63 respectively.

“Store a word” means a 32-bit write. “Store a byte” retains only the least significant eight bits of the calculated value. Byte reads used as selectors are unsigned, ranging from 0 to 255. Unsigned right shifts insert zeros.

The following descriptive names identify retained values, not implementation identifiers:

- **Header:** fourth input shifted right by 25; range 0–127.
- **Header component:** Header multiplied by `0x100000`.
- **Selected byte:** the second read from Header component plus 1.
- **Main result:** a later unsigned 32-bit dependency result.
- **Reduced result:** Main result masked with `0x3fffffff`, then shifted right by one.
- **Reduced fragment:** Reduced result masked with `0x3fffff`.

Unsigned 32-bit calculations use the represented unsigned width. Calculations expressed through signed 32-bit multiplication or left shift do not establish portable overflow behavior. ## Behavior

### Ordering conventions

Dependency calls are observable operations, including those with no explicit arguments.

To describe recurring call sequences precisely:

- A **four-call interval** comprises four consecutive F-4754d4041d16-D02 invocations with no explicit arguments. When a result is identified, it is the fourth invocation’s result; the preceding three have no explicitly consumed result.
- A **forwarding pair** comprises one F-4754d4041d16-D02 invocation followed immediately by F-4754d4041d16-D03 receiving its 32-bit result.

These descriptions do not imply purity, interchangeable targets, or absence of register inputs. Retained values remain distinct from subsequent memory reads.

### 1. Entry and initial byte update

F-4754d4041d16-D01 receives the first explicit input. A forwarding pair follows, then a four-call interval produces an initial address result.

At four times that result, the operation reads a word at offset `0x74`, then stores the word’s low byte at offset 1. The read precedes the write.

F-4754d4041d16-D03 then receives the scaled address. A four-call interval follows before the next direct store.

### 2. Header-derived word updates

The operation stores Header multiplied by `0x80000` at four times the early secondary-register value plus `0x14`. F-4754d4041d16-D03 then receives that secondary-register value.

F-4754d4041d16-D04 produces a 64-bit result. F-4754d4041d16-D05 receives, in order, its low word, high word, and low word again.

A four-call interval produces a destination result. The operation stores the early secondary-register value shifted left by three at four times that result plus `0x14`. F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives four times the destination result.

After another four-call interval, the operation stores:

- Value: Header component multiplied by `0x80000`.
- Address: Header component multiplied by `0x200000`, plus `0x14`.

Both products are zero under the represented unsigned 32-bit arithmetic: Header component already contains a factor of \(2^{20}\). Thus this represented operation writes a zero word at `0x14`. F-4754d4041d16-D05 immediately receives three copies of the calculated stored value, consequently three zeros.

A four-call interval supplies another destination. Header component is stored at four times that destination plus `0x14`, and F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives the corresponding scaled destination.

### 3. Header-selected byte updates and word propagation

A four-call interval produces the **early byte-update result**.

The operation reads a byte at Header component plus 1 and stores the low byte of four times the early byte-update result at that selector plus `0x11`. F-4754d4041d16-D05 then receives, in order:

1. The selector shifted right by three.
2. Four times the early byte-update result.
3. Four times Header component.

A four-call interval produces a separate result. The operation rereads Header component plus 1; this second selector becomes Selected byte. It stores the low byte of the early byte-update result masked with `0x3fffffff` and shifted right by 20 at Selected byte plus `0x11`.

Immediately afterward, F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives four times the separate result—not four times the early byte-update result.

After a four-call interval, four times the early byte-update result is stored at four times Selected byte plus `0x14`. F-4754d4041d16-D05 receives Selected byte three times, followed by Selected byte shifted right by one.

The following word propagation then occurs:

| Destination and value | Immediately following explicit-argument invocation |
|---|---|
| After a four-call interval, store the retained scaled early byte-update result at four times the returned destination plus `0x14`. | F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives four times that destination. |
| After another four-call interval, store four times its returned result at sixteen times that result plus `0x14`. | F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives the stored scaled value. |

### 4. Further propagation and the adjacent word read

After the final scaled value above is passed to F-4754d4041d16-D03, a forwarding pair executes.

A four-call interval supplies a destination. The preceding phase’s final scaled value, masked with `0x3fffffff` and shifted right by one, is stored at four times that destination plus `0x14`. F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives the scaled destination.

Another four-call interval produces a **live-read result**. Four times this result is stored at sixteen times the result plus `0x14`.

The operation then reads a word at four times the live-read result plus `0x14`. **No dependency invocation intervenes between this store and read.** The unsigned word value shifted right by three supplies the argument to the next F-4754d4041d16-D03 invocation.

The two addresses have different scaling. Consequently, the read cannot generally be treated as reading the preceding store. Dependency interference between these two accesses is not a supported explanation; there is no intervening call.

A four-call interval then supplies another destination. The retained four-times-live-read value is stored at four times that destination plus `0x14`, and F-4754d4041d16-D03 receives its scaled destination.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance cases, not runtime tests.

1. **Header boundary:** Fourth input `0x02000000` gives Header 1, Header component `0x00100000`, and the first Header-derived stored word `0x00080000`. Fourth inputs zero and `0x01ffffff` both give Header zero, without establishing identical overall behavior.

2. **Maximum Header:** Fourth input `0xffffffff` gives Header 127, Header component `0x07f00000`, and the first Header-derived stored word `0x03f80000`.

3. **Unsigned product collapse:** For every Header, the unsigned products used for the Header-component self-scaled store are zero. Its represented destination is `0x14`, and the following three explicit arguments are zero.

4. **Byte truncation:** A byte-update result of `0xff` produces stored byte `0xfc` when multiplied by four. A masked high-field calculation from `0x3fffffff` produces `0x3ff` before byte truncation and stores `0xff`.

5. **Adjacent store/read distinction:** If the live-read result is 1, the word store targets `0x24`, while the immediately following read targets `0x18`. No dependency runs between them. If the word at `0x18` is `0x40`, the next explicit argument is 8.

6. **Selector reread:** If separate selector reads yield `0x20` and `0x30`, their selector-plus-`0x11` writes target `0x31` and `0x41`. Retaining the first selector for both writes would violate the contract.

7. **Main-phase result provenance:** If the retained shift result is 3 and the zero-write forwarding result is 5, the immediate post-write single arguments are respectively 12 and 20. The later four-argument invocation receives three copies of `0x01800000`, then `0x06000000`, all derived from 3.

8. **Final argument provenance:** If the first final-byte result is 3, the next separate result is 5, the third write’s selector is `0x2a`, and the last separate result is 7, the corresponding immediate post-store arguments are 12, 20, `0x2a`, and 28.

9. **Repeated fixed clearing:** Reaching the complete clearing phase produces four distinct 32-bit zero writes to `0x24`, with intervening dependency activity. One final zero write is not an equivalent observable sequence.
