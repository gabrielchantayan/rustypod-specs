# F-5b3065c0baa0

## Purpose

F-5b3065c0baa0 performs ordered, address-dependent memory modifications and prepares several external control transfers. Its established behavior concerns access widths, address selection, arithmetic, and the timing of observations relative to overlapping writes. No application-level purpose, public record schema, ordinary result, or memory-management service is established.

The contract below groups repeated behavior into nine routing rounds. “Routing” describes relationships between memory observations; it does not establish a traversal algorithm or valid data structure.

A late byte write consumes a value whose origin is not established. Requirements after that point are conditional on meaningful execution continuing. The representation does not establish that any incoming state permits successful completion.

### Width and validity requirements

All offsets are measured in bytes. Byte, halfword, and word accesses occupy 8, 16, and 32 bits.

A **displacement** is a signed 16-bit observation promoted to signed 32 bits. Its range is −32768 through 32767. A **character** is an 8-bit character observation or conversion; its signedness is unresolved. Character-derived addresses therefore cannot be assumed nonnegative. An explicitly retained **low byte** means eight retained bits, independently of character signedness.

Address arithmetic, multiplication, and shifts have signed 32-bit operands. A defined signed-arithmetic interpretation requires representable intermediate results. Left shifts additionally require a suitable nonnegative operand and representable result. Later byte or halfword conversion does not establish wraparound for an earlier invalid calculation.

Every reached access requires valid storage and applicable alignment. Byte order, integer-to-address interpretation, and out-of-range signed narrowing are **documented_only**. No bounds, null, alignment, or overflow checks are represented.

## Behavior

### Observation model

Ordering below concerns represented reads and writes. It does not establish volatile behavior, atomicity, synchronization, or a physical bus transaction count.

A retained observation is distinct from a later read of the same location. A retained address preserves address selection, not the contents at that address. No relevant storage regions are established as disjoint.

The roles used for each routing round are:

- **Destination:** incoming address receiving the opening byte modification.
- **Carrier:** incoming word used as a payload and, in some rounds, an address.
- **Source:** address selected by the round’s external location.
- **Anchor:** address receiving the round’s externally supplied word.
- **Link, displacement, character, snapshot:** observations defined below.

These names describe behavioral roles, not fields of a proven object layout.

### Entry conditions established in memory

The first three writes establish, in order, the seed at outer-anchor offset 68, argument 2 at seed offset 20, and argument 4 at initial-destination offset 17. These are respectively word, word, and byte writes.

Only afterward are three observations made, in this order: a word at primary offset 100, a word at initial-destination offset 68, and a signed halfword at the sum of initial destination and seed. They are the entry snapshot, entry link, and entry displacement.

Installation of the marker at primary offset 84 precedes a fresh word read at seed offset 20. That read selects the first continuing destination; it need not reproduce argument 2.

A subsequent word read at seed offset 68 supplies the initial carrier. Finally, the selected destination receives its own address as a word at offset 84.

### Routing-specific constraints

**Round 1.** Following capture, the marker is installed at primary offset 84 before the outgoing destination is selected. That destination’s offset 13 receives a character conversion of the source before a fresh incoming-carrier-offset-20 word selects the closing address. The primary address is installed at marker offset 84 before completion.

**Round 2.** After capture, source offset 29 receives a character conversion of the marker. Completion follows.

**Round 3.** A fresh primary-offset-100 word is written at displacement offset 84 after capture. The promoted captured character’s offset 13 then receives a character conversion of the incoming carrier. A subsequent source-offset-100 word selects the **retained reference address**, before completion. Later primary snapshots do not reselect this reference.

**Round 4.** Between capture and completion, four modifications have fixed precedence: primary offset 1 receives a source-derived character; source offset 13 receives a carrier-derived character; source offset 68 receives the retained reference; and promoted-character offset 13 receives a source-derived character. The final byte write or closing halfword may overlap source offset 68. The outgoing carrier is the later word observation, not necessarily the reference previously installed there.

**Round 5.** Incoming-carrier offset 116 selects the closing address before incoming-destination offset 20 selects the outgoing destination. Captured-link offset 13 receives a character conversion of the closing address before completion.

**Round 6.** Incoming-carrier offset 21 first receives a character conversion of the source. The incoming carrier is then installed at source offset 68. Only afterward does captured-link offset 116 select the closing address. There is no new primary snapshot in this round; its snapshot-derived opening byte uses round 5’s observation.

**Rounds 7 and 8.** Both select the outgoing destination through displacement offset 20, but at different points. Round 7 selects immediately after capture. Round 8 first writes the incoming destination at incoming-carrier offset 84, writes the captured snapshot’s low byte at incoming-destination offset 17, and writes the source at captured-link offset 68. Its destination selector is read afterward. The same snapshot low byte is retained for round 9.

After selection, both rounds impose these ordering constraints:

- The captured link’s self-address installation at offset 84 precedes installation of the retained reference at source plus incoming carrier.
- That reference installation precedes installation of the captured link at source offset 20.
- The source-offset-20 write precedes a fresh word read at retained-reference offset 68.
- The reference observation, character-converted, is written at source offset 13 before a character conversion of the captured link is written at outgoing-destination offset 13.
- Both byte writes precede completion.

The reference address is retained, but its offset-68 contents are observed separately in each round.

### Terminal selection and products

Round 9’s source becomes the **terminal address**. After capture, the same two-write pattern occurs twice: displacement offset 84 receives the displacement as a word, then terminal offset 17 receives a character conversion of the incoming destination. Repetition is observable when storage overlaps; neither pair may be silently removed from this contract.

A three-word selection chain starts at displacement multiplied by captured link, plus 68. The next observation is at the resulting address plus 116; the third is at that result plus 20. The third word selects the **selected address**.

A word read at selected-address offset 20 establishes the **successor address** before that location is overwritten with the incoming destination shifted left one bit. A fresh successor-offset-20 word is then multiplied by the displacement to form the **first product**. In particular, aliasing with the overwritten location can change this product.

Before the next product-dependent read, displacement offset 84 again receives the displacement, and terminal offset 17 receives a character conversion of the successor.

The word observed at first-product offset 116 is the **prepared word**, associated with preparation offset 264. Multiplying the displacement by the first product forms the **final product**.

Another displacement self-address installation precedes a terminal-offset-17 byte write whose payload has no established prior definition. The later extraction of the prepared word’s low byte does not supply a definition for this earlier use.

### Conditional continuation

The remaining requirements apply only if meaningful execution continues past the unresolved byte consumption.

A word at final-product offset 68 is observed before a word at primary offset 116. Terminal offset 100 then receives the prepared word, followed by a character conversion of the earlier final-product observation at terminal offset 9.

A second, fresh final-product-offset-68 observation supplies the character-converted byte written at final-product offset 17. Intervening terminal writes may make the two observations differ.
