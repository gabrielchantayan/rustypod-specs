## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Explicit input projection.** Under 32-bit bit-vector shifts, third inputs `0x0000007F` and `0x1234567F` both produce `0x7F000000` at explicit left-by-24 uses. Whole-function equivalence does not follow.

2. **Widening and extraction.** Unsigned byte `0xE2` widens to `0x000000E2`. Unsigned 64-bit value `0xFEDCBA9876543210`, shifted right 36 and narrowed to 32 bits, yields `0x0FEDCBA9`.

3. **Initial zero reread.** With stable ordinary memory and successful ordinary accesses, the initial root-plus-`0x14` reread returns zero. An overlapping zero-word store does not change this conclusion; the widened-byte store at root plus `0x34` is disjoint.

4. **Small index and fixed writes.** Under bit-vector arithmetic, late result 5 produces small index `0x14`. Independently captured saved link `0x1234` supplies word `0x2468` at fixed address `0x54`; the later fixed byte at `0x15` receives `0x28`. Small index `0x12` is outside the arithmetic range.

5. **Reachable shift counts.** Saved links `0x26`, `0x27`, and `0x28` produce working bases `0x08`, `0x18`, and `0x28`, giving counts 8, 24, and 40. Count 40 requires unresolved above-width semantics.

6. **Negative shift operand.** At count 8, operand bits `0x80001200` give logical result `0x00800012` or arithmetic result `0xFF800012`. Both narrow to byte `0x12`.

7. **Overlapping late writes.** Suppose execution reaches the interleaving with working base `0x18`, global base `0x1000`, terminal anchor `0x1054`, and captured shift operand `0x12345678`. Assume stable little-endian memory, successful accesses, and conventional count-24 shifting. The word store at `0x1054` is followed by byte `0x12` at `0x1055`, then word `0x00000018` at `0x1054`. Immediately after these three writes, byte `0x1055` is zero. Moving the byte store after the second word store would instead leave `0x12` there.

8. **Captures versus rereads.** A fixed write at `0x60` affecting the global’s storage does not replace the captured global base. Conversely, a later forwarding read of `0x67` uses its contemporaneous contents if intervening writes changed that byte.

9. **Late narrowing and partial completion.** Word `0x123456AB` read at working base plus `0x14` supplies byte `0xAB` at newly loaded base plus `0x11`, before the decimal-100 word store. A preceding fault prevents this requirement from being reached; no rollback is established.
