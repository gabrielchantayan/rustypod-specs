# F-32e214a73322

## Purpose

F-32e214a73322 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms dependency-derived values, destructively updates computed and fixed memory locations, and makes a final indirect invocation.

An application-level purpose is not established. The numeric constants do not independently establish text output, object initialization, collection management, or a device protocol.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** interfaces, accesses, conversions, and ordering represented in the supplied body.
- **[INFERENCE]:** consequences requiring an explicitly stated execution model.
- **documented_only:** unresolved details or behavior not exhaustively specified here.

Every described effect is conditional on execution reaching and completing that operation. No runtime tests have been performed.

### Arithmetic and address formation

Masks select low-order fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 20, 19, 17, and 13 bits, together with single-bit selections. The subsequent shifts and scaling remain significant; using an unmasked original value is not generally equivalent.

Explicitly unsigned right shifts are logical. Shifting a 64-bit container logically right by 34, 36, or 44 bits selects its high word shifted right by two, four, or twelve bits respectively, not its low word.

[INFERENCE] Under 32-bit truncating arithmetic:

- Successive left shifts by eight, eighteen, and eight bits eliminate all original bits.
- Successive left shifts by 28 and 28 bits produce zero.
- Successive left shifts by 31 and 30 bits produce zero.
- Left shifts and multiplication discard bits above bit 31.

These consequences do not eliminate surrounding invocations or memory events. Signed overflow, signed left-shift validity, and architectural address wrapping are not independently resolved.

Near the direct-memory boundary, one dependency result is multiplied by 2²⁵ to form a base. Another result is shifted left eight bits, masked to 15 bits, then shifted logically right six bits.

[INFERENCE] Under the same 32-bit model, the latter compact quantity ranges from zero through 508 in multiples of four. Its double ranges from zero through 1016 in multiples of eight. Derived accesses can therefore address low memory; an ordinary allocated-object interpretation is not established.

The final paired result before direct mutation supplies its high word as one base and its low word multiplied by 256 as another. Whether such containers represent actual 64-bit returns or grouped register outputs remains unresolved.

### Opening memory effects

The opening region writes a retained dependency-derived value shifted left 25 bits as a word at offset 0x14 from the final paired result’s high-word-derived base.

An explicit byte at offset nine from another retained base first receives the character-converted high result portion and later the low byte of that retained 25-bit-shifted value. Between these byte writes, a word at offset 0x54 from the paired result’s low-word-times-256 base receives the separately formed 2²⁵-scaled base.

The latter base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared. An unsigned byte at its offset five is captured and later stored, zero-extended, as a word at offset 0x34. A word at offset 0x14 from the doubled compact base is cleared before that word publication.

The earlier cleared offset-0x14 word is then read after intervening accesses. It cannot unconditionally be replaced with zero: intervening destinations may overlap it, and ordinary unobserved storage semantics are not established.

After another clearing of the doubled compact base’s offset-0x14 word, its offset-0x15 byte receives two consecutive stores of the same character-converted captured word value.

The fixed global at 0x05 is subsequently read and converted to unsigned 32-bit form. That converted quantity becomes an address base. It is not established to lie between zero and 255 and is distinct from the earlier computed unsigned-byte load. The following assignment to the fixed global at 0x01 supplies the low byte of the doubled compact base.

### Linked and scaled updates

This region combines retained values, pointer-like word reads, and scaling by two, four, and eight.

Explicit byte offsets include 0x01, 0x05, 0x09, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19. Word offsets include 0x04, 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.

Important ordering and retention constraints include:

- An offset-0x11 byte receives a character-converted retained shifted value and is later cleared.
- An offset-0x54 word is captured before replacement. Its former value supplies a retained root for later scaling and addresses.
- Offset-0x74 words supply subsequent bases.
- One nested update uses two reads of the same offset-0x74 expression. Their equality and their relative evaluation order within that update are not independently established.
- The retained root is stored into its own offset-0x24 word twice, interleaved with repeated byte stores elsewhere.
- Another offset-0x14 word receives a scaled value and then the retained root twice.
- A fixed-global assignment at 0x54 supplies the retained root shifted left one bit.
- A fixed-global assignment at 0x15 supplies a truncated computed base.

An explicit unsigned byte loaded at offset five is also used directly as the base of a word read at offset 0x74. That byte-derived base is bounded by 255; the unrelated fixed-global-derived base from 0x05 is not.

Further offset-0x64 word reads supply linked destinations receiving byte updates at offset 0x0D and word updates at offset 0x14. Repeated offset-five byte reads occur at different points among writes and must not be assumed equal.

For the later phases, the **doubled-link base** means twice the value obtained from a later word read at offset 0x74 from an explicit unsigned-byte-derived base. The **published scaled base** means four times that same linked value.

### Constants and transition into repeated publication

The linked value supports explicit stores of 0x3A using three widths: a byte at its four-times-scaled address, a halfword at its eight-times-scaled address, and a word at offset 0x20 from its four-times-scaled address.

The **adjusted quantity** is the retained root multiplied by four and then reduced by 0x96. The **adjusted base** is four times that adjusted quantity. These are distinct quantities. No checked or saturating interpretation is represented.

At adjusted-base offset 0x20, the ordered word values are:

- 0x20.
- 0x2F.
- A zero-extended byte read from adjusted-base offset four.
- 0x20 again.

The byte read occurs after the first two word stores.

The transition into publication has an additional strict boundary: **the publication-base global is captured before the fixed global at 0x60 receives 0x20, and that fixed assignment precedes the first publication word store.**

The captured publication base is retained for subsequent accesses. A later assignment at 0x60 does not retroactively change that captured value, even if the globals overlap. Conversely, its unknown physical write extent may affect memory reached by the following publication stores. Moving the capture after that assignment is not equivalent.

### Repeated publication

Each publication group has four ordered access roles:

1. A word at offset 0x54 of a computed base receives the published scaled base.
2. A byte at publication-base offset 0x19 receives the adjusted base’s low byte.
3. A byte at adjusted-base offset 0x11 receives the publication base’s low byte.
4. A word at publication-base offset 0x34 receives a scaled retained quantity.

The first group differs from later groups. Its initial word destination uses eight times the retained root, and its final word value is also eight times that root. Later groups use eight times the adjusted quantity for both that destination base and the final word value.

### Final captures and the intervening fixed mutation

The final capture sequence is ordered as follows:

- The fixed global associated with 0xA9 is captured into a 32-bit container.
- A word at published-scaled-base offset 0x74 is captured.
- The fixed global at 0x3A is assigned a value formed from the adjusted quantity.
- A word at doubled-link-base offset 0x14 is captured.
- A word at adjusted-base offset four is captured.

The 0x3A assignment is an intervening mutation, not part of an uninterrupted read-only snapshot. Its supplied arithmetic value is the adjusted quantity converted to a character, multiplied by eight, then increased by 0x30; assignment subsequently applies the destination’s conversion.

Character signedness, character-conversion behavior for out-of-range quantities, destination width, and resulting destination conversion remain unresolved. Neither an unsigned-byte interpretation nor a particular final numeric value may be imposed without additional declarations.

This assignment may overlap either following word read. Both captures therefore observe memory after the 0x3A mutation, subject to actual memory semantics. The earlier 0xA9 and offset-0x74 captures occur before it and remain retained values. Capturing the two later words before the assignment would violate the represented order.

The 0xA9 capture’s 32-bit container does not independently establish a physical 32-bit global load.

The fixed global at 0x67 receives the low eight bits of the captured 0xA9-derived value. It is mutable state, not a permanent alias for that captured byte.

After a word read at doubled-link-base offset 0x44, a fresh read of 0x67 supplies the assignment to the fixed global at 0x21. Later, two distinct fresh reads of 0x67 supply explicit byte stores at adjusted-base offset nine.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

1. **Byte truncation.** An explicit byte store supplied with 0x123456AB writes 0xAB if completed. This does not establish a word write.

2. **Unsigned byte promotion.** An explicit offset-five unsigned-byte load yielding 0xFE contributes 254 when subsequently stored as a word.

3. **Fixed-global conversion.** If additional declarations made the 0x05 global unsigned eight-bit storage containing 0xFE, its unsigned 32-bit conversion would be 254. Signed eight-bit storage containing −2 would instead convert to 0xFFFFFFFE. The supplied body selects neither declaration.

4. **Publication capture precedence.** Suppose the publication-base global initially supplies one address, and the subsequent 0x60 assignment overlaps its storage and changes its contents. Publication still uses the earlier captured address. Recapturing the global afterward is not equivalent.

5. **Final capture boundary.** If the 0x3A assignment overlaps the doubled-link offset-0x14 word, that word is captured after the assignment’s effect. The earlier published-scaled-base offset-0x74 capture remains its pre-assignment value.

6. **Character-dependent arithmetic.** Assume eight-bit characters, an adjusted quantity of 0xFE, conventional signed or unsigned character conversion, and a 32-bit destination preserving the arithmetic result. Signed character interpretation gives −2, producing 32 after multiplication and addition; unsigned interpretation gives 254, producing 2080. Neither result is unconditional without the character and destination contracts.

7. **Destination width remains separate.** Supplying 0xAB after explicit eight-bit truncation to the fixed global at 0x67 does not establish a one-byte physical write or neighboring-byte preservation.

8. **Fresh global observations.** Assuming 0x67 is unsigned-byte storage, an intervening overlapping store changing it from 0xAB to 0xA8 makes the next reread supply 0xA8. Retaining the original captured byte is not equivalent.

9. **Paired extraction.** A 64-bit container equal to 0x1122334455667788 has high word 0x11223344 and low word 0x55667788. Logical right shift by 36 yields 0x01122334.

10. **Failure boundary.** A fault after completed stores has no specified rollback. Reaching F-D002 establishes its displayed argument boundary, not successful return.
