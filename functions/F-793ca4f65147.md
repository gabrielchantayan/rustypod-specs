# F-793ca4f65147

## Purpose

F-793ca4f65147 performs an ordered series of memory mutations, prepares stack-associated values, and transfers control to an incoming executable target. If that target returns, further assignments precede a call to F-cdec09f5d432. Return from that dependency leads to a terminal sequence, not an established ordinary return.

The supported contract is a **memory-access and control-transfer protocol**. The supplied representation does not establish an application-level purpose, object type, list operation, allocation scheme, or cleanup protocol.

All behavior below is derived from static evidence. “Before” and “after” describe ordering in that representation; they do not independently establish hardware transactions, atomicity, or concurrent visibility.

## Behavior

### Early publication and link relationships

The primary-base byte at offset 17 first receives the fourth argument. Before that byte is replaced, three observations occur in order: the word at first-argument offset 100, the word at primary-base offset 68, and the signed two-byte value at the address formed from the primary base plus the secondary value.

These observations establish the **initial word**, **first link**, and **signed displacement value**. The replacement byte is the low byte of the initial word. A word publication of the primary base at second-argument offset 84 separates the observations from that replacement.

The first link subsequently receives the secondary value at offset 68 and itself at offset 84. Between those two publications, a word read at offset 20 from the signed displacement value establishes the **second link**. Thus, the first publication can influence that read through aliasing; the later self-publication cannot precede it.

The secondary-value region receives several related publications. Their represented order is:

- A word containing the tertiary base at the address formed from the secondary value plus the second argument.
- A word containing the first link at secondary-value offset 20.
- A byte at secondary-value offset 13, obtained from a four-byte read at tertiary-base offset 68.
- A byte containing the first link’s low eight bits at second-link offset 13.
- Two bytes containing the first link’s low sixteen bits at the signed displacement value plus the secondary value.

Only after this group is the word at secondary-value offset 68 captured. The second link is then published as a word at its own offset 84.

### Anchor selection and working state

The two anchor-global evaluations are separate. The first establishes the **anchor**. The second supplies the value published at first-link offset 68. The previously captured secondary-region word is then published at anchor offset 20, followed by publication of the initial word’s low byte at second-link offset 17.

Neither the initial word nor the anchor is refreshed for these uses.

Two further observations establish the computational state: a word at second-link offset 68 becomes the **multiplier**, and a signed two-byte read at the second link plus the anchor becomes the **working base**.

Before the dependent traversal begins, two identical publication pairs occur. Each pair publishes the working base as a word at its own offset 84, then publishes the second link’s low byte at anchor offset 17. Their repetition and alternating order are part of the represented behavior.

### Traversal, overwrite, and products

The traversal starts with a four-byte read at offset 68 from the product of the working base and multiplier. Two dependent word reads follow, at offsets 116 and 20 respectively. Each uses the preceding read’s result as its base. The final result is the **chain base**.

The word at chain-base offset 20 is captured as the **saved chain value**, and that location is then overwritten with the second link shifted left by one bit. A later word read uses the saved chain value as a base at offset 20. It occurs after the overwrite: preserving an address does not preserve the contents reachable through that address.

The working base multiplied by this later read produces the **first product**. Before acquiring the payload, the function again publishes the working base at its own offset 84, followed by the saved chain value’s low byte at anchor offset 17.

A word read at first-product offset 116 establishes the **payload base**. Its assignment to stack-associated coordinate `0x108` belongs to this stage, before the remaining product-derived publications.

The **second product** is the working base multiplied by the first product. It is not merely another name for the first product. Another working-base publication at offset 84 precedes publication of the payload base’s low byte at anchor offset 17.

### Final addressed-memory effects

Two snapshots precede the next publications: first a word from second-product offset 68, then a word from first-argument offset 116.

The payload base is published as a word at anchor offset 100. The first snapshot’s low byte is then published at anchor offset 9. Only afterward is second-product offset 68 read again, as a full word, to supply the byte published at second-product offset 17.

This fresh read cannot be replaced by the earlier snapshot. Either intervening anchor-relative publication may overlap its source.

The final working-base self-publication at offset 84 precedes a byte publication of the payload base at offset 17 from twice the second product.

Two final word reads occur in order:

- Offset 68 from the product of the working base and second product.
- First-argument offset 20, establishing the **destination base**.

The addressed-memory phase ends with three byte publications, in this order:

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes valid relevant storage, supported arithmetic, and no unstated interference.

1. **Initial byte replacement.** With fourth argument `0x7e` and an initial word capture of `0x123456ab`, primary-base offset 17 first receives `0x7e` and later `0xab`. The intervening observations and second-argument-relative publication retain their stated ordering.

2. **Signed address contribution.** A two-byte input pattern of `0xffff` contributes −1, not 65,535. Its subsequent offset-20 address is therefore 19, where that address and arithmetic are meaningful.

3. **Read width survives narrowing.** The tertiary-base-offset-68 source is read as four bytes even though its destination receives only one byte. Byte accessibility alone is insufficient to satisfy that read’s preconditions.

4. **Low-bit publications.** A first link with bit pattern `0x12345678` supplies byte `0x78` and two-byte value `0x5678`. The in-memory byte ordering of the latter remains target-dependent.

5. **Separate global evaluations.** If the first anchor-global evaluation yields A and the second yields B, anchor-relative accesses use A while first-link offset 68 receives B.

6. **Saved address is not a snapshot of its target.** If the saved chain value equals the chain base, its later offset-20 read reaches the location after the shift-derived overwrite. It does not restore the displaced word.

7. **Fresh observation under overlap.** If anchor offset 100 overlaps second-product offset 68, the earlier snapshot precedes that publication. The later read accounts for the overlap and for any additional overlap from anchor offset 9.

8. **Repetition remains represented.** Where individual accesses are observable, the first two working-base self-publications alternate with the two second-link byte publications at anchor offset 17. A single pair is not equivalent.

9. **Preparation timing.** The assignment associated with `0x108` precedes the remaining product-derived memory publications. Moving it into the final preparation group changes the represented ordering.

10. **Non-returning first dependency.** If F-000000000001 does not return, the function’s continuation performs none of the five post-call assignments and reaches neither the later call nor the terminal sequence.

11. **Late global mutation.** If F-000000000001 changes the late-call global and returns, F-cdec09f5d432 receives its value as evaluated after the intervening assignments, including any effects of their aliases.

12. **No ordinary completion.** If preceding operations complete and both dependencies return, execution proceeds into the terminal sequence rather than producing an established normal result.
