# F-a93746e25e2e

## Purpose

F-a93746e25e2e accepts an opaque 32-bit input, performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations and numerical transformations, mutates memory through computed addresses and fixed-address globals, and ends with another indirect invocation.

No domain-level purpose is established. The supplied representation does not independently establish parsing, allocation, initialization, rendering, or text output. Constants resembling characters establish numerical values, not an encoding or output channel.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** explicit operations, access widths, ordering, and value dependencies in the supplied representation.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations not required for acceptance; no domain interpretation is adopted here.
- **documented_only:** unresolved information that must not be replaced with assumed behavior.

No runtime tests have been performed.

### Initial mutation and zero preservation

The following roles identify retained values, not object types:

- **Shift-derived base:** a retained result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** another retained result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Small-index base:** twice the unsigned value obtained by shifting an originating result left eight, masking to 15 bits, and shifting right six.
- **Retained shifted word:** a different result previously shifted left eight.
- **Final pair:** the last paired result before direct mutation.

Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, the small-index base equals eight times the originating result’s low seven bits. It ranges from zero through 1016 in multiples of eight. Its offset-0x14 word access therefore begins between addresses 20 and 1036.

The initial stores place the shift-derived base at final-pair-high offset 0x14, then place the final pair’s high word’s low byte at shift-derived-base offset 9. The scaled base is stored at offset 0x54 from 256 times the final pair’s low word. The byte at shift-derived-base offset 9 is then replaced with the shift-derived base’s low byte.

The scaled-base offset-0x14 word is zeroed before a byte is read from scaled-base offset 5. The small-index-base offset-0x14 word is then zeroed, and the captured byte is zero-extended into a word store at scaled-base offset 0x34. The scaled-base offset-0x14 word is subsequently reread.

Under ordinary stable memory and supported address arithmetic, this reread yields zero. The same-base offset-0x34 store is disjoint from offset 0x14. The other intervening store contains only zero bytes, including wherever it overlaps.

The small-index zero store is repeated. Two subsequent byte stores place the reread’s low byte at small-index-base offset 0x15; both therefore write zero under those assumptions.

Read-sensitive or write-sensitive storage and asynchronous modification can invalidate the ordinary-memory conclusion. The represented overlapping zero stores alone cannot introduce a nonzero value.

### Early global evaluation and the address-0x54 read

After those duplicated byte stores, the global at 0x05 is evaluated and converted to an unsigned 32-bit value, called the **early global-derived base**. Its source access width, signedness, and extension behavior are unresolved. It is not established as a zero-extended byte or bounded by 255.

The global at 0x01 is then assigned the small-index base explicitly narrowed to eight bits. Its destination width remains unresolved.

The following ordering is established:

1. Early-global-derived-base offset 0x15 receives a byte containing the low eight bits of half the small-index base.
2. Scaled-base offset 0x11 receives the retained shifted word’s low byte.
3. Zero-reread offset 0x54 receives the retained shifted word through a 32-bit store.
4. Scaled-base offset 0x11 receives a zero byte.
5. Early-global-derived-base offset 0x54 is read as a 32-bit word.
6. That same word destination receives the earlier zero-reread value.

Call the word captured by the fifth effect the **saved word**.

Under the preceding ordinary-memory assumptions, the third effect is a 32-bit store at address 0x54. Only the zero-byte store at scaled-base offset 0x11 intervenes before the saved-word read. The assignment to the global at 0x01 occurs earlier, not within this interval.

With supported multiplication and addressing, the scaled base is a multiple of 0x02000000. Adding 0x11 cannot place that byte within addresses 0x54–0x57. Consequently, **if the retained early global-derived base is zero, the saved word equals the retained shifted word** under these assumptions. The following store then writes zero at address 0x54.

This computed 32-bit store must be distinguished from the later named-global assignment at 0x54, whose destination width is unresolved.

### Saved-word and address-bearing updates

The small-index-base offset-0x34 field receives half the small-index base. A word loaded from scaled-base offset 0x74 supplies another destination base. An additional update uses a word obtained through small-index-base offset 0x74.

The saved word is used as both address and data. Repeated effects include self-referential word stores at saved-word offset 0x24 and byte stores at a loaded base’s offset 9. The loaded base’s offset-0x54 field first receives a scaled dependency result and later the retained shifted word.

A small-index-base offset-0x14 store containing the scaled dependency result precedes assignment of the saved word shifted left one to the global at 0x54. The small-index field subsequently receives the saved word twice.

After two further byte stores at small-index-base offset 0x15, the global at 0x15 receives the small-index base narrowed to eight bits. This narrowing does not establish a byte-wide global store.

Later updates use fourfold and eightfold scalings of the saved word and doubled bases obtained from loaded words. Word destinations include offsets 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54; byte destinations include offsets 9, 0x0d, and 0x15.

Some constructions explicitly read a byte at computed offset 5, zero-extend it, and use it as the base for an offset-0x74 word read. These word reads begin between 0x74 and 0x173, subject to supported addressing. Unlike the global at 0x05, these constructions have explicit byte-source evidence.

Separate byte evaluations remain separate. Intervening stores can change later values through aliasing.

### Constant stores and adjusted bases

One word obtained through a byte-derived offset-0x74 read supplies the **loaded value**. Three constant stores occur in this order:

- An 8-bit 0x3a at four times the loaded value.
- A 16-bit 0x003a at eight times the loaded value.
- A 32-bit 0x0000003a at offset 0x20 from four times the loaded value.

Define these additional descriptive roles:

- **Doubled loaded base:** twice the loaded value.
- **Fourfold loaded base:** four times the loaded value.
- **Adjusted value:** four times the saved word minus 0x96.
- **Adjusted fourfold base:** four times the adjusted value.

Adjusted-fourfold-base offset 0x20 receives four ordered word stores: 0x20, 0x2f, a freshly read and zero-extended byte from that base’s offset 4, and 0x20 again. Intermediate accesses remain part of the behavior even though the final supplied constant repeats.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

### Input preservation and paired extraction

For input 0x89abcdef, the initial invocation receives that same 32-bit pattern.

For paired result 0x1234567889abcdef, an argument construction using its low word shifted left eight and unchanged high word produces 0xabcdef00 and 0x12345678 under 32-bit bit-vector semantics.

### Small-index boundary and initial zero

An originating result whose low seven bits equal 127 produces small-index base 1016 under the stated arithmetic interpretation. Its offset-0x14 word access begins at 1036.

Under ordinary stable memory and supported arithmetic, the scaled-base reread yields zero even if the intervening small-index zero store overlaps it. The subsequent explicit zero-reread-relative word store therefore targets address 0x54.

### Zero early global-derived base

Suppose the retained early global-derived base is zero, the retained shifted word is 0x12345600, and the ordinary stable-memory and supported-arithmetic assumptions hold.

The computed word store at address 0x54 supplies 0x12345600. The only intervening memory operation before the saved-word read is a zero-byte store at scaled-base offset 0x11. Because that address does not overlap 0x54–0x57, the read captures **0x12345600**. The next word store replaces address 0x54 with zero.

The global assignment at 0x01 precedes the computed word store. Its unresolved width does not create an intervening-write uncertainty for this read.

### Global conversion is not byte evidence

If evaluation of the global at 0x05 converts to unsigned 32-bit value 0x100, the retained early-global-derived offset-0x54 read begins at 0x154. Missing declarations do not exclude this value. Whether it equals the retained shifted word is not established merely by this address construction.

### Fresh evaluations and widths

The computed constant-0x3a stores affect one, two, and four bytes respectively. Replacing them all with byte stores is not equivalent.
