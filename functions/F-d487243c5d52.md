# F-d487243c5d52

## Purpose

F-d487243c5d52 coordinates an ordered series of indirect invocations. Two dependencies produce signed integer results, and each result supplies a transformed argument to the next dependency. Those results affect arguments, not destination selection.

The ordinary sequence contains twelve invocations. A subsequent terminal operation has an exceptional-operation designation and a non-return annotation. Nevertheless, the supplied representation includes a continuation that invokes the destination returned by that operation, if a result and compatible continuation are available.

The established behavior is an invocation protocol, not an application-level computation. No business purpose, success status, transaction boundary, resource-management policy, or reliable normal-return path is established.

Throughout this contract:

- **Established** means supported by the supplied static representation.
- **Derived** means an arithmetic consequence under explicitly stated assumptions.
- **Inferred intent** means suggested, but not guaranteed, by an annotation or operation designation.
- **documented_only** identifies unresolved behavior; it does not impose an invented implementation requirement.

## Behavior

### Ordered invocation protocol

The dependency table defines the complete represented invocation order. Positions 1–12 are ordinary invocations; position 13 is the terminal operation; position 14 is conditional on continuation from that operation.

Each later invocation is contingent on the preceding execution returning compatibly. The representation contains no local loop, retry, or producer-result-dependent branch within the ordinary sequence.

There are two independent producer–consumer exchanges:

- F-U02 supplies the sole explicit argument to F-U03 after transformation.
- F-U07 supplies the sole explicit argument to F-U08 after transformation.

Each consumer immediately follows its producer. The first exchange completes before the second producer is entered. The results are neither combined nor exchanged between consumers.

Among the ordinary invocations, only F-U03 and F-U08 have represented explicit arguments. Each receives one integer argument. The other ordinary roles have no represented explicit arguments; implicit context remains possible.

A zero producer result does not select a skip path. A result does not select an alternative destination or change the number of represented ordinary invocations.

Each consumer receives its producer’s signed result shifted left by two bit positions.

Within that domain, the argument is numerically \(4x\).

There is no represented local check for negativity or range, and no local saturation, rejection, or diagnostic. The relationship must not be replaced with an unconditional promise of arbitrary-precision multiplication or modular wrapping.

### Routing-state timing and interference

Routing bases are prepared ahead of use. In particular, F-U02’s basis is established before F-U01 is entered.

For every ordinary role from F-U03 through F-U12, its basis is established after the role two positions earlier returns and before its immediate predecessor is entered. Thus, each numerical consumer’s fixed basis is already prepared when its producer executes.

This ordering concerns preparation of the basis. It does not establish when the complete displaced destination is materialized, nor a physical register assignment, stack location, or externally visible memory write.

The distinction matters for interference: a dependency executes between preparation and use of later routing state. No independent preservation contract for globals, registers, memory, or other execution state is supplied. The static relationships therefore apply subject to compatible continuation; they must not be justified by assuming an unknown dependency preserves everything needed.

The exceptional-operation designation and non-return annotation suggest that ordinary continuation is not intended. They do not establish a particular processor fault, signal, handler, process exit, or resumption mechanism.
