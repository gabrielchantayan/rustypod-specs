# F-67f16c7479b6

## Purpose

F-67f16c7479b6 computes an unsigned 64-bit result from two unsigned 32-bit input words and an unsigned 32-bit selector. It selects between a two-word shift-and-merge construction and a zero-extended shift of the upper input word.

For selectors from 1 through 63, the established arithmetic behavior matches a logical right shift of the conceptual 64-bit value whose upper half is the second input word and whose lower half is the first input word.

A split-word logical-right-shift helper is therefore the inferred intent. That interpretation does not establish behavior for every selector:

- Selector zero requests a 32-bit left shift by 32 in the displayed construction.
- Other selectors can request word shifts by 32 through 255.
- Branch selection depends on a signed interpretation of a subtraction, not simply on whether the unsigned selector is less than 32.
- The individual shift counts retain eight bits, rather than being explicitly restricted to five or six bits.

The contract therefore distinguishes results established by ordinary 32-bit shift semantics from cases requiring additional execution-model evidence. It neither assigns modulo-64 semantics to all selectors nor assumes that selectors of at least 64 return zero.

## Behavior

### Arithmetic model and branch selection

The input words and selector are treated as 32-bit unsigned quantities. Selector subtraction wraps modulo 2³². The branch decision then interprets the wrapped value of “selector minus 32” as a signed 32-bit value and tests whether it is negative.

Under a two’s-complement signed interpretation, the branches cover these unsigned selector ranges:

| Selected construction | Selector range |
|---|---|
| Two-word merge | `0x00000000` through `0x0000001F` |
| Upper-word-only result | `0x00000020` through `0x8000001F` |
| Two-word merge | `0x80000020` through `0xFFFFFFFF` |

These ranges depend on the stated signed interpretation. The supplied representation does not independently establish the execution environment’s rules for converting out-of-range unsigned values to signed values.

Each individual shift count is reduced to its low eight bits. Its resulting range is 0 through 255, not 0 through 31.

Logical right shifts fill vacated high positions with zero. For an in-range left shift of an unsigned 32-bit word, bits shifted beyond bit 31 are discarded. The contract does not extend these ordinary word-shift rules to counts of 32 or greater.

### Two-word merge

The selected result has an upper 32-bit half and a lower 32-bit half.

The upper result half is the upper input word shifted logically right by the selector’s low byte.

The lower result half is the bitwise OR of:

- The lower input word shifted logically right by the selector’s low byte.
- The upper input word shifted left by the low byte of “32 minus selector.”

The result places the upper result half in bits 63 through 32 and the lower result half in bits 31 through 0. Combining the halves is concatenation: there is no carry between them.

Both effective counts are within the ordinary range 0 through 31 exactly when the selector’s low byte is from 1 through 31. For those selectors, the right-shift count and left-shift count sum to 32.

Consequently:

- Selectors 1 through 31 yield the corresponding logical right shift of the conceptual combined input.
- Larger selectors selecting this branch also have fully determined ordinary-shift results when their low byte is from 1 through 31.
- Within this branch, selectors having the same low byte produce the same result whenever the relevant word shifts have established semantics.

The remaining low-byte values require at least one excessive word-shift count:

| Selector’s low byte | Right-shift count | Left-shift count | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 0 | 32 | Left shift is outside the ordinary range. |
| 1 through 31 | 1 through 31 | 31 through 1 | Both shifts are in range. |
| 32 | 32 | 0 | Right shifts are outside the ordinary range. |
| 33 through 255 | 33 through 255 | 255 through 33 | Both counts are outside the ordinary range. |

No result is assigned here to the excessive-count cases without additional evidence about the underlying shift semantics.

### Upper-word-only result

The upper input word is shifted logically right by the low byte of “selector minus 32.” The resulting 32-bit value is zero-extended to 64 bits.

Thus:

- Result bits 63 through 32 are zero.
- The lower input word does not contribute to this branch’s result.
- The effective count is in the ordinary range exactly when the selector’s low byte is from 32 through 63.

For selectors 32 through 63, the effective count is selector minus 32, and the result matches a logical right shift of the conceptual combined input by the selector.

For larger selectors selecting this branch, the effective count repeats every 256 selector values. This periodicity applies within the selected branch; branch selection must still be evaluated from the full selector.

An effective count of 32 or greater remains **documented_only**.

### Boundaries and result constraints

At selector 31, the merge construction can retain one bit in the upper result half. At selector 32, construction changes to a zero-extended copy of the upper input word. At selector 63, the result is zero or one according to the upper input word’s most significant bit.

Selector zero is not established as an identity operation. Its merge construction contains a left shift by 32, even though a mathematical 64-bit shift by zero would return the combined input unchanged.

Selector 64 is not established as producing zero. Its selected construction contains a right shift of a 32-bit word by 32.

For every selector whose displayed word shifts are all within the ordinary range, result bit 63 is zero:

- The merge construction then right-shifts its upper half by at least one.
- The other construction always zero-extends a 32-bit value.

Accordingly, the established domain does not make every 64-bit pattern reachable. No explicit reserved error value is visible, but that is distinct from claiming that every return pattern is possible.

All fully determined cases described here are consistent with conventional modulo-64 logical-shift results. Those cases therefore do not, by themselves, distinguish such normalization from the displayed construction. They also do not establish that normalization for selectors involving excessive word shifts.
