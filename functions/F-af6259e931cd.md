## Behavior

The first group contains four ordered writes:

1. A as a 32-bit word at B plus `0x54`.
2. x-byte at Y plus `0x19`.
3. y-byte at X plus `0x11`.
4. B as a 32-bit word at Y plus `0x34`.

A second four-write group then occurs **exactly ten times**, consecutively. Every repetition has this order:

1. A as a 32-bit word at D plus `0x54`.
2. x-byte at Y plus `0x19`.
3. y-byte at X plus `0x11`.
4. D as a 32-bit word at Y plus `0x34`.

The complete initial phase therefore contains **44 writes**: four initial writes and forty repeated writes. There is no represented data-dependent termination condition.

Within this phase, Y plus `0x19` and X plus `0x11` each receive eleven byte writes. Y plus `0x34` receives B once, followed by D ten times. The D-based A store occurs ten times in addition to the initial B-based A store; these addresses can coincide.

Repeated identical values do not make the stores dispensable. An observer, overlapping access, or fault can distinguish the full sequence from a single final store.

### Early snapshots and absolute-byte update

After all ten repetitions, the function reads the 32-bit value at G169, then the 32-bit value at A plus `0x74`. Both values are retained for later use.

It next writes this byte to G58:

\[
(2 \times \text{x-byte} + 0x30) \bmod 256.
\]

The resulting byte does not depend on whether the intermediate character value is interpreted as signed or unsigned. It is not limited to printable or decimal-digit values.

After the G58 write, the function reads and retains the 32-bit value at Z plus `0x14`, followed by the 32-bit value at X plus `0x04`. If G58 overlaps either read, that read occurs after the byte mutation.

The following writes then occur in order: x-byte at Z plus `0x0D`; byte `0x6C` at X plus `0x08`; y-byte at X plus `0x0D`; y-byte at X plus `0x09`; Z as a 32-bit word at Z plus `0x54`; and the low byte of the retained G169 word at G103.
