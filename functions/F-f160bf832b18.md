# F-f160bf832b18

## Purpose

F-f160bf832b18 conditionally obtains a signed numeric result from F-ae3bc1bfd2a8 and converts that result into a smaller signed value using an add-seven adjustment followed by division by eight with truncation toward zero.

The operation is selected by a leading discriminator in the input object. A null input or a discriminator other than 6 produces zero without calling the dependency. A matching discriminator causes an indirect four-byte field read and exactly one dependency invocation, provided those reads and the invocation complete normally.

The apparent intent is a rounded size or count conversion. That interpretation is **inferred**, not established: neither the units of the dependency result nor the meaning of discriminator 6 is supplied. In particular, the arithmetic is not generally equivalent to mathematical ceiling division for negative inputs.

### Return-value arithmetic

Let \(r\) denote the signed 32-bit result from F-ae3bc1bfd2a8.

The visible expression first forms an add-seven intermediate. Its sign is then used to select a correction of either zero or seven. The corrected intermediate is shifted right by three positions as a signed quantity.

Under a 32-bit two’s-complement interpretation with wrapping addition and arithmetic signed right shifts:

1. Let \(t\) be the signed interpretation of the low 32 bits of \(r+7\).
2. The correction is zero when \(t\) is nonnegative and seven when \(t\) is negative.
3. The final value is
   \[
   \operatorname{trunc}(t/8),
   \]
   where truncation is toward zero.

The unsigned portion of the expression matters. A negative sign-expanded intermediate becomes an all-one 32-bit unsigned value; shifting it right by 29 positions produces 7, not 1. A nonnegative intermediate produces 0.

For negative \(t\), adding that correction before the arithmetic shift changes floor-style division into truncation toward zero. This correction cannot itself overflow a signed 32-bit value: a negative \(t\) plus seven lies between \(-2{,}147{,}483{,}641\) and 6. The width-sensitive overflow risk is the initial addition of seven to \(r\).

### Non-overflowing domain

When
\[
-2{,}147{,}483{,}648 \le r \le 2{,}147{,}483{,}640,
\]
the initial addition fits in signed 32-bit arithmetic. Subject to arithmetic signed shifts, the result is:

\[

For nonnegative values in this domain, this equals ceiling division of \(r\) by eight.

For negative values, it must not be generalized to ceiling division. For example, \(r=-8\) produces zero, whereas the mathematical ceiling of \(-8/8\) is \(-1\).
