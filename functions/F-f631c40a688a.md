### Arithmetic model

Under a conditional 32-bit machine-bitvector interpretation, arithmetic retains the low 32 bits, logical right shifts insert zeros, and narrowing discards high bits.

Arithmetic must be applied at the represented intermediate widths. For example, a 32-bit value shifted left by 28 and subsequently left by 28 becomes zero under this model. It does not retain an unbounded 56-bit shift result.

Address multiplication and subtraction are likewise width-sensitive. The bitvector interpretation provides conditional consequences, not an established language-level policy for signed overflow.

### Descriptive address roles

The following roles identify access relationships without asserting object types:

- **Primary base:** a retained dependency result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Tagged base:** a retained low result component shifted left 25 bits.
- **Shifted payload:** a separate retained result shifted left eight bits.
- **Narrow selector:** another result shifted left eight bits, masked with `0x7fff`, then logically shifted right six.
- **Selector base:** twice the narrow selector.
- **Final pair:** the low and high 32-bit components of the last combined result before direct mutation.
- **Retained link:** a word saved from an additional base at offset `0x54` before that location is replaced.

Under 32-bit bitvector arithmetic, the narrow selector ranges over multiples of four from zero through 508. The selector base ranges over multiples of eight from zero through 1016. Neither range consists of all values permitted by the mask width alone.

These names describe values and addresses only. They establish no ownership, allocation boundaries, or non-aliasing.

### Opening memory transaction

The opening transaction first writes the tagged base as a word at the final pair’s high component plus `0x14`, then writes that high component’s low byte at tagged-base offset `9`.

Next, it writes the primary base as a word at 256 times the final pair’s low component plus `0x54`. The byte at tagged-base offset `9` is then overwritten with the tagged base’s low byte.

The primary-base word at offset `0x14` is cleared before an unsigned byte is read at primary-base offset `5`. A selector-base word clear at offset `0x14` intervenes before that byte is written, zero-extended, as a word at primary-base offset `0x34`.

The primary-base word at offset `0x14` is subsequently read. Its actual read result is retained. A second selector-base word clear at offset `0x14` follows, then two byte stores at selector-base offset `0x15`, both using the retained word’s low byte.

The read-back word is not categorically zero. Intervening writes may overlap its location, and access-sensitive memory has not been excluded.

## Side effects

### Memory and ordering

Direct effects include byte, halfword, and word stores, and byte and word reads. Access ranges can overlap. For example, a word store at offset `0x14` overlaps a byte at offset `0x15`.

The contract therefore does not permit:

- Widening byte stores or narrowing word stores.
- Eliminating repeated accesses merely because retained values match.

- Treating absolute-address storage as isolated from computed pointers.
- Moving the computed-byte write past the closing saved-region or scaled-region reads.
- Replacing any of the three closing reads of `0x67` with the saved `0xa9` byte.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Selector boundary.** Under 32-bit bitvector arithmetic, a selector-producing result of `0x7f` produces narrow selector 508 and selector base 1016. Result `0x80` produces zero.

2. **Unsigned byte extension.** If primary-base offset `5` reads `0xfe`, the associated word store at offset `0x34` writes `0x000000fe`, not `0xfffffffe`.

3. **Overwritten access.** With ordinary disjoint storage, tagged-base offset `9` ultimately receives the tagged base’s low byte. The preceding high-component-byte store remains part of the access trace.

4. **Closing write boundary.** If absolute address `0x3a` overlaps the later saved-region-offset-`0x14` read, that read observes the applicable post-write memory state. Reading it before the computed-byte store does not satisfy the represented order.

5. **Fresh read for absolute `0x21`.** If the fresh read of `0x67` produces `0xb2` after its earlier store supplied `0xa1`, the byte written at `0x21` is `0xb2`. The saved-region-offset-`0x44` word read precedes this fresh byte read.

6. **Independent repeated reads.** If the two later reads of `0x67` produce `0x11` and `0x22`, their respective stores at scaled-region offset `9` use those values. Final memory may differ because another later store writes that same offset.

7. **Width distinction.** The three `0x3a` stores use byte, halfword, and word widths at their specified scaled addresses. Three byte stores are not equivalent.

8. **Intermediate values.** Access-observable memory sees the scaled-region-offset-`0x20` word writes as `0x20`, `0x2f`, the freshly loaded zero-extended byte, and `0x20`.

9. **Failure boundary.** A reached fault may prevent subsequent accesses and the final transfer. Completed side effects remain observable.
