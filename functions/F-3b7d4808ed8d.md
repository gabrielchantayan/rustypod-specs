### Width and layout conventions

A **word** is 32 bits; a **byte** is eight bits. A **pair result** is a represented 64-bit result split into low and high 32-bit words. This split describes numeric significance, not memory endianness.

For the conditional machine interpretation used below:

- Arithmetic results and computed addresses retain their low 32 bits.
- A **scaled** result is its word value multiplied by four.
- Left shifts discard bits beyond bit 31.
- Byte stores retain only bits 0–7.
- Selector reads are unsigned bytes, ranging from 0 through 255.
- Masking away the top two bits and shifting right by 20 produces a ten-bit value, ranging from 0 through 1023.

Several represented operations use signed values in overflowing products or left shifts. Modular calculations in this contract are conditional on the 32-bit execution interpretation; they are not portable source-language guarantees.

No object layout is established. Address additions such as 17 or 20 are literal computed-address offsets, not evidence of fields within an allocated object.

## Behavior

### Sequencing conventions

The following phases describe one iteration in execution order. Descriptive value names identify retained data relationships, not recovered program symbols.

An “intervening call” has no explicit arguments in the representation and no result consumed by this function. It remains observable and may have implicit inputs or arbitrary external effects. Each stated call must remain in order; none is assumed inert.

### 1. Publish the current payload

The first dependency call receives the current first and second arguments. Its target depends on the incoming link-like state on entry and on an assigned link-like value in later iterations.

Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call. Its result selects a word destination at 20 plus four times that result. The current payload is stored there.

A dependency immediately receives four times that address-selecting result. Three intervening calls and another word-producing call follow. Retain this latter result as the **early result**, together with its scaled value.

### 2. Update and clear through the entry selector

Read a selector byte at the current base plus one. Store a byte at the selector’s numeric value plus 17. The stored byte contains bits 20–27 of the fourth argument: the represented mask-and-shift first produces ten bits, then byte narrowing discards its upper two.

The subsequent interactions occur in this order:

1. A dependency receives the current payload shifted left by 12.
2. A word-producing dependency supplies a value forwarded to another dependency alongside the early result shifted left by 30.
3. A pair-producing dependency supplies low and high words to a forwarding dependency, low word first.
4. One intervening call precedes another word-producing call.

Then freshly read the selector at the current base plus one and store zero at its value plus 17. Immediately afterward, a dependency receives four times the last word-producing result.

The second selector access is not reuse of the first. Intervening calls or aliasing writes may change its value.

### 3. Publish a scaled result at expanded and ordinary addresses

Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call. Retain its scaled result as the **first publication value**.

Store that value as a word at 20 plus 4096 times the scaled early result. Then call a dependency with four arguments:

- The early result shifted left by 12.
- The same value again.
- The early result shifted left by 14.
- The first publication value.

The first two arguments arise from multiplying the scaled early result by 1024. They are not generally equal to the third argument.

Three intervening calls precede another word-producing call. Store the first publication value at 20 plus four times this new result. Immediately call a dependency with four times that same address-selecting result.

### 4. Perform two repeated publication stages

Each stage has the following ordered behavior:

- Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call.
- Retain four times that result as the stage value.
- Store the stage value at 20 plus 16 times its unscaled defining result.
- Call a dependency with the stage value in all three explicit argument positions.
- Three intervening calls precede a new word-producing call.
- Store the stage value at 20 plus four times this new address-selecting result.
- Immediately call a dependency with four times that address-selecting result.

Both stages include this final argument-bearing call before the next group of calls begins.

Retain the second stage’s secondary address-selecting result for a later shift-based word store. Separately, the second stage’s unscaled defining result multiplied by 2³⁰ establishes the **quarter-range base**. Under modular arithmetic, that base depends only on the defining result’s low two bits.

### 5. Clear two direct byte locations twice

Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call. Retain its result as the **lookup selector input**.

Store zero bytes first at the quarter-range base plus 17, then at that base plus one. A dependency then receives four times the lookup selector input.

Another dependency produces a word, which is forwarded as the sole explicit argument to the next dependency. Three intervening calls precede another word-producing call.

Repeat the two zero-byte stores in the same order. Then call a dependency with four times the latest result.

These are four distinct byte stores. Dependency effects between the pairs are unknown.

### 6. Perform a direct word lookup

Three intervening calls precede another word-producing call. Retain four times its result as the **lookup-stage scaled value**.

Before the lookup, store a word at 20 plus 16 times this result. The stored word is the second repeated stage’s retained secondary address-selecting result shifted left by 31. Under modular arithmetic, only its low bit contributes.

Next read a word at 20 plus the ten-bit value obtained by masking and shifting the lookup selector input. The address lies from 20 through 1043 and need not be word-aligned.

Call a dependency with the lookup-stage scaled value. The next word-producing call supplies the **retained late result**.

Then call a dependency with the looked-up word shifted right by 22 and subsequently left by 28. Because the lookup is retained as an unsigned word, the right shift is logical. Under truncation, only original bits 22–25 contribute.

### 7. Publish zero and perform late selector updates

Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call. Store a zero word at 20 plus four times its result, then immediately call a dependency with that same four-times result.

Three intervening calls precede a call producing the **late field word**.

Freshly read the selector at the quarter-range base plus one. At its value plus 17, store the low byte of four times the late field word. Then:

- Call a dependency with four times the late field word.
- Obtain another word result.
- Forward that result alongside the late field word shifted left by 30.
- Execute three intervening calls and another word-producing call.

Freshly read and retain the selector at the quarter-range base plus one as the **saved selector**. At the saved selector plus 17, store bits 20–27 of the late field word.

Call a dependency with four times the most recent word-producing result. After four intervening calls, store the retained late result shifted left by two as a word at the saved selector plus 20.

This final word destination uses the saved selector, irrespective of subsequent changes to selector memory.

Immediately after that word store, capture the displayed global value into a 32-bit unsigned **global snapshot**.

### 8. Process the first late pair

Call a dependency with the lookup-stage scaled value. Three intervening calls precede a pair-producing call.

Four times its low word establishes the **first pair-scaled value**. Before passing its high word onward, freshly read the selector at the quarter-range base plus one and store the low byte of the global snapshot shifted right by 22 at the selector’s value plus 17. This stores original snapshot bits 22–29.

Call a dependency with the pair’s high word. Obtain another word result and forward it alongside the first pair’s low word shifted left by 30.

Three intervening calls precede another word-producing call. Freshly read the selector at the quarter-range base plus one and store zero at its value plus 17. Immediately call a dependency with four times the last word result.

### 9. Perform the second expanded publication

Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call. Call a dependency with four times that result in both explicit argument positions.

Three intervening calls precede another word-producing call. Its scaled result is the **late publication value**.

Store the late publication value at 20 plus 4096 times the first pair-scaled value. Call a dependency with the first pair-scaled value shifted left by 14.

Three intervening calls precede a new word-producing call. Store the late publication value at 20 plus four times this result, then immediately call a dependency with that four-times result.

### 10. Process the second late pair

Three intervening calls precede a pair-producing call. Four times its low word establishes the **second pair-scaled value**.

Read a selector at one plus 16,384 times the first pair-scaled value. At the selected byte value plus 17, store bits 20–27 of the late publication value.

Call a dependency with the second pair’s high word. Obtain another word result and forward it as a single argument. Obtain a further pair result and forward its low and high words, in that order.

One intervening call precedes another word-producing call. Read a selector at one plus the second pair-scaled value multiplied by 2²⁸. Store zero at that selector’s value plus 17, then call a dependency with four times the latest word result.

Under modular arithmetic, this selector base depends only on the second pair’s unscaled low word modulo four.

### 11. Establish loop-carried state

Three intervening calls precede a word-producing call. Retain its result as the **final result** and four times it as the **final scaled value**.

Establish the next base as four times the second pair-scaled value. Freshly read the selector at this next base plus one and store zero at its value plus 17.

Call a dependency with the second pair-scaled value shifted left by ten. Obtain another word result and forward it alongside the retained late result shifted left by 28.

Three intervening calls precede another word-producing call. Freshly read the selector at the next base plus one and again store zero at its value plus 17. Immediately call a dependency with four times the latest result.

After four intervening calls:

- Establish the next payload as the final result shifted left by 14.
- Store it as a word at 20 plus 4096 times the final scaled value.
- Replace the fourth argument with this next payload.
- Call a dependency with the final scaled value shifted left by 14.
- Establish the link-like value used for the next iteration and execute one final intervening call.
- Replace the first argument with the fourth argument shifted left by 28.
- Replace the second argument with the represented extra result-register value.

The next iteration uses the new base and payload. Under word-truncating arithmetic, its first argument is zero: the two payload-related shifts discard all original bits. Its fourth argument is divisible by 2¹⁴. Neither fact establishes termination or convergence of the remaining state.

7. **Unaligned lookup.** A lookup selector input of `0x00100000` selects a word read starting at address 21. No alignment adjustment is represented; success depends on the environment.

8. **Pair forwarding order.** A directly forwarded pair with low word `0x11223344` and high word `0x55667788` supplies those two arguments in that order.

9. **Global snapshot retention.** A captured global value of `0x3FC00000` yields selected byte `0xFF`. Later changes to the global do not change the retained snapshot.
