# F-4ab968b3c975

## Purpose

F-4ab968b3c975 invokes a long ordered sequence of indirect dependencies, transforms dependency-produced words, and then mutates memory through fixed and computed addresses. Its last represented action is an unresolved indirect transfer supplying four argument words.

The static evidence does not establish an application-level purpose. Repeated offsets do not prove a structure definition; character-like constants do not prove text processing. Descriptions such as parser, allocator, initializer, or collection operation would exceed the evidence.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** access widths, transformations, ordering, and data dependencies visible in the supplied representation.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations not imposed as behavioral requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior or detail not specified sufficiently here for executable acceptance.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Inputs and outputs

The represented interface has no explicit parameters and no declared result value. Implicit inputs include:

- Incoming continuation-related machine state used to form the first indirect target.
- Ordinary and combined dependency results.
- Additional machine-result words with unresolved ABI significance.
- Fixed-address memory.
- Memory reached through dependency results, arithmetic-derived addresses, and subsequently loaded words.

The first indirect target is formed using incoming continuation-related state plus `0xffe`. Subsequent targets are predominantly formed from embedded continuation-like values plus the same displacement. Their executable interpretation and calling convention are **documented_only**.

There is no explicit success flag, error result, returned allocation, or output length. Observable outputs are dependency effects, memory mutations, and the terminal transfer.

That transfer supplies, in order:

1. `0x72`.
2. A word loaded late in the final region from offset `0x64` of a loaded base.
3. A word captured earlier from offset `0x14` of the final traversal base.
4. A later word loaded from offset `0x74` of the same loaded base used for argument 2.

A return is represented after the transfer. Whether the target returns normally, never returns, or follows a conventional ABI is **documented_only**.

### Widths and arithmetic

| Operation or quantity | Represented width |
|---|---:|
| Byte access | 8 bits |
| Halfword store | 16 bits |
| Word access, ordinary arithmetic word, address-like word | 32 bits |
| Combined dependency result | 64 bits |

Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Halfword stores retain the low sixteen bits. Byte loads used in arithmetic are unsigned and therefore widen to values from 0 through 255.

Combined results are split into low and high 32-bit words. Unsigned extractions by 34, 36, and 44 bits operate on the 64-bit result before narrowing, not on an already narrowed word.

Where this document gives unsigned bit-vector consequences, arithmetic is evaluated at the stated width; 32-bit left shifts retain the low 32 bits and unsigned right shifts insert zeros. Address comparisons below use an ordinary byte-addressed 32-bit interpretation, conditionally including modulo-\(2^{32}\) arithmetic. Signed-looking shifts, multiplication overflow, and address formation are not assigned a portable language-level overflow contract.

Endianness, alignment requirements, and access atomicity are **documented_only**.

## Behavior

### Dependency invocation and transformation phase

Execution begins with a long straight-line dependency sequence. No explicit conditional branch, bounded loop, retry, or status-based exit is represented.

Call order is part of the observable behavior. Calls with ignored results cannot be discarded as effect-free. Calls with no displayed arguments may still consume implicit state.

Later invocations receive combinations of:

- Returned words shifted left by eight bits.
- Shifted low result words alongside unchanged high result words.
- High words shifted by several different fixed counts.
- Previously captured words retained across intervening invocations.
- Masked fields, shifted fields, repeated arguments, and explicit zeros.
- Additional machine-result words whose conventional return-value status is unresolved.

Distinct invocation sites are not merged merely because their argument shapes match. Dependencies may modify globals or reachable memory; a captured value is not interchangeable with a later reload.

Field transformations include low-24-bit and low-22-bit masking, narrower masks, and unsigned extraction. One recurring extraction retains the low twenty bits and shifts right by ten, producing a ten-bit field. Several intermediate values are explicitly zeroed and subsequently shifted without reassignment; those particular derived values remain zero.

A 32-bit left shift by eight followed by an unsigned right shift by eight retains only the original low twenty-four bits. Likewise, successive shifts must preserve intervening truncation rather than being evaluated as an unlimited-width expression.

The complete per-site invocation inventory and dependency semantics are **documented_only** here. The established requirements are the represented ordering and width-sensitive data dependencies, not a guessed common purpose for the calls.

### Opening mutations and the zero reread

The final combined dependency result provides two words for initial addressing. A word store at offset `0x14` from its high word writes the shifted base. A byte store at offset `0x09` from the shifted base writes the low byte of that high word. Next, a word store at offset `0x54` from 256 times the final low word writes the scaled base. The earlier byte destination is then overwritten with the low byte of the shifted base.

The opening zero-and-widening group has this ordering:

- The word at scaled base plus `0x14` is zeroed before the byte at scaled base plus `0x05` is read.
- A zero word is written at small base plus `0x14`.
- The captured byte is widened unsigned and written as a word at scaled base plus `0x34`.
- The word at scaled base plus `0x14` is reread.
- The zero word store at small base plus `0x14` is repeated.
- Two byte stores at small base plus `0x15` write the low byte of the reread word.

**Under ordinary stable memory semantics, the reread is zero.** The widened-byte store is thirty-two bytes beyond the zeroed word, so its four-byte range is disjoint from the reread range. Any overlap caused by the intervening small-base word store still writes only zero bytes. Those writes provide no alias-based mechanism for making the reread nonzero.

A nonzero reread would require additional behavior not established by these operations: asynchronous modification, read-sensitive or write-sensitive devices, or a nonordinary address mapping. Such behavior remains **documented_only** and is separate from ordinary address aliasing.

### Fixed-address accesses and early traversal

The fixed byte at `0x05` is read after the duplicate small-base byte stores and before the byte write at `0x01`. The write to `0x01` supplies the low byte of the small base. The captured unsigned byte from `0x05` becomes a base for later accesses; it is not reloaded for those uses.

That captured-byte base receives a byte at offset `0x15` containing the low byte of the small quantity. The scaled base receives, at offset `0x11`, first the low byte of the retained payload and subsequently zero. Between those byte writes, a word store at offset `0x54` from the zero-reread-derived base writes the retained payload. Under the stable-memory condition above, that particular store addresses fixed location `0x54`.

The traversal word is captured from the captured-byte base plus `0x54` before that location is overwritten with the reread word. Subsequent addressing uses the captured traversal word even if its source storage changes.

Later in this region, fixed address `0x54` receives a word containing twice the traversal word. This occurs between successive word stores at small base plus `0x14`: an earlier store supplies the retained dependency word scaled by \(2^{26}\), and later stores supply the traversal word.

After duplicate byte stores at small base plus `0x15` containing the low byte of four times the traversal word, fixed address `0x15` receives the low byte of the small base. These fixed writes are not merely unnamed field effects and must remain ordered relative to the derived-address operations.

### Derived-field traversal

The middle region uses byte offsets including `0x01`, `0x05`, `0x09`, `0x0d`, `0x11`, `0x15`, and `0x19`, and word offsets including `0x04`, `0x14`, `0x24`, `0x34`, `0x44`, `0x54`, `0x64`, and `0x74`. These are access offsets, not an established layout or minimum object size.

One represented assignment contains two reads of small base plus `0x74`: one supplies the destination base and the other the stored word. Their relative evaluation order is **documented_only**. If the storage changes between reads, their values need not match.

### Constant stores and captured global base

The constant `0x3a` is written with three distinct widths: a byte at the constant-write base, a halfword at eight times the late link, and a word at constant-write base plus `0x20`.

At adjusted base plus `0x20`, successive word stores supply `0x20`, `0x2f`, a zero-extended byte loaded from adjusted base plus `0x04`, and `0x20` again. The byte read occurs after the first two stores. This is an ordered overwrite sequence, not a single final-value assignment.

A word at fixed address `0x087fbbbc` is then read once and captured as the **global base**. This capture precedes the word store of `0x20` at fixed address `0x60`. If that store or later stores affect the source storage, the captured base is still reused; there is no represented base reload.

A long fixed sequence of store groups follows. Within each group, the order is:

1. A word store to a scaled destination plus `0x54`.
2. A byte store at global base plus `0x19`, containing the low byte of the adjusted base.
3. A byte store at adjusted base plus `0x11`, containing the low byte of the global base.
4. A word store at global base plus `0x34`.

The first group uses eight times the traversal word for its scaled destination and final stored word. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted quantity. The word written at each scaled destination plus `0x54` is the constant-write base.

The groups are explicitly repeated, not controlled by a visible runtime count. Their exact cardinality is **documented_only** in this contract. Collapsing the region to its final memory state would discard represented intermediate effects.

The byte value is established independently of whether it has any textual meaning.

Words are then captured from final traversal base plus `0x14` and adjusted base plus `0x04`, before the following mutations. The earlier fixed word captured at `0xa9` remains a full captured word.

- The first follows intervening writes through the global base and replacement of the word at adjusted base plus `0x10`.
- The second follows another derived byte store, a word store at adjusted base plus `0x44`, and a word read at adjusted base plus `0x54`.

The function is not established as pure, transactional, idempotent, reentrant, or thread-safe. No synchronization or rollback protocol is represented.

No explicit allocation, release, reference-count adjustment, or ownership transfer is visible in the direct-memory phase. Dependencies may nevertheless allocate, release, or invalidate storage. A returned address-like word proves neither ownership nor lifetime.

There is no represented error-handling path. Invalid memory, invalid targets, dependency failure, or nonreturning dependencies have no established recovery behavior. Partial effects may precede termination or a fault.

Repeated stores and reloads matter for aliasing and for shared, volatile, device, or asynchronously observed storage. The representation establishes access order, not hardware visibility, atomicity, or inter-thread ordering guarantees.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static obligations, not executed tests.

1. **Word truncation.** A word `0x12345678` shifted left by eight produces `0x34567800` under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, not a widened forty-bit value.

2. **Independent result halves.** For high word `0x11223344` and low word `0x55667788`, forwarding the shifted low word and unchanged high word supplies `0x66778800` and `0x11223344`.

3. **Field extraction.** Retaining the low twenty bits of `0x000abcde` and shifting unsigned right by ten gives `0x2af`; shifting that result left by six gives `0xabc0`.

4. **Low-address bound.** An all-ones source word produces small quantity 508 and small base 1016. Treating that base as an arbitrary pointer loses an established constraint.

5. **Zero reread.** With stable ordinary memory, the reread at scaled base plus `0x14` is zero, even if the intervening small-base zero store overlaps it. The word at scaled base plus `0x34` cannot make it nonzero by ordinary range overlap.

6. **Fixed-byte capture.** The read at `0x05` precedes the write at `0x01`. If a nonordinary mapping makes the latter affect the former, subsequent addressing still uses the byte already captured.

7. **Captured global base.** The base read at `0x087fbbbc` precedes the word write at `0x60`. Later groups reuse that captured base rather than performing a new read.

8. **Observable overwrites.** The adjusted-base `0x20` destination receives `0x20`, `0x2f`, the widened loaded byte, and `0x20` in order.

9. **Reload after aliasing.** If an intervening derived store changes byte `0x67`, a subsequent adjusted-base byte store uses the newly read byte. Reusing the byte originally written to `0x67` would not satisfy the represented dependency.

10. **Terminal arguments.** Reaching F-UNKNOWN-TERMINAL supplies first argument `0x72` and the three specified captured words. It implies neither successful completion nor cleanup.
