# F-403d54236ce9

## Purpose

- The upper component comes from a shared 32-bit value, adjusted downward by 84.

## Behavior

### Lower-component transformation

The second argument is treated as a signed 32-bit quantity. The visible expression applies a right shift of nine positions followed by a left shift of one position.

Under the signed arithmetic-right-shift interpretation indicated by the recovered expression, the right shift preserves the sign. For mathematical description, let the second argument be \(x\). The lower component represents:

\[
2\left\lfloor \frac{x}{512}\right\rfloor
\]

encoded in 32 bits.

This description matters for negative inputs: rounding is toward negative infinity, not toward zero. For example, an input of −1 produces the 32-bit representation of −2, rather than zero.

The transformation has these consequences under that interpretation:

- The lowest nine input bits do not affect the result independently; they select a position within a 512-value group.
- The result’s least significant bit is always zero.
- Every result from this transformation is even.
- Inputs from 0 through 511 produce zero.
- Inputs from −512 through −1 produce the 32-bit representation of −2.
- The mathematical transformed value ranges from −8,388,608 through 8,388,606.

The final left shift is a 32-bit transformation. Its result occupies only the lower half of the return value. Negative results remain negative-looking 32-bit patterns within that half; they do not make the upper half all ones.

The evidence is a recovered representation rather than an independently verified machine-instruction trace. Exact treatment of signed shifts is therefore a qualification of the contract, detailed under **Uncertainties**.

### Upper-component transformation

The function reads the shared offset source and adjusts it by −84, equivalently subtracting 84.

The adjusted value supplies the upper 32 bits of the result. No carry, borrow, or other arithmetic interaction with the lower component is visible.
