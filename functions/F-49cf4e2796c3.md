# F-49cf4e2796c3

## Purpose

F-49cf4e2796c3 transforms selected fields of a caller-supplied memory region in place. Under the 32-bit unsigned-word interpretation supported by the supplied representation, it:

- Reverses the four bytes within nine individual words.
- Reverses the bytes within four additional words while exchanging those words in two adjacent pairs.
- Leaves six intervening words unchanged.

The selected layout occupies 19 consecutive 32-bit words, or 76 bytes. The function does not transform all 19 words uniformly.

The established behavior is a fixed-layout byte-order transformation. Its use as an encoding conversion, decoding conversion, or structure normalization operation is inferred intent, not established context. The identities and meanings of the fields are unknown.

## Behavior

### Byte transformation

For a 32-bit word whose bytes, from most significant to least significant, are A, B, C, and D, byte reversal produces the word whose bytes are D, C, B, and A.

For example:

- `0x01234567` becomes `0x67452301`.
- `0x00000001` becomes `0x01000000`.
- `0x80000000` becomes `0x00000080`.

This is reversal of byte positions, not reversal of bits within a byte.

The arithmetic uses unsigned shifts and bitwise combinations. Under the 32-bit interpretation, left-shifted bits outside the word are discarded and right shifts introduce zero bits. There is no signed interpretation, sign extension, carry-dependent addition, or data-dependent branch.

### Access order

The supplied representation establishes this order of memory operations:

1. Word 0 is read and then overwritten with its byte reversal.
2. The same read-then-write operation is performed separately for words 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 13, and 14, in that order.
3. Word 15 is read, followed by word 16. Both original values are captured before either destination is written.
4. Word 15 is overwritten with the byte reversal of original word 16.
5. Word 16 is overwritten with the byte reversal of original word 15.
6. Word 17 is read, followed by word 18. Both original values are captured before either destination is written.
7. Word 17 is overwritten with the byte reversal of original word 18.
8. Word 18 is overwritten with the byte reversal of original word 17.

Words 6 and 8 through 12 are neither read nor written by the shown operation. There is no full-region copy or preliminary read of the complete layout.

This ordering explains why each pair exchange preserves both original values despite operating in place. It also means that the represented operation does not make the complete transformation atomic.

The sequence above is established at the supplied representation level. Exact machine-instruction ordering, instruction count, access atomicity, and externally observable behavior under concurrent access are not independently established.

### Boundary and repeated-use behavior

There is no length-dependent stopping condition. The function always addresses the same selected positions, including word 18.

All 32-bit word values are handled by the same transformation. Zero, all-one values, and values with the high bit set do not select special behavior.

For valid, stable ordinary memory, applying the function twice restores the original region. Individual byte reversals undo themselves; each pair exchange with byte reversal also undoes itself. The untouched words remain unchanged throughout.

Some inputs are fixed points. An individually transformed word is unchanged if its bytes are symmetric under reversal. A transformed pair is unchanged when each word is the byte reversal of the other.

## Side effects

The explicit side effects are writes to words 0–5, 7, and 13–18 of the supplied region.

Each selected word has a write in the supplied representation even if its transformed numeric value equals its original value. Numeric equality therefore does not establish absence of a represented write.

There are no explicit accesses to global storage, external resources, or separate output buffers. No logging, allocation, deallocation, synchronization, or callback operation is shown.

The operation is incremental rather than transactional. Earlier fields are written before later fields are read. No validation phase, rollback mechanism, or recovery path is present.

The function does not check for a null address, insufficient storage, inaccessible memory, unsuitable alignment, or concurrent modification. Such inputs do not have a defined error result in this contract. Whether an invalid access traps, terminates execution, or has some other platform-dependent consequence is unknown.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional acceptance examples derived from the supplied representation. They have not been executed.

### Individual-word byte reversal

Given valid writable storage and word 0 equal to `0x01234567`, word 0 must become `0x67452301`.

If word 1 contains `0x80000000`, it must become `0x00000080`. This distinguishes unsigned byte reversal from an operation that propagates the high bit during a right shift.

Other selected positions must still receive their specified transformations.

### Preservation of skipped fields

Given arbitrary values in word 6 and words 8–12, those values must remain unchanged.

For example, word 6 equal to `0x12345678` must remain `0x12345678`, not become `0x78563412`. This distinguishes the selective layout from a whole-region conversion.

The established access sequence also excludes reads of those skipped words.
