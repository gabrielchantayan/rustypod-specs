# F-06c735336736

## Purpose

F-06c735336736 describes a memory-mutating operation whose visible paths end at invalid-instruction boundaries, not at a normal return. Its established behavior consists of ordered accesses to argument-derived addresses, low numeric addresses, fixed storage, symbol-derived addresses, and addresses derived from apparent stack state.

No application-level purpose is established. Exception-related storage labels do not prove exception-handler installation, and numeric use of function symbols does not establish calls.

This contract distinguishes three evidence levels:

- **Established static evidence:** access ordering, explicitly represented widths, value dependencies, and visible control-flow boundaries.
- **Inferred intent:** no application-level interpretation is adopted.
- **documented_only:** unresolved machine semantics, layouts, entry conditions, and behavior beyond the visible boundaries.

The contract records the supplied representation. It does not establish that every represented operation corresponds to a valid executable instruction.

### Results and obligations

There is no visible return value or success status. Memory accesses and the two control-flow boundaries are the established outputs.

No allocation, release, ownership transfer, or cleanup is visible. Nothing establishes exclusive ownership of any accessed region.

Reaching an access requires that earlier accesses and arithmetic have completed under the execution environment’s rules. No validation of addresses, bounds, alignment, or permissions is visible. A valid early access does not establish that later accesses are valid.

## Behavior

### Value and address conventions

All offsets in this contract are byte offsets. Explicit byte, halfword, and word accesses are respectively 8, 16, and 32 bits.

Several descriptive quantities recur:

- The **wrapped difference** is the second argument minus 0xA8, evaluated as unsigned 32-bit arithmetic.
- The **halfword base** is twice that difference, also with unsigned 32-bit wraparound.
- The **masked difference** clears the wrapped difference’s highest bit.
- The **index** is the masked difference shifted right by one. Its range is zero through 0x3FFFFFFF.
- The **fourth-derived destination** comes from shifting the fourth argument left by two and then right by thirty-two.
- The **first symbol value** is the numeric value associated with F-e47d7b2e9c88.
- The **first symbol base** comes from interpreting that value as signed 32 bits and shifting it right by thirty-two.

The two shifts by thirty-two do not have a portable result for the represented 32-bit operands. Neither zero, sign extension, nor hardware shift-count masking is adopted as a substitute.

Explicit unsigned calculations wrap at 32 bits. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, and unresolved address calculations must not automatically receive those same semantics. Outcomes involving signed right shifts are conditional on the execution environment’s treatment of negative operands.

### Initial memory footprint

The opening effects establish several destinations before the branch condition is examined.

The first write places the index’s low byte at the fourth argument plus 0x0897E8B8. An assignment of 0xB8 to fixed storage at 0x0897E8C0 follows. Its physical width is not established. Next, the first argument’s low byte is written at the first argument plus six; that address addition uses the first argument’s unsigned arithmetic.

The second argument is then written as a 32-bit value at the fourth-derived destination. The halfword region receives 0xFFFF at offsets four and six, in that order. A byte containing 0xB8 is subsequently written four bytes beyond the fourth-derived destination, before the halfword region receives 0xFFFF at offset two.

Fixed storage at 0x0897EA28 then receives a value consisting of the halfword base’s low sixteen bits. This establishes the assigned value, not independently the destination’s access width.

The next destination depends on memory rather than directly on an argument: an unsigned halfword is read at the halfword base and zero-extended into an address. A 32-bit payload formed by masking the first argument with 0x897E8B80 is written there. This destination lies numerically between zero and 0xFFFF.

Earlier writes may overlap the halfword read and affect its result. No disjointness guarantee applies to these regions.

### First symbol and the early boundary

The first symbol value contributes a sign-derived halfword: its signed 32-bit interpretation is shifted right by thirty-one and narrowed to sixteen bits. That halfword is written at first-symbol-base offset four **before** the sign branch.

If the signed first symbol value is negative, the visible path ends at the first invalid-instruction boundary. All later phases are absent from that path.

On the nonnegative path, the sign-derived halfword is zero. A halfword write at first-symbol-base offset 0x12 precedes a 32-bit self-address write: the first symbol value is both the destination address and the payload.

A signed halfword is then read at the first symbol base plus the index. Both preceding writes—the halfword at offset 0x12 and the self-address word—are repeated afterward, in that order. They remain distinct accesses, even when their payloads agree.

The saved halfword, rather than a new read, supplies a 32-bit write at first-symbol-base offset eight. Its signed promotion is shifted right by sixteen. With arithmetic right shifting, the resulting word is zero for halfwords below 0x8000 and 0xFFFFFFFF for halfwords at or above 0x8000.

### Exception-associated state and low-address records

An abort-associated global is assigned zero before the low-address records are updated.

A selector is obtained by adding 0x58 to the first symbol value’s bit-2 contribution. It is therefore 0x58 or 0x5C. A 32-bit write stores the first symbol base at selector plus four: address 0x5C or 0x60.

Next, a small exception-associated field receives the masked difference shifted right by seventeen and converted to unsigned sixteen bits. Its value ranges from zero through 0x3FFF. An instruction-associated global subsequently receives the index’s low byte, zero-extended to a sixteen-bit assigned value.

The full index is then written as 32 bits at four times the selector plus 0x10. The possible addresses are 0x170 and 0x180. A supervisor-associated global is assigned the index afterward.

For globals without complete declarations, these value conversions do not independently establish physical access widths or non-overlapping storage.

### Stack-associated memory

The stack-derived pointer identifies a region subject to overlapping writes. Its base first receives the selector as a halfword, then a word formed by shifting the first symbol base left by eight. The same word is written at offset twenty. A later word write at the base replaces the earlier base contents with the pointer’s own numeric value.

A separate 32-bit read uses twice the pointer’s numeric value as its address. The low half of this saved word is written at the pointer’s base. The complete saved word then becomes both the destination address and payload of another 32-bit write.

The address of the apparent stack position—not the pointer value—is used for the next destination. That address is multiplied by thirty-two and increased by 0x18. The 32-bit payload is the pointer’s signed interpretation shifted right by thirty-one.

Only after those effects does the instruction-associated update read its associated byte view. The new assigned value is a 24-bit composition with numeric value 0x000500 plus that freshly read byte. Its high sixteen-bit component is five; its low eight-bit component comes from memory. The global’s physical assignment width remains unresolved.

Finally, a pointer-valued store writes the stack-derived pointer at four times its numeric value plus 0x18. This is represented as a 32-bit pointer store.

These overlapping accesses establish ordering, not a unique final byte pattern. Endianness, aliases, and subsequent writes remain relevant.

### Cached state and fresh address reads

A signed 32-bit word is read from fixed storage at 0x0897EA50 and retained. This **cached word** remains the input to subsequent calculations even if that fixed storage changes.

The numeric value associated with F-8afdaab91693 is also used, without a visible invocation.

Fixed 32-bit storage at 0x0897EA80 is assigned four times the stack-derived pointer’s numeric value. Its contents subsequently supply both the destination and payload of an indirect 32-bit self-address write. The representation does not establish the relative evaluation order of those two operand reads.

After that indirect write, another read of 0x0897EA80 supplies the base for a 32-bit index write at offset four. This later destination must not be replaced with the earlier assigned value. If aliasing or other permitted memory behavior changes the global, the fresh read matters.

The cached word has different semantics. Four times that retained value supplies both address and payload for another 32-bit self-address write. The following destination combines the original cached word with a transformed version: left shift by six, signed right shift by seventeen, then addition of the original cached word. The payload there is the second symbol value shifted left by two. The signed arithmetic limitations stated above apply to these calculations.

### Final updates and boundary

The cached word’s upper sixteen bits are assigned to a reset-associated halfword. An interrupt-associated global is then assigned zero.

A base derived from eight times the second symbol value receives two 32-bit zero writes, first at its base and then at offset four. A byte write at offset sixteen follows. Its payload retains the low byte of the base address plus eight.

The remaining fixed-storage assignments are ordered as follows:

| Destination or role | Assigned value | Width established by the representation |
|---|---|---|
| 0x00000086 | 0x897EA700 | Unresolved |
| 0xEA700000 | 0x66 | Unresolved |
| 0x0897EB26 | 0xEAEC | Unresolved |
| 0x12FD4C34 | 0x1A | Unresolved |
| 0x897EA65C | Its own address | Pointer-valued; physical width unresolved |
| Reset-associated composite | Current reset halfword as high half, 0xEA70 as low half | 32-bit constructed value; destination layout unresolved |
| 0x12FD4CB8 | 0x5C | Unresolved |

The reset halfword is read freshly for the composite assignment, after the assignment at 0x897EA65C. It is not replaced by the upper half originally extracted from the cached word. The constructed value is the current unsigned halfword multiplied by 0x10000, plus 0xEA70.

After the assignment at 0x12FD4CB8, the continuing path reaches the final invalid-instruction boundary.

## Side effects

The established data-output channel is memory. The footprint includes low addresses, fixed storage, argument-related regions, symbol-related regions, and two distinct kinds of stack-derived addresses.

The supplied representation warns that larger global-like objects overlap smaller symbols at the same address. Consequently:

- Global assignments are not necessarily independent state changes.
- An indirect write may affect a later named-field read.
- A larger assignment may overwrite bytes belonging to a smaller view.
- Repeated writes remain observable accesses; equal payloads do not make them removable.
- Final bytes cannot generally be determined without layouts, access widths, endianness, and alias information.

There is no visible rollback. A fault or invalid-instruction event may leave only a prefix of the described changes completed. No atomicity, synchronization, concurrency, volatile-access, or memory-mapped-device guarantees are established.

The boundaries describe the visible control flow; they do not specify whether the environment traps, terminates, emulates, or otherwise handles an invalid instruction.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. Unless an example states otherwise, its relevant accesses must be reached successfully and its arithmetic must have a defined interpretation in the execution environment.

1. **Subtraction and wraparound.** With second argument 0xA8, the wrapped difference, halfword base, and index are zero. The constant halfword writes target addresses four, six, and two in that chronological order, with the separate fourth-derived byte write between the latter two. With second argument 0xA7, the wrapped difference is 0xFFFFFFFF, the halfword base is 0xFFFFFFFE, and the index is 0x3FFFFFFF. This does not establish accessibility of those addresses.

2. **Different truncation widths.** With second argument 0x2A8, the index is 0x100. Its initial byte payload is zero, while later full-width index payloads are 0x100. The instruction-associated sixteen-bit assigned value derived from that byte is also zero.

3. **Memory-selected destination.** If the halfword read at the halfword base yields 0x1234, the following masked first-argument word is written at address 0x00001234. It is not written at the halfword base merely because that address supplied the read.

4. **Negative first symbol.** If the first symbol value has its 32-bit sign bit set, the initial effects and first sign-derived halfword write precede the early boundary. No selector, stack-associated, or final-phase operation is visible on that path.

5. **Signed saved halfword.** Under arithmetic right shifting, a continuing-path halfword read of 0x7FFF produces a later word payload of zero at first-symbol-base offset eight. A read of 0x8000 produces 0xFFFFFFFF. Intervening repeated writes do not replace the saved halfword with a fresh read.

6. **Selector alternatives.** A clear bit 2 in the first symbol value selects 0x58, with the corresponding word destinations 0x5C and 0x170. A set bit 2 selects 0x5C, producing destinations 0x60 and 0x180.

7. **Overlapping stack writes.** The word write of the pointer’s numeric value covers the byte locations of the earlier selector halfword at the same base. This is an overwrite of those locations, not proof that their resulting values differ. Coincidental equality and later aliases remain possible.

8. **Fresh versus retained values.** If an intervening write changes 0x0897EA80 before its later address read, that later value determines the index destination. By contrast, changing 0x0897EA50 after its initial read does not replace the cached word used in the final calculations.

9. **Fresh reset composition.** If the initial reset-halfword assignment supplies 0x1234 but an intervening alias changes the field to 0xABCD, the final composition is 0xABCDEA70, provided no further change intervenes. It is not 0x1234EA70.

10. **No invented success path.** Completing every visible continuing-path access leads to the final invalid-instruction boundary. Neither dependency is called, and no normal success return is established.

## Uncertainties

The following remain **documented_only**:

- Whether the supplied representation accurately identifies executable instructions throughout.
- Results of both right shifts by thirty-two.
- Signed left-shift, overflow, negative-right-shift, and address-calculation semantics.
- Complete widths and layouts of incompletely declared globals.
- Exact overlap relationships and endianness-dependent byte effects.
- Physical timing and number of loads used to obtain apparent stack entry state.
- Concrete numeric dependency-symbol values.
- Initial memory contents and concrete indirect aliases.
- Behavior on invalid, inaccessible, unaligned, or special-purpose addresses.
- External mutation or special access semantics, if any.
- Behavior after either invalid-instruction boundary.
- Any application-level purpose.

These gaps prevent an unconditional portable execution contract. They do not justify omitting accesses, collapsing repeated writes, replacing fresh reads with cached values, or assigning conventional initialization semantics.
