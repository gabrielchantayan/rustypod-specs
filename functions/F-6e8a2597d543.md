# F-6e8a2597d543

## Purpose

F-6e8a2597d543 performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, transforms values obtained through those invocations, updates memory through fixed and derived addresses, and invokes a final indirect target.

**Established static evidence:** The supplied representation exposes two explicit 32-bit inputs, additional implicit execution-state inputs, mixed-width memory accesses, repeated publications, and a terminal invocation with four argument positions. No application-level result or error status is represented.

**Inferred intent:** Shifts and masks suggest packed-field processing; address-following and repeated writes suggest state publication. These interpretations do not establish domain types, conventional data structures, device semantics, or ownership.

This contract describes represented behavior, not an independently validated executable interface. The complete individual ordering and argument/result associations of the long pre-update invocation sequence remain **documented_only and incomplete in this contract**. That coverage limitation is distinct from the established dispatch arithmetic described below.

### Output and completion

No ordinary return value is represented. The final invocation receives:

1. The 32-bit constant 0x72.
2. A word loaded late in execution from offset 0x64 of a subsequently selected base.
3. A word saved earlier from offset 0x14 of the doubled selected base defined below.
4. A later word loaded from offset 0x74 of the subsequently selected base.

An ordinary return follows the final invocation in the representation. Reaching that return requires the terminal target to return normally.

Address calculations and most retained arithmetic use 32-bit quantities. Unsigned operations have their represented fixed-width behavior. ## Behavior

### Indirect dispatch and value preparation

The first target is formed by adding **0xffe to incoming continuation-related state**. It receives both explicit inputs and the additional incoming state shifted left by five.

Subsequent targets before the direct-update phase are formed from represented numeric bases or code-label address bases, again with **0xffe added**. This construction is established independently of dependency identity or semantics. For example, the first represented numeric bases after the initial invocation are 0x087f65d3, 0x087f65d5, and 0x087f65d7. Their construction is not a lookup through an inferred dispatch table.

The sequence includes invocations with no explicit arguments, explicit zero positions, shifted primary results, high and low portions of wider results, secondary result state, and values retained across intervening invocations. No-argument sites are not guaranteed to have no implicit inputs. Unused results do not imply absent side effects.

Established transformations include:

- Left shifts by eight, with some results also shifted by other counts through thirty-one.
- Reuse of a high result word both unchanged and shifted.
- Twenty-bit masking followed by a logical right shift of ten, yielding a ten-bit field.
- Twenty-four-bit masking followed by a right shift of twenty, yielding a four-bit field.
- Twenty-two-bit masking followed by a left shift of seven, producing at most twenty-nine significant bits.
- Extraction from a 64-bit result after a logical right shift of forty-four, yielding its upper twenty bits.
- Explicitly zeroed values whose subsequent represented shifts remain zero.

A left shift followed by a right shift need not recover the original input: high bits discarded by the first operation are lost.

**Coverage boundary:** The complete per-invocation ordinal list, numeric or label base, explicit argument tuple, producing-result association, and secondary-result association are not specified individually here. That portion is **documented_only/incomplete**, not intrinsically unknowable from the supplied representation. No summarized pattern is permission to reorder, combine, or omit invocations.

### Direct-update terminology

The following names describe address relationships only:

- **Packed base:** a retained shifted word used for the opening byte destination.
- **Scaled seed:** an earlier retained result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Small scaled base:** twice a field derived from a shifted result masked with 0x7fff and shifted right by six.
- **Old link:** the word read at offset 0x54 from the zero-extended byte obtained at fixed address 0x05.
- **Fourfold link:** four times the old link.
- **Adjusted link:** the fourfold link minus 0x96.
- **Working base:** four times the adjusted link.
- **Selected link:** a later word obtained at offset 0x74 using a zero-extended byte as its base.
- **Doubled selected base:** twice the selected link.
- **Quadrupled selected base:** four times the selected link.
- **Publication base:** the word loaded from fixed address 0x087fbbbc.

These names establish neither allocation boundaries nor disjoint objects. All values remain subject to the arithmetic qualifications above.

### Opening publications, clears, and fixed low-address state

The final pre-update invocation supplies a wider result. Its high word is used as a base for a word store at offset 0x14 and as the source of a byte store at packed-base offset 9. Between that byte store and its overwrite, the scaled seed is written at offset 0x54 from a base formed by multiplying the final result’s low word by 256. The overwrite at packed-base offset 9 uses the retained packed value’s low byte.

The scaled seed’s offset-0x14 word is cleared, its offset-5 byte is read, and the small scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared. The loaded byte is published as a zero-extended word at scaled-seed offset 0x34. The cleared scaled-seed word is then loaded. A second small-base clearing store and two separate identical byte stores at small-base offset 0x15 follow.

The next fixed-address relationship is concrete: **the byte at 0x05 is read before the byte at 0x01 is written**. The latter receives the small scaled base’s low byte. The saved, zero-extended 0x05 byte becomes an address base; its offset-0x15 byte receives the un-doubled small field’s low byte.

Further opening effects write and then clear the byte at scaled-seed offset 0x11. Between those writes, the retained shifted word is stored at offset 0x54 using the previously loaded cleared word as its base.

The old link is read from offset 0x54 of the saved byte-derived base before that location is replaced by the previously loaded cleared word. Subsequent derived accesses cannot retroactively change the saved 0x05 byte or old link, but may affect other later loads.

### Link-following and scaled destinations

The next region follows words at offsets 0x74 and 0x64, uses scaled values as addresses, and repeatedly overwrites offset-0x14 and offset-0x54 words.

Its observable constraints include:

- Two represented reads of small-scaled-base offset 0x74 occur within a nested destination/value relationship. They are separate reads; no equality between their observed values is guaranteed for access-sensitive memory.
- Old-link offset 0x24 receives the old link itself twice, interleaved with associated byte and word publications.
- Small-scaled-base offset 0x14 receives a seed-derived shifted value, then the old link twice.
- **The word at fixed address 0x54 receives twice the old link**, between the seed-derived offset-0x14 write and the old-link replacements.
- Two further identical byte stores at small-scaled-base offset 0x15 precede **a byte write to fixed address 0x15**, which receives the small scaled base’s low byte.

Later effects publish fourfold and eightfold old-link values, load a word through a zero-extended byte plus 0x74, and follow another word at offset 0x64. A word read at offset 4 supplies another base. Bytes at offset 5 are read at separate points, with intervening writes.

The selected link is obtained from the later byte-derived offset-0x74 read. Its doubled value becomes the doubled selected base. This is literal byte-derived addressing, not evidence of an array index or an invented table base.

The region also writes 0x3a as a byte at the quadrupled selected base, as a halfword at eight times the selected link, and as a word at quadrupled-selected-base offset 0x20.

### Adjusted publications

At working-base offset 0x20, four word writes occur in this order: 0x20, 0x2f, a zero-extended byte read from working-base offset 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs for the third write, after the first two writes.

Next, **the publication-base word at 0x087fbbbc is read before the word 0x20 is written to fixed address 0x60**. Both precede the first publication store. The saved publication base is not reloaded merely because subsequent writes could alias its original location.

Each publication group has four ordered effects:

- A word receives the quadrupled selected base at an eightfold-derived base plus 0x54.
- Publication-base offset 0x19 receives the working base’s low byte.
- Working-base offset 0x11 receives the publication base’s low byte.
- Publication-base offset 0x34 receives the corresponding eightfold-derived value.

The first group uses eight times the **old link** for its first destination and fourth value. Subsequent groups use eight times the **adjusted link**. Thus the first basis is not the unadjusted fourfold link.

The groups are explicitly repeated in the representation, not controlled by a visible data-dependent loop. Their exact repetition count remains **documented_only** in this contract. Repeated writes and their order cannot be replaced by final memory contents.

### Final saved reads and fixed-byte observations

After the publication region, a **32-bit word at fixed address 0xa9 is saved**. The word at quadrupled-selected-base offset 0x74 is then saved. **The byte write to fixed address 0x3a occurs next**, before the saved reads at doubled-selected-base offset 0x14 and working-base offset 4.

The value written to 0x3a is the low byte of eight times the adjusted link plus 0x30. The two following word reads retain values later used to restore a field or supply the terminal invocation.

Initial final-phase writes publish the working base’s low byte at doubled-selected-base offset 0x0d, write byte 0x6c at working-base offset 8, and publish the publication base’s low byte at working-base offsets 0x0d and 9. Doubled-selected-base offset 0x54 temporarily receives that base itself.

**Fixed address 0x67 then receives the saved 0xa9 word’s low byte.** A word is read from doubled-selected-base offset 0x44 before **0x67 is read as a byte for the write to fixed address 0x21**. If that intervening word read has access-sensitive effects, the 0x21 value need not equal the byte originally written to 0x67.

The loaded offset-0x44 word supplies a byte at working-base offset 0x10 and a word at publication-base offset 0x54. A signed right-shift expression supplies the byte at doubled-selected-base offset 1; its count is the working base’s low byte. Publication-base offset 0x54 is subsequently overwritten by the working base.

A word from working-base offset 0x40 is saved and copied to offset 0x10. Before that copy, publication-base offset 0x74 receives the earlier saved working-base offset-4 word. Publication-base offset 0x44 then receives the doubled selected base.

Two additional observations of 0x67 occur later:

- A fresh byte read supplies a write to working-base offset 9, before a byte write to doubled-selected-base offset 9 and a word write to working-base offset 0x44.
- After a word is loaded from working-base offset 0x54 as a new base, another fresh byte read supplies another write to working-base offset 9.

These are **three separate reads of 0x67 in total**, counting the read for 0x21. Intervening derived writes may alias 0x67. The two later reads must not be replaced by unconditional reuse of the saved 0xa9 byte.

### Remaining updates and terminal invocation

The saved quadrupled-base offset-0x74 word supplies low-byte publications at new-base offset 0x0d and doubled-selected-base offset 0x11. The earlier saved doubled-base offset-0x14 word is restored at doubled-selected-base offset 0x54.

A further working-base offset-0x14 word is loaded and later supplies a byte at new-base offset 0x11. Working-base offset 9 receives the new base’s low byte, and offset 0x44 receives the new base as a word.

A word read from doubled-selected-base offset 4 selects a **successor base**. The halfword 0x65 is written at twice that base, after which its offset-0x64 word is loaded for the terminal invocation’s second argument. New-base offset 0x54 receives decimal 100.

The successor’s offset-0x74 word is then loaded for the terminal invocation’s fourth argument. A word from doubled-selected-base offset 0x64 selects a final destination base. The successor’s low byte is published at doubled-selected-base offset 9; final-destination-base offset 0x54 receives the saved successor offset-0x64 word. A word store at twice that saved word receives the final destination base.

The terminal invocation then receives 0x72, the saved successor offset-0x64 word, the saved doubled-base offset-0x14 word, and the saved successor offset-0x74 word.

Narrow fields can overlap words. A byte at offset 0x11 lies within the four-byte range beginning at offset 0x10; its numerical effect depends on byte order. Access at offset 0x74 requires the addressed four-byte range, not an inferred allocation size.

No locking, barriers, transaction boundary, rollback, or atomicity guarantee is established. Sequentially represented accesses define the documented ordering; actual concurrency and device observability remain unresolved.

No explicit allocation or release operation is identified. Dependencies may nevertheless allocate, release, retain references, or modify shared state. No ownership transfer is established.

Invalid targets, inaccessible storage, unsupported alignment, and unresolved arithmetic cases have no represented local recovery or error result.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static implications, not executed tests.

- **Initial forwarding:** Explicit inputs 0x11223344 and 0x55667788 retain those bit patterns in the initial invocation. Incoming shifted state 3 produces third argument 0x60 when the represented shift is valid.
- **Dispatch arithmetic:** Incoming continuation-related state 0x1000 produces initial target 0x1ffe. A subsequent numeric base 0x087f65d3 produces target 0x087f75d1. Neither calculation identifies dependency semantics.
- **Field extraction:** Masking 0x000ffc00 to twenty bits and logically shifting right by ten yields 0x3ff.
- **Truncation:** Under truncating 32-bit shifts, shifting 0x12345678 left by eight and logically right by eight yields 0x00345678, not the original value.
- **Low-address consequence:** If the cleared scaled-seed word remains zero until loaded, its later offset-0x54 destination is address 0x54. No null check replaces that access.
- **Publication-base retention:** If the 0x60 write changes bytes of the publication-base source through aliasing, publications still use the base saved before that write.
- **Fresh 0x67 observations:** If an intervening derived write changes 0x67 from 0x12 to 0x34, a subsequent read for working-base offset 9 observes 0x34 under ordinary coherent memory. Reusing 0x12 would violate the documented read boundary.
- **Terminal completion:** Normal return is represented only after the terminal target returns normally. No earlier success return is established.
