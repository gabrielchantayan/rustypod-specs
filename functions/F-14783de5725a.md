# F-14783de5725a

## Purpose

F-14783de5725a performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms implicit entry values and dependency results, mutates computed-address and fixed-address memory using several access widths, and ends with an unresolved indirect invocation.

The supplied static material does not establish an application-level purpose. Character-like constants do not establish text formatting, and pointer-following accesses do not establish a particular container or ownership model.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented calls, arithmetic, access widths, captured values, and access ordering.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations that require assumptions beyond the representation.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which no executable guarantee is supplied.

No runtime tests have been performed.

No explicit parameters or scalar return value are represented. Implicit inputs include:

## Behavior

### Dependency sequencing

The first invocation uses an entry-state-derived target with an added displacement of `0xffe`. Later targets are predominantly derived from distinct represented call sites using the same displacement.

The represented sequence is straight-line: no conditional validation, retry, or alternate success path is shown. Each later invocation depends on earlier invocations returning.

Calls carry zero to four explicitly represented arguments. Absence of represented arguments does not establish a verified zero-argument calling convention. Some results are ignored locally; others are saved across intervening calls, split into low and high words, or taken from an additional result channel. Ignored results do not make calls removable.

Early arguments include the entry quantity shifted by 3, 7, and 26, and saved shifted values transformed again. Later patterns repeatedly pass a low word shifted by 8 alongside a high word, transformed high word, saved value, or explicit zero.

Other transformations include masks of widths 11 through 24 at particular sites, extraction of bits 10–19, and unsigned 64-bit right shifts by 34, 36, and 44. These fields have no established application meaning. A high-word extraction from a wide result is distinct from shifting an already truncated word.

Dependency effects on globals, memory, ownership, and control are unknown. No preservation guarantee may be inferred from a locally unused result.

### Arithmetic boundaries

Successive word-width transformations must not be replaced by unbounded arithmetic that retains discarded high bits.

**[INFERENCE]:** Under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, left shift by 25 followed by left shift by 11 yields zero; two successive left shifts by 28 do not retain a 56-bit result. The representation does not independently settle instruction-level signed overflow, signed-looking left shifts, or address wraparound. Those executable details remain **documented_only**.

Unsigned right shifts are zero-filling. Narrow stores discard high bits. Byte-to-word conversions are zero-extending.

Under the same bit-vector interpretation, the small index is a multiple of four between 0 and 508 inclusive. Twice that index is between 0 and 1016. This tighter bound follows from the left shift by 8 before masking; it establishes no memory validity.

A final-phase signed-looking word is right-shifted by the low byte of the adjusted base. The count can exceed 31. Signed right-shift behavior and oversized-count handling are **documented_only**, not automatically masked or defined as zero.

### Initial direct-memory phase

After the final wide dependency result, the high word supplies a base for a word store at offset `0x14`, carrying the saved shifted base. Its low byte is then stored at shifted base plus `0x09`. Between that byte store and a second store to the same byte, the large base is written as a word at offset `0x54` from the final result’s low word scaled by 256. The second byte store carries the low byte of the saved shifted base.

The large-base word at `0x14` is cleared before reading the byte at large base plus `0x05`. A word at twice the small index plus `0x14` is then cleared, and the captured byte is zero-extended into the large-base word at `0x34`. Only afterward is large base plus `0x14` read back. The small-index-derived word is cleared again, followed by two identical byte stores at its offset `0x15`, carrying the low byte of the read-back.

The read-back is an actual read. Replacing it with zero requires assumptions about aliasing and memory behavior that are not established.

Next, fixed-address byte `0x05` is captured and zero-extended as an address base. Fixed-address byte `0x01` receives the low byte of twice the small index. Computed writes then publish the small index, shifted payload, and a zero byte. In particular, the shifted payload is written through the earlier read-back plus `0x54`, without validating that read-back.

The captured link is read from the byte-derived base plus `0x54` before that word is overwritten with the earlier large-base read-back. Subsequent word and byte effects follow links loaded at offsets `0x74` and `0x54`; one represented self-related store uses the word at twice the small index plus `0x74` in both its destination derivation and its stored value.

### Linked updates and fixed-address interleaving

A word reached from large base plus `0x74` receives a shifted saved dependency result at offset `0x54`. The captured link is written to its own offset `0x24`, paired with a byte write through that reached word at offset `0x09`. A shifted-payload store separates the two occurrences of this self-related word-and-byte pair.

The word at twice the small index plus `0x14` next receives the saved dependency result shifted by 26. **Before that location receives the captured link, fixed address `0x54` receives a 32-bit store carrying twice the captured link.** The small-index-derived word then receives the captured link twice.

Two byte writes at twice the small index plus `0x15` carry the low byte of the fourfold link. **After both, fixed address `0x15` receives a byte store carrying the low byte of twice the small index.** This fixed-address store precedes the next shifted-payload word store at fourfold link plus `0x54` and the fourfold-link store at twice the small index plus `0x34`.

Further effects scale captured words by 2, 4, and 8, use loaded words as new bases, and interleave writes at offsets `0x09`, `0x0d`, `0x14`, `0x15`, `0x44`, and `0x54`.

Two byte-based lookups must remain distinct:

- A byte read from fourfold link plus `0x05` supplies the base for an initial word read at offset `0x74`.

Those later reads cannot automatically reuse an earlier byte. Computed writes may alias either byte location. The final working base retains twice the late link.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Unsigned byte promotion.** A captured byte `0xe7` produces word value `0x000000e7` where zero-extension is represented.

2. **Read-back semantics.** The cleared large-base word reads as zero only if intervening writes do not alter it and the memory surface has ordinary stable semantics.

3. **Fixed-address interleaving.** A trace records the word store at `0x54` between the shifted-result store and captured-link stores at twice the small index plus `0x14`. The byte store at `0x15` follows both adjacent computed byte stores.

4. **Captured publication base.** If writing `0x20` at `0x60` changes the storage from which the publication base was loaded, publications still use the earlier captured base.

5. **Capture boundary at `0x3a`.** If the byte store at `0x3a` overlaps a subsequent offset-`0x14` capture, that capture reflects the write. The preceding `0xa9` and offset-`0x74` snapshots remain unchanged.

6. **Narrowed adjusted value.** Under compatible arithmetic semantics, an adjusted quantity with low byte `0x21` produces byte `0x38` at `0x3a`.

7. **Fresh `0x67` values.** If `0x67` initially contains `0x12` and an intervening computed write changes it to `0xab`, a later represented read obtains `0xab`. Reusing `0x12` is insufficient.

8. **Intermediate constants.** A trace-sensitive surface retains all four adjusted-base offset-`0x20` stores, including `0x2f` and the byte-derived value.

9. **Width distinction.** The halfword `0x3a` write is not interchangeable with its separate byte or word counterparts.
