- The unsigned quotient in bits 31–0.

For numerator \(N\), divisor \(D\), quotient \(Q\), and remainder \(R\), this result satisfies:

### Arithmetic-path selection

The function chooses among paths using unsigned comparisons against the numerator shifted logically right.

For a stable nonzero divisor, the first decision has these consequences:

- If \(D > \lfloor N/16 \rfloor\), the quotient is at most 15 and requires **at most four bits**. This path resolves quotient weights 8, 4, 2, and 1.
- Otherwise, if \(D > \lfloor N/256 \rfloor\), the quotient is between 16 and 255, inclusive. This path resolves weights 128, 64, 32, and 16 before continuing through weights 8, 4, 2, and 1.
- Otherwise, the quotient is at least 256, and the function uses a scaled-divisor path.

The first bound includes four-bit quotients. In particular, numerator 15 and divisor 1 select the first path and produce quotient 15.

Equality belongs to the larger-path side of each selection threshold. For example, divisor 1 with numerator 16 proceeds beyond the first threshold, and divisor 1 with numerator 256 proceeds beyond the second.

### Fit tests and residual updates

The arithmetic maintains a residual numerator and constructs quotient bits from higher weight to lower weight.

For each candidate quotient weight, an unsigned comparison determines whether the corresponding divisor multiple fits in the current residual. A successful fit contributes a set quotient bit and reduces the residual by that multiple. An unsuccessful fit contributes a clear quotient bit and leaves the selected residual unchanged.

Fit comparisons are inclusive. Equality therefore selects subtraction. Exact division produces remainder zero rather than retaining one divisor in the remainder.

The comparisons against shifted residuals avoid needing an overflowing divisor multiple merely to decide whether it fits. Under the stable nonzero-divisor precondition, every selected reduction is mathematically nonnegative.

At the last quotient position, the representation also forms a candidate subtraction before selecting between that candidate and the unchanged residual. If the divisor exceeds the residual, that candidate is not selected. Thus, an intermediate wrapped candidate does not imply a wrapped returned remainder.

### Scaled-divisor processing

For larger quotients, the function selects a divisor scaled left by 6, 12, 18, or 24 bits.

The selection compares the scaled values with the original numerator shifted right by eight bits. The earlier scale is selected when its scaled divisor exceeds that comparison value; otherwise selection proceeds to a larger scale.

With a stable nonzero divisor, the preceding conditions bound each selected shift sufficiently to keep the scaled divisor representable in 32 bits. The ordinary division result does not depend on losing significant divisor bits during these shifts.

Processing then resolves quotient bits in groups of six. Within each group, the candidate multiples of the current scaled divisor have weights:

\[
128,\ 64,\ 32,\ 16,\ 8,\ 4.
\]

Between continuing groups, the working divisor is shifted logically right by six bits. Two final decisions, at weights 2 and 1 relative to the final working divisor, complete the quotient.

Some displayed control values are negative when interpreted as signed integers; they do not represent negative quotients. The continuation decision is based on unsigned carry behavior, not a signed comparison or signed-overflow condition.
