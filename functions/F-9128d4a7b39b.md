# F-9128d4a7b39b

## Purpose

F-9128d4a7b39b conditionally replaces a caller-selected integer. Two external factors govern the operation: one determines eligibility for replacement, and the other scales the replacement value.

Eligibility requires a negative evaluated product of the selected integer and the selection factor. Zero and positive results do not enable replacement. When replacement is enabled, the operation evaluates those inputs again, reverses the sign of their new product, and applies the scaling factor.

This behavior does not guarantee an absolute value, a nonnegative result, or preservation of magnitude. A negative selected integer can remain unchanged, and replacement can produce a positive value, a negative value, or zero.

Normal completion returns a 32-bit zero regardless of whether replacement occurred. The return value is not a change indicator.

These are static behavioral findings. Application intent, exceptional execution, and target-specific arithmetic remain separate from the established contract.

## Behavior

### Eligibility and the no-assignment outcome

Eligibility depends on whether the evaluated product of the selection factor and selected integer is less than zero.

The comparison concerns the result under the applicable arithmetic rules, not an unbounded mathematical product. Opposite operand signs imply eligibility only when the effective types, conversions, and representability make that reasoning valid.

When the comparison does not establish a negative result:

- No assignment to the selected object occurs.
- The scaling factor is not evaluated by the visible operation.
- Normal completion returns zero.

For ordinary stable storage with no other writer, the selected integer consequently retains its prior value. The stronger, directly supported guarantee is absence of an assignment by this operation. It does not prevent an independent agent from changing storage.

Preservation does not involve writing the old value back. This matters for read-only storage and for environments in which a write is independently observable.

### Enabled replacement

An eligible invocation obtains replacement inputs through renewed references to the selection factor and selected integer, together with the scaling factor. The replacement is the sign-reversed new product, multiplied by the scaling factor.

The eligibility product is not explicitly saved as the replacement input. With stable values and consistent arithmetic, the two products agree. Stability is not enforced by this operation.

Eligibility is decided once. There is no second eligibility check after obtaining the replacement inputs. If a valid access model permits the later inputs to differ, the replacement can proceed even when those later values would not independently satisfy the original condition.

One explicit assignment delivers the replacement to the selected object. There is no subsequent adjustment, validation, or alternative return value indicating that the assignment occurred.

No overflow detection, saturation, clamping, range validation, or recovery mechanism is established.
