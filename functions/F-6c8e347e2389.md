# F-6c8e347e2389

## Purpose

F-6c8e347e2389 performs an ordered chain of unresolved indirect calls, transforms their results and secondary result state, modifies byte-addressed memory through fixed and computed addresses, and invokes an unresolved terminal destination.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Printable constants do not establish text output; reciprocal-looking stores do not establish a container or ownership protocol.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** operations, widths, value dependencies, and ordering represented in the supplied body.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations that impose no acceptance requirement.
- **documented_only:** properties not resolved by the available evidence.

No runtime tests have been performed. Valid execution, successful completion, and normal return are not established.

## Behavior

### Execution structure and dependency interactions

The represented body is straight-line: a long indirect-call stage, a direct-memory stage, and a terminal indirect invocation. No explicit conditional branch or loop is represented. Dependencies may nevertheless fault, fail to return, or transfer control nonlocally.

The first indirect destination incorporates incoming control-flow state plus displacement 0xFFE. Subsequent destinations are predominantly formed from represented code-location values plus the same displacement. This does not establish that the sites call one shared helper or distinct independent functions.

Calls consume various combinations of:

- A word result shifted left by eight.
- A combined result’s low word shifted left by eight and its unchanged high word.
- Additional shifted, masked, or extracted result fields.
- Explicit zero arguments.
- Secondary result state.

An explicit zero argument is distinct from an argument position not represented at a site. Missing represented arguments must not be filled with assumed zeros or treated as proof of the actual machine signature.

Calls with unused results remain interactions. Their effects cannot be discarded, merged, reordered, or presumed to preserve globals read later.

### Transformation constraints

The arithmetic includes fixed left shifts ranging from small field repositionings through shifts by 28–31 bits, logical right shifts, masks, and successive narrowing operations.

Material distinctions include:

- A word shifted left by eight and then logically right by eight loses its original high byte.
- A 24-bit mask excludes the original upper byte before subsequent scaling.
- A 22-bit mask followed by a shift by seven or eight differs from an unrestricted word shift.
- Masking to 20 bits, shifting right by ten, then shifting left by five or six extracts a bounded field.
- Combined-result shifts by 34, 36, or 44 occur at 64-bit width before narrowing.
- Consecutive word-width shifts discard bits at each intermediate step; unlimited-width arithmetic is not equivalent.
- Values explicitly initialized to zero remain zero through ordinary bit-vector shifts and masks, without making their surrounding calls removable.

Near the end of the call stage, a word result is shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, and logically shifted right by six. Under the conditional bit-vector interpretation, this **small quantity** ranges from 0 through 508 in increments of four. Its doubled value, the **small doubled base**, ranges from 0 through 1016 in increments of eight.

### Memory layout and aliasing

Observed byte-relative offsets include 1, 4, 5, 8, 9, 0x0D, 0x10, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19. Word-relative offsets include 4, 0x10, 0x14, 0x20, 0x24, 0x34, 0x40, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. Halfword destinations are formed by multiplying loaded values by two or eight.

These offsets do not establish a coherent record type. Bases originate from call results, scaling, loaded words, or single-byte values. Different apparent fields can alias.

Byte offset 0x15 overlaps a word at offset 0x14 when their bases coincide. Byte and word accesses at offset 0x10 also overlap. Width and order are established; numeric reconstruction requires byte order.

### Initial mutation and traversal

The last combined call result supplies the first direct-memory destinations. A word at its high word plus 0x14 receives a retained transformed value. Another retained base’s byte at offset 9 is written twice, first using the low byte of that high result word and later using the low byte of the retained transformed value. Between those byte writes, a word at 256 times the final low result word plus 0x54 receives an earlier scaled result.

The earlier scaled base’s word at offset 0x14 is cleared before its byte at offset 5 is read. The small doubled base’s word at offset 0x14 is cleared before that captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at offset 0x34 of the earlier scaled base. The earlier cleared word is then read back; another clear of the small doubled base follows. Two identical byte stores at its offset 0x15 consume the read-back value’s low byte.

Under ordinary, nonaliasing, noninterfering RAM assumptions, that read-back is zero. A subsequent word store through the captured value plus 0x54 therefore targets fixed address 0x54 under those assumptions. This consequence does not justify introducing null avoidance.

Next, fixed address 0x05 is read as a byte and zero-extended for use as a base. Fixed address 0x01 receives the low byte of the small doubled base. Computed byte writes and a word write through the earlier read-back value occur before a word at offset 0x54 of the byte-derived base is captured and overwritten.

The captured traversal word drives later scaling by four and eight. Traversals through offset 0x74 supply further bases. One nested update separately represents two reads of the same offset-0x74 location, one supplying a destination and one supplying stored data. Their relative evaluation order is not resolved; equality of their results is not guaranteed.

Further updates include repeated self-valued stores at offset 0x24, intervening byte stores at offset 9, successive word stores at offset 0x14 of the small doubled base, and a fixed-address word store at 0x54.

A later pair of identical byte stores at offset 0x15 of the small doubled base uses the low byte of four times the captured traversal word. **After this later pair**, fixed address **0x15 receives an 8-bit store containing the low byte of the small doubled base**. Only afterward does the following word store at offset 0x54 of the four-times-scaled traversal base occur. The fixed-address write is an independent mutation and may affect subsequent computed accesses through aliasing.

Remaining traversal operations use offsets 4, 5, 0x44, 0x64, and 0x74. In particular, a zero-extended byte read at offset 5 becomes a base for an offset-0x74 word read. Later byte reads of an apparently repeated location are fresh reads, not guaranteed copies of earlier values.

### Constant stores and repeated region

For this region:

- The **adjusted retained value** is four times the captured traversal word, reduced by 0x96.
- The **derived base** is four times that adjusted retained value.
- The **linked base** is twice the word obtained from the later byte-indexed offset-0x74 traversal.
- The **scaled link value** is four times that same loaded word.

These are descriptive value roles, not asserted object types.

The value 0x3A is stored through three access shapes: an 8-bit store at the scaled link value, a 16-bit store at twice that address-related value, and a 32-bit store at its offset 0x20.

At the derived base’s offset 0x20, successive word stores contain 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte freshly read at the derived base’s offset 4, and 0x20 again.

**The separate global word is captured next, before the 32-bit store of 0x20 to fixed address 0x60.** The captured global value supplies the repeated region’s base even if the fixed-address store subsequently aliases or otherwise changes the global’s storage. No reread after the 0x60 store is represented.

The repeated region uses four-store groups: a word store at a scaled base’s offset 0x54, a byte store at captured-global-base plus 0x19, a byte store at derived-base plus 0x11, and a word store at captured-global-base plus 0x34.

The byte values are respectively the low byte of the derived base and the low byte of the captured global value. The offset-0x54 stores contain the scaled link value.

The representation contains many explicit repetitions, not a represented loop. This contract preserves their interaction character but does not provide a counted trace of the entire region. Repeated identical stores are not automatically removable.

### Final captures and fixed-address mutation

The final region begins with a word capture from fixed address **0xA9**, followed by a word capture at **scaled link value plus 0x74**.

**Before the next two word captures**, fixed address **0x3A receives a byte derived from eight times the adjusted retained value plus 0x30**. Under ordinary low-byte truncation, the stored byte is that arithmetic result modulo 256.

Only after the 0x3A store are the words at **linked-base plus 0x14** and **derived-base plus 4** captured, in that order. Consequently, the earlier pair of captures precedes this fixed-address mutation, while the latter pair can observe its effects if their storage aliases or mapped-memory behavior couples the accesses.

Subsequent writes place the low byte of the derived base at linked-base plus 0x0D; 0x6C at derived-base plus 8; and the low byte of the captured global value at derived-base plus 0x0D and plus 9. A word at linked-base plus 0x54 then receives the linked base.

### Fresh reads of fixed address 0x67

Fixed address **0x67 first receives the low byte of the captured 0xA9 word**. A computed word read at **linked-base plus 0x44 occurs next**. Only afterward is **0x67 freshly read as a byte to supply the write to 0x21**.

The value written to 0x21 is therefore the value observed by that byte read, not unconditionally the originally captured low byte.

The computed word read supplies a byte at derived-base plus 0x10, a word at captured-global-base plus 0x54, and the source of a variable-count right shift whose low byte is written at linked-base plus 1. The global-derived offset-0x54 word is then overwritten with the derived base. A word read at derived-base plus 0x40 supplies a word store at derived-base plus 0x10. Between that read and store, captured-global-base plus 0x74 receives the earlier captured derived-base-plus-4 word. Captured-global-base plus 0x44 then receives the linked base.

**A new byte read of 0x67 supplies a write at derived-base plus 9.** Afterward, linked-base plus 9 receives the low byte of the derived base, derived-base plus 0x44 receives the captured 0xA9 word, and a word at derived-base plus 0x54 is captured as another base.

**Another fresh byte read of 0x67 then supplies a second write at derived-base plus 9.** These reads are separated by memory operations and need not return equal values. Neither may be replaced unconditionally by the original captured byte or by the earlier read used for 0x21.

### Completion and terminal invocation

The low byte of the earlier scaled-link-plus-0x74 capture is stored at the newly loaded base’s offset 0x0D. The earlier linked-base-plus-0x14 capture is restored through linked-base plus 0x54.

A fresh word read at derived-base plus 0x14 later supplies a byte at the newly loaded base’s offset 0x11. Intervening mutations include a byte at linked-base plus 0x11, and byte and word stores at derived-base plus 9 and plus 0x44 using the newly loaded base.

A word read at linked-base plus 4 supplies the address-related value for a **16-bit store of 0x65 at twice that value**. Its offset 0x64 word is then captured. The newly loaded base’s offset 0x54 receives a **32-bit decimal 100** before another word is captured from the former value’s offset 0x74.

2. The offset-0x64 word used in the final doubled-address store.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static requirements, not executed tests.

### Combined-result width

Given high word 0x12345678 and low word 0x00ABCDEF, a consumer represented as receiving the shifted low word and unchanged high word receives 0xABCDEF00 and 0x12345678 under 32-bit bit-vector semantics.

### Narrowing and small bases

A word shifted left by 28 and then left by 28 becomes zero under that interpretation. The late small doubled base is an eight-byte multiple no greater than 1016; this range is not evidence of a validated pointer.

### Fixed-address traversal write

If the small doubled base is 0x108, the later fixed-address byte store to 0x15 contains 0x08. Its position is after the later two identical computed offset-0x15 stores and before the following scaled-base offset-0x54 word store. Keeping only a layout inventory does not preserve this behavior.

### Global capture before 0x60

If the separate global read returns a particular base, subsequent repeated groups use that captured base. Even if the following word store at 0x60 changes the global’s storage, substituting a post-store reread is not equivalent.

### Interposed write to 0x3A

If the adjusted retained value is 0x21, ordinary low-byte arithmetic gives 0x38 for the byte written at 0x3A. The 0xA9 and scaled-link-plus-0x74 captures precede this write; the linked-base-plus-0x14 and derived-base-plus-4 captures follow it.

### Fresh 0x67 observations

Suppose the initial write to 0x67 contains 0xAB, but the intervening computed word read has mapped-memory effects causing the subsequent 0x67 read to return 0xCD. The write to 0x21 contains 0xCD. Later computed writes consume their own fresh 0x67 observations, not necessarily either earlier byte.

### Overlap and repeated events

With coincident bases, byte offset 0x15 overlaps word offset 0x14. Two identical represented byte stores remain two interactions when writes are observable. Matching only final RAM contents is insufficient for that interaction interpretation.

### Partial execution and terminal arguments

A nonreturning dependency establishes no later direct-memory accesses and no rollback. If execution reaches the terminal invocation, its first represented argument is 0x72; normal return and application-level meaning remain unresolved.
