## Behavior

### Ordered dependency phase

Before the first explicit data-memory store, the operation performs an extensive straight-line sequence of indirect calls interleaved with integer transformations.

The visible call sites supply between zero and four explicit values. Some results are consumed as single words; others are separated into low and high words. Additional values are taken from secondary result state even when the corresponding call is not represented as returning a pair.

An empty visible argument list does not establish that a dependency has no implicit inputs. Likewise, an unused primary result does not establish that a call has no effects.

The sequence repeatedly:

- Shifts a primary word left by eight bits before passing it onward.
- Passes a high result word unchanged alongside transformed low-word values.
- Retains selected earlier results across intervening calls.
- Supplies the same transformed value in multiple argument positions.
- Supplies explicit zeros in selected argument positions.
- Extracts bounded unsigned fields using masks and logical right shifts.

These are ordered interactions, not interchangeable pure computations. Unknown dependencies may change global state, access memory, or perform externally observable work. The representation provides no basis for coalescing repeated calls or assuming that repeated-looking destinations identify the same function.

### Bit-field and arithmetic requirements

The visible transformations include left shifts from one through 31 bits and unsigned right shifts by several fixed counts. Significant masks select low fields of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits.

The following distinctions are behaviorally material:

- Selecting the low 20 bits, shifting right by ten, and then left by six produces a ten-bit field positioned at bits 6–15.
- Selecting the low 24 bits and shifting left by six produces at most 30 significant bits; upper input bits do not contribute.
- Selecting the low 22 bits and shifting left by eight differs from shifting the same field left by seven.
- Unsigned right shifts do not propagate a sign bit.
- Extracting a 64-bit result above bit 32 produces different fields at shifts of 34, 36, and 44: respectively, the high word shifted right by two, four, and twelve bits.

Several retained values are explicitly initialized to zero and subsequently shifted. Those particular arithmetic results remain zero under the 32-bit interpretation. This does not imply that intervening calls can be removed.

Sequential left shifts must preserve intermediate word-width truncation. For example, a retained word shifted left by 28 bits and later again by 28 bits becomes zero under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation; it is not a surviving 56-bit quantity.

### Transition into direct memory mutation

The final calls in the dependency phase supply values used as calculated memory bases and stored data. For this section, the following descriptive names denote values, not validated objects:

- **shifted base:** a retained dependency word shifted left by 25 bits.
- **doubled selector:** twice a selector extracted from a dependency word.
- **selector:** an unsigned value formed by shifting a word left by eight, selecting its low 15 bits, and shifting right by six.
- **mutation base:** a subsequently derived address described below.

Under a 32-bit interpretation, the selector lies between 0 and 508 and is a multiple of four. The doubled selector therefore lies between 0 and 1016. These small ranges do not establish that the resulting addresses are mapped or safe.

The initial direct-memory effects have a significant order:

1. A word derived from an earlier result is written at offset 0x14 from the high word of the final paired result.
2. A byte is written at offset 9 from a retained shifted value.
3. A word is written at offset 0x54 from a base formed by multiplying the final low result word by 256.
4. The byte at the earlier offset-9 destination is overwritten with another value.
5. A word at shifted-base offset 0x14 is cleared.
6. A byte at shifted-base offset 5 is read.
7. A word at doubled-selector offset 0x14 is cleared.
8. The saved byte is zero-extended and written at shifted-base offset 0x34.

These accesses may alias. In particular, the later word read must not be replaced unconditionally with zero merely because an earlier store cleared its apparent destination: intervening writes can affect the same storage.

## Acceptance examples

### Paired-result forwarding

### Masked field extraction

If a retained unsigned word is 0xFFFFFFFF, selecting its low 20 bits, shifting right by ten, and then left by six produces 0xFFC0. Bits above bit 19 must not affect that value.

### Selector range

If the dependency word used for selector extraction has its low seven bits all set, the selector is 508 and the doubled selector is 1016. Higher input bits do not contribute under the stated 32-bit transformation.

### Byte extension

If a relevant offset-5 byte read yields 0xE1, its subsequent word-valued copy is 225, not a sign-extended negative value.

### Aliasing-sensitive reload

If an intervening store overlaps a previously cleared word, a later read must observe the resulting memory contents. Accepting an unconditional zero in place of the reload is invalid.

### Repeated destination writes

If mutation-base offset 4 supplies byte 0xAB, the offset-0x20 word-write sequence is 0x20, 0x2F, 0xAB, 0x20, provided all accesses succeed and no aliasing changes that byte first. Observing only the final 0x20 does not prove trace equivalence.

### Invalid destination

If a calculated word destination is inaccessible, successful completion is not required. No error return or rollback is established; prior effects may remain.

### Terminal argument ordering

If execution reaches the final transfer, 0x72 is the first visible argument and the two saved words remain in their represented third and fourth positions. No return-value interpretation is assigned.
