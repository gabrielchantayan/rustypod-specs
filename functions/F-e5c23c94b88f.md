### Arithmetic consequences

Under the modulo-32-bit interpretation, Z is always zero: shifting R left by 26 and then multiplying by \(2^{10}\) discards every original bit. Thus the accesses through Z+1 reduce to address 1, and the later Z-shifted argument is zero.

These simplifications do not remove intervening calls or repeated memory reads. In particular, separate reads from address 1 can observe different bytes.

Multiplication by \(2^{28}\) retains only the input’s low four bits in the resulting address contribution. Multiplication by \(2^{26}\) retains only its low six bits. Neither operation represents an unbounded integer calculation.
