# F-a1644764d35e

## Purpose

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation declares four 32-bit arguments. Its behavior depends on additional execution state, dependency results, and existing memory contents. Direct effects include 8-bit, 16-bit, and 32-bit stores, repeated overwrites, dependent address loads, and accesses to small absolute addresses.

## Behavior

### Phases and ordering rules

The visible operation has three ordered phases:

1. Indirect dependency calls with intervening arithmetic.
2. Direct-memory accesses and repeated writes.
3. A final indirect transfer with four explicit operands.

Later phases are reached only if preceding operations complete. No explicit loop or conditional branch is visible in the supplied body; this does not constrain dependency-internal control flow.

The order described below is the order represented by the supplied evidence. It is not an independently established hardware memory-ordering guarantee.

Captured values remain distinct from later memory reads. No non-aliasing guarantee exists between computed destinations, absolute locations, or the separate global word. A store can therefore affect subsequent reads even when their descriptions use different address families.

### Widths and arithmetic

Direct accesses have widths of 8, 16, or 32 bits. Narrow stores retain the corresponding low-order bits. Byte loads widened for arithmetic or word stores are zero-extended.

Address calculations and most arithmetic intermediates are represented with 32-bit widths. Some calculations use signed intermediates. Consequently:

- Modulo-2³² arithmetic is a conditional machine-bit interpretation, not an established portable overflow contract.
- Signed overflow and signed left-shift behavior remain **documented_only**.
- Unsigned right shifts select high-order fields without sign extension.
- Intermediate truncation cannot be ignored when combining shifts.
- Logical shifts of 64-bit results by 36 or 44 select their high 32-bit portions shifted right by four or twelve, respectively.

Visible masks select low fields of 24, 22, 19, and 17 bits, among others. Masking with 0xFFFFF and then shifting right by ten selects bits 10–19. Masking with 0x3FFFF and shifting right by eleven selects bits 11–17.

A tail operation right-shifts a signed 32-bit loaded word by a count obtained from the low eight bits of a computed address. Negative-input behavior and counts of at least 32 are unresolved.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Initial operand:** argument 4 equal to 1 supplies 0x80 as the initial call’s second explicit operand; argument 1 is unchanged.

2. **Small-base boundary:** under bit-vector arithmetic, a contributing result whose low seven bits are 0x7F produces Small base 1016. Low seven bits equal to zero produce Small base zero.

3. **Global capture:** if the absolute 0x60 store changes the backing storage of the separate global word, repeated-region accesses still use the value captured before that store. Reloading afterward is not equivalent.

4. **Constant overwrite trace:** completion of the Working-base-plus-0x20 region includes stores of 0x20, 0x2F, a widened freshly read byte, and 0x20, in that order.

5. **Tail operand timing:** if the byte store at 0x3A overlaps the storage later read at Tail base plus 0x14, the final third operand reflects that later word read, not a hypothetical pre-store capture.

6. **Saved absolute word:** if the word read at 0xA9 is 0x123456AB, its explicit later byte store to 0x67 writes 0xAB. This does not constrain later reads of 0x67 to return 0xAB.

7. **Independent byte copies:** if the three later reads of 0x67 yield 0xAB, 0xCD, and 0xEF, the copy to 0x21 receives 0xAB, and the two computed copies receive 0xCD and 0xEF at their respective positions. Later overwrites can change their final contents.

8. **Failure:** if the 32-bit read at 0xA9 fails, no successful final-transfer behavior or rollback is established. Earlier effects have already been reached.
