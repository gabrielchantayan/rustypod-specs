# F-432c35dc410a

## Purpose

F-432c35dc410a performs an extended sequence of indirect dependency invocations, followed by ordered memory mutations and a final unresolved indirect transfer.

The supplied static body establishes access widths, arithmetic relationships, capture points, and ordering constraints. It does not establish an application-level purpose. Allocation, formatting, serialization, object construction, and collection management are not supported interpretations.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** behavior explicitly represented in the supplied body.
- **Inferred intent:** possible interpretations that create no acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved details, or details explicitly excluded from an exact executable acceptance oracle.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### 1. Widths and arithmetic

A byte is 8 bits, a halfword 16 bits, and a word 32 bits. Unsigned byte captures propagated into words are zero-extended. Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Halfword and word stores affect two and four bytes respectively.

Arithmetic examples below use a conditional 32-bit bit-pattern interpretation: shifts, scaling, addition, and subtraction retain the low 32 bits. Discarded bits do not reappear in subsequent right shifts. This interpretation does not establish the executable’s treatment of signed overflow or signed left shifts.

Splitting a 64-bit result into low and high words selects bits 0–31 and 32–63 numerically; it does not establish memory byte order. Extractions beyond bit 31 on a 64-bit result must not be treated as oversized 32-bit shifts.

The prefix uses masks selecting several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 15, 14, and 13 bits. These selections do not imply saturation or sign extension.

One late byte payload uses a represented signed right shift. Its count is the low eight bits of the publication base. Negative-operand behavior and counts of 32 or greater remain **documented_only**.

### 2. Dependency-driven prefix

Before the first explicit data-memory store, a long straight-line sequence of indirect calls is represented. There is no explicit conditional branch or loop.

The first destination is derived from incoming control-link state. Subsequent destinations are derived from embedded location values. Calls include no represented explicit arguments, literal zeros, shifted earlier results, masked selections, both words of 64-bit results, and incompletely specified secondary result channels.

The third argument initially contributes its six-bit-left-shifted word. A later argument retains the low 20 bits of that word, shifts the selection right by ten, then left by six. Under the stated bit-pattern interpretation, original input bits 4–13 become output bits 6–15.

Later transformations depend on newly obtained results, not solely on that input. Several explicitly zero-initialized quantities remain zero through shifts, but calls receiving them remain observable.

Calls must not be removed because their primary results are unused, merged because their argument shapes resemble one another, or assumed harmless because arguments are zero. Unknown dependencies may mutate globals, alter or invalidate storage, fail, or not return.

The complete prefix invocation trace is not enumerated here. Exact call-by-call acceptance, resolved destinations, and complete calling conventions are **documented_only**; this does not authorize changing the represented order.

### 3. Entry into explicit mutation

The prefix retains several address-like numerical values:

- A result shifted left by 25 bits.
- Another result scaled by 0x02000000.
- A result shifted left by eight bits.
- A bounded quantity formed by shifting a result left by eight, retaining 15 bits, and shifting right by six.
- The low and high words of a final 64-bit result.

Under 32-bit bit-pattern arithmetic, the bounded quantity is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Twice that quantity ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

The opening mutations occur in this order: the final high word’s offset-0x14 word receives the retained 25-bit-shifted value; that retained value’s offset-9 byte receives the high word’s low byte; the final low word, scaled by 256, supplies an offset-0x54 word destination for the other retained scaled result; and the earlier offset-9 byte receives the retained value’s own low byte.

Each store is distinct even if addresses alias.

### 4. Reset, capture, and cross-link region

The base formed by scaling a result by 0x02000000 has its offset-0x14 word cleared before its offset-5 byte is captured. The doubled bounded base’s offset-0x14 word is then cleared. The captured byte is published as a zero-extended word at the scaled base’s offset 0x34.

The scaled base’s cleared word is read back after these intervening stores. It cannot unconditionally be replaced with zero because overlapping writes may have changed it. The doubled bounded base is cleared again, followed by two distinct offset-0x15 byte stores of the readback’s low byte.

An unsigned byte captured at absolute address 0x05 becomes another numerical base. An absolute-address 0x01 byte write and several derived writes precede capture of the word at that byte-derived base’s offset 0x54. That word is captured before being replaced.

Call this captured value the **link word**. Its two-, four-, and eight-times-scaled forms drive subsequent accesses.

The following region includes repeated offset-0x24 self-valued word stores, writes through words read at offset 0x74, repeated offset-9 and offset-0x15 byte stores, and writes at absolute addresses 0x54 and 0x15. These are not implicitly coalesced.

Important capture relationships include:

- An offset-5 byte becomes the unsigned base for an offset-0x74 word read.
- A word loaded at four-times-link-word offset 0x64 supplies a base for an offset-0x0D byte store and an offset-0x14 self-valued word store.
- A word captured at four-times-link-word offset 4 supplies subsequent offset-5 byte-read bases.
- Those offset-5 byte reads are separate observations with mutations between them.

The later byte-to-offset-0x74 chain produces the **selected word**. Twice and four times this captured word remain important throughout the final region.

These access relationships do not establish object types, record boundaries, list invariants, or ownership.

### 5. Constants and repeated publication

The selected word determines three ordered constant stores: byte 0x3A at four times the selected word, halfword 0x003A at eight times the selected word, and word 0x0000003A at four-times-selected-word offset 0x20.

Define:

- **Adjusted quantity:** four times the link word minus 0x96.
- **Publication base:** four times the adjusted quantity.
- **Captured global base:** the unnamed global word captured at the point specified below.

These terms denote arithmetic values, not validated pointers.

At publication-base offset 0x20, successive word payloads are 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte freshly read from publication-base offset 4, and finally 0x20. The byte capture occurs after the first two stores and before the last two.

The unnamed global word is then captured, **before** absolute-address word 0x60 receives 0x20. Later global-derived accesses use the captured value, not a reread after that absolute store. This matters if the global’s storage overlaps address 0x60.

Repeated publication uses four times the selected word as its word payload. The first group writes that payload at eight-times-link-word offset 0x54, then writes the publication base’s low byte at captured-global-base offset 0x19, the captured global base’s low byte at publication-base offset 0x11, and eight times the link word at captured-global-base offset 0x34.

Subsequent groups preserve that four-event order and the byte destinations and payloads. They instead use eight times the adjusted quantity for the offset-0x54 destination base and the offset-0x34 word payload.

The first group therefore differs in both scaled destination and final word payload. The repetition uses fixed captured expressions, not established traversal over changing records. Exact multiplicity is excluded from this contract’s executable oracle and remains **documented_only**. Repeated events are nevertheless not licensed for collapse.

### 6. Final captures, restoration, and terminal linkage

For this section, the **doubled-selected base** is twice the selected word. It is distinct from four times the selected word, the publication base, and the captured global base.

Immediately after publication, the function captures the absolute-address 0xA9 word and then the word at four-times-selected-word offset 0x74. A byte is next written at absolute address 0x3A. Only after that store are the doubled-selected-base offset-0x14 word and publication-base offset-4 word captured.

The byte at 0x3A receives the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30. Either later word capture can observe this mutation if its bytes overlap 0x3A; the preceding captures cannot retroactively change.

Subsequent stores propagate the publication and global bases’ low bytes, write byte 0x6C at publication-base offset 8, and write the doubled-selected base itself into its offset-0x54 word.

Absolute address 0x67 receives the low byte of the captured 0xA9 word. A word is then captured at doubled-selected-base offset 0x44 before the byte at 0x67 is read and copied to absolute address 0x21.

That offset-0x44 word supplies an offset-0x10 publication-base byte payload and an offset-0x54 captured-global-base word payload. Its signed-right-shift-derived low byte is written at doubled-selected-base offset 1. The captured-global-base offset-0x54 word is subsequently replaced with the publication base.

A fresh word read at publication-base offset 0x40 precedes copying the earlier offset-4 capture to captured-global-base offset 0x74 and writing the fresh word at publication-base offset 0x10. The earlier byte store and this later word store at offset 0x10 are distinct.

Further mutations include captured-global-base offset 0x44 receiving the doubled-selected base, fresh reads of byte 0x67 for publication-base offset-9 writes, and publication-base offset 0x44 receiving the earlier absolute-address 0xA9 capture.

A word read at publication-base offset 0x54 establishes the **late write base**. After another fresh byte-0x67 copy to publication-base offset 9, the low byte of the earlier four-times-selected-word offset-0x74 capture is written at late-write-base offset 0x0D.

**Restoration destination:** the previously captured doubled-selected-base offset-0x14 word is then stored at **doubled-selected-base offset 0x54**. The destination is not late-write-base offset 0x54.

Immediately after that restoration, a **fresh publication capture** reads the word at publication-base offset 0x14. This read is neither a reuse of the earlier doubled-selected-base offset-0x14 capture nor a reread deferred until its byte is consumed.

Before the next loaded base is obtained, doubled-selected-base offset 0x11 receives the earlier offset-0x74 capture’s low byte; publication-base offset 9 receives the late write base’s low byte; and publication-base offset 0x44 receives the late write base as a word.

A word read at doubled-selected-base offset 4 now establishes the **late loaded base**. A halfword 0x0065 is stored at twice that base. The word at late-loaded-base offset 0x64 is then captured as the **earlier linkage result**.

Only after both the halfword store and that offset-0x64 capture does late-write-base offset 0x11 receive the fresh publication capture’s low byte. This byte store precedes the decimal-100 word store at late-write-base offset 0x54. Consequently, neither the fresh publication capture nor its byte consumption may be moved across potentially overlapping intervening writes.

The late-loaded-base offset-0x74 word is then captured for the terminal transfer. A fresh word read at doubled-selected-base offset 0x64 establishes the **final destination base**. Doubled-selected-base offset 9 next receives the late loaded base’s low byte.

The final two word stores have this explicit order:

- Final-destination-base offset 0x54 receives the earlier linkage result.
- The address equal to twice the earlier linkage result receives the final destination base.

The destination and payload values remain their captured values even if the first store overlaps storage from which either was obtained. If the two destinations overlap, their final shared bytes reflect this order according to the environment’s byte order.

The terminal arguments are 0x72, the earlier linkage result, the preserved doubled-selected-base offset-0x14 capture, and the late-loaded-base offset-0x74 capture. No cleanup or rollback intervenes.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Input selection.** With third-input bit pattern 0x00000010, the initial six-bit shift yields 0x00000400. The later low-20-bit selection, right shift by ten, and left shift by six yield 0x00000040.

2. **Discarded input bits.** Under 32-bit bit-pattern arithmetic, input 0x04000000 becomes zero after the initial shift. Calls consuming that zero remain observable.

3. **Unsigned byte propagation.** A captured byte 0xE2 published as a word becomes 0x000000E2, not a sign-extended value.

4. **Global capture.** If the global aliases word 0x60 and contains 0x00000180 when captured, subsequent global-derived accesses use 0x00000180 despite the following absolute-address store of 0x20.

5. **Interleaved 0x3A write.** If either later capture of doubled-selected-base offset 0x14 or publication-base offset 4 overlaps 0x3A, it observes the byte store according to environmental memory semantics. The preceding two captures remain unchanged.

6. **Restoration destination.** If the doubled-selected base is 0x200 and the late write base is 0x500, restoration writes the preserved word at 0x254, not 0x554. Later decimal-100 publication targets 0x554.

7. **Fresh capture lifetime.** If publication-base offset 0x14 contains 0x112233A7 at the fresh capture, a subsequent overlapping halfword store may change its storage but not the captured low-byte payload 0xA7. That byte is stored at late-write-base offset 0x11 after the late-loaded-base offset-0x64 read and before decimal 100 is published.

8. **Final linkage order.** If the earlier linkage result is 0x100 and the final destination base is 0x1AC, both final word destinations equal 0x200. They receive 0x100 first and 0x1AC second. Ordinary writable memory therefore ends with word 0x1AC there.

9. **Width distinction.** The 0x0065 halfword store affects two bytes; replacing it with a byte store does not satisfy the access-width requirement.

10. **Interrupted execution.** If a prefix dependency does not return, later explicit stores and the terminal transfer are not guaranteed. No rollback is promised.
