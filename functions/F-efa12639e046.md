# F-efa12639e046

## Purpose

F-efa12639e046 consumes two explicit 32-bit inputs, executes an extended sequence of indirect dependency calls and width-sensitive transformations, and then performs ordered byte, halfword, and word memory accesses before a final unresolved indirect transfer.

No domain-level purpose is established. Character-valued constants do not establish text output; scaled addresses and self-referential stores do not establish a container or memory-management protocol.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented operations, widths, dependencies, and access ordering.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations requiring an execution-model assumption.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior that cannot support an unconditional executable requirement.

Execution reaching a later phase is conditional on all preceding calls returning and all preceding accesses completing.

In this document:

## Behavior

### Dependency prefix

The initial indirect dependency receives three represented arguments: the first input, the second input, and the second input shifted left by six. Its target depends on incoming execution state.

The subsequent prefix is a long, straight-line sequence of indirect calls. No local conditional branch or runtime loop governs that represented sequence. Dependencies may nevertheless branch, fail, diverge, or alter shared state.

Calls include:

- No explicitly represented arguments.
- One to four explicitly represented words.
- Primary word results shifted before forwarding.
- Represented 64-bit results split into low and high words.
- Secondary machine-result state consumed independently of a conventional return declaration.
- Explicit zeros, repeated arguments, and values saved across intervening calls.

Calls without explicit arguments are not proven independent of machine state. Calls whose explicit results are unused are not proven side-effect-free. Similar argument patterns do not establish common target identity.

Call order must be retained. No preservation contract permits assuming that an intervening dependency leaves globals, memory, implicit state, or memory ownership unchanged.

### Field transformations

The prefix uses word-width left shifts through 31 bits, unsigned right shifts, masks, and scaled values. Masks select fields including low 24-, 22-, 21-, 20-, 19-, 18-, 17-, 16-, 15-, 14-, 13-, 12-, and 11-bit portions. Some extractions operate on high portions of represented 64-bit results.

Intermediate width boundaries matter. Two successive word-width shifts cannot generally be replaced by unbounded multiplication because the first operation may discard bits.

Masking a word to 20 bits and shifting unsigned right by ten selects bits 10–19, producing 0–1023. Shifting that field left by six produces multiples of 64 from 0 through 65472.

A late variable right shift has a signed-looking operand and a count taken from the low byte of the repeated base. Its sign behavior and treatment of counts of 32 or more remain **documented_only**. No five-bit count mask is established.

### Initial direct mutations

The last represented two-word dependency result supplies a high word and a low word for the first direct mutations.

The opening access sequence places the shifted base as a word at high-word-plus-0x14, writes the high word’s low byte at shifted-base-plus-9, and places the large base as a word at 256-times-low-word-plus-0x54. It then overwrites shifted-base-plus-9 with the shifted base’s low byte.

The large-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared before a byte is read at large-base-plus-5. The small-base word at offset 0x14 is then cleared, and the byte just read is zero-extended into a word at large-base-plus-0x34.

A word is subsequently read back from large-base-plus-0x14. The small-base zero store is repeated, followed by two distinct byte stores at small-base-plus-0x15, both receiving the read-back word’s low byte.

The read-back is not unconditionally zero. Intervening accesses may alias it, and mapped-memory or external effects are unresolved. Repeated equal-valued writes remain distinct represented access events.

### Early fixed-address accesses and linked updates

After those two byte stores, a byte is read from fixed address **5** and zero-extended into a computed base. This read precedes the byte write to fixed address **1**, whose value is the small base’s low byte.

The captured base from address 5 is then used for a byte store at offset 0x15 containing the extracted field’s low byte. The fixed-address-1 write does not retroactively change the captured base, even if storage aliases.

The large-base byte at offset 0x11 receives the saved payload’s low byte. A word store through the earlier read-back word at offset 0x54 receives the saved payload; afterward, large-base-plus-0x11 is cleared.

The word at captured-base-plus-0x54 is read as the **saved link**, before that location is replaced with the earlier read-back word. Small-base-plus-0x34 receives the extracted field as a word.

Further linked updates use words read at offsets 0x74 and 0x64 as new bases. They include repeated self-referential word stores at offset 0x24 and repeated low-byte stores at offset 9. One expression represents two reads from small-base-plus-0x74; their separate occurrence is visible, but their evaluation order relative to each other is not independently established.

The next meaningful fixed-address effects occur within these computed updates:

- After small-base-plus-0x14 receives a dependency-derived value shifted left by 26, a **32-bit store at address 0x54** receives the saved link shifted left by one.
- Small-base-plus-0x14 then receives the saved link twice as separate word stores.
- Two byte stores at small-base-plus-0x15 receive the low byte of four times the saved link.
- A **byte store at address 0x15** then receives the small base’s low byte.

These fixed and computed writes are interleaved, not separate transactions. Unknown aliasing affects later values, not the represented ordering.

Subsequent computed accesses use four and eight times the saved link, doubled loaded words, and byte-derived lookups. They copy and overwrite words at offsets 0x14, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54; write low bytes at offsets 9, 0x0d, and 0x15; and revisit byte-at-offset-5 lookups after intervening writes. Such revisits cannot be replaced by unconditional reuse of an earlier byte.

### Constants and preparation for repetition

Using the lookup word defined above, the routine stores numeric 0x3a:

- As a byte at four times the lookup word.
- As a halfword at eight times the lookup word.
- As a word at four-times-lookup-word-plus-0x20.

The adjusted value is four times the saved link minus 0x96; the repeated base is four times that adjusted value.

The word-store trace at repeated-base-plus-0x20 contains, in order, 0x20, 0x2f, a zero-extended byte read from repeated-base-plus-4, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the 0x2f store and before its own word store.

Next, the **global base is read as a word from address 0x087fbbbc before the 32-bit store of 0x20 at address 0x60**. If that store affects overlapping storage, the already captured global base is still the value used by the following repeated region.

The numerical constants do not establish strings, encoding, termination, or output-device behavior.

### Repeated-update contract

Both the destination and the final word source differ between the first group and subsequent groups:

| Group | Leading word destination | Leading word value | Final word value at global-base-plus-0x34 |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Eight-times-saved-link-plus-0x54 | Four times the lookup word | Eight times the saved link |
| Subsequent | Eight-times-adjusted-value-plus-0x54 | Four times the lookup word | Eight times the adjusted value |

Within each group, the leading word store precedes a byte store at global-base-plus-0x19 containing the repeated base’s low byte. A byte store at repeated-base-plus-0x11 then receives the captured global base’s low byte, followed by the final word store at global-base-plus-0x34.

The global base and byte values are captured for this region; the representation does not reload that global word for every group.

The first and subsequent destinations are not assumed disjoint. Nor are any group’s four destinations assumed disjoint from one another. An aliased later store may overwrite an earlier store in the same group.

The region contains explicit static repetitions, not an established runtime retry policy. Repeated groups must not be collapsed when access traces or mapped-memory effects are observable.

### Post-repetition fixed accesses

After the repeated region, the routine reads a **word at fixed address 0xa9**, then reads a word at four-times-lookup-word-plus-0x74. Only afterward does it write a **byte at fixed address 0x3a**.

The byte at 0x3a is derived from the adjusted value, not from the word at 0xa9. Under low-byte truncation, it is:

**the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted value’s low byte, plus 0x30.**

This is arithmetic, not a textual-formatting guarantee. It need not produce an ASCII digit.

The word read from 0xa9 is retained across subsequent accesses. Its low byte is later written to **address 0x67**. Between the 0x3a write and that 0x67 write, the routine captures additional words at doubled-lookup-word-plus-0x14 and repeated-base-plus-4 and performs computed byte and word mutations.

After writing 0x67, the routine reads a word at doubled-lookup-word-plus-0x44. It then **reads the byte at 0x67 for a byte write to address 0x21**. This is a represented memory read, not merely an unconditional reuse of the saved low byte.

Two still-later byte stores at repeated-base-plus-9 each obtain their value from a **fresh read of address 0x67**. Computed writes intervene between the original 0x67 write and these reads, and between the later reads themselves. Those writes may alias 0x67. Consequently, the later reads need not return either the originally saved byte or the same value as each other.

### Final computed mutations and transfer

The final region also includes:

- A byte store of 0x6c at repeated-base-plus-8.
- Byte updates at offsets 9, 0x0d, 0x10, and 0x11.
- A byte at doubled-lookup-word-plus-1 obtained from the signed-looking variable right shift.
- A word read at repeated-base-plus-0x40, followed later by its word store at repeated-base-plus-0x10.
- Interleaved word updates at offsets 0x44, 0x54, and 0x74.

The word store beginning at offset 0x10 overlaps the earlier byte store at that offset and storage through offset 0x13.

A word loaded from doubled-lookup-word-plus-4 supplies another base. A halfword 0x65 is written at twice that loaded base. Additional words are read from its offsets 0x64 and 0x74. A separate computed destination receives decimal 100 as a word at offset 0x54.

Near the end, a word read from doubled-lookup-word-plus-0x64 selects another offset-0x54 destination. That destination receives the word previously loaded from the newer base’s offset 0x64. A final direct word store writes the selected destination base at twice that previously loaded word.

The final unresolved transfer receives four represented arguments: 0x72, the word loaded from the newer base’s offset 0x64, an earlier saved word from doubled-lookup-word-plus-0x14, and the most recently loaded word from the newer base’s offset 0x74.

A return is represented afterward. Whether this transfer is an ordinary returning call or another control-transfer form remains **documented_only**.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Entry forwarding.** With second input 3, the initial dependency receives that input unchanged and its shifted form 192. The first input remains unchanged.

2. **Word truncation.** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, second input 0x04000000 yields zero after the six-bit left shift. Saturation or rejection would add unsupported behavior.

3. **Field extraction.** A word of 0x000fffff, masked to 20 bits and shifted right by ten, yields 1023; shifting that result left by six yields 0xffc0.

4. **Fixed read before write.** If address 5 yields byte 0xfe, the captured base is 254. That read precedes the byte write to address 1; it is not a signed base of −2.

5. **Distinct first repeated group.** With saved link 0x40 and ordinary arithmetic, the adjusted value is 0x6a. The first leading destination is 0x254; subsequent leading destinations are 0x3a4. The corresponding word values written at global-base-plus-0x34 are 0x200 and 0x350. Neither destination is assumed disjoint from other storage.

6. **Arithmetic byte at 0x3a.** For that adjusted value of 0x6a, eight times its low byte plus 0x30 is 0x380; the stored byte is 0x80. A word read at 0xa9 occurs before this byte write.

7. **Fresh reads at 0x67.** If the original 0x67 write stores 0xab and nothing changes it before the copy, the byte write at 0x21 receives 0xab. If an intervening aliased computed write later changes 0x67 to 0xcd, a subsequent computed byte store reading 0x67 receives 0xcd, not necessarily 0xab.

8. **Intermediate write trace.** An observable trace at repeated-base-plus-0x20 includes 0x20, 0x2f, the loaded byte value, and 0x20 in that order. Checking only the final word does not prove equivalence.

9. **Failure and final transfer.** An unmapped destination has no promised clean error return or rollback. If execution reaches the final transfer, its first argument is 0x72, not a returned status.
