# F-3f5fa90d134c

## Purpose

F-3f5fa90d134c returns the difference between two independently scaled integer products. The positive contribution uses the first and fourth inputs. The subtracted contribution uses the second and third inputs.

Under the 32-bit word and 64-bit product interpretation supported by the supplied expression, each contribution retains product bits 16 through 47. Bits below and above that range do not contribute to the resulting word. Scaling is therefore performed separately for the two products, before their contributions are subtracted.

A fixed-point determinant or cross-product interpretation is plausible but **inferred intent**, not an established requirement. No units, coordinate system, fixed-point format, or application-specific input restrictions are supplied.

This contract separates three levels of confidence:

- **Established static evidence:** the argument pairing, signed widening before multiplication, arithmetic structure, return interface, and absence of explicit external operations.
- **Conditional numerical behavior:** the retained-bit and result interpretation under specified widths and machine-word semantics.
- **documented_only unknowns:** the target type definitions, treatment of certain signed operations and conversions, and actual runtime behavior.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Product selection

The positive product combines the first and fourth inputs. The subtracted product combines the second and third inputs.

Both operands of each product are widened with their signs preserved before multiplication. Under the stated widths, the behavior is based on full 64-bit signed products, not on 32-bit products that have already lost their upper bits.

A negative input consequently participates as a negative factor. It is not treated as a large positive unsigned input for multiplication.

### Contribution layout

Under the conditional machine-word interpretation, each product contributes the following 32-bit slice:

| Product bit positions | Contribution bit positions |
|---|---|
| 0–15 | Discarded |
| 16–31 | 0–15 |
| 32–47 | 16–31 |
| 48–63 | Discarded |

Bit zero denotes the least significant bit.

The selected word has no rounding increment. Discarding a nonzero low portion does not increase the retained portion. Discarding a nonzero high portion does not cause saturation, rejection, or an overflow indication.

An equivalent numerical description is to divide the signed product by 65,536, round downward, and retain the remainder modulo 4,294,967,296. This describes the contribution’s word value, not necessarily its eventual signed interpretation.

Consequences include:

- Positive products below 65,536 contribute zero.
- A product of 65,536 contributes one.
- A product of −1 contributes the all-ones word under the two’s-complement bit interpretation.
- A product of −65,537 contributes the word corresponding to −2.
- Products whose only nonzero bits lie above bit 47 contribute zero under the conditional bit-slice model.

For negative products, this word behavior differs from division rounded toward zero. Negative products with a nonzero discarded low portion have the downward-rounded contribution.

### Contribution difference and return value

The second/third contribution is subtracted from the first/fourth contribution.

Under the 32-bit type interpretation, both assembled contributions have unsigned type because their bitwise combinations include unsigned operands. The subtraction therefore occurs in an unsigned 32-bit domain. A negative mathematical difference is reduced modulo 4,294,967,296 rather than causing signed subtraction overflow.

This distinction is material: **the final subtraction is not itself a signed-overflow operation under these assumptions**.

Returning the unsigned difference through the signed result interface is a separate issue:

- A word from zero through 2,147,483,647 is representable directly as the corresponding nonnegative signed result.
- A larger word is not representable as a positive signed 32-bit value.
- Under a target conversion that preserves the 32-bit pattern and interprets it as two’s complement, such a word returns as its value minus 4,294,967,296.
- The supplied material does not establish that conversion rule for the actual target.

Thus modular subtraction is supported by the expression’s unsigned type relationships, while the interpretation of an out-of-range returned word remains target-dependent.

### Independent scaling

Each contribution loses its low 16 product bits independently. The function does not first subtract the full products and then scale their difference.

For example, products of 65,536 and 1 contribute one and zero, respectively. Their contribution difference is one. Subtracting the full products first would produce 65,535, whose scaled contribution would instead be zero.

Any replacement must preserve this distinction. Algebraic rearrangement across the separate scaling operations can change the returned value.

## Uncertainties

### Exact widths — documented_only

### Signed contribution formation — documented_only

Contribution formation includes conversion of an extracted upper product portion to a signed integer, followed by a signed left placement.

For a negative product under the 64-bit two’s-complement interpretation, the extracted upper portion can exceed the positive range of a signed 32-bit integer. Its conversion is therefore an additional target-dependent boundary.

If that conversion produces a negative value, the subsequent signed shift is not generally portable. Even with a nonnegative value, the left placement can exceed the signed representable range. Products with significant bits above the retained slice are therefore not automatically safe merely because those bits disappear in the desired word result.

The bit-slice model captures the conditional numerical behavior; it does not resolve these signed-operation issues.

### Return conversion — documented_only

The assembled contributions and their subtraction are unsigned under the stated widths. The remaining issue at the return boundary is conversion to the signed result type when the unsigned word exceeds 2,147,483,647.

Two’s-complement interpretation gives the negative results shown in the examples, but that conversion behavior is not independently established for the actual target.
