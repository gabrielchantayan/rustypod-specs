# F-6073849c14de

## Purpose

F-6073849c14de performs an unconditionally repeating sequence of indirect dependency calls, memory reads and writes, address calculations, and a software breakpoint. The supplied static representation contains no ordinary return, exit condition, or success result.

A higher-level purpose—such as initialization, decoding, or device control—is not established. This contract specifies the represented behavior without assigning such an interpretation.

A completed iteration is conditional on all reached calls returning, all memory accesses succeeding, and execution resuming after the breakpoint. Static evidence alone does not establish that these conditions can hold in an actual execution.

### Widths and arithmetic

The representation distinguishes:

- 8-bit byte accesses;
- 16-bit halfword accesses;
- 32-bit word accesses, address values, and ordinary integer results;
- 64-bit paired results, split into low bits 0–31 and high bits 32–63.

Unsigned halfword loads are zero-extended. Narrow stores retain the low 8 or 16 bits of the supplied value. Splitting a paired result into words does not establish memory endianness.

Unsigned right shifts are logical. The explicitly unsigned 32-bit left shift of F-d009’s low word discards its top two bits.

Several other calculations use signed 32-bit intermediates, including additions, left shifts, and multiplication. Their exact behavior for negative operands or overflow is **documented_only**. This contract does not replace those unresolved cases with an unconditional modulo-arithmetic guarantee.

## Behavior

### Dependency identification

F-d001 through F-d037 identify the indirect call sites in their execution order within an iteration. Distinct identifiers do not imply distinct callees. A call without listed explicit arguments may still consume implicit machine state.

The ordering below covers direct accesses and call boundaries. Dependency effects may occur between those boundaries and may affect later reads.

### Entry and first-object updates

At the start of each iteration, the byte value later stored at P + `0x15` is initialized to zero. F-d001 is then invoked with two explicit zero arguments.

Only **after F-d001 returns**, the separate byte value used in F-d006’s argument calculation is initialized to zero. F-d002 and F-d003 follow. Thus the representation does not establish both byte initializations before F-d001; the later initialization cannot be assumed to supply an implicit input to that call.

After F-d003 returns, G1 is read once and its 32-bit contents retained as P. F-d004 then runs, followed by F-d005.

F-d005 supplies a paired result. F-d006 receives its low word as the first argument. Its second argument combines the high word, low word, and the zero-valued byte initialized after F-d001. The word addition has no explicit carry reporting or range check; signed-intermediate overflow remains unresolved.

After F-d006 returns, one byte from its secondary register output is stored at P + `0x11`. F-d007 follows, after which the earlier zero byte is stored at P + `0x15`.

F-d008 then returns a 16-bit primary value and a secondary address value. The primary value is stored as a halfword at secondary address + `0x0C`.

Both stores through P use the captured G1 contents. They do not reread G1 after intervening calls.

### Paired-result propagation and the breakpoint

F-d009 supplies another paired result. A zero halfword is stored at high word + `0x2C`. The low word, interpreted as unsigned and shifted right by 26, is stored as a word at B + `0x26C`.

The unsigned constant `0x21C48960` is then established. F-d010 runs, followed by a word read from B + `0x320`; that word is retained as an address. F-d011 and F-d012 run before the low word from F-d009, shifted left by two at unsigned 32-bit width, is stored at retained address + `0x54`.

F-d013, F-d014, F-d015, F-d016, and F-d017 run in that order. An unsigned halfword is then read from `0x21C48994`. Its zero-extended value is retained as an address base for later reads.

F-d018 runs before the word at B + `0x280` is read and supplied as F-d019’s explicit argument. After F-d019 returns its 32-bit result, software breakpoint `0x92` executes.

The breakpoint precedes every remaining operation in the iteration. If it does not resume, earlier effects remain; the representation supplies neither rollback nor an alternative return path.

The two stores derived from F-d009’s low word have different meanings: the right-shifted value is in the range 0–63, while the left-shifted value loses the original top two bits.

### Post-breakpoint addresses and halfword stores

On continuation, F-d019’s result is shifted left by two. Separately, the unsigned constant `0x21C48960` is shifted right by 18 and multiplied by four, producing `0x21C4`.

A word is read at retained halfword base + `0x44` and retained as Q. F-d020 runs, then F-d021 supplies a word result. A word is subsequently read at retained halfword base + `0x04`; F-d022 receives F-d021’s result first and this newly read word second.

G2 is then read once. F-d023 runs before a halfword store whose destination is the captured G2 value plus the shifted F-d019 result. The stored halfword is the low 16 bits of that same captured G2 value. Neither the destination calculation nor the stored value rereads G2 after F-d023.

F-d024 runs before a word is read at `0x2218`, the address formed from `0x21C4 + 0x54`. That word is retained as an address. F-d025 then supplies another address-like result, at whose offset `0x04` the halfword `0x21C4` is stored.

F-d026 and F-d027 follow. An unsigned halfword is then read at offset `0x34` from the address captured at `0x2218`; its zero-extended value becomes U. F-d028 subsequently supplies the paired result designated L and H.

### Base adjustment and remaining updates

L is first stored as a word at B + `0x258`. The unsigned halfword at H + `0x14` is then read and zero-extended as V.

The representation forms an address component by shifting L left by six and another by multiplying H by four. It then decreases B by `0xF4`. This adjustment occurs after the store at B + `0x258`; that store therefore uses the pre-adjustment base.

A further component, four times U, is formed. L is stored as a word at `4 × U + 0x74`. F-d029 runs, after which V is stored as a word at Q + `0x54`. F-d030 runs before a second word store of V at Q + `0x64`.

After F-d031, the low 16 bits of Q are stored at the address formed from shifted L plus F-d031’s secondary register output.

F-d032 and F-d033 follow. One byte is then read from `5 × U` and retained across F-d034. After F-d034 returns, that byte is stored at V + R + `0x19`.

F-d035 runs before the value four times H is stored as a word at Q + `0x44`. F-d036 then runs.

The continuation-related state is next assigned `0x08712031`. A word is read at `4 × H + 0x44` and passed to F-d037. If F-d037 returns, another iteration begins.

Because U is an unsigned halfword:

- `4 × U` ranges from zero through `0x3FFFC`;
- `4 × U + 0x74` ranges from `0x74` through `0x40070`;
- `5 × U` ranges from zero through `0x4FFFB`.

These particular calculations do not overflow 32 bits. Both stores of V are 32-bit stores of a zero-extended 16-bit value.

### Repetition

There is no represented loop-boundary restoration of B. The next iteration uses the decreased base in the represented data flow and initializes the two byte values again in the same order around F-d001.

F-d001’s target is formed from continuation-related state plus `0xFFE`. Its first invocation depends on incoming state. After the explicit assignment near an iteration’s end, the represented next target calculation yields `0x0871302F`. This is a static address calculation, not proof of an executable target or a guarantee about unrepresented dependency effects.

Memory changes and dependency effects are not rolled back at the loop boundary.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes that the relevant operations are reached and their accesses succeed.

### Initialization across the first call

On entry to F-d001, the representation has initialized the byte later stored at P + `0x15`. It has not yet performed the separate initialization of the byte used in F-d006’s argument calculation.

After F-d001 returns, that second initialization occurs before F-d002. An account placing both initializations before F-d001 does not match the established ordering.

### Captured global addresses

If G1 supplies P and F-d004 changes G1 to another address, the two direct byte destinations remain P + `0x11` and P + `0x15`, provided execution can still reach them.

Likewise, if G2 supplies `0x12345678` and F-d019 returns 3, the later halfword destination is `0x12345684` and its value is `0x5678`. A change to G2 during F-d023 does not itself redirect this store.

### Unsigned shift behavior

If F-d009’s low word is `0xFC000003`, the word stored at B + `0x26C` is 63. The later word stored through the address captured from B + `0x320` is `0xF000000C`.

### Maximum halfword-derived index

If U is `0xFFFF`, the word-store address derived from U is `0x40070`, and the later byte-read address is `0x4FFFB`. Neither calculation sign-extends U.

### Paired-result propagation

If L is 2, H is `0x1000`, and the halfword at `0x1014` is 7:

- Pre-adjustment B + `0x258` receives 2.
- `4 × U + 0x74` receives 2.
- Q + `0x54` and Q + `0x64` each receive the 32-bit value 7.
- Q + `0x44` receives `0x4000`.
- F-d037 receives the word read from `0x4044` at its specified late read point, not a value assumed from earlier memory contents.

### Interrupted and repeated execution

If breakpoint `0x92` does not resume, no post-breakpoint effects are required; earlier effects are not undone.

If two iterations complete without an external change to B and without arithmetic failure, the second begins with B decreased by `0xF4`, and its own adjustment produces a total decrease of `0x1E8`.

A completed iteration has 15 direct stores. Stopping partway through an iteration does not imply that all 15 occurred.
