## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial truncation.** Under 32-bit bit-vector shifts, second inputs 1 and 129 both produce 0x02000000 for the first invocation’s shifted argument. Whole-function equivalence does not follow.

2. **Explicit byte widening.** If the explicit offset-5 byte read at the shifted-result base yields 255, its corresponding offset-52 word store carries 255, not a sign-extended negative value.

3. **Unresolved address-5 width.** A candidate must not reject or truncate an address-5-derived base solely because it exceeds 255. Such a restriction requires the missing global declaration.

4. **Fixed-address doubling.** If the saved replacement word is 7 and arithmetic is defined, the word at address 84 receives 14. This write occurs after the shifted-value offset-20 store and before both subsequent saved-word offset-20 stores.

5. **Fixed-address narrowing.** If the small selected quantity is 132, the doubled small base is 264. Its low byte, 8, is written at fixed address 21 after the duplicate derived offset-21 byte stores and before the following scaled offset-84 store.

6. **Captured global base.** If writing 32 at address 96 would change the global supplying the repeated group’s base, the group still uses the value captured by the preceding global read.

7. **Ending read boundary.** If the address-58 byte write changes a later offset-20 or offset-4 read through aliasing, that read uses the post-write contents under the applicable memory model. Preloading both words before the write is not established equivalent.

8. **Mixed-width constants.** The three stores of 58 retain their 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. Replacing all three with byte stores does not preserve the represented accesses.

9. **Overlap and rereads.** The word store at offset 16 accounts for overlap with offset-17 byte storage. Later fixed-address-103 reads are not replaced with the original saved byte without an equivalence argument.

10. **Final transfer.** If reached, the transfer receives 114 first and preserves the three remaining arguments’ distinct loaded and saved origins. No normal-return requirement follows.
