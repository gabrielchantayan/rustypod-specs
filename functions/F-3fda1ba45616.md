### 2. Unsigned computation and overflow predicate

After the interrupt, the operation derives an unsigned intermediate from the primary word:

- It performs a logical right shift by two.
- It adds seven.

For a 32-bit unsigned primary word, the shifted value ranges from 0 through `0x3FFFFFFF`. The intermediate therefore ranges from 7 through `0x40000006`. This addition cannot wrap at 32 bits.

Independently, the operation determines whether adding `0xC6`, or 198, to the secondary word logically shifted right by one would overflow signed 32-bit arithmetic.

The shifted secondary word ranges from 0 through `0x7FFFFFFF`. Consequently:

- The overflow predicate is false when the secondary word is at most `0xFFFFFE73`.
- The overflow predicate is true when the secondary word is at least `0xFFFFFE74`.

This is a signed-overflow test on the addition, not an unsigned carry test and not a test of the secondary word’s original sign bit. The resulting sum is not otherwise used in the supplied fragment.

### 3. Required byte read

### 5. 32-bit destination write

When the overflow predicate is false, the stored value is the quotient obtained by dividing the original intermediate by `0x800000` and discarding the remainder.

### 6. Loop and terminal behavior
