# F-37d613206c1e

## Purpose

F-37d613206c1e specifies an operation whose externally relevant behavior consists of ordered indirect dependency interactions, computed-address byte overwrites, two 16-bit memory reads, one global assignment, and one direct 32-bit overwrite. The represented terminal continuation does not return normally.

No application-level purpose is established. The operation must not be characterized as allocation, initialization, reset, object construction, or successful publication merely from names or recurring access patterns.

This contract separates three evidence levels:

- **Established static evidence:** access widths, value relationships, and ordering visible in the supplied representation.
- **[INFERENCE]:** consequences requiring an additional interpretation, particularly low-32-bit machine arithmetic.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which this contract supplies no executable guarantee.

The contract concerns the represented outer operation. It does not establish that every dependency returns, that computed addresses are accessible, or that execution can reach the terminal portion.

## Behavior

### Ordering and arithmetic interpretation

The displayed outer flow contains no conditional branch or loop. Repeated interactions are distinct ordered occurrences, not an input-controlled iteration.

Dependencies intervene between many explicit memory effects. They remain observable even when no arguments are displayed or no primary result is subsequently consumed. The contract does not permit treating these interactions as no-ops, merging stores across them, or assuming that earlier memory contents survive them.

Scaling factors below describe numerical relationships in the representation. They do not authorize unlimited-precision arithmetic.

Many operands are represented as signed 32-bit quantities. Negative left-shift operands, signed overflow, and numerical-to-address conversion are not resolved. Consequently, their concrete executable consequences are **documented_only** where those issues arise.

For explicitly unsigned 32-bit scaling, the result is width-limited. An 8-bit payload captured from that result retains its low eight bits.

One exceptional calculation right-shifts a represented 32-bit operand by 32 after fourfold scaling. No numerical result is established for that calculation. The contract does not choose zero, sign propagation, a masked shift count, or a widened operand.

### Payload progression

The explicit byte effects occur in this broad order:

1. Repeated uses of the incoming byte.
2. Six literal-zero overwrites.
3. One destination-derived byte overwrite.
4. Repeated uses of a byte captured from an unsigned result.
5. One extracted-byte overwrite from that same unsigned result.
6. Repeated uses of a three-bit payload derived from separate retained arithmetic state.

The first 16-bit read and the global assignment occur during the captured-byte portion. The second 16-bit read and the direct 32-bit overwrite occur during the three-bit portion.

These are payload phases, not separate buffers or transactions. Their destinations may alias.

### Early byte effects and wide exchanges

Four indirect interactions precede the first explicit memory overwrite. The fourth supplies a primary word whose eightfold numerical value is used as the first byte destination. The incoming byte is stored before the next interaction.

The opening 64-bit exchange receives four times that same primary word. Its consumer receives three component words in the order low, high, low.

Most later incoming-byte overwrites use eight times a fresh primary result as their numerical destination. The immediately following consumer commonly receives two equal arguments, each 16384 times that result.

The following exceptions are material:

- One incoming-byte overwrite uses twice its associated result as the destination and paired arguments scaled by 4096.
- One consumer receives four arguments. The first, second, and fourth are the same 16384-scaled word; the third is the incoming signed word scaled by 1024.
- The interaction immediately after that four-argument consumer receives eight times a secondary-state exposure from it.
- One later incoming-byte overwrite retains the eightfold destination relationship but is followed by paired arguments scaled by four.

During this early portion, a destination-producing primary word is scaled by 16384 and retained for much later use. Subsequent primary results do not replace that retained arithmetic value.

The four wide exchanges have different consumer layouts:

| Exchange position | Consumer word order |
|---|---|
| Opening | Low, high, low |
| First intermediate | Low, high, high |
| Second intermediate | Low, high, high |
| Later, after the unresolved shift calculation | Low, high |

Both intermediate wide exchanges precede the literal-zero portion. Repeated component arguments remain distinct argument positions even when their values happen to be equal.

Intervening interactions frequently consume fourfold-scaled primary results. Other interactions forward words unchanged, or consume a scaled result returned by an earlier consumer. None of these words is established as a handle, size, status, or object identity.

### Literal-zero and destination-derived effects

There are six separate explicit zero-byte overwrites. Each uses eight times its associated primary result as its numerical destination. Each following consumer receives two equal 16384-scaled arguments derived from that same result.

This is not an established contiguous clear. The six addresses can coincide, and dependencies between occurrences can modify previous destinations.

The subsequent destination-derived overwrite stores the low eight bits of its own computed destination’s numerical value. The destination is eight times its associated primary result. Its following consumer receives two equal 16384-scaled arguments and then the numerical destination as a third argument.

The payload is derived from the address value, not from reading the destination’s existing contents.

### Captured byte and retained arithmetic

A later unsigned 32-bit result supplies a captured byte: the low eight bits of its eightfold value. The first overwrite using this byte has a destination equal to sixteen times that unsigned result. Its following consumer receives two equal arguments scaled by 32768.

Subsequent captured-byte overwrites reuse this byte even when other results determine their destinations. They do not recapture a byte from each new address-producing result.

Several nonstandard address relationships occur during this portion:

| Source category | Numerical destination factor | Following equal-argument factor |
|---|---:|---:|
| Earlier retained arithmetic word | 65536 | 134217728 |
| Fresh primary words at several occurrences | 8192 | 16777216 |
| Additional retained arithmetic state | 4096 | 8388608 |

The additional retained state is formed by multiplying the earlier retained word by 32768. It is distinct from both the original primary result and the earlier 16384-scaled word. Its upper byte, obtained through an unsigned interpretation, is captured separately.

**[INFERENCE]** Under low-32-bit arithmetic, the combined scaling from the originating primary result is \(2^{29}\). Only that result’s lowest three bits survive, in positions 29–31. The retained upper byte would therefore have five low zero bits, with its upper three bits reproducing those originating low three bits. This does not establish validity of overflowing signed operations.

Later captured-byte overwrites return to eightfold destinations. Most have the usual paired 16384-scaled consumer arguments. The final captured-byte occurrence instead uses paired fourfold-scaled arguments. A nearby forwarding interaction consumes twice its primary result rather than the common fourfold value.

### First read and global assignment

At the first explicit read event, one fresh primary word determines both memory addresses:

- The captured byte is overwritten at the word’s 8192-scaled numerical address.
- A 16-bit payload is then read at the word’s 4096-scaled numerical address plus 56 bytes.
- The consumer receives the 4096-scaled base followed by the read payload.

The byte overwrite precedes the read, and the read precedes entry to the consumer. No outer-operation dependency interaction separates that overwrite and read.

Later, the captured byte is assigned to the global destination immediately before an interaction receiving two zero arguments.

This establishes the assignment’s position and supplied source value. It does not establish reset semantics, synchronization, or preservation of the assigned value by later dependencies.

### Extracted byte and three-bit payload

After further captured-byte effects, a separate overwrite stores bits 19–26 of the original unsigned result. Its destination is eight times a fresh primary result. Its following consumer receives two equal fourfold-scaled arguments.

The final 8-bit conversion excludes higher bits that may survive an earlier intermediate operation. In particular, bit 27 does not contribute to this stored byte.

The following payload family uses the upper three bits of the separately captured upper byte. Its value is always in the range zero through seven, without requiring the conditional relationship to the original arithmetic result.

Most overwrites in this family use eightfold primary-result destinations and paired 16384-scaled consumer arguments. Important exceptions are:

- The unresolved shift-by-32 result supplies a twice-scaled destination and paired 4096-scaled arguments.
- A secondary-state pointer supplies a 256-scaled byte destination and paired 524288-scaled arguments.
- A retained secondary-state word supplies a 2048-scaled byte destination and paired 4194304-scaled arguments.
- Some overwrites are followed by interactions with no displayed arguments.

The retained secondary word is also supplied as the second argument in four later interactions. Their first arguments are four times separate fresh primary results. Independently exposed secondary words are forwarded elsewhere; they are not interchangeable with this retained word.

The pointer has three distinct access relationships:

| Access | Numerical address relationship |
|---|---|
| Earlier byte overwrite | Pointer value scaled by 256 |
| Later 16-bit read | Pointer value scaled by 128, plus 32 bytes |
| Direct 32-bit overwrite | Pointer used directly |

These relationships do not establish offsets within one valid object.

At the second read event, an eightfold-destination byte overwrite precedes a 16-bit read at the pointer-derived read address. The following consumer receives that read payload. No outer-operation dependency interaction separates the overwrite and read.

At this point, the retained upper-byte state itself is reduced to the three-bit value. Later overwrites reuse that reduced value; they do not repeatedly divide it by another factor of 32.

### Direct word overwrite and termination

Near the end, a fresh primary result determines an eightfold byte destination and a fourfold 32-bit word payload.

The three-bit byte overwrite occurs first. The word payload is then written directly through the retained pointer, with no intervening dependency interaction. The following consumer receives the word payload in three argument positions.

That word payload is retained for one later additional argument despite intervening primary results. A separate later primary result is duplicated into two arguments for another consumer.

Further interactions and byte overwrites follow the direct word write. The last explicit byte overwrite is also followed by additional indirect interactions before the terminal facility.

No ordinary-return path, rollback, or explicit cleanup sequence is established.

## Side effects

The established order is the outer operation’s execution order. It is not a guarantee of volatile semantics, physical bus ordering, atomicity, inter-thread visibility, or synchronization.

- A later byte overwrite may replace an earlier byte.
- The direct word write may overlap its immediately preceding byte write.
