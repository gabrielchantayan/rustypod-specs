### Early fixed-byte dependency

After another clear and two identical byte stores at offset `0x15` of the doubled-small-value base, the function reads the **byte at fixed address `0x05`** and zero-extends it.

That read precedes the byte store to **fixed address `0x01`**, whose payload is the low byte of the doubled-small-value base.

The saved byte from `0x05` subsequently acts as an address component:

- Its offset-`0x15` byte receives the low byte of the small value.
- Its offset-`0x54` word is read, then replaced with an earlier loaded word.

The first of those derived writes occurs after the fixed `0x01` store. Other derived writes intervene before the offset-`0x54` read. Consequently, this dependency is on the byte observed at the specified read point, not necessarily the entry-time byte at `0x05`.

The word obtained from that offset-`0x54` read supplies a major base for subsequent transformations. For exposition, this contract calls that saved word the **root value**. This name establishes no object type.

### Constant stores and repetition-base capture

A later loaded link value determines three destinations receiving `0x3A`: an 8-bit store at four times that value, a 16-bit store at eight times it, and a 32-bit store at offset `0x20` from four times it.

At work-base offset `0x20`, the ordered word payloads are `0x20`, `0x2F`, a zero-extended byte read from work-base offset `0x04`, and `0x20` again.

After these stores, the function reads the **32-bit fixed word at `0x087FBBBC`**. Its saved value is the **repetition anchor**. This read occurs **before** the 32-bit store of `0x20` to fixed address **`0x60`**.

The fixed word is not reread for every repeated group. Its captured value supplies destinations at offsets `0x19` and `0x34`, and its low byte supplies a work-base offset-`0x11` payload. Even if the fixed `0x60` store overlaps the anchor’s derived destinations, it does not change the already captured anchor value.

The first four-write group uses an eightfold-root destination and stores eight times the root at anchor offset `0x34`. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted value instead. Each group retains this order:

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

- **Third input:** If the third input is 1 and ordinary 32-bit shifting applies, its early forwarded argument is 64.
- **Unsigned widening:** A reached unsigned byte read yielding `0xFE` contributes 254 to a widened word payload, not a negative value.
- **Masked field:** Selecting 24 low bits all equal to one and shifting left by six yields `0x3FFFFFC0`.
- **Intermediate truncation:** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, two successive left shifts by 28 yield zero.
- **Early fixed read:** If the fixed `0x05` read yields `0xFE`, its saved address component is 254. The subsequent `0x01` write does not retroactively change it.
- **Anchor capture:** If the fixed anchor-word read yields address A, subsequent groups use captured A even if later writes alter the fixed word’s storage.
- **Saved word versus reread:** If the `0xA9` read yields `0x123456AB`, the explicit `0x67` store receives `0xAB`. The later full-word store still uses `0x123456AB`. If an intervening aliased write changes `0x67` to `0xCD`, a subsequent reread must observe the value applicable at that point, not automatically `0xAB`.
- **Distinct byte copies:** The `0x21` copy and two work-base offset-`0x09` copies may carry different values when intervening accesses modify `0x67`.
- **Repeated stores:** An access observer distinguishes the four work-base offset-`0x20` word stores in their represented order.
- **Failure:** Failure after a write does not imply restoration of earlier memory.
- **Final invocation:** Reaching the final destination establishes first argument `0x72`, not a direct forwarding contract for the original three inputs.
