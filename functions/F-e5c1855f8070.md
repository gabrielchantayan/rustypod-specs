# F-e5c1855f8070

### Width and arithmetic rules

A byte access transfers eight bits, a halfword access sixteen bits, and a word access thirty-two bits. The represented paired results contain sixty-four bits, subsequently separated into low and high 32-bit portions. Whether those pairs correspond to scalar return values or a recovered register-result convention is **documented_only**.

Byte-selector reads are unsigned. Consequently, a destination selected by such a byte plus `0x11` lies between `0x11` and `0x110`, inclusive.

Unsigned right shifts are logical. Narrowing a word to a byte retains its low eight bits. Extracting a paired result’s high word retains bits 32–63.

For compactness, a **four-scaled slot** means the word address obtained from four times a supplied index plus `0x14`. Other accesses described as unscaled do not use this transformation.

Modulo-\(2^{32}\) treatment of address arithmetic and signed left shifts is an inferred machine interpretation, not a blanket language-level guarantee. Several represented signed operations can overflow or shift negative values. Unsigned operations, including the repeated right shifts that establish zero, do not have that particular ambiguity.
