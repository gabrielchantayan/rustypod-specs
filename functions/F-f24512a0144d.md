### Arithmetic conventions

For a wide result, its low word consists of bits 0–31 and its high word consists of bits 32–63. Extracting these words is a numeric operation and does not itself establish memory byte order.

Left shifts discard bits beyond bit 31 when their result is used as a word. Right shifts explicitly described as logical introduce zero bits.

- Derived Word E is zero.
- The explicit argument to F-000000000012 is zero.
- The second explicit argument to F-000000000020 is zero.

These conclusions hold for every 32-bit Result R under the stated bitvector interpretation. They do not depend on Result R being small or nonnegative.

### Final destination calculation

Address Field is the uppermost 23 bits of Result V, expressed as a nonnegative word. Its range is **0 through 8,388,607**.

The visible calculation treats Address Field as signed for a right shift by 11. Because its sign bit is necessarily clear, this has the same numeric result as a logical right shift. Call the result **Index**.

Therefore:

- Index is Result V logically shifted right by **52**.
- Index ranges from **0 through 4,095**.
- The destination address is Index plus the integer quotient of Index divided by 4.
- The destination ranges from **0 through 5,118**, inclusive.

No overflow occurs in this address addition.

The final operation stores a **16-bit numeric value of 0x00CD** at that address. There is no visible base pointer, allocation, bounds check, null check, or alignment adjustment.

The address mapping is not contiguous: increasing Index can skip addresses at quarter boundaries. Some generated addresses are odd. Whether such halfword stores succeed, fault, or receive architectural handling is unknown.
