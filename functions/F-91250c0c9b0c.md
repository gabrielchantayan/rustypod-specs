# F-91250c0c9b0c

## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

Execution begins with an extended, ordered sequence of indirect calls. The first destination depends on incoming machine state plus 0xFFE. Later destinations generally derive from fixed execution-location anchors plus the same displacement.

These expressions select destinations; they do not establish reads from a function-pointer table or identify one shared helper.

Represented argument lists range from empty to four arguments. Empty lists do not establish complete prototypes or absence of implicit inputs. Visible transformations include:

- Shifting word results before forwarding them.
- Separating paired results into low and high halves.
- Forwarding transformed low halves alongside unchanged or separately transformed high halves.
- Reusing saved results after intervening calls.
- Passing explicit zeros together with unresolved secondary-result values.
- Masking low-order bits before further shifts.

Under the stated arithmetic interpretation:

- Retaining 24 low bits and shifting left six produces a value below 2³⁰.
- Retaining 22 low bits and shifting left eight also produces a value below 2³⁰.
- Retaining 24 low bits and shifting right twenty produces a value from zero through fifteen.
- Retaining 19 low bits, shifting left two, and then right twenty-three produces zero.
- Two successive left shifts of twenty-eight bits produce zero.

Explicitly zeroed saved values remain zero through their represented shifts without intervening reassignment. This says nothing about memory or globals modified by callees.

No visible conditional branch uses a result to skip the remaining sequence. No retry, validation failure, or early-success policy is established. Exact call-site enumeration is documented_only in this contract; this section is not a complete external call protocol.

### Initial memory updates and early globals

Direct mutation begins by storing the secondary base as a word at offset 0x14 from the last pair’s high half. The secondary-base byte at offset 0x09 first receives the high half’s low byte. A word store of the primary base at offset 0x54 from the last pair’s low half scaled by 256 intervenes before that same secondary-base byte receives the secondary base’s low byte.

The primary-base word at 0x14 is cleared before the byte at primary-base offset 0x05 is read. The compact-base word at 0x14 is then cleared, followed by storing that byte, zero-extended, at primary-base offset 0x34. A fresh word read from primary-base offset 0x14 supplies a saved value used as a later base and byte source. Another compact-base clear follows.

The next explicit-global dependencies are ordered as follows:

- Two consecutive byte stores at compact-base offset 0x15 both receive the saved word’s low byte.
- **Only after those duplicate stores**, the byte global at **0x05 is read** and zero-extended to form a new base.
- The byte global at **0x01 receives the compact base’s low byte**.
- **After the write at 0x01**, a byte store through the newly obtained base, at offset 0x15, receives the compact extraction’s low byte.

Thus the read at 0x05 is not an entry-state input capture, and the write at 0x01 must not be moved after the following computed-address write.

Further primary-base byte writes and a word write through the saved word precede a word read at offset 0x54 from the byte-derived base. Call this loaded word the **saved loaded base**. That location is subsequently overwritten with the earlier primary-offset-0x14 read, while compact-base offset 0x34 receives the compact extraction.

Additional reads at primary-base and compact-base offset 0x74 feed computed destinations. Some represented expressions read compact-base offset 0x74 more than once; the evidence does not establish a disjoint, immutable object field.

After intervening self-value and byte updates, compact-base offset 0x14 receives the original primary-producing result shifted twenty-six bits. Then:

- The word global at **0x54 receives twice the saved loaded base**.
- Subsequent compact-base offset-0x14 stores both receive the saved loaded base.
- Two subsequent compact-base offset-0x15 byte stores receive the low byte of four times the saved loaded base.
- The byte global at **0x15 then receives the compact base’s low byte**.

The global word store at 0x54 therefore precedes these compact-base updates. The global byte at 0x15 receives the compact base, not the compact extraction and not the quadrupled saved loaded base.

### Computed-memory relationships

The direct phase uses byte offsets including 0x01, 0x05, 0x08, 0x09, 0x0D, 0x10, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19. Word offsets include 0x04, 0x14, 0x20, 0x24, 0x34, 0x40, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.

Subsequent operations use doubled, quadrupled, and octupled forms of loaded words. Words read at 0x64 and 0x74 become later bases. Some word stores place a base value at an offset from that same base.

Byte reads at offset 0x05 also become bases for word reads at offset 0x74. These use the zero-extended byte itself; they are not established full-width pointers.

Repeated byte reads remain separate inputs. In particular, a later offset-0x05 read follows intervening stores and feeds another offset-0x74 word read. Call the result of this latter word read the **chain value**, and twice it the **double-chain base**.

These relationships establish neither ownership links nor object invariants.

### Late global reads and alias-sensitive propagation

After the repeated stores, the function reads the word global at **0xA9**, retaining that word for subsequent stores. It next reads the word at offset 0x74 from four times the chain value. Only then does the byte global at **0x3A** receive the low byte of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30.

Thus the 0xA9 read precedes the computed offset-0x74 read and the 0x3A write. After the 0x3A write, further word reads obtain double-chain-base offset 0x14 and working-base offset 0x04. The former is saved for the final invocation.

Several computed stores follow, including a byte 0x6C at working-base offset 0x08 and a self-value word at double-chain-base offset 0x54. The byte global at **0x67 then receives the low byte of the saved 0xA9 word**.

There are **three distinct subsequent reads of the byte at 0x67**:

- **First read:** after a word load from double-chain-base offset 0x44, the current byte at 0x67 supplies the write to global byte **0x21**. The intervening word load is part of the ordering; the value supplied to 0x21 is a read of 0x67, not merely a forwarded copy of the original 0xA9 byte.
- **Second read:** after further computed stores and a working-base offset-0x40 word read, the current byte at 0x67 supplies a byte store at working-base offset **0x09**.
- **Third read:** before another store to that same working-base byte, execution writes a byte at double-chain-base offset **0x09**, writes the saved 0xA9 word at working-base offset **0x44**, and reads the word at working-base offset **0x54**. The function then reads 0x67 again to supply the second working-base-offset-0x09 store.

The stores between the second and third reads may alias 0x67. The two computed-address byte stores therefore need not receive equal values. Forwarding either the originally stored byte or the second read’s value into the third use is not an equivalent contract.

Other late effects include:

- A byte store derived from the loaded double-chain-offset-0x44 word shifted by the working base’s low byte.
- Global-derived-base offset 0x54 receiving that loaded word and subsequently the working base.
- A word copied from working-base offset 0x40 to offset 0x10, overlapping an earlier byte store at offset 0x10.
- Subsequent overwrites of working-base offsets 0x09 and 0x44 using a newly loaded base.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

### Narrowing and widening

A byte store supplied 0x12345678 writes 0x78. A byte read returning 0xFE and subsequently stored as a word supplies 0x000000FE.

### Compact boundary

If the compact-producing result has all seven low bits set, the extraction is 508 and the compact base is 1016. A compact base of 1022 is inconsistent with the extraction’s required low zero bits.

### Early global timing

If either duplicate compact-base-offset-0x15 store changes address 0x05 through aliasing, the following global-byte read uses the resulting byte. It must not use an entry-time snapshot. The global-0x01 write still precedes the next byte write through the byte-derived base.

### Fresh reads at 0x67

Suppose the second read of 0x67 returns 0x12. If an intervening computed store changes 0x67 to 0x34 before the third read, the two corresponding working-base-offset-0x09 stores receive 0x12 and 0x34, respectively. Requiring both to use the original low byte of the 0xA9 word would be incorrect.

### Global-base capture

If the 0x60 word write aliases the additional global base’s source, the already loaded global-derived base remains the value captured before that write. Subsequent accesses are not redirected by rereading that source.

### Overwrites and partial execution

Without aliasing interference, working-base offset 0x20 receives 0x20, 0x2F, the loaded byte as a word, and 0x20. Its final value alone does not establish the intermediate events. If execution faults before final dispatch, no successful invocation or rollback is required by the visible evidence.

### Final dependency

If F-D003 is reached, its first represented argument is 0x72. Requiring it to free an argument, preserve globals, or return normally adds an unsupported dependency contract.
