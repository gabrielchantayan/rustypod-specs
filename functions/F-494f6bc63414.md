# F-494f6bc63414

## Purpose

F-494f6bc63414 performs an ordered mutation of interconnected memory, partially initializes an outgoing storage region, and invokes dependencies. Its observable contract is primarily the relationship between **retained values, fresh observations, and potentially overlapping publications**.

A publication may change a later observed value or address. It does not change a value already retained. Implementations that assume disjoint objects, collapse repeated publications, or reuse an earlier observation may therefore behave differently.

**Established static evidence:** sixteen selection stages; byte, halfword, and word accesses; changing address operands; a dependent final traversal; partial outgoing initialization; conditional dependency interactions.

**Inferred intent:** the operations resemble link maintenance and outgoing-call preparation. No application-level data structure, transaction, success condition, or ownership transfer is established.

### Width and validity requirements

Offsets are byte offsets. Word accesses occupy 32 bits; displacement observations are signed 16-bit accesses; byte accesses occupy 8 bits. Displacements are promoted before participating in address calculations.

Three byte observations are interpreted as plain characters and subsequently used numerically, including as address operands. Their interpretation depends on character signedness. Publications involving character or signed-halfword conversion require the applicable narrowing rules; not every conversion is established as a portable unsigned low-bit extraction.

Address-producing additions, products, doubling, and left shifts involve signed 32-bit operands. Valid execution requires the execution model’s arithmetic and shift conditions. Modular wraparound is not an established replacement for those conditions.

Every reached access requires valid address interpretation, bounds, alignment, and permissions. Every reached call requires a compatible callable target and calling convention. There is no represented validation, error return, or recovery path.

## Behavior

### Observation model

The following semantic roles describe the contract without assigning application meanings:

- **Context:** a retained address operand that several stages replace.
- **Selection:** the value retained from the current stage’s fixed source.
- **Predecessor:** the address receiving the next selection publication.
- **Incoming link:** a retained word published into selection offset 0x14.
- **Effective base:** the address controlling the stage’s concluding halfword destination and link refresh; normally the selection.
- **Address record:** outgoing word at offset 0x34, first initialized during stage 6.
- **Traversal record:** outgoing word at offset 0x108, first initialized during stage 16.

The records are mutable storage, not immutable snapshots. Addressed writes may overlap either record, the selection sources, the continuation source, or other outgoing fields. A subsequent record use observes its current contents unless the contract identifies a separately retained value.

Ordering below is local ordering. It establishes neither atomicity nor synchronization with external activity.

### Entry guarantees

The first retained observations are, in order, the word at entry-context offset 0x44 and the displacement at entry context plus entry base.

The supplied byte is then published at primary offset 0x15. Only afterward is the first predecessor observed at offset 0x14 through the retained context-derived word. Consequently, overlap can affect that predecessor observation but not the two preceding retained values.

The entry payload reaches entry-base offset 0x24 before a separate word is retained from entry-base offset 0x74. That separate word supplies later stage-2 and stage-3 data; it is not interchangeable with the payload.

The remaining entry ordering is:

- Full primary address at entry-base offset 0x14 precedes its character conversion at entry-context offset 0x11.
- Entry-base character conversion at predecessor offset 0x09 precedes predecessor halfword conversion at entry base plus retained displacement.
- A fresh incoming-link observation at entry-base offset 0x44 precedes context self-publication at entry-context offset 0x54.

### Shared selection-stage contract

Each of the sixteen stages has the same selection boundary:

- Its fixed source is observed to retain the selection.
- A second observation of that source supplies the word published at predecessor offset 0x44.
- The retained incoming link is published at selection offset 0x14.
- An initial byte is published at entering-context offset 0x11 before displacement is observed at entering context plus original selection.

There is no explicit intervening publication or call between the two source observations. External stability is not established.

Stages 1, 2, 4–6, 9–11, and 14 derive their initial byte by shifting the entering context left one bit and narrowing. The remaining stages use previously retained auxiliary data.

Stages 1–15 share a conclusion: a halfword conversion of the **reached value** is published, the incoming link is freshly observed at effective-base offset 0x44, and the current context is published at its own offset 0x54, in that order. The full reached value becomes the next predecessor.

Normally the halfword destination is effective base plus retained displacement. Stage 6 instead uses selection plus twice context.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. All require valid reached arithmetic and accesses. Criteria after unresolved-byte consumption additionally require an execution model permitting continuation.

1. **Entry overlap:** When the supplied-byte destination overlaps the first predecessor field, predecessor observation may change. The earlier context-derived word and displacement remain retained.

2. **Distinct entry data:** When entry payload differs from the retained entry-base-offset-0x74 word, stage 2 publishes the latter at primary offset 0x54; stage 3 uses its byte conversion.

3. **Displacement addressing:** Displacement −1 makes a displacement-relative offset-0x44 observation use −1 as its base, not original selection minus one.

4. **Exceptional destination:** Stage 6 obtains the address-record source using displacement, but its halfword destination uses selection plus twice context.

5. **Replacement boundary:** Stage 8’s replacement effective base controls halfword destination and link refresh. Displacement still originates through entering context plus original selection.

6. **Character signedness:** Interpreting byte representation 0x80 as −128 rather than 128 changes the reached numeric value, its halfword publication, and the next predecessor address.

7. **Freshness:** If an intervening publication overlaps intermediate offset 0x44, the final traversal’s two observations can differ. Reusing the first is not generally equivalent.

8. **Independent shifts:** With address record 0x00001234, valid arithmetic, and no interference, the four-bit and eight-bit results are 0x00012340 and 0x00123400.

9. **Dependency mutation:** If F-U1 changes continuation-source storage and subsequent publications do not overwrite it, F-cdec09f5d432 receives the changed word.

10. **Nonreturn:** If F-U1 does not return, continuation-field publications and later dependency interactions are not reached.
