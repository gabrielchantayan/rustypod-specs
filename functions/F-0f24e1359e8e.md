### Widths and layouts

A **byte**, **halfword**, and **word** mean 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Composite dependency results contain 64 bits and are split numerically into low and high 32-bit words. This numerical split does not establish memory byte order.

Unsigned halfword reads are zero-extended when retained as wider values. Byte and halfword stores write only the corresponding low bits. Word stores of zero-extended halfwords overwrite all 32 destination bits.

### Arithmetic rules

The second argument to F-D06 is formed in this order:

1. Convert the high and low words of F-D05’s result separately to signed 32-bit values.
2. Add those values using signed 32-bit arithmetic.
3. Convert the signed intermediate to unsigned 32-bit.
4. Add an unsigned zero operand.

The final addition is unsigned. It does not make the preceding signed addition wrapping arithmetic. An overflowing signed intermediate remains **documented_only**. F-D06’s first argument is the low result word converted to signed 32-bit.

F-D09’s low-word logical right shift by 26 and left shift by two use unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. The left shift retains the low 32 bits.

The final byte-store destination has independently established unsigned semantics. If **H3** is the zero-extended halfword used there, its address is:

\[
\bigl(H3 + (R \bmod 2^{32}) + \mathtt{0x19}\bigr) \bmod 2^{32}.
\]

H3 is held as an unsigned 32-bit value. Consequently, R is converted to unsigned 32-bit before the first addition, and both additions use unsigned 32-bit arithmetic. Negative R values and overflowing sums are covered by this modulo rule; they are not unresolved signed-address arithmetic.

Multiplying the later unsigned halfword **H2** by four also uses unsigned arithmetic and cannot overflow 32 bits. The byte-source expression combining H2 and that product is unsigned and likewise cannot overflow for their established ranges.

Other expressions include signed additions, multiplications, and left shifts. Behavior outside their defined signed ranges remains **documented_only**. The representation does not justify a universal wrapping rule for those expressions. Conversion of an out-of-range unsigned word to signed 32-bit is also not independently resolved by an external language or machine specification.

### New composite result and base adjustment

2. Read an unsigned halfword at Q plus `0x34`; retain its zero-extended value as **H2**.
3. Invoke F-D28. Designate its low result word **L** and high result word **U**.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not execution results. Unless interruption is the subject, preceding operations must complete, dependencies must return, reached memory must be accessible, and relevant signed arithmetic must be defined.

1. **Defined mixed arithmetic.** If F-D05 supplies high word 2 and low word 3, F-D06 receives signed 3 first and unsigned 5 second.

2. **Unsigned final type does not repair signed overflow.** If F-D05 supplies high word `0x7FFFFFFF` and low word 1, the initial signed addition overflows. This contract does not require second argument `0x80000000`.

3. **Defined negative intermediate, conditional signed conversion.** If conversion of low word `0xFFFFFFFF` yields −1 and the high word is zero, the signed sum is −1 without overflow. Conversion to unsigned and addition of unsigned zero produce `0xFFFFFFFF`. This does not settle unsigned-to-signed conversion conventions.

4. **Unsigned composite transformation.** If F-D09 supplies low word `0xFC000003` and a high word whose signed conversion is valid base A, the stores are halfword zero at A plus `0x2C`, word 63 at B plus `0x26C`, and word `0xF000000C` at destination base plus `0x54`.

5. **Global snapshot.** If G1 contains A after F-D03 and F-D04 changes G1, both first-base byte destinations still use A.

6. **Narrow address base.** If H1 is `0x1234`, P is read at `0x1278`, and F-D22’s second argument is read at `0x1238`. No additional pointer read through H1 is implied.

7. **Indexed truncation.** If T is `0x12348000` and F-D19 returns three, I is 12. The halfword store goes to `0x1234800C` and writes `0x8000`.

8. **Full-width stores.** If H3 is `0xFEDC`, both H3 word stores write `0x0000FEDC`.

9. **Retained byte and narrow boundary.** If H2 is seven, J is 28 and the source byte is read at address 35. F-D34 changing that location does not change the retained byte. At H2 equal to 65535, J is 262140, the L-store destination is `0x40070`, and the byte-source address is 327675.

10. **Negative R and unsigned wrap.** If H3 is zero and R is −1, R converts to `0xFFFFFFFF`. Adding `0x19` wraps, giving final byte-store address `0x18`. If H3 is `0x10` with the same R, the first addition wraps to `0x0F`, and the second produces `0x28`. Neither result depends on signed-overflow behavior.

11. **Unsigned low-boundary crossing.** If H3 is zero and R is −26, the final byte-store address is `0xFFFFFFFF`. The address calculation is established; accessibility of that byte is not.

12. **Shared target, distinct invocation.** F-D05 and F-D06 calculate the same target address. This does not require their results or effects to match: the calls occur separately, and their explicit argument and result uses differ. The other four listed target-equality pairs have the same address-equality requirement.

13. **Interruption and repetition.** Stopping at the breakpoint requires no later operation in that iteration; earlier effects may remain. A completed iteration performs all 15 explicit stores. If B persists without dependency interference, the next iteration starts with B reduced by `0xF4`.
