### Second destination and byte replacement

This distinction matters. A contract that simply promises a write 97 bytes beyond an arbitrary native pointer would be stronger than the evidence supports. If the integer representation cannot preserve the address, the resulting destination need not remain relative to the original storage.

Where the conversion and addition are valid and address-preserving, the second assignment affects precisely relative byte 97. It replaces the prior byte with 1. For example, a prior value of 128 becomes 1, not 129.
