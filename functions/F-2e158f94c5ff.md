# F-2e158f94c5ff

## Purpose

F-2e158f94c5ff specifies a visible execution prefix that interleaves byte and 32-bit memory accesses with unresolved indirect calls. Addresses come from entry-state values, unsigned bytes, loaded words, and dependency results.

**Established static evidence:** The supplied representation contains seven explicit memory reads, thirteen explicit memory writes, and fifty-eight indirect call occurrences. Its final marker indicates termination of recovered control flow because of invalid instruction data. No ordinary return is established.

**Inferred intent:** The repeated scaling, fixed offsets, and calls may implement a low-level state transformation. They do not establish an application operation, object type, device protocol, or resource-management scheme.

This contract covers the represented access and call sequence. It does not assume that unresolved dependencies preserve globals, leave memory unchanged, or return normally. It also does not establish that the supplied representation completely or faithfully recovers executable behavior.

## Behavior

### Dependency naming and sequencing

A call described as having unspecified arguments has no explicit argument list established by the representation. This does not mean it receives no machine-state inputs.

### Initial selection and entry-state writes

The prefix begins by reading an unsigned byte at entry base plus 1. This captured value is the **initial selector**. The first write stores the entry byte at absolute address initial selector plus 17.

F-U01 through F-U05 follow in order. The next explicit writes store the low byte of the entry word at entry base plus 13, then the captured initial selector at absolute address 13.

The **doubled selector** is twice the initial selector and lies between 0 and 510. F-U06 receives, in order, the doubled selector, 152, and 608. F-U07 follows; F-U08 then receives the auxiliary unsigned output logically shifted right by three. F-U09 through F-U11 follow, and F-U12 supplies a consumed 32-bit result.

The captured selector is not reloaded during this phase. Intervening changes to the original memory byte do not replace that captured value in the represented dataflow.

### First scaling chain and loaded address basis

F-U13 receives the F-U12 result scaled left by two and supplies another consumed result. Before F-U14, two byte writes occur in order:

- The low byte of the entry word is stored at doubled selector plus 13.
- Byte value 192 is stored at absolute address 13.

F-U14 receives the F-U13 result scaled left by two. F-U15 through F-U17 follow, and F-U18 supplies a consumed 32-bit result.

The next explicit store writes the F-U18 result scaled left by two as a 32-bit word at doubled selector plus 36. A 32-bit read then occurs at doubled selector plus 116. Call the loaded value the **pointer word**; this name describes its later address use, not a validated pointer type.

The F-U18 result is also scaled left by five, producing the **wide-scaled value**. F-U19 receives four explicit arguments:

1. Pointer word.
2. Pointer word scaled left by one.
3. Wide-scaled value.
4. Pointer word with bit 31 cleared, logically shifted right by two.

After F-U20 through F-U23, the wide-scaled value is written as a 32-bit word at pointer word plus 36. A fresh 32-bit read at pointer word plus 116 supplies F-U24’s explicit argument.

The two reads at offset 116 use different bases and occur at different points. Neither may be replaced by the other. In particular, the second follows F-U19 through F-U23 and the intervening word store.

### Paired results and indexed stores

F-U25 through F-U27 precede F-U28. The represented 64-bit F-U28 result is separated into low and high 32-bit halves.

The first low half scaled left by two becomes the **retained scaled value**. The full entry word is stored at four times the first high half plus 20. F-U29 receives the retained scaled value three times, followed by the first high half logically shifted right by three.

F-U30 supplies a second represented 64-bit result. F-U31 receives its low half, high half, low half again, and high half logically shifted right by three. Unlike the first low half passed to F-U29, this second low half is not scaled first.

F-U32 through F-U34 precede F-U35, whose 32-bit result becomes an index. The retained scaled value from F-U28 is written as a 32-bit word at four times this index plus 20. F-U36 receives four times the index. F-U37 through F-U39 follow.

The later indexed store retains the earlier F-U28-derived data. It does not substitute a result from F-U30 or F-U35.

The representation’s half extraction is established; whether the actual ABI returns one 64-bit value or paired 32-bit outputs is not.

### Control-derived byte updates

F-U40 supplies an unsigned 32-bit **control word**. Its value scaled left by two is the **control-scaled value**.

A fresh unsigned byte is read at pointer word plus 1. The low byte of the control-scaled value is stored at absolute address 17 plus that byte.

F-U41 and F-U42 follow, then F-U43 supplies a result retained for F-U44. Before F-U44, another unsigned byte is read at pointer word plus 1. At absolute address 17 plus this newly read byte, the operation stores the low byte of the control word after clearing its top two bits and logically shifting right by twenty.

That mask-and-shift produces up to ten bits. The byte write discards the upper two of those ten bits, so the final stored byte consists of control-word bits 20 through 27.

F-U44 receives the F-U43 result scaled left by two. F-U45 through F-U47 follow, then F-U48 supplies a consumed result.

The two reads at pointer word plus 1 are separate observable accesses. Intervening dependencies may change memory, so their associated writes need not have the same destination.

### Clearing operations and final calls

The clearing-read address is formed from the control-scaled value multiplied by 1024, plus 1, subject to the unresolved arithmetic qualifications above.

An unsigned byte is read there. Zero is then written at absolute address 17 plus the byte just read. F-U49 receives:

1. Control-scaled value scaled left by three.
2. The same scaled value again.
3. Control-scaled value.
4. F-U48 result scaled left by two.

F-U50 through F-U52 follow, then F-U53 supplies a result. A second unsigned byte read occurs at the clearing-read address, followed by a zero write at absolute address 17 plus this newly read byte.

F-U54 receives the F-U53 result scaled left by two. F-U55 through F-U58 follow, ending at the invalid-instruction recovery marker.

Both clearing reads and both zero writes remain part of the represented sequence. Equal addresses or an already-zero destination do not eliminate an access.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes valid required accesses, applicable arithmetic semantics, and normal return from dependencies preceding the stated observation.

1. **Maximum initial selector and distinct ranges.** If the initial selector is 255, write 1 targets 272 and F-U06 receives 510, 152, and 608. Write 4 targets 523, outside the direct-byte-plus-17 range. Write 6 starts at 546 and writes a full 32-bit word. The first word read starts at 626.

2. **Minimum selector and aliasing.** If the initial selector is zero, write 3 stores zero at address 13, write 4 stores the low byte of the entry word there, and write 5 stores 192 there. Write 6 starts at 36. Dependency effects between these writes remain possible.

3. **Separate F-U18 scalings.** If F-U18 returns 3, write 6 stores 12, F-U19’s third argument is 96, and write 7 stores 96.

4. **Masked pointer argument.** If the pointer word is 2,147,483,652, F-U19’s fourth argument is 1: clearing bit 31 leaves 4, then shifting right by two yields 1. This does not establish validity of accesses using that pointer word.

5. **Earlier paired result retained.** If F-U28 produces low half 7 and high half 2, write 8 stores the entry word at address 28 and the retained scaled value is 28. If F-U35 later returns 9, write 9 stores 28 at address 56, independently of F-U30’s result.

6. **Fresh pointer-based selectors.** If the first and second reads at pointer word plus 1 yield 4 and 9, writes 10 and 11 target 21 and 26 respectively. Reusing the first selector would violate the represented sequence.

7. **Byte truncation.** If the control word is 1,073,741,823, the masked-and-shifted intermediate for write 11 is 1023, but the byte stored is 255.

8. **Independent clears.** If the clearing reads yield 1 and then 2, writes 12 and 13 store zero at addresses 18 and 19. Both writes remain required even if those locations already contain zero.

9. **Interrupted dependency.** If F-U19 does not return, the represented continuation establishes no later explicit access for that execution. Earlier writes are not undone by anything shown.
