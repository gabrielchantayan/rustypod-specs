### Initial direct-memory effects

The opening stores place the shifted base at the terminal high word plus 0x14, place the terminal high word’s low byte at the shifted base plus 0x09, and place the packed base at 256 times the terminal low word plus 0x54. The byte at shifted base plus 0x09 is then overwritten with the shifted base’s own low byte.

Next, the packed base’s word at 0x14 is cleared. A byte is read at packed base plus 0x05. The narrow base’s word at 0x14 is cleared, and the sampled byte is zero-extended into a word store at packed base plus 0x34. The word at packed base plus 0x14 is then read and retained. After another clear at narrow base plus 0x14, its low byte is written twice at narrow base plus 0x15.

The retained word read after the clear must not automatically become a cached zero. Intervening accesses can overlap the cleared storage; ordinary memory behavior and disjointness are not established.

The next absolute read/write boundary is significant:

- The byte at absolute address 0x05 is read and retained as the loaded byte base.
- Absolute address 0x01 receives the narrow base’s low byte.
- Loaded byte base plus 0x15 receives the extracted field’s low byte.
- Packed base plus 0x11 receives the transfer word’s low byte.

Thus, the 0x01 write occurs after the 0x05 read and before those computed writes. Later aliasing cannot retroactively change the retained loaded byte base.

A word is then read at loaded byte base plus 0x54; call this the **loaded anchor**. That location is overwritten with the retained packed-base word, and narrow base plus 0x34 receives the extracted field.

Subsequent updates follow words loaded at packed base plus 0x74 and narrow base plus 0x74.
