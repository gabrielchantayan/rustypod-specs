## Behavior

### Selection contract

Two roles are assigned to observations of the same address-relative field: one supplies a candidate return value, and the other determines whether that candidate is used.

The static representation places the candidate observation before the selection observation. A nonzero selection observation preserves the earlier candidate. A zero selection observation causes the result to come from the fallback instead.

Consequently, a nonzero selection observation does not guarantee a nonzero result. The retained candidate may have been zero. Conversely, a nonzero candidate does not guarantee its own return: a later zero observation selects the fallback.

The field value is not incremented, normalized, range-checked, or otherwise transformed on the field-return path. The relevant distinction is zero versus nonzero, not positive versus negative.

### Stable storage

When the field remains unchanged across both represented observations, the behavior reduces to ordinary zero-based default selection:

- Any representable nonzero field value is returned unchanged.
- A zero field selects the fallback.
- A selected fallback of zero produces zero.
- A selected negative fallback is not rejected merely because of its sign.

There is no second default, retry, or validation stage after fallback selection. The fallback need not be nonzero.
