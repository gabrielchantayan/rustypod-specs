## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

1. **Byte truncation.** If B is `0x123456ab` and the first access completes, the first store writes `0xab` at C + D, regardless of whether later accesses complete.

2. **Signed extension.** If the signed halfword at A + H has bit pattern `0xff80`, the word written at E + 40 is `0xffffff80`.

3. **Retained input.** If E was read as 7, a subsequent change to R + B does not change the retained E used to form E + 40.

4. **Unwritten first halfword.** If no other write aliases Q, the three `0xffff` stores change Q + 2 through Q + 7 while preserving the halfword subsequently read at Q.

5. **Negative dependency identity.** If F is negative and all preceding operations complete, the halfword store at B₀ + 4 precedes abnormal termination. No later store through F belongs to that branch. B₀ still requires unresolved shift semantics.

6. **Repeated accesses.** On the nonnegative branch, both stores of F at address F remain represented accesses; collapsing them would lose potentially observable behavior.

7. **Partial overwrite at P.** The later halfword store at P replaces only two bytes of the preceding word there. Its complete final word cannot be determined without byte order, R₀, and aliasing information.

8. **Fresh G₄ read.** Suppose the self-value indirect store completes, and the next read of G₄ produces address `0x2000`. The subsequent M word store targets `0x2004`, even if the earlier indirect destination was different.

9. **Value width is not store width.** Assigning `0xeaec`, `0x1a`, or `0x5c` establishes those assigned values, not respective halfword or byte destination accesses. Likewise, truncating U shifted right by 17 to 16 bits does not establish a halfword global store.

10. **No success return.** Completing the final constant assignment does not establish success or caller resumption; the next represented endpoint is abnormal.
