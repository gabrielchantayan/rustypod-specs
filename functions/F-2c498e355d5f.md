# F-2c498e355d5f

## Purpose

F-2c498e355d5f describes a fixed sequence of indirect calls, interspersed with three external-data reads and several 32-bit arithmetic transformations.

The established behavior is orchestration: values obtained from earlier calls or external data are supplied to later calls in a specific order. No application-level purpose is established. In particular, the available evidence does not establish that the sequence performs initialization, rendering, communication, memory management, or any other named subsystem operation.

The supplied representation ends at an instruction-decoding boundary. Consequently, this contract describes the visible prefix of execution, not a verified complete routine. It does not establish normal return, deliberate termination, or behavior beyond that boundary.

### Arithmetic and result handling

The two additions of `0xFF` are independent. The first uses F-D03’s result; the second uses F-D09’s result. There is no visible clamp, saturation, range check, or carry propagation.

For F-D07, all three shifted arguments originate from the same low word of F-D06’s represented 64-bit result. They are not derived from one another. Bits shifted beyond bit 31 are discarded under the 32-bit interpretation.

The F-D07 argument order is significant: the high word is second, between the 12-bit and 16-bit shifts of the low word. The representation does not support interpreting this as an ordinary sequence of four adjacent fields without additional evidence.

F-D08 reuses the 16-bit-shifted word computed for F-D07. It does not visibly recompute that word from a new result.

F-D23 forwards both halves of F-D22’s result without a visible arithmetic transformation, then appends `0xFF`.

For F-D30, logical right shift by 16 yields a value in the inclusive range 0 through 65,535. Its first two arguments are equal. The low 16 bits of the underlying secondary word do not contribute to either argument.

Splitting represented 64-bit results into words establishes argument bit positions, not an in-memory byte layout. No endianness-dependent serialization is visible.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional specification examples derived from the visible data flow. They are not reported runtime observations. Each assumes that execution reaches the relevant call and that preceding dependencies return.

### Initial arguments

F-D01 must receive four zero words. If the secondary word available afterward is `0x12345678`, F-D02 receives `0x12345678` followed by zero.

The secondary word’s semantic meaning remains unspecified.

### Addition without saturation

If F-D03 returns `0x00000001`, F-D04 receives `0x00000100`.

Under the 32-bit machine-word interpretation, if F-D03 returns `0xFFFFFF80`, F-D04 receives `0x0000007F`. It must not receive a saturated maximum or an additional carry argument.

The same rule applies independently to F-D09 and F-D10.

### Shifted low word and preserved high word

Suppose F-D06’s represented result has high word `0xA1B2C3D4` and low word `0x12345678`.

F-D07 then receives, in order:

1. `0x45678000`
2. `0xA1B2C3D4`
3. `0x56780000`
4. `0x78000000`

If the secondary word available after that call is `0xCAFEBABE`, F-D08 receives `0xCAFEBABE` and `0x56780000`.

This example does not assign pixel, coordinate, address, or other domain meaning to those words.

### Late external-data observation

If data item A contains one value on entry but F-D04 changes it before returning, F-D05 receives the value read after F-D04, not necessarily the entry value.

Likewise, a change to data item C during F-D18 can affect F-D19’s second argument.

These are conditional examples of the established read timing, not assertions that those callees actually write those locations.

### Two-word forwarding

If F-D22’s represented result has high word `0x11223344` and low word `0x55667788`, F-D23 receives:

1. `0x55667788`
2. `0x11223344`
3. `0x000000FF`

No byte reversal or combination into one wider argument is visible.

### Final upper-half extraction

If the final secondary word is `0xABCD1234`, F-D30 receives `0x0000ABCD`, `0x0000ABCD`, zero, and `0xFFFFFF3B`.

Changing only the low 16 bits of that secondary word leaves the first two arguments unchanged.

### Interrupted progression

If F-D12 does not return, the visible sequence does not reach F-D13 through F-D30. There is no established recovery path that resumes later calls.

This does not prescribe whether non-return means blocking, termination, fault, or another transfer of control.
