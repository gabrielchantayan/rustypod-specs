## Behavior

### Widths and arithmetic

The visible memory operations use 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, and 32-bit words. Two dependency results are represented as 64-bit values.

For each wide result:

- **Low word** means bits 0–31.
- **High word** means bits 32–63.

These are numeric divisions of the represented result, not assumptions about memory byte order.

**Established unsigned arithmetic:**

- The initial destination is the address-like input with its bottom two bits cleared, plus 20, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).
- The first selector address is the hidden word plus one, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).
- Unsigned right shifts introduce zero bits.
- The second low word’s unsigned left shift by two discards bits above bit 31. Its result is four times that word modulo \(2^{32}\).
- Masking the second low word to 30 bits and shifting right by 21 produces a value from 0 through 511. Adding one produces an address from 1 through 512 without overflow.
- Adding 17 to an unsigned selector byte produces an address from 17 through 272 without overflow.

**Signed-arithmetic limitations:**

Several other calculations are represented using 32-bit signed intermediates: index multiplication by four, index left shifts, addition of 20 to scaled indices, and addition of 20 to the saved base. The first wide result’s low word is also converted to a signed intermediate before its left shift.

These expressions must not be silently assigned unsigned wraparound semantics. Signed overflow, negative signed left shifts, and conversions outside the signed range do not establish portable behavior from this representation. Their exact executable behavior remains documented_only.

Where signed calculations are defined and representable, the formulas below give their arithmetic results. They do not establish a universal wrapping rule for those calculations. The incoming link-related target calculation has a corresponding unresolved signed-addition boundary.
