## Behavior

### Indirect invocation prefix

The first indirect target is the incoming control-link value plus 4094. A long straight-line sequence of further indirect invocations follows. No caller-side loop or conditional branch is represented in this prefix.

Represented arguments include shifted primary results, high components of combined results, retained earlier results, secondary outputs, equal argument pairs, masked subfields, and explicit zeros. Left shifts by eight recur, sometimes followed by additional shifts of the already shifted value.

Every invocation is a distinct event. Similar arguments or nearby target expressions do not establish identical dependencies. An invocation with no represented arguments may still consume machine-register state. Explicit zero arguments do not establish that unspecified registers are zero.

Dependencies are not assumed to preserve globals or accessible memory. Their effects may influence later state loads even when their represented return values are unused.

### Arithmetic boundaries

Most subsequent calculations remain 32-bit calculations rather than becoming 64-bit merely because an earlier result was combined.

Established arithmetic properties include:

- Masking to 20 bits and then shifting unsigned right by ten produces 0–1023.
- A 24-bit value shifted left by six is at most 0x3fffffc0.
- A 22-bit value shifted left by eight fits within 30 bits.
- A 32-bit left shift by eight followed by unsigned right shift by eight retains the original low 24 bits.
- Successive left shifts by 25 and seven yield zero **if** each operation retains only its low 32 bits.
- Unsigned shifts of a combined result by 34, 36, and 44 respectively extract its high component shifted right by two, four, and twelve.

Signed left shifts, multiplication, subtraction, and address addition may exceed signed range. Their actual machine interpretation remains **documented_only** where exceptional behavior is possible. Arithmetic conclusions explicitly described below as “low-32-bit” are conditional on retaining the low 32 bits after the relevant operations.

- Adjusted base plus four is 0x0c modulo 16.
- Its four-byte capture cannot include address 0x3a.

The twofold linked base has a different arithmetic dependency. Its offset-0x14 capture can include 0x3a under a consistent choice of linked value.

### Opening mutations and early state capture

The opening writes store a retained word at offset 0x14 through the high component of the final combined result. Two byte writes target offset nine of an earlier derived base. Between them, a word store through the final low component multiplied by 256, at offset 0x54, receives the high-position base.

The high-position base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared, and its offset-five byte is read. The small base’s offset-0x14 word is then cleared before the captured byte is stored, zero-extended, at high-position base plus 0x34.

A word is read from high-position base plus 0x14. This remains a represented read, not permission to replace the access with a constant. Another clear at small base plus 0x14 precedes two equal byte writes at small base plus 0x15, both using the low byte of that captured word.

Only after those byte writes does execution:

- Read fixed-address state at 0x05 and retain its converted value as the early state base.
- Write the low byte of the small base to fixed address 0x01.
- Write the low byte of the small index to early state base plus 0x15.

Later overlap with storage at 0x05 does not change the captured early state base. Earlier overlapping writes, however, may affect the value captured there.

The prefix word supplies a byte at high-position base plus 0x11 and a word stored through the captured opening word at offset 0x54. The byte at high-position base plus 0x11 is subsequently cleared.

The word at early state base plus 0x54 is captured as the retained field value before that field receives the captured opening word. Later arithmetic uses the retained value, not an implicit reload of its former location.

### Linked writes and fixed-address updates

Subsequent accesses use loaded words as addresses, including offset-0x74 loads followed by offset-0x54 writes.

One linked update contains the small base’s offset-0x74 load in both its destination and value expressions. It must not universally be modeled as a single cached read. Evaluation order within that compound expression is not independently established.

The retained field value is stored at its own offset 0x24. Its low byte is written at offset nine through another loaded base. Both effects recur around an intervening offset-0x54 update.

The fixed word update at 0x54 is ordered between related derived writes:

- Small base plus 0x14 receives the high-position-producing dependency result shifted left by 26.
- Fixed address 0x54 receives the retained field value shifted left by one.
- Small base plus 0x14 receives the unshifted retained field value twice.

Four times the retained field value supplies another derived base. Two byte stores at small base plus 0x15 receive its low byte. Immediately afterward, fixed byte 0x15 receives the low byte of the small base. That fixed store precedes the next offset-0x54 write through the fourfold retained-value base and the small base’s offset-0x34 update.

Fixed address 0x15 and a derived address ending in offset 0x15 are separate destinations unless their complete addresses coincide.

Further mutations use twice, four times, and eight times retained or loaded values. They include self-address stores, byte-to-word promotions, and fresh offset-five byte reads. Observable offsets include 0x0d, 0x14, 0x15, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54. Fresh reads reflect preceding writes where addresses overlap; no object invariant licenses stale substitution.
