### Opening storage effects

The last 64-bit call result supplies a low and high word. The opening writes place the tag at high word plus 0x14, the high word’s low byte at tag plus 0x09, and the call-scale at 256 times the low word plus 0x54. The tag-relative byte is then overwritten with the tag’s low byte.

The call-scale-relative word at 0x14 is cleared. An unsigned byte is read at call-scale plus 0x05. A word at small base plus 0x14 is cleared, and the retained byte is promoted into a word write at call-scale plus 0x34. Only then is the word at call-scale plus 0x14 read as the **early saved word**.

**Ordinary storage consequence:** The early saved word is zero if the clear succeeds and storage has ordinary, stable address semantics. Between that clear and the read, the only explicit writes are another zero clear and the word write at the same call-scale base plus 0x34. The latter occupies a disjoint range from the word at offset 0x14. Any overlap caused by the other clear still writes zero. Ordinary address overlap among these writes therefore cannot explain a nonzero early saved word.

A nonzero observation requires an explicitly qualified external modification, read-sensitive behavior, unusual address mapping, or another unmodeled storage effect. Such effects are not established.

Another small-base clear precedes two byte writes of the early saved word at small base plus 0x15.

The symbolic source at 0x05 is then read and converted to the fixed-source base. A low-eight-bit conversion of small base is assigned to symbolic storage at 0x01; the small index’s low byte is written at fixed-source base plus 0x15.

The shifted payload supplies a byte at call-scale plus 0x11 and a word at early saved word plus 0x54. The call-scale-relative byte is explicitly cleared afterward.

The saved base is read at fixed-source base plus 0x54 before that location receives the early saved word. Small index is then written as a word at small base plus 0x34.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Entry shift:** Fourth input 3 supplies 384 to the initial call. Under explicitly assumed 32-bit bit-vector semantics, input 0x02000000 supplies zero after truncation. This assumption does not resolve signed shifts generally.

2. **Unsigned-byte promotion:** An explicit unsigned-byte read yielding 0xfe supplies word value 254, not −2. This does not automatically apply to symbolic storage at 0x05.

3. **Early cleared word:** With successful ordinary storage operations and no external or read-sensitive effects, early saved word is zero, including when the intervening small-base clear overlaps it. A nonzero observation cannot be justified merely by those intervening writes aliasing storage.

4. **Read before scaled-base writes:** If the small-base-relative offset-0x74 word initially contains 7 and a following scaled-base write changes that same storage to 32, the retained doubled value is 14, not 64, assuming defined arithmetic. The later write at saved base plus 0x44 uses 14.

5. **Fixed-source conversion:** If the unsigned conversion from symbolic storage at 0x05 yields 0xfe, the saved-base read uses 0x152. If it yields 0x100, that read uses 0x154. No implicit byte truncation or record stride applies.

6. **Mask placement:** A twenty-four-bit mask of 0xffabcdef followed by an unsigned 32-bit left shift by six yields 0x2af37bc0.

7. **Late copy ordering:** Suppose repeat base plus 0x74 equals working base plus 0x10, the saved offset-0x04 word is 0x11223344, and the retained offset-0x40 word is 0xaabbccdd. The shared destination receives 0x11223344 first and 0xaabbccdd second. Reversing those writes changes the result.

8. **Fresh symbolic observation:** If the assignment at 0x67 supplies low byte 0xab but an explicitly qualified external or read-sensitive effect makes the next read supply 0xcd, the assignment at 0x21 uses the fresh observation, subject to its unresolved destination type.

9. **Final paired stores:** If second terminal argument is 0x1000 and final destination base is 0x2000, the paired writes place 0x1000 at 0x2054, then 0x2000 at 0x2000. Neither argument is replaced by a later reload.

10. **Retained third argument and repeated effects:** Later changes to terminal work base plus 0x14 do not replace the retained third argument. Where storage exposes individual writes, preserving only final contents does not preserve the repeated-store behavior.
