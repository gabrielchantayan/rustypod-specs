### Arithmetic rules and limits

Most consumed intermediates are represented as 32-bit quantities. Selected results have 64-bit representations with separately consumed lower and upper 32-bit components.

Unsigned right shifts are logical. Shifting an unsigned 64-bit result right by 34, 36, or 44 yields its upper 32-bit component shifted right by 2, 4, or 12 respectively.

Masks and shift order are significant. For example, selecting the low 24 bits before shifting left by six yields at most 0x3FFFFFC0. A 32-bit left shift followed by another shift is not unlimited-precision multiplication: under modulo-\(2^{32}\) interpretation, shifting left by 28 and then by 28 leaves zero.

Some arithmetic uses signed intermediates. Signed overflow, shifts of negative values, oversized shift counts, conversion to addresses, and address wraparound remain **documented_only** where target semantics are needed. ## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

### Extracted-field boundaries

Third arguments 0 and 1023 yield field zero; 1024 yields one; 0x000FFFFF and 0xFFFFFFFF yield 1023; 0x00100000 yields zero.

For field one, the three scales produce 256, 32,768, and 65,536. The scale-32,768 operation receives two equal arguments. Changing bits outside 10–19 preserves this extraction, not necessarily complete execution.

### Address-5 declaration conditions

If independent evidence establishes an **unsigned eight-bit** global at address 5, and it evaluates to 0xE1, the saved base is 225. Its offset-0x15 byte destination is 0xF6; its offset-0x54 word source is 0x135.

Without that declaration, this byte-based conclusion is unavailable. If instead an independently established unsigned 32-bit global evaluates to 0x1234, the saved base is 0x1234 and those addresses are 0x1249 and 0x1288, assuming defined address arithmetic.

An independently established signed eight-bit source holding −31 would convert to unsigned 32-bit value 0xFFFFFFE1, not 225.

### Narrowed value versus access width

The assignment at address 0x67 supplies 0xAB when the saved value’s low eight bits are 0xAB. This alone does not establish a one-byte store.

If both globals at 0x67 and 0x21 are independently established as unsigned eight-bit objects and remain unchanged between assignment and re-read, the copy to 0x21 supplies 0xAB. Wider declarations require their own access footprints and conversions.

### Global 0x3A assignment

If the adjusted value’s character conversion yields one, the promoted expression supplied to the global assignment is 0x38.

If that conversion yields unsigned 255, the expression is 2088, or 0x828. An unsigned eight-bit destination would retain 0x28; a sufficiently wide unsigned destination could retain 0x828. A declaration is required to choose between them.

### Ordering and overlap

The saved association base is obtained before the assignment at 0x60. Overlap cannot justify replacing it with a later global evaluation.

The 0x3A assignment precedes the two saved-word reads described above. Any overlapping effect must be interpreted using the actual global declaration and target memory rules, not an assumed one-byte footprint.

Later evaluations of 0x67 must observe applicable intervening changes; substituting its originally assigned value is not generally equivalent.

### Explicit widths

An explicitly unsigned byte 0xE1 widened into a word supplies 225. A sixteen-bit store of 0x0065 is not a byte store of 0x65.

The working-base offset-0x20 history remains 0x20, 0x2F, the freshly widened byte, then 0x20 for an observer sensitive to those accesses.
