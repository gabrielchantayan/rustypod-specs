### Arithmetic interpretation

Most address and argument transformations have represented 32-bit intermediates. Under a truncating 32-bit machine interpretation, additions, multiplications, and left shifts retain the low 32 bits at each relevant intermediate step. Unlimited-precision arithmetic is not an equivalent substitute.

Consequences under that interpretation include:

- Two successive left shifts by 28 produce zero.
- A value first shifted left by 14 becomes zero after a further left shift by 23.
- A value shifted left by 16 has zero low 16 bits.
- Multiplication by 0x02000000 retains information from only the original low seven bits.

These are bit-vector consequences, not claims about portable semantics for signed overflow.

Unsigned right shifts are logical where established by the representation. A late signed right shift uses a count selected from another quantity’s low byte. Negative-operand handling and counts outside the normal 32-bit range remain **documented_only**.

- **Shifted token:** another dependency result shifted left by eight.

The first mutation region establishes these ordering requirements:

- The byte at link token +0x09 first receives the upper portion’s low byte. Between that store and its overwrite with the link token’s low byte, the packed base is stored at the final composite’s lower portion multiplied by 256, +0x54.
