# F-053b0183abbf

## Purpose

F-053b0183abbf performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, derives address-like values from their results, mutates shared and indirectly addressed memory at several widths, and reaches a final indirect dispatch with four represented operands.

**Established static evidence:** The supplied representation contains extensive call-result dependencies, 32-bit transformations, selection of portions of paired results, repeated stores, explicit global accesses, and retained memory values used after intervening mutations.

**Inferred intent:** Some updates resemble links between records. Constants such as 0x3A, 0x20, and 0x65 resemble character values. Neither observation establishes record management, formatting, textual output, or a device protocol.

**Contract boundary:** This is a source-facing contract for represented behavior, not a complete executable replacement specification. Entry requirements, dependency contracts, memory mappings, and some arithmetic and control-transfer semantics remain **documented_only**.

## Interface

### Entry and inputs

The nominal interface declares no parameters and no return value. Its effective inputs nevertheless include:

- An incoming control-link value used to derive the first indirect target.
- Two incoming machine-state values used in early shifted operands.
- Primary and secondary results of indirect operations.
- Paired results represented with 64-bit containers.
- Global values and values read from indirectly addressed memory.

An empty declared parameter list does not establish that an ordinary parameterless invocation supplies sufficient execution context.

## Behavior

### Preparation and indirect interactions

The preparation phase is a long ordered chain of indirect operations. Many targets are derived by adding 0xFFE to control-derived or embedded location-like values.

The chain consumes earlier results, retains selected values across subsequent calls, passes shifted operands, separates paired-result portions, duplicates operands in multiple positions, and supplies explicit zeros. Calls with no displayed operands are still interactions. An unused primary result does not make its producing call removable: secondary results or external effects may matter.

No alternative preparation route is visibly selected by a local condition. Dependencies may still branch, fail, fault, or prevent continuation. Their effects must not be replaced by a presumed pure computation of the values eventually used for mutation.

### Arithmetic boundaries

Explicit unsigned extractions include masked fields, logical right shifts, and portions selected from paired results. A logical shift of an unsigned 64-bit container by 44 selects its high 32-bit portion shifted right by 12, not its low portion.

Represented mask families include low 24-, 22-, 21-, 20-, 19-, 18-, and 17-bit regions, together with smaller fields selected by 0x3FFF, 0x1FFF, 0xFFF, 0x7FF, and 1. Masking and shifting order matters.

Several expressions successively shift 32-bit values far enough that a truncating machine interpretation would discard all contributing bits. That simplification is conditional: signed left-shift and overflow behavior are not established merely by the represented widths.

The late variable-count right shift has a **signed 32-bit left operand**. Its count is the low eight bits of a derived address-like value. Two separate uncertainties apply:

- Counts of 32 or greater have no established portable 32-bit shift result.
- For a negative operand, even an in-range count does not establish zero-filling under portable signed-integer semantics.

The contract neither masks that count to five bits nor assigns arithmetic or logical shifting to a negative operand without platform evidence.

### Initial mutation and early globals

Immediately before explicit mutation, preparation supplies:

- A base formed by multiplying a result by 2²⁵.
- A compact quantity formed by shifting another result left by eight, masking with 0x7FFF, and logically shifting right by six; doubling this quantity supplies another base.
- A base formed by shifting an earlier result left by 25.
- Low and high portions of a final paired result.

These are arithmetic values, not validated references.

The first update group stores a word at the high paired-result portion plus 0x14, writes a byte at the shifted base plus 9, stores the large scaled base through the low paired-result portion multiplied by 256 plus 0x54, and rewrites the byte at offset 9.

Subsequent clearing and propagation preserve read boundaries: a word at the large scaled base plus 0x14 is cleared; a byte at offset 5 is read; the doubled compact base plus 0x14 is cleared; and the byte is stored as a zero-extended word at the large base plus 0x34. The large-base word at 0x14 is then read, followed by another compact-base clear and repeated byte stores at offset 0x15.

The following global interactions are explicit:

| Global address | Access and value dependency | Ordering |
|---|---|---|
| 0x00000005 | Read a global value, converted to unsigned 32 bits and retained as an indirect base. | Before the write at 0x00000001. |
| 0x00000001 | Write the low byte of the doubled compact base. | Before the retained global-derived base is used for its offset-0x15 byte store. |
| 0x00000054 | Write a 32-bit value formed by doubling a retained previous word. | Between a compact-base offset-0x14 word store and later replacement stores there. |
| 0x00000015 | Write the low byte of the doubled compact base. | After two byte stores at that base plus 0x15, before the next scaled-base update group. |

The declaration of the global at 0x00000005 is not supplied. Its conversion and subsequent use are visible; its storage width is **documented_only** rather than established solely by that conversion.

The “previous word” above is read from the retained global-derived base plus 0x54 before that location is replaced. This retained word supplies later doubled, fourfold, and eightfold values. It must not be recomputed by rereading its original storage.

### Relationship-like updates and aliasing

The middle region uses retained words, newly loaded words, and byte-selected bases to update offset locations. Observable constraints include:

- Retaining a previous word before replacing its location.
- Loading words at offset 0x74 and using them as further bases.
- Reading unsigned bytes at offset 5 and using those byte values as bases for word reads at offset 0x74.
- Repeating self-relative stores and byte stores.
- Writing fourfold and eightfold versions of the retained previous word.
- Retaining a word read through offset 0x64.
- Reading an offset-5 byte again after intervening mutations rather than reusing its earlier value.

A byte-selected base is not the full-width address of the apparent containing record.

Aliasing is unrestricted by the evidence. A word store at offset 0x14 spans the byte at offset 0x15. Named globals may also overlap indirectly addressed destinations. A contract based only on disjoint “fields” or final values would lose supported behavior.

### Constants, embedded global, and repeated updates

For the remaining description, the following are descriptive mathematical quantities, not source identifiers:

- **Retained previous word:** the old offset-0x54 value described above.
- **Adjusted value:** four times that retained word, reduced by 150.
- **Fourfold adjusted base:** four times the adjusted value.
- **Eightfold adjusted base:** eight times the adjusted value.
- **Selected word:** the later word obtained through an unsigned-byte-selected offset-0x74 read.
- **Doubled selected base:** twice the selected word.
- **Fourfold selected base:** four times the selected word.
- **Embedded base:** a word read from the embedded global at 0x087FBBBC.

All arithmetic remains subject to the width and signedness limitations already stated.

The constant region writes 0x3A as a byte at four times the selected word, as a halfword at eight times that word, and as a word at the fourfold location plus 0x20.

At the fourfold adjusted base plus 0x20, the represented word-store sequence is 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from that base plus 4, and 0x20 again. The read occurs between the second and third stores.

**The embedded global is then read before the 32-bit write of 0x20 at 0x00000060.** Its retained value supplies the embedded base used by subsequent updates. The write at 0x00000060 cannot be assumed unrelated to that global or to indirect destinations without mapping evidence.

The repeated region maintains ordered updates at offset 0x54 of an eightfold-derived destination, byte offset 0x19 of the embedded base, byte offset 0x11 of the fourfold adjusted base, and word offset 0x34 of the embedded base.

The first group differs from subsequent groups: its offset-0x54 destination uses eight times the retained previous word, and its embedded-base offset-0x34 value is that same unadjusted eightfold quantity. Later groups use the eightfold adjusted base. The byte values continue to derive from the fourfold adjusted base and embedded base.

Repeated events must not be collapsed into one final assignment merely because displayed destinations and values recur. Exact correspondence to machine-level repetition remains **documented_only**.

### Final globals and retained reads

The final region has an important ordering boundary:

1. A 32-bit global at 0x000000A9 is read and retained.
2. A word at the fourfold selected base plus 0x74 is read and retained.
3. The byte global at 0x0000003A is written.
4. A word at the doubled selected base plus 0x14 is read and retained.
5. A word at the fourfold adjusted base plus 4 is read and retained.

This describes the order of significant memory observations, not an implementation prescription.

The byte written at **0x0000003A** is the low-byte result of **eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30**. It is not simply a copy of a retained address byte. Its write precedes the following offset-0x14 and offset-4 word reads; those reads may observe it if their accessed ranges overlap that global.

The next mutation group writes the previously derived byte values at selected offsets, writes 0x6C at the fourfold adjusted base plus 8, and temporarily stores the doubled selected base into its own offset-0x54 location.

The global byte at **0x00000067** then receives the low byte of the retained word originally read at 0x000000A9. The subsequent observations of 0x00000067 are **three distinct reads**:

- After an indirect word read at the doubled selected base plus 0x44, the first read supplies the byte write at **0x00000021**.
- After additional indirect writes, the second read supplies a byte write at the fourfold adjusted base plus 9.
- After further writes and a word read at the fourfold adjusted base plus 0x54, the third read supplies another byte write at that same offset-9 destination.

The later reads are not specified to reproduce the first read or the value initially assigned to 0x00000067. Intervening indirect writes can alias that global and change what each read observes.

### Final mutation and dispatch

The remaining region preserves these constraints:

- A loaded offset-0x44 word supplies temporary byte and word writes and the signed variable-shift operand.
- The embedded-base offset-0x54 word is temporarily assigned that loaded word, then replaced by the fourfold adjusted base.
- A word at the fourfold adjusted base plus 0x40 is read before being stored at offset 0x10.
- The retained global word from 0x000000A9 is stored at offset 0x44, which is subsequently replaced.
- The retained doubled-selected-base offset-0x14 word is restored at offset 0x54.
- A later offset-4 word selects a doubled address for the halfword store of 0x65.
- A further indirect base receives the word constant 100 at offset 0x54.
- A final word store uses twice a retained offset-0x64 loaded value as its destination.

The terminal dispatch receives, in order:

1. Constant 0x72.
2. A retained word loaded through offset 0x64.
3. The earlier retained word from the doubled selected base plus 0x14.
4. A later retained word loaded through offset 0x74.

These operands are retained values, not fresh reads after mutation.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional review scenarios, not executed tests.

### Unsigned propagation

If an explicitly unsigned byte source contains 0xFE when reached, its subsequent word propagation carries 254, not a sign-extended negative value.

### Early global ordering

If the read at 0x00000005 supplies an indirect base, the write at 0x00000001 occurs afterward, before the represented offset-0x15 update through that retained base. Moving the read after the write is unjustified where overlap or read-sensitive storage is possible.

### Adjusted-value byte

If the retained previous word is 46 and the relevant arithmetic is defined, the adjusted value is 34. The byte written at 0x0000003A is therefore the low byte of 320: **0x40**.

That write precedes the subsequent retained offset-0x14 and offset-4 word reads.

### Separate global-byte observations

Suppose the retained word from 0x000000A9 has low byte 0x12. With no intervening aliasing change, the first read of 0x00000067 supplies 0x12 to 0x00000021.

If an intervening indirect write changes 0x00000067 to 0xA5 before its second read, the later offset-9 byte store uses 0xA5. Reusing a retained 0x12 would violate the read boundary. The third read independently observes the then-current contents.

### Mixed-width overlap

If a word store begins at a base plus 0x14, a later byte store at that base plus 0x15 modifies part of that word’s storage. The resulting numeric word depends on byte order.

### Paired extraction

If a paired result has high portion 0x12345678, logically shifting the complete unsigned container right by 44 yields 0x00012345.

### Signed variable shift

If the late signed operand is negative and the count is in range, zero-filling is not an established requirement. If the low-eight-bit count is 0x88, no result is promised without target-specific semantics; silently reducing it modulo 32 is unsupported.

### Retention and interrupted execution

If later writes alter storage from which a terminal operand was retained, dispatch still uses the retained value. If an earlier dependency never returns, later accesses and dispatch need not occur; rollback is not promised.
