# F-a89e8fc39c63

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven prefix

F-opaque-01 receives the first argument, the third argument shifted left by five bits, the unchanged third argument, and the first argument again.

Its result is represented as two 32-bit words. The following dependency receives the lower word shifted left by eight bits, the unchanged upper word, and the third explicit argument shifted left by five bits.

A long indirect-call prefix follows without a represented conditional branch or explicit loop. Later operations are reached only if earlier dependencies return.

Recurring interactions include:

- Forwarding an upper word unchanged and in shifted forms.

These dependencies are not established as pure arithmetic helpers. Calls without explicit arguments may still access memory, consume implicit state, fail, or not return. Repeated argument shapes do not identify a common implementation.

### Width and arithmetic rules

Words and address candidates are predominantly 32 bits. Some results are represented as 64-bit quantities for extraction.

A logical right shift of such a result by 32 selects its upper word. Shifts by 34, 36, and 44 select that upper word shifted logically right by 2, 4, and 12 bits.

Masks of different widths are used and are not interchangeable. The body includes masks retaining 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits. Masking an already shifted word must not be replaced by masking the original word without proving equivalence.

Byte stores retain eight bits; halfword stores retain sixteen. Byte values explicitly widened to words are zero-extended.

Sequential word shifts retain their intermediate width. Under truncating 32-bit arithmetic, shifting a word left by 28 and then left by 28 leaves zero, rather than producing a 64-bit left shift by 56.

Signed left shifts, signed scaling, address addition, and signed right shifting require execution-model corroboration. Modular 32-bit arithmetic is used below only where expressly conditional. Exceptional variable shift counts and right shifts of negative signed values remain **documented_only**.

### Opening memory effects and first fixed-storage read

The first direct store writes the shifted base as a word at offset `0x14` from the upper word of the last two-word result. A byte at shifted-base offset `9` first receives the low byte of that upper word. A separate word destination, formed from the last lower word multiplied by 256 plus `0x54`, receives the scaled base. The shifted-base byte at offset `9` is then overwritten with the shifted base’s own low byte.

Next, scaled-base offset `0x14` receives a zero word. A byte is read from scaled-base offset `5`; small-base offset `0x14` receives zero; and the saved byte is zero-extended into a word at scaled-base offset `0x34`.

The word at scaled-base offset `0x14` is then reloaded. Small-base offset `0x14` receives zero again, followed by two identical byte stores at small-base offset `0x15`, each using the low byte of the reloaded word. The reload cannot be replaced with a remembered zero without additional memory and aliasing guarantees.

The next fixed-storage interaction reads the value at address `0x05` and converts it to a 32-bit value used as a base. Its original access width is not declared in the supplied material and remains **documented_only**. This read precedes the byte store to fixed address `0x01`, which receives the low byte of the small base.

The captured base from `0x05` then participates in computed accesses: its offset `0x15` receives the extracted value’s low byte, and its offset `0x54` is read to obtain the linked word before being overwritten with the earlier scaled-base reload. Writing fixed address `0x01` does not itself trigger a reread of `0x05`.

### Linked mutations and remaining early fixed writes

The following region combines self-referential stores, repeated byte stores, and address-producing word loads through offsets including `4`, `0x64`, and `0x74`. It does not establish structure boundaries.

A word loaded from scaled-base offset `0x74` supplies a destination base. A separate expression loads small-base offset `0x74` twice, once for destination construction and once for the stored word. Both loads are represented; their relative evaluation order is not established.

After repeated stores through the linked word and the destination obtained from scaled-base offset `0x74`, small-base offset `0x14` receives a shifted dependency-derived word. Fixed address `0x54` then receives a 32-bit word equal to the linked word shifted left by one. Small-base offset `0x14` is subsequently overwritten twice with the linked word.

Two byte stores at small-base offset `0x15` use the fourfold base’s low byte. Fixed address `0x15` then receives the small base’s low byte.

Later chaining includes a byte read at a computed offset `5`, widened and used as a base for an offset-`0x74` word read. These byte-indexed accesses are not equivalent to following a word-sized pointer at offset `5`.

The later word obtained through this chain supplies the late doubled and late quadrupled bases. The region writes `0x3a` as a byte at the late quadrupled base, as a halfword at twice that base, and as a word at its offset `0x20`.

At repetition-base offset `0x20`, successive word writes contain `0x20`, `0x2f`, a zero-extended byte read from repetition-base offset `4`, and `0x20` again.

**The represented ordering next loads the repeat pointer from fixed address `0x087fbbbc`, then writes the 32-bit word `0x20` to fixed address `0x60`.** The pointer load is not represented after the fixed-word write. Machine-level confirmation of this ordering is unavailable.

### Repeated region

The repeated region uses the captured repeat pointer, not repeated loads of fixed address `0x087fbbbc`.

Its opening group writes the late quadrupled base at eightfold-base offset `0x54`, then writes:

- The repetition base’s low byte at repeat-pointer offset `0x19`.
- The repeat pointer’s low byte at repetition-base offset `0x11`.
- The eightfold base as a word at repeat-pointer offset `0x34`.

Later groups instead use the repeated destination for the word destination at offset `0x54` and for the value stored at repeat-pointer offset `0x34`. The byte-store relationship remains the same.

The first offset-`0x34` value therefore differs in derivation from subsequent values. Duplicate writes and interleaving are represented effects, not permission to retain only final memory contents. Exact group multiplicity remains **documented_only** in this contract.

After the repeated region, the following ordering and dataflow are established:

| Phase | Required relationship |
|---|---|
| Initial reads | A 32-bit word is read from fixed `0xa9`, followed by a word from late-quadrupled-base offset `0x74`. |

Thus the third transfer argument is saved before the intervening mutations, not reread near the transfer.

The word from late-doubled-base offset `0x44` supplies subsequent stores, including a byte obtained by right shifting it by the low byte of the repetition base. Counts outside the ordinary 32-bit range have no specified normalization rule.

Further mutations save a word from repetition-base offset `0x40`, place the earlier repetition-base-offset-`4` word at repeat-pointer offset `0x74`, and write the offset-`0x40` word at repetition-base offset `0x10`. The last operation overlaps the byte destination at offset `0x10`; no resulting combined value is specified without endianness assumptions.

Fixed `0x67` is read again for a byte store at repetition-base offset `9`. Between that read and another read of fixed `0x67`, the function writes a byte at late-doubled-base offset `9`, writes the saved fixed-`0xa9` word at repetition-base offset `0x44`, and loads a word from repetition-base offset `0x54`. The second fixed-byte read then supplies another store at repetition-base offset `9`.

These are distinct reads. Computed stores may alias fixed `0x67`, so the later read is not required to match the earlier one.

The newly loaded repetition-base-offset-`0x54` word becomes the **mutation base**. Its offset `0x0d` receives the low byte of the saved late-quadrupled-base-offset-`0x74` word. Late-doubled-base offset `0x54` is restored from the saved third transfer argument.

A word is then saved from repetition-base offset `0x14`. Afterward, late-doubled-base offset `0x11` receives the saved offset-`0x74` word’s low byte, repetition-base offset `9` receives the mutation base’s low byte, and repetition-base offset `0x44` receives the mutation base as a word.

### Final argument-producing loads and transfer

The final sequence has these alias-sensitive ordering constraints:

1. **Address capture and halfword mutation.** A word loaded from late-doubled-base offset `4` becomes the **final address**. A 16-bit store of `0x65` occurs at twice the final address before either remaining transfer argument is loaded.
2. **Second argument capture.** A 32-bit load from final-address offset `0x64` supplies the future second argument.
3. **Intervening mutations.** Mutation-base offset `0x11` receives the low byte of the word saved from repetition-base offset `0x14`. Mutation-base offset `0x54` then receives the 32-bit word decimal `100`.
4. **Fourth argument capture.** Only after those stores, a word loaded from final-address offset `0x74` supplies the future fourth argument.
5. **Destination capture.** A separate word is loaded from late-doubled-base offset `0x64`. This is a different base from the offset-`0x64` load producing argument two. It determines the base of the subsequent offset-`0x54` word destination.
6. **Final mutations.** Late-doubled-base offset `9` receives the final address’s low byte. The destination captured in the preceding phase receives argument two at offset `0x54`. A word store at twice argument two then writes that captured destination base.
7. **Transfer.** F-opaque-02 receives `0x72`, saved argument two, the earlier saved third argument, and saved argument four.

The captures remain the supplied arguments even if later stores overwrite their original memory locations. Conversely, earlier stores can affect later captures through aliasing. Moving all argument loads to the end is not equivalent.

## Acceptance examples

These are static conditional criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial argument order.** With first argument `0x12345678` and third argument `3`, F-opaque-01 receives `0x12345678`, `96`, `3`, and `0x12345678`.

2. **Two-word propagation.** If its lower and upper results are `0x00123456` and `0x89abcdef`, the following invocation receives `0x12345600`, `0x89abcdef`, and `96`.

3. **Width boundary.** Under truncating 32-bit shifts, third-argument bit pattern `0x08000000` produces zero after shifting left by five. This does not skip subsequent operations.

4. **Small address.** Under that model, an originating word with low seven bits `0x7f` produces extracted value `508` and small base `1016`.

5. **Unsigned byte widening.** A scaled-base-offset-`5` byte of `0xfe` contributes word value `254` to the associated offset-`0x34` store.

6. **Fixed-byte reread.** If a computed store changes fixed `0x67` between its two later reads, the subsequent repetition-base-offset-`9` store uses the newly read byte, not the earlier captured byte.

7. **Fixed-pointer timing.** The represented repeat-pointer capture precedes the fixed-`0x60` word write. Subsequent memory changes do not themselves cause another pointer fetch.

8. **Final capture boundary.** If the decimal-`100` store overlaps final-address offset `0x74`, argument four reflects the later load. Argument two remains its earlier captured value.

9. **Saved third argument.** If intervening stores change late-doubled-base offset `0x14`, argument three remains the value saved before those mutations.

10. **Distinct offset-`0x64` loads.** The final-address load supplies argument two; the later late-doubled-base load supplies a store destination. Equal offsets do not make their roles interchangeable.

11. **Failure.** A dependency that does not return prevents requirements for later effects; earlier effects need not be undone.
