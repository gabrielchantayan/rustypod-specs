# F-270b33a97c43

## Purpose

F-270b33a97c43 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms dependency-derived values, modifies computed and fixed-address memory locations, and ends with an unresolved four-argument invocation whose first argument is 0x72.

The supplied static evidence does not establish an application-level purpose. It does not justify describing the function as an initializer, formatter, collection operation, or protocol handler. Printable constants do not establish a text-output interface.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** widths, data relationships, accesses, and ordering represented in the supplied material.
- **Inferred intent:** interpretations that are not behavioral requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior whose meaning or executable semantics remain unresolved.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven computation

The initial phase is a long, straight-line sequence of indirect invocations interleaved with scalar transformations. No explicit conditional branch, retry, loop, or dependency-error branch is represented.

Invocation order is part of the static behavior. Similar arguments do not establish identical target identities. Calls with unused results cannot be discarded on the assumption that they lack side effects.

The data relationships include:

- Left shifts of 32-bit result values, commonly by eight bits.
- Further shifts of already shifted values.
- Extraction and separate forwarding of low and high components.
- Forwarding high components either unchanged or transformed.
- Literal zero arguments in multiple positions.
- Reuse of one transformed value in multiple argument positions.
- Masking before shifts or extraction.
- Consumption of additional machine-result state not described by a complete dependency signature.

Masks of several distinct widths occur, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit. These operations must not be collapsed into one generic field extraction. A mask width also does not establish that the original result was restricted to that width.

For example, retaining the low 24 bits and then shifting right by eight yields at most a 16-bit numeric value. Retaining the low 20 bits and shifting right by ten yields at most ten bits.

Right shifts of a combined 64-bit result must retain their operand width. A shift by 36 extracts the high component shifted right by four; it is not a 32-bit shift by 36.

**documented_only:** The dependency map supplies no opaque IDs for the indirect targets. This contract therefore does not assign them invented identities or application-level meanings.

### Arithmetic boundaries

Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, a left shift discards bits shifted beyond bit 31. Later shifts cannot recover those bits. Consequently:

- Shifting left by eight discards the original high byte.
- Shifting left by 25 and then by 11 yields zero.
- Shifting left by 14 and then by 23 yields zero.
- Shifting left by eight, then 18, then eight yields zero.
- Shifting left by sixteen leaves the low sixteen bits zero.

These are conditional width constraints, not proof that every displayed signed operation has fully recovered executable semantics. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, and address arithmetic remain **documented_only** where the underlying semantics are unavailable.

A later right shift uses the low byte of a computed base as its count. The count can therefore be represented by values from zero through 255. The behavior for counts of 32 or more, and the exact signed-right-shift semantics, are unresolved.

### Entry into direct memory effects

The following descriptive labels refer to captured values, not verified object types:

- **Primary base:** a retained dependency-derived word scaled by 0x02000000.
- **Extracted value:** bits 6–14 of another result after its represented eight-bit left shift.
- **Secondary base:** twice the extracted value.
- **Retained payload:** an earlier result shifted left by eight.
- **Retained link:** a retained low result component shifted left by 25.
- **Final pair:** the combined result of the last invocation before direct memory accesses.

Under ordinary 32-bit bit-vector semantics, the extracted value is a multiple of four between zero and 508. The secondary base is therefore a multiple of eight between zero and 1016. These numeric constraints do not establish valid storage.

The opening memory effects establish and overwrite several relationships before proceeding:

- The retained link is stored as a word at offset 0x14 from the high component of the final pair.
- Its offset-nine byte receives the low byte of that high component, then receives the low byte of the retained link after an intervening word store.
- The intervening word store publishes the primary base at offset 0x54 from the low final-pair component scaled by 256.
- Primary-base offset 0x14 is cleared before a byte is read at primary-base offset five.
- Secondary-base offset 0x14 is cleared before that captured byte is zero-extended into a word at primary-base offset 0x34.
- Primary-base offset 0x14 is then read as a word. Secondary-base offset 0x14 is cleared again, followed by two byte stores of the captured word’s low byte at secondary-base offset 0x15.

The word read after the primary clear cannot be replaced unconditionally with zero. Intervening destinations may alias it, and the environment’s memory semantics are not established.

### Low-address state and loaded links

Next, a byte read from absolute address 0x05 is zero-extended and used as a base. The low byte of the secondary base is written to absolute address 0x01.

The extracted value’s low byte is written at offset 0x15 of the byte-derived base. The primary base temporarily receives the retained payload’s low byte at offset 0x11. Between that byte store and its clearing, the full retained payload is stored at offset 0x54 from the previously captured primary-offset-0x14 word.

The word at byte-derived-base offset 0x54 is captured before being replaced with that previously captured word. The extracted value is then stored as a word at secondary-base offset 0x34.

Subsequent accesses follow words loaded at offsets 0x74 and 0x64 and use them as further bases. The effects include:

- Word stores containing their own destination-base values.
- Byte stores containing truncated address-like quantities.
- Repeated stores to the same locations.
- Replacement of earlier payload stores by later relationship stores.
- Reads through bases obtained from zero-extended bytes.

The word captured from byte-derived-base offset 0x54 is scaled by two, four, and eight. Scaled results serve both as payloads and as bases. Twice that captured word is written as a word at absolute address 0x54. A truncated secondary-base value is written to absolute address 0x15.

These accesses have no represented null-address or low-address guard. Where one displayed access contains multiple reads without an independently established evaluation order, this contract does not invent a finer ordering.

### Constant writes and repeated publication

A later region writes 0x3A with three distinct widths: a byte at one scaled base, a halfword at another scaled base, and a word at offset 0x20 from the first scaled base.

Another derived base is formed by subtracting 0x96 from a previously computed quantity and scaling the result by four. No underflow or range check is represented. At that base plus 0x20, the represented word-store order is:

1. 0x20.
2. 0x2F.
3. A zero-extended byte read from that base plus four.
4. 0x20 again.

A global word is captured as a publication base after this sequence and before the word store of 0x20 to absolute address 0x60.

The publication region repeatedly establishes relationships among this captured global base, the derived base, and scaled destinations. Each group contains, in order:

- A word store at a scaled base plus 0x54.
- A byte store at global-base offset 0x19 containing the derived base’s low byte.
- A byte store at derived-base offset 0x11 containing the global base’s low byte.
- A word store at global-base offset 0x34 containing the relevant scaled base.

The first group uses a different scaled relationship from the subsequent repeated groups. The global base is not reloaded for each group.

These are repeated static stores, not evidence of a retry protocol or synchronization mechanism. Their exact multiplicity is **documented_only** in this contract; no runtime repetition count is asserted.

### Final stores and terminal invocation

The terminal sequence distinguishes a late-loaded base, the second terminal argument, and a separate destination-base word. They are not interchangeable.

The **late-loaded base** is read from offset four of the retained base. A halfword containing 0x65 is written at twice that late-loaded base. The **second terminal argument** is then captured as a word from late-loaded-base offset 0x64.

Before the fourth argument is captured, the sequence writes a truncated earlier value at offset 0x11 of another loaded base and writes decimal 100 as a word at that base’s offset 0x54. The **fourth terminal argument** is then captured from late-loaded-base offset 0x74.

A separate **destination-base word** is subsequently read from retained-base offset 0x64. A byte containing the late-loaded base’s low byte is written at retained-base offset nine.

The last two direct word stores have distinct roles and this order:

| Order | Destination | 32-bit payload |
|---|---|---|
| First | Destination-base word plus 0x54 | Captured second terminal argument |
| Second | Twice the captured second terminal argument | Captured destination-base word |

Thus, the separate offset-0x64 load supplies the first store’s base and the second store’s payload. It does **not** supply both payloads. Both values are captured before these stores; aliasing does not turn either store into a fresh load.

The terminal invocation receives:

1. Literal 0x72.
2. The captured word from late-loaded-base offset 0x64.
3. The earlier captured word from retained-base offset 0x14.
4. The captured word from late-loaded-base offset 0x74.

Its target is unresolved and its result is not visibly consumed.

## Side effects

### Memory ordering and aliasing

The direct-memory phase is not transactional. No represented action restores earlier stores if a later access or invocation fails.

No bases are established as disjoint. Byte stores may change portions of later word reads; word stores may cover earlier byte stores. A captured value retains the value obtained at its read point, whereas a later read observes memory at its own position in the sequence.

In particular, the two final word-store destinations may coincide. Their order still matters: the second store then overwrites the first with the destination-base word.

Byte order is not established. Exact byte patterns produced by overlapping accesses of different widths are **documented_only** unless the environment supplies endianness.

No synchronization, atomicity guarantee, barrier, or reentrancy guard is represented.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional criteria, not executed tests.

### Width-sensitive forwarding

If a reached forwarding operation shifts 0x12345678 left by eight with 32-bit bit-vector semantics, the forwarded value is 0x34567800. A widened value retaining the discarded high byte does not satisfy that condition.

### Masked extraction

If a reached operation retains the low 24 bits and shifts right by eight, input 0xAB123456 yields 0x1234. Producing 0xAB1234 incorrectly retains bits outside the mask.

### Duplicate memory events

If execution reaches the two consecutive byte stores at secondary-base offset 0x15, both represented stores use the same captured low-byte payload. Merely preserving the final byte value does not preserve that access sequence.

### Aliased clear and read

With ordinary memory semantics and no intervening overlapping destination, the primary-offset-0x14 read after its clear yields zero. If intervening writes overlap it, acceptance depends on those writes rather than on the clear alone.

### Mixed-width constants

The 0x3A stores retain their byte, halfword, and word widths. Treating all three as character writes does not preserve the represented contract.

### Distinct final payloads

Assume valid, nonoverlapping storage, no arithmetic overflow, second terminal argument 0x2000, and destination-base word 0x3000. The final stores write:

- Word 0x2000 at address 0x3054.
- Then word 0x3000 at address 0x4000.

Writing 0x3000 to both destinations is incorrect.

### Coincident final destinations

Assume second terminal argument 0x2000 and destination-base word 0x3FAC. Both final destinations are then 0x4000. The first store writes 0x2000; the second writes 0x3FAC. Under ordinary memory semantics, the resulting word is 0x3FAC, while the captured second terminal argument remains 0x2000.

### Terminal arguments survive intervening stores

If the last stores overlap the original locations from which terminal arguments were captured, the invocation still receives the captured values. Re-reading those source locations at invocation time would change the represented behavior.

### Failure without rollback

If a reached computed address faults after earlier stores complete, no represented behavior restores those earlier locations. Acceptance must not require transactional recovery.
