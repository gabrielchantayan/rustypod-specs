### State assignment and memory writes

After F-D07 returns, its result is assigned to the state object named `SupervisorCall`.

Next, the function performs these writes in order:

1. Store one zero byte through the additional-output pointer.
2. Store a zero-valued two-byte quantity at the address obtained by logically shifting the incoming unsigned 32-bit register value right by nine bits.

Only after both stores complete does the function attempt F-D08.

For the second store, the logical right shift produces an address in the inclusive range `0x00000000` through `0x007FFFFF`. No base address is added. The low nine bits of the source value do not contribute to this address.

A two-byte write starting at the maximum derived address also reaches the next byte, `0x00800000`. The shift does not guarantee two-byte alignment: its result can be odd.

The function does not establish that the two destinations are distinct. They may overlap each other or other relevant state, subject to the execution environment. No non-aliasing or isolation guarantee is supported.

For negative results, or positive results above `536,870,911`, the representation alone is insufficient to promise a defined signed-language result. A machine-level interpretation may use a 32-bit shifted bit pattern, but that is not established as a portable arithmetic contract.
