## Behavior

### Ordering and semantic roles

The following descriptions preserve represented access order. Earlier captures are not interchangeable with later rereads. Storage may overlap fields, external sources, argument storage, or stack-associated storage. Repeated writes remain distinct represented effects; independently observable physical transactions are not established.

The narrative uses these semantic roles:

- **Anchor:** a word discovered in selection one and assigned to stack-associated offset `0x34`.
- **Current base:** the base receiving recurring offset-`0x11` byte writes and offset-`0x54` self-references; initially the ambient base.
- **Carried word:** a captured word passed between selections.
- **Retained link:** initially captured from selection one’s selected base at offset `0x74`, then replaced in selection five.
- **Selected base:** the word obtained from a selection’s external source.
- **Saved anchor word:** the most recently saved word from anchor offset `0x64`.

These labels identify dependencies, not types, ownership, or isolated storage. Stack-associated assignments do not establish protection against aliasing.

### Entry mutations

The first memory effect writes the fourth input at ambient-base offset `0x11`.

Three reads follow: a word at first-input offset `0x64`, a word at ambient-base offset `0x44`, and a signed halfword at ambient base plus ambient index. Only then does second-input offset `0x54` receive the ambient base.

The first captured word’s low byte replaces ambient-base offset `0x11`. The captured ambient-base word identifies a base whose offset `0x44` receives the ambient index.

Next come word reads at entry-halfword plus `0x44`, then ambient-index offset `0x14`. The earlier replacement byte is written again at ambient-base offset `0x11`; the latter word is narrowed into a halfword store at entry-halfword plus ambient index.

Ambient-index offset `0x44` then supplies the initial carried word. Ambient-base offset `0x54` receives the ambient base itself.

### Selection one: anchor discovery

After the common actions, ambient-base offset `0x11` receives the low byte of the ambient base shifted left once.

A signed halfword at ambient base plus selected base selects a word read at that halfword value plus `0x44`. This word becomes the anchor and is assigned to stack-associated offset `0x34`. Selected-base offset `0x14` is then reread.

The entry-derived word read from entry-halfword plus `0x44` identifies a **word** read at offset `0x64`; its low byte replaces ambient-base offset `0x11`. Narrowing the value does not narrow the access.

Anchor offset `0x04` is captured, then selected-base offset `0x74` establishes the retained link. The anchor-offset-`0x04` capture is stored at ambient-base offset `0x54`.

A halfword at twice the ambient base plus selected base receives the narrowed selected-base-offset-`0x14` capture. Selected-base offset `0x44` supplies the outgoing carried word. Ambient-base offset `0x54` then receives the ambient base itself.

### Selections two and three: early traversal

Selection two writes the saved anchor-offset-`0x04` byte at ambient-base offset `0x11`. It reads anchor offset `0x64`, ambient-base offset `0x44`, and a signed halfword at ambient base plus selected base, in that order.

Anchor offset `0x54` receives the retained link. A fresh selected-base-offset-`0x14` read establishes the current base, whose offset `0x0d` receives the selected base’s low byte. A halfword at the captured signed-halfword value plus selected base receives the low 16 bits of the ambient-base-offset-`0x44` capture.

Selected-base offset `0x44` supplies the outgoing carried word, followed by a current-base-offset-`0x54` self-reference.

Selection three writes the saved anchor-offset-`0x64` byte at current-base offset `0x11`, captures current-base offset `0x44`, and reads a signed halfword at current base plus selected base. Anchor offset `0x54` receives the retained link again, before selected-base offset `0x14` advances the current base.

The advanced base’s offset `0x0d` receives the selected base’s low byte. Independently, incoming-carried-word offset `0x14` supplies a traversal result. Retained-link offset `0x54` receives the anchor.

The halfword destination is the signed-halfword value plus traversal result, not plus selected base. It receives the low 16 bits of the earlier current-base-offset-`0x44` capture. Traversal-result offset `0x44` supplies the outgoing carried word; the advanced current base then receives itself at offset `0x54`.

### Selections four through six: character-derived addressing

Each selection writes the low byte of current base shifted left once at current-base offset `0x11`, reads a signed halfword at current base plus selected base, and reads a character at that halfword value plus incoming carried word.

The character participates in address arithmetic and is converted to a halfword for storage. Both uses depend on unresolved character signedness.

**Selection four:** selected-base offset `0x1d` receives the retained link’s low byte. The converted character is stored as a halfword at signed-halfword value plus selected base. Selected-base offset `0x44` supplies the outgoing carried word, followed by the current-base self-reference at offset `0x54`.

**Selection five:** a fresh word from anchor offset `0x64` is written at signed-halfword-derived-base offset `0x54`. Character-derived-base offset `0x0d` receives the incoming carried word’s low byte. A word read at selected-base offset `0x64` replaces the retained link.

After that replacement read, the character converted to a halfword is stored at the sum of the signed-halfword-derived base and the selected base. This store precedes the selected-base-offset-`0x44` outgoing carried-word read. The current-base self-reference follows.

**Selection six:** the saved anchor word is refreshed before the signed-halfword and character reads. Anchor offset `0x01` receives the selected base’s low byte; selected-base offset `0x0d` receives the incoming carried word’s low byte.

Selected-base offset `0x44` then receives the replacement retained link. Character-derived-base offset `0x0d` receives the selected base’s low byte. The converted character is stored as a halfword at signed-halfword value plus selected base. Selected-base offset `0x44` is reread for the outgoing carried word, followed by the current-base self-reference.

The intervening byte or halfword store may overlap that reread field. Its outgoing value is therefore not necessarily the retained link just written.

### Selections seven and eight: distinct traversal sources

The halfword at signed-halfword value plus traversal result receives the narrowed capture. ### Selections nine and ten: sum-addressed mutations

Selection nine writes the low byte of current base shifted left once at current-base offset `0x11`, refreshes the saved anchor word, captures current-base offset `0x44`, and captures a signed halfword at current base plus selected base.

Its value is the low 16 bits of the earlier current-base-offset-`0x44` capture. This store precedes the outgoing carried-word read at selected-base offset `0x44`. The advanced current base then receives itself at offset `0x54`.

Selection ten initially writes the saved anchor-derived byte, refreshes the saved anchor word, captures current-base offset `0x44`, and captures a signed halfword.

Incoming-carried-word offset `0x54` receives the old current base. The refreshed anchor word’s low byte is written at that old current base’s offset `0x11`. The captured offset-`0x44` word’s offset `0x44` receives the selected base, before the read at signed-halfword value plus `0x14` advances the current base.

The remaining sequence matches selection nine: captured-base self-reference; retained-link word store at selected base plus incoming carried word; selected-base-offset-`0x14` replacement; fresh retained-link-offset-`0x44` read supplying the selected-base-offset-`0x0d` byte; and the captured word’s low byte written at advanced-current-base offset `0x0d`.

Then the halfword destination is the pre-advance signed-halfword value plus selected base, and its value is the low 16 bits of the earlier current-base-offset-`0x44` capture. It occurs after both offset-`0x0d` writes and before the outgoing selected-base-offset-`0x44` read. The advanced current-base self-reference follows.

Thus selections three through ten each end with a current-base self-reference after the outgoing carried-word read. Additional captured-base self-references in selections nine and ten do not replace these writes.

### Selection eleven: product-derived state

The remaining sequence reads second-product-base offset `0x44`, then anchor offset `0x74`. Selected-base offset `0x64` receives the retained product word, and selected-base offset `0x09` receives the first read’s low byte.

Second-product-base offset `0x44` is separately reread; its low byte is written at that base’s offset `0x11`. Another halfword-derived self-reference follows. The retained product word’s low byte is written at twice the second product base plus `0x11`.

The captured anchor-offset-`0x74` word’s low byte is written at retained product word plus `0x01`. The fresh base then receives the further product-derived word’s low byte at offset `0x11`, followed by twice the second product base’s low byte at offset `0x09`.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

1. **Entry order:** The fourth input is written before the first word read.
2. **Read width:** A word containing `0x123456ab` supplies byte `0xab`; substituting a byte-only access does not satisfy the established word read.
3. **Signed selection:** Halfword bits `0xfffe` contribute −2, not 65534, wherever used in address arithmetic.
4. **Selection-five destination:** With captured signed halfword −2 and selected base `0x2000`, valid arithmetic and memory imply a halfword destination of `0x1ffe`. The converted character is stored after the retained-link replacement read and before the outgoing carried-word read.
5. **Selection-nine and selection-ten destination:** In either selection, a captured signed halfword of −2 and selected base `0x4000` imply destination `0x3ffe`. A prior offset-`0x44` capture of `0x12345678` supplies halfword `0x5678`, irrespective of the subsequently advanced current base. Both offset-`0x0d` byte writes precede this store.
6. **Character boundary:** Character bits `0xfe` may convert to halfword `0xfffe` or `0x00fe`; associated addresses remain conditional on signedness.
7. **Aliasing reread:** In selection six, overlapping narrow writes can change the outgoing carried word after the retained-link assignment.
8. **Repeated effects:** Selection three performs its own anchor-offset-`0x54` write. Selections three through ten retain their final current-base self-references even when other self-reference writes occur.
9. **Product capture:** The retained product word comes from first-product-base offset `0x74`, before the second multiplication.
10. **Final capture order:** Selected-base writes may affect the second-product field’s reread, but not its earlier capture. A retained-product-word-plus-`0x01` write cannot retroactively change the already captured fresh anchor-offset-`0x14` base.
11. **Unresolved byte:** Its early use cannot be replaced with zero or its later assigned value.
12. **Calls and arithmetic:** A non-returning first call prevents the seventh-input clear. A narrow destination does not legitimize overflowing signed arithmetic.

All address examples require every preceding access and arithmetic operation to be valid.
