## Behavior

### Arithmetic and width rules

Ordinary arithmetic is predominantly represented using 32-bit words. Direct computed-address writes normally affect one byte. The two halfword reads each obtain 16 bits; the late pointer-directed word write affects 32 bits.

“Low byte” means the least significant eight bits of a value. “High byte” of an unsigned 32-bit interpretation means bits 24 through 31. These descriptions identify bit patterns without attaching signed meaning to stored bytes.

Unsigned operations support ordinary 32-bit bit-vector reasoning. Signed multiplication and left shifts do not independently establish wraparound semantics. Consequently, numerical examples involving those operations are conditional on defined arithmetic and valid addresses. This contract does not silently widen calculations or impose modulo arithmetic on unresolved signed expressions.

The rightward extraction of the upper half of a represented 64-bit result is distinct from the unresolved operation that shifts a represented 32-bit signed value rightward by 32 positions. The former identifies a 32-bit half; the latter has no established result here.

The body contains many unresolved indirect invocations. Their ordering is observable even where no ordinary result is subsequently used.

### Retained-value provenance

The later byte values and larger-scale destinations are not all derived from the most recent dependency return.

**Saved unsigned result and saved byte.** One unsigned 32-bit dependency result is retained. Its low byte after multiplication by eight becomes the saved byte. The initial destination for that byte is sixteen times the retained unsigned result. Later uses preserve the same byte even when fresh dependency results supply different addresses.

That retained unsigned result also supplies a later extracted-field byte. The represented mask retains its lowest 29 bits; a logical rightward displacement by 19 positions and byte truncation leave original bits 19 through 26. Original bits 27 and 28 do not survive the byte write.

**Earlier scaled result.** A separate retained word originates at an earlier entry-byte boundary: it is that boundary’s producing result scaled by 16,384. Later, the saved byte is written to a destination scaled by 65,536 from this retained word, and an associated call receives two equal words scaled by 134,217,728 from it.

The same retained word is also scaled by 32,768 to establish another retained value. That derived value subsequently supplies a destination scaled by 4,096, two equal call arguments scaled by 8,388,608, and the high byte used for three-bit propagation.

Thus, the high byte used later is not an unconstrained fresh byte. **Conditionally, under ordinary 32-bit wraparound arithmetic,** the combined scaling retains only the original producing result’s lowest three bits, placed in positions 29 through 31. The extracted high byte then has its lowest five bits clear, and its rightward displacement by five recovers those three bits. This simplification is not unconditional because the signed scaling semantics remain unresolved.

**Fresh larger-scale destinations.** Other saved-byte writes use fresh results scaled by 8,192, with equal call arguments scaled by 16,777,216. These coexist with the retained-value destinations; they are not interchangeable.

**Retained auxiliary scalar.** After the auxiliary pointer is introduced, a separate auxiliary word is retained. It later supplies a byte destination scaled by 2,048 and two equal call arguments scaled by 4,194,304. The same scalar is supplied unchanged as the second argument at four later boundaries whose first argument comes from a fresh result scaled by four.

Much later, another two-argument boundary uses the retained product associated with the four-byte write as its second argument. That product is not the earlier auxiliary scalar.

### Three-bit propagation and unresolved addressing

The saved high byte is repeatedly displaced rightward by five to supply a byte between zero and seven. A separate saved copy of that three-bit value supports subsequent writes. Near the end, the original saved high byte is itself replaced by its three-bit form; later writes use that form directly. The value is not repeatedly reduced by another factor of 32 at each destination.

One destination depends on the unresolved full-width right shift described earlier. Its unresolved result supplies both a doubled byte-write address and two equal arguments scaled by 4,096. Neither address nor arguments may be replaced with zero or any other guessed value.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static-review cases, not executed tests. Address validity, arithmetic semantics, and reaching the relevant boundary are prerequisites.

1. **Entry-byte propagation.** For an applicable producing result of 5 and entry byte 0xA7, the byte destination is numeric address 40. The write affects one byte. At an ordinary associated boundary, both explicit arguments are 81,920.

2. **Four-argument position.** With producing result 5 and additional entry word 3, the exceptional boundary receives 81,920 in positions one, two, and four, and 3,072 in position three. Three equal values followed by the entry-derived value would be incorrect.

3. **Literal zero.** An entry byte of 0xA7 does not affect a literal-zero boundary. That boundary writes zero.

4. **Saved-byte lifetime.** A retained unsigned result of 0x23 supplies saved byte 0x18 and initial destination 0x230. Later saved-byte writes still use 0x18 despite different address-producing results.

5. **Retained scaling.** Under 32-bit wraparound semantics, an originating result of 7 supplies retained scaled word 114,688. Scaling that word by 32,768 gives bit pattern 0xE0000000; its high byte is 0xE0 and the later three-bit byte is 7.

6. **Field truncation.** A retained unsigned result of 0x1D580000 yields a ten-bit intermediate field of 0x3AB. Its stored byte is 0xAB.

7. **Read-after-write aliasing.** If the first halfword source overlaps its immediately preceding byte destination, the read observes memory after that write. The resulting halfword depends on byte order and the other addressed byte.

8. **Scaled pointer read.** For retained pointer numeric value 0x100, the second halfword source is 0x8020, not 0x120. The eventual four-byte write through that pointer instead targets 0x100.

9. **Paired-result ordering.** Low portion 0x11223344 and high portion 0x55667788 produce the ordered triple low, high, high at a three-argument paired-result boundary.

10. **Late overlap.** If the late byte destination overlaps the pointer-directed four-byte destination, the byte write happens first. The word write can overwrite it. The subsequent three-equal-argument call occurs only after both writes.

11. **No repeated narrowing.** Once the propagated byte is 7, later direct uses write 7, not zero from an additional rightward displacement.

12. **Unresolved endpoint behavior.** Neither a fabricated result for the full-width shift nor a specific terminal exception is an unconditional acceptance expectation. Reaching the represented endpoint does not promise normal return.
