# F-b78a14481506

## Purpose

F-b78a14481506 performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, derives values from their results and incoming machine state, then mutates memory through computed and fixed addresses before one final indirect invocation.

The supplied static representation does not establish an application-level purpose. Character-like constants, scaled addresses, and recurring offsets do not establish formatting, allocation, parsing, initialization, or a verified data structure.

This is an interaction-and-memory contract. Its observable behavior is not determined by the three explicit inputs alone.

Evidence classifications used throughout:

- **Established static evidence:** accesses, conversions, invocations, and ordering explicitly represented in the supplied material.
- **Conditional deduction:** consequences under a specified arithmetic or memory interpretation.

## Interface

## Behavior

### Dependency phase and arithmetic

The opening dependency phase is represented as straight-line work without a controlling conditional branch or loop. Each invocation follows the return of its predecessor. Invocations with no displayed arguments or unused results remain observable; their complete interfaces are unknown.

Arguments include primary words, low and high portions of 64-bit results, secondary results, zeros, repeated values, and shifted or masked derivatives. Results are not visibly tested as success flags, bounds, null pointers, or error codes.

The representation distinguishes 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, 32-bit words, and 64-bit aggregates. Aggregate extraction above bit 31 occurs before narrowing: shifts by 34, 36, and 44 correspond to extracting the high word and logically shifting it by 2, 4, and 12 bits respectively.

Under a **32-bit bit-vector interpretation**:

- Left shifts and multiplication discard bits above bit 31.
- Successive left shifts by 28 and 28 produce zero.
- Successive left shifts by 31 and 30 produce zero.
- Masking to 24 bits and shifting left by six produces at most a 30-bit value.
- Shifting left by eight and logically right by eight retains the original low 24 bits.
- Shifting left by eight, masking with 0x7FFF, then logically shifting right by six produces four times the original low seven bits: 0–508 in multiples of four.

These are conditional arithmetic deductions, not confirmation of executable instruction semantics. Signed-looking overflow and shifts cannot be assigned portable language behavior from this material.

### Mutation-boundary terminology

The following labels describe values, not verified object types or implementation identifiers:

- **Shifted base:** a retained result shifted left by 25 bits.
- **Scaled base:** another retained result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Compact value:** the seven-bit-derived multiple of four described above.
- **Compact base:** twice the compact value.
- **Final low word** and **final high word:** the portions of the last aggregate dependency result.
- **Retained shifted word:** an earlier dependency result shifted left by eight.

Under the stated bit-vector interpretation, the compact base ranges from 0 to 1016 in multiples of eight. Treating it as an ordinary allocation-backed object would change the represented addressing behavior.

No intermediate dependency invocation appears between the start of direct mutation and the final invocation.

### Opening mutation and first fixed-location accesses

The opening group writes the shifted base as a word at final-high-word-relative offset 0x14. A byte at shifted-base offset 9 first receives the low byte of the final high word. A word at 256 times the final low word plus 0x54 then receives the scaled base, after which the same shifted-base byte receives the low byte of the shifted base.

The scaled-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared before a byte is sampled at offset 5. The compact-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared before that sampled byte is zero-extended and stored as a word at scaled-base offset 0x34. The scaled-base word at offset 0x14 is then read and retained. The compact-base word is cleared again, followed by two byte writes at compact-base offset 0x15 using the low byte of the retained word.

**Only after those two byte writes**, the fixed location **0x05 is read**. Its value is converted to an unsigned 32-bit value and used as an address base. The declaration of the fixed storage is absent, so its actual read width and any narrower-value extension rule are **documented_only**; the conversion alone does not prove a byte read.

Next, **one byte at fixed address 0x01 is written** with the low byte of the compact base. This precedes a byte write at offset 0x15 from the base obtained through 0x05, carrying the low byte of the compact value.

The scaled-base byte at offset 0x11 receives the low byte of the retained shifted word. A word relative to the previously retained offset-0x14 value receives that shifted word at offset 0x54. The scaled-base byte at 0x11 is then cleared.

A 32-bit word is subsequently read at offset 0x54 from the base obtained through 0x05. Call this the **link word**. That location is overwritten with the earlier scaled-base-relative offset-0x14 value. Thus, the link word is a retained read result, not a guaranteed description of the location’s later contents.

### Linked updates and fixed writes at 0x54 and 0x15

The next accesses combine the compact base, the link word, and words loaded at offset 0x74. They include self-relative stores, repeated byte writes, and word stores at offsets 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, and 0x54.

One operation reads the compact-base-relative offset-0x74 word twice: once to establish a destination base and once to establish the stored value. Both reads participate; their relative evaluation order is not established.

The compact-base word at offset 0x14 receives a value derived from the result underlying the scaled base. **After this computed-address store, fixed location 0x54 receives the link word shifted left by one bit.** The representation presents a word-valued global write, but the fixed symbol’s declaration is absent; its exact storage width is **documented_only**, rather than independently verified as 32 bits.

These fixed and computed locations are not assumed disjoint. In particular, the fixed byte write at 0x15 can overlap a computed word or byte access.

### Byte widening

### Fixed-access ordering

### Fresh fixed-byte reads

These conditions do not determine the final contents of 0x21 if subsequent accesses also alias it.

### Intermediate states and termination

An observer able to observe the workspace offset-0x20 stores can distinguish 0x20, 0x2F, the widened sample, and 0x20. One final store is not equivalent.
