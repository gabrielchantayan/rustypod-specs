### Widths and arithmetic

A byte is 8 bits, a halfword is 16 bits, and a word is 32 bits in the supplied representation. Some dependency results are represented as 64 bits.

Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Unsigned byte reads widened to words produce values from 0 through 255. Halfword stores retain sixteen bits; their width is not interchangeable with byte or word stores carrying the same numeric constant.

The dependency region uses masks selecting fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit. Mask placement matters: selecting bits before a displacement is not generally equivalent to selecting them afterward.

Unsigned right displacements are logical in the represented expressions. Displacements of 64-bit results by 34, 36, or 44 bits are wide-result extractions, not excessive shifts of a 32-bit operand.

Under a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector interpretation**, left displacements and multiplication by powers of two retain only the low word. Successive left displacements whose total reaches or exceeds 32 can eliminate all bits. Such arithmetic simplification does not justify deleting intervening dependency interactions.

Signed-overflow behavior, signed-left-shift semantics, address wraparound, and the late signed right displacement are not independently established as machine behavior. In particular, the late variable shift uses a count selected from the low eight bits of a computed word; the representation does not establish results for every possible count.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial forwarding.** With second input 3, the first invocation receives 3 unchanged and 192 in its shifted position. The first input remains unchanged.

2. **Width boundary.** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, second-input bit pattern `0x04000000` becomes zero after the initial six-bit displacement.

3. **Equal truncated bytes.** If the two early offset-`0x09` writes derive from words ending in the same byte, both writes still occur in their represented order, with the intervening word write preserved. Unequal bytes are not required.

4. **Byte widening.** A reached unsigned byte read of `0xFE` contributes word value 254 when widened, not −2.

5. **Initial-group distinction.** Under non-overflowing arithmetic, captured link `0x100` gives adjusted quantity `0x36A`. The initial first-word destination is `0x854`; subsequent first-word destinations are `0x1BA4`. The corresponding final word values are `0x800` and `0x1B50`.

6. **Global capture timing.** If the `0x60` store overlaps the global word through the target’s addressing model, the repeated region still uses the value captured before that store.

7. **Late capture timing.** If the `0x3A` write overlaps the word at `0xA9`, the retained word is the one read before that write, while subsequent captures observe memory at their own later points.

8. **Fresh `0x67` reads.** If an intervening computed mutation changes `0x67`, a later working-base offset-`0x09` assignment uses the newly read byte, not necessarily the byte originally stored there.

9. **Wide extraction.** A logical displacement of a 64-bit result by 36 equals its high word logically displaced by four; substituting a 32-bit displacement by 36 is not equivalent.

10. **Final transfer.** On reaching the final target, the displayed arguments are `0x72`, the captured final offset-`0x64` word, the earlier linked-base offset-`0x14` capture, and the captured final offset-`0x74` word.
