# F-af9bc0871b42

## Purpose

F-af9bc0871b42 advances a counter held in caller-provided storage. Most invocations change only that counter. An invocation beginning with counter 31 instead leaves the counter at zero and increases an adjacent associated quantity by four.

The boundary is exact. Beginning at 30 advances to 31 without changing the associated quantity; beginning above 31 does not cause a reset. Negative counter values are not rejected or normalized.

**Inferred intent:** The operation may track progress in groups of 32, with four units added to an associated quantity for each completed group. Neither the meaning of those units nor a requirement that callers maintain a counter between zero and 31 is established.

This contract describes behavior supported by static evidence. It does not report runtime observations.

## Interface

### Input, output, and layout

The operation receives one pointer and has no return value. Its results are changes to the designated storage.

The pointer identifies the associated quantity. The counter is the next consecutive element of the same signed-integer type.

| Element position | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Element designated by the argument | Associated quantity |
| Immediately following element | Counter |

The separation is one element size, not an established number of bytes. The evidence does not establish integer width, pointer width, element size, numerical alignment, byte order, or signed representation. In particular, an increase of four is a numerical change, not evidence of a four-byte storage unit.

There is no supplied length, status destination, configuration value, or separate result location.

### Storage requirements

The caller must supply a pointer with a valid lifetime, suitable alignment, and an object arrangement permitting access to the following element. Mere adjacency of unrelated objects is not enough to establish that relationship.

The counter must contain a readable signed-integer value and must be writable. When the invocation takes the boundary path, the associated quantity must also contain a readable value and be writable. On other defined paths, its value is not read or replaced.

This path-dependent access requirement does not remove the need for a valid pointer and valid consecutive-element addressing. It does mean that a nonboundary invocation does not require an arithmetic interpretation of the associated quantity’s contents.

The operation does not explicitly allocate, release, retain, or transfer the supplied storage. The caller remains responsible for making that storage available for the invocation. Ownership before or afterward is not otherwise established.

### Arithmetic domain

Let **M** denote the maximum representable value of the signed-integer type.

For defined arithmetic:

- The initial counter must not exceed **M − 1**.
- If the initial counter is 31, the associated quantity observed during boundary processing must not exceed **M − 4**.

Both adjustments are positive, so they introduce no additional restriction at the minimum representable value.

These conditions are preconditions, not validations performed by the operation. There is no error result through which a violation is reported.

## Behavior

### State transformation

For valid storage, no interfering modification, and representable arithmetic, the required final state is:

| Initial counter | Final counter | Associated quantity |
|---|---:|---|
| 31 | 0 | Increased by four |
| Any other admissible value | Initial counter plus one | Not accessed |

The boundary decision comes from the same initial counter observation that supplies the increment. It is not based on a later reread of the stored counter.

Consequently:

- Reaching 31 is different from beginning at 31.
- A counter above 31 continues increasing rather than being brought back into a nominal range.
- A counter of −1 becomes zero without a boundary adjustment.
- A final counter of zero alone does not identify which path occurred.
- The associated quantity does not influence the branch.

There is no general modulo operation, saturation, range repair, or rejection of negative values.

### Access and ordering guarantees

Nonboundary behavior excludes access to the associated quantity, not merely a change to its final value. An otherwise equivalent operation that unnecessarily reads that quantity would not match the established abstract access behavior.

The boundary path has an intermediate counter state of 32 before the reset to zero. The reset precedes the associated-quantity observation used for the increase. The associated quantity is therefore not specified as a snapshot taken at entry alongside the counter.

These are abstract evaluation and storage-order properties. They do not establish a separately visible machine write of 32, an exact instruction sequence, or a physical memory-access count. The evidence does not identify the accesses as volatile or atomic.

The update of the pair is not an established atomic transaction. No synchronization is supplied, and the intermediate-state description does not authorize unsynchronized inspection by another execution context.

### Arithmetic limits and invalid operations

The counter increment must be representable even on the boundary path. Resetting the counter later does not remove that requirement.

An initial counter of **M − 1** becomes **M** without accessing the associated quantity. An initial counter of **M** would require an unrepresentable addition. The boundary comparison is not an overflow check.

For a boundary invocation, an associated quantity of **M − 4** becomes **M**. A larger value would require an unrepresentable result. Negative associated quantities are allowed when the storage and arithmetic requirements hold.

On a nonboundary invocation, even an associated quantity equal to **M** does not prevent successful advancement: that quantity does not participate in the operation.

Under the signed-arithmetic semantics represented by the supplied material, unrepresentable addition lies outside defined behavior. No wraparound, saturation, exception, diagnostic, or recovery result is specified. Target-specific manifestations remain unresolved.

In particular, the ordering of a boundary-path reset before the associated-quantity adjustment does not guarantee that the reset survives an invalid addition. There is no promised partial commit or rollback once the invocation falls outside the defined domain.

### Repeated invocations

With valid storage, no intervening modification, and representable arithmetic throughout, a counter beginning at zero reaches 31 after 31 invocations. The next invocation resets it and increases the associated quantity by four.

For an initial counter between zero and 31 inclusive, the next boundary transition occurs after **32 minus the initial counter** invocations.

This grouping property is not universal over signed inputs. A counter initially above 31 increases toward its arithmetic limit without encountering the boundary. A negative counter can advance toward the boundary over successive defined invocations.

Repeated boundary transitions also consume the associated quantity’s available range. A cycling counter does not establish that indefinite use is valid.

## Evidence

**Established static evidence:** The supplied operation accepts one pointer and returns no value. Its storage references concern a designated signed-integer element and its consecutive successor. One initial counter observation determines advancement and boundary selection. Boundary processing resets the counter before observing the associated quantity for its increase. No explicit calls, validation, diagnostics, or memory-management operations appear.

**Derived behavior:** Within representable arithmetic, an initial counter of 31 uniquely selects the boundary transition. The state tables, path-dependent access requirements, range limits, and repeated-invocation properties follow from those observations.

**Inferred intent:** Tracking groups of 32 with a four-unit associated increase is plausible, but application meaning and caller invariants are not established.

**Evidence limits:** Target representation, physical execution details, invalid-operation outcomes, concurrency arrangements, and application-wide ownership remain unresolved. No runtime tests have been performed.
