# F-244c266b815a

## Purpose

F-244c266b815a is a four-argument, globally controlled byte-transformation routine. Internal dispatch selects argument capture, preparation, resets, byte updates, transitions, or a zero return.

**Established static evidence:** direct stores are one byte wide. Invocation-local working values persist across dispatch iterations. Several transformation paths replace values subsequently used for addressing, arithmetic, or preparation by other selectors.

**Inferred intent:** the calculations resemble multiple transformation passes over encoded data. No encoding, traversal order, or complete pass sequence is established.

**Unknown:** global values and the reachable transition graph are unavailable. Preparation before use, valid addresses, termination, and normal completion are not guaranteed.

### Arithmetic and observation conventions

Calculations use represented 32-bit arithmetic except where explicit character, short, byte, or sixteen-bit conversions intervene. They must not be interpreted as unrestricted mathematical integers.

The following descriptive symbols identify global roles:

- \(E\): multiplier captured once at entry.
- \(s,m,a\): address scale, index multiplier, and additive word.
- \(k,w,r\): mask, contribution, and recurrence multipliers.
- \(p,h,z\): predicate scale, byte-combination multiplier, and combination offset.

Except for \(E\), these symbols denote observations at the described points, not immutable configuration. Repeated occurrences may represent separate global reads.

For compact description, define:

\[

\[
L(x;m,a,k)=mx+a+E\sim T(x;k),
\]

\[
D(x;m,a,k)=L(x;m,a,k)+aT(x;k).
\]

These describe partial and completed decodes. They do not authorize replacing repeated global observations with one snapshot.

For character-valued operands, define:

\[
B(x,y;t)=
\operatorname{char}(E)
\left[
(\operatorname{char}(h)x-1)
\mathbin{|}

The subtractions feeding this OR have unsigned 32-bit behavior in the representation. Separate appearances of \(h\) can be separate reads.

A byte store retains the low eight bits. Masking with `0xFFFF` retains sixteen bits. Masking with `0xFFFEFFFF` clears bit 16 only; higher bits remain.

- Replaces the helper with the low sixteen bits of shared arithmetic state times a recurrence-multiplier observation.

**Selector 9** places the completed primary decode in both the index-expression and contribution holders. It captures the destination base in the first auxiliary base. It does not replace the index/offset holder.

These are conditional static obligations, not executed tests.

1. **Immediate completion:** an initial selector of 5 returns zero after entry and dispatch observations, without a dependency call or direct byte access.

2. **Selector 12 recurrence grouping:** if its previous primary value is 1 and its post-store recurrence and mask observations are 2 and 3, the retained result is
   \[

3. **Shared-state precedence:** selector 14 with zero neighbor working state assigns `0xC2FB` to shared arithmetic state. A later selector 6 replaces it with the third argument; reversing the order reverses precedence.

4. **Mask boundary:** `0x00030001` masked with `0xFFFEFFFF` becomes `0x00020001`, not `0x00000001`.

5. **Neighbor-state persistence:** second and third derived indices of 7 and 11 leave secondary state 7 and primary state 11 after the neighbor path. Subsequent selector 1 consumes 7 unless another selector replaces it.

6. **Source-to-destination-only transition:** after selector 8, selector 3 uses the source-derived index-expression holder for addressing but the previously retained contribution holder. After selector 12, both holders reflect that selector’s replacements.

7. **Neighbor-to-source transition:** absent intervening replacement, selectors 8 and 12 consume the index/offset holder left by neighbor processing. Selector 8 uses it directly; selector 12 scales it.

8. **Observation timing:** dependency mutation of the additive global does not replace retained iteration-entry additive observations. A selector 12 store aliasing recurrence storage can affect its subsequent recurrence observation. Selector 8’s store cannot retroactively change its already captured recurrence multiplier.

9. **Aliased neighbor access:** a later neighbor read aliasing an earlier store observes the intervening write under ordinary sequential memory semantics.

10. **Unhandled selector:** it executes the neighbor path and requires its addresses to be valid. A reachable cycle avoiding selector 5 has no established normal return.
