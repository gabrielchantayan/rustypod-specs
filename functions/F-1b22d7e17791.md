# F-1b22d7e17791

## Purpose

F-1b22d7e17791 combines two operands, each supplied as two 32-bit words, and returns a 64-bit pattern.

**Established static behavior:** shared masks select ordinary paired-word arithmetic or exceptional handling. Exceptional handling can preserve an operand, construct a result from high-word information, or delegate to an unresolved indirect target. Ordinary arithmetic includes alignment, carries, subtraction, normalization, and branch-specific rounding.

**Inferred intent:** binary64-style addition, using accumulation for matching signs and magnitude subtraction for different signs.

This is not an unconditional IEEE 754 addition contract. Shared values, some target-machine arithmetic semantics, and the indirect-transfer ABI are unavailable. Numerical interpretations do not supersede the word-level requirements below.

## Behavior

### Entry classification

For each operand, add `0x00100000` to its high word, double the result, and retain 32 bits. Ordinary arithmetic is selected only when both resulting words intersect the G-mask value read for their respective tests.

The first operand’s test precedes the second. If the first fails, the second test and its G-mask read are skipped. Otherwise, the second test uses another G-mask read.

These configurable tests are not independently established classifications of finite values, zeros, infinities, or NaNs.

### Exceptional operand selection

Exceptional handling reads G-mask again and sets bit `0x00200000` to form a selection mask.

Direct operand selection applies when the selection mask contains at least one bit absent from doubled A-high and at least one bit absent from doubled B-high. Doubling wraps to 32 bits. The first condition short-circuits the second.

Within direct selection, return the first applicable result:

1. If doubled A-high intersects the selection mask, return A unchanged.
2. Otherwise, if doubled B-high intersects it, return B unchanged.
3. Otherwise, if both low words are zero and both high words are `0x80000000`, return A unchanged.
4. Otherwise, return all-zero bits.

An operand selected by either intersection rule retains its entire low-word payload. No payload modification or quieting is established.

### Exceptional token resolution

When the direct-selection condition fails, form two 32-bit tokens. Each token is its operand’s doubled high word, plus one if its low word is nonzero. Arithmetic wraps to 32 bits. The magnitude of a nonzero low word does not affect its token.

Both tokens are formed before their mask comparisons.

Direct token resolution requires **both tokens to be at most the selection mask**. Compare A-token first. If it is greater, dispatch without comparing B-token against the mask. Otherwise, compare B-token and dispatch if it is greater.

When both tokens qualify, return:

| Condition | High word | Low word |
|---|---|---|
| Equal tokens, matching sign bits | A-high | `0` |
| Equal tokens, different sign bits | G-invalid | `1` |
| Unequal tokens, A-token equals `0xffe00000` | A-high | `0` |
| Other unequal-token cases | B-high | `0` |

Token equality does not imply equality of the original operands. Only the differing-sign equal-token branch reads G-invalid.

The destination is the incoming link value with its low two bits cleared. The supplied representation models the transfer as a call returning a 64-bit value, which F-1b22d7e17791 returns unchanged. Whether the actual mechanism is a conventional call or another form of transfer remains unresolved.

For mode 9, the `0x00100000` test precedes the possible `0x00070000` test. The derived control word uses a further G-control read: shift left by 15, complement the retained 32-bit word, then shift left once, retaining 32 bits.

The status-derived word is `0x30000000`, with bit 27 additionally set when the incoming status quantity’s low bit is one.

On the mode-8 path with bit `0x40` clear, G-template-A is read first, then G-payload, then G-control is read for bit `0x10`. If that bit is set, the first outgoing word becomes zero and G-template-B supplies the second. The earlier template and payload reads still occur.

No local retry, result correction, restoration, or cleanup follows the transfer.

### Ordinary arithmetic: common structure

Bit 31 of the high words selects matching-sign or different-sign arithmetic.

Both paths extract a high-word field by shifting right by 20. The field includes the sign bit; it is not an unbiased exponent. Arithmetic aligns contributions, combines paired words with carries or borrows, and retains information about discarded bits.

Numerical interpretations are conditional on suitable shared values and target shift semantics.

### Matching-sign arithmetic

Operands are arranged in descending unsigned high/low pair order, with the low word breaking high-word ties.

A further G-mask read supplies an arithmetic right shift by one. Its complement masks the smaller high word, after which bit `0x00100000` is forced on.

Alignment has two regimes:

- For extracted-field differences through 32, shifted portions of both smaller words contribute to the retained pair. Low-word additions contribute separate high-word carries.
- For larger differences, the high contribution is reduced further. It is added when the residual difference is below 32. The smaller low word’s zero/nonzero status contributes discarded-bit information.

Large separation does not establish a universal return-without-rounding rule.

A high accumulation above `0x000fffff` enters right normalization. On the larger-separation path, a change in the reconstructed high field can also select normalization. Normalization shifts the retained pair right, transfers a high-word bit into the low word, and records the removed low bit.

Rounding considers the leading discarded bit and branch-specific remaining information. Applicable exact-halfway corrections clear the retained least-significant bit after a tentative increment. A low-word increment that wraps to zero increments the high word.

Range checking is branch-specific. Where checked, the doubled candidate high word must be below `0xffe00000`; otherwise an overflow-template result is constructed. Other returns must not be assigned an additional range check absent from their path.

### Overflow construction

Two sites construct overflow results: one in the normalization route and one after shared rounding correction.

Both first read and retain G-overflow, then evaluate a sign-selection predicate. If the predicate is false, the retained value supplies the result high word. If true, a **second** G-overflow read supplies the high word, with bit 31 set. The result low word is zero.

The first read is not skipped on the true branch.

The predicate combines the candidate high word with an address-like constant and applies a signed test. Its target interpretation is unresolved, so this contract does not assign numerical overflow polarity to either outcome.

### Different-sign arithmetic

The second high word’s sign bit is toggled first. Conditional rearrangement uses paired subtraction, carries, borrows, and another sign toggle. It is not specified as the matching-sign path’s unsigned swap.

**Inferred intent:** arrange a magnitude difference while retaining the appropriate result sign.

The adjusted second operand contributes a negative pair. Its low contribution is the 32-bit negation of its adjusted low word. Its high contribution depends on **two separate G-mask reads**:

- The **minuend sample**, interpreted as signed and arithmetically shifted right by two, supplies the subtraction’s minuend.
- The **masking sample**, independently interpreted as signed and arithmetically shifted right by two, is complemented to form the mask applied to the adjusted second high word.
- The masked high word is increased by one when the adjusted second low word is nonzero. That adjusted quantity is subtracted from the shifted minuend, with 32-bit word arithmetic.

Neither sample is required to equal the other, the entry-classification samples, or any earlier selection value. The supplied expression establishes the two reads and their distinct uses, **but not their relative evaluation order**. An implementation contract must not collapse them into one snapshot or require either role’s read to occur first.

Alignment distinguishes extracted-field differences through 32 from larger differences. A difference of at least 62 takes the large-separation return without further rounding. Smaller separations combine the aligned negative contribution with the retained pair and branch on a signed high intermediate.

The direct rounding route examines a discarded word’s sign bit. When required, it increments the retained low word, propagates wrap, and applies the represented exact-halfway even-result correction.

Cancellation-related paths can shift the retained pair left with an additional discarded-information bit, perform branch-specific rounding, shift right for a small-result check, or normalize left using leading-zero counts across both words.

Leading-zero normalization clears bit `0x800` in the extracted field before exponent adjustment. A negative adjusted signed exponent returns all-zero bits.

A separate small-result check can return zero after comparing the doubled high word with `0x001fffff`. Other exits return a zero high word with a nonzero low word. Neither universal gradual underflow nor universal suppression of subnormal-shaped results is established.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests. Shared-value conditions apply to the particular reads reached.

### Operand priority and zero-shaped selection

If direct selection applies and both doubled high words intersect the selection mask, return A’s exact pattern, including A-low.

If neither intersection return applies, both high words are `0x80000000`, and both low words are zero, return high word `0x80000000` and low word zero. If that exact condition fails, return all-zero bits.

### Token boundaries and comparison order

Given entry into token handling:

| A-token | B-token | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| At most mask | At most mask | Direct resolution |
| Greater than mask | At most mask | Dispatch; skip B-token’s mask comparison |
| At most mask | Greater than mask | Dispatch after both comparisons |
| Greater than mask | Greater than mask | Dispatch; skip B-token’s mask comparison |

Equality with the mask qualifies for direct resolution, provided the other token also qualifies.

Equal qualifying tokens with matching signs return A-high and zero low. With differing signs, they return G-invalid and low word `1`, not either original low payload.

### Distinct mask samples in negative-contribution construction

Suppose different-sign arithmetic reaches contribution construction with adjusted second high word `0x00300000` and a nonzero adjusted second low word.

If the minuend sample is `0xffc00000`, its arithmetic right shift by two is `0xfff00000`. If the masking sample is `0xff800000`, its arithmetic right shift is `0xffe00000`; its complement is `0x001fffff`.

The masked adjusted high word is therefore `0x00100000`. Including the nonzero-low adjustment makes the subtrahend `0x00100001`, producing high contribution `0xffdfffff`.

Reusing the minuend sample for masking would instead produce `0xffefffff` and would not satisfy these conditions.

This example fixes values by **read role**, not chronological order. It does not establish a complete returned result without the remaining arithmetic and target semantics.

### Dispatch arguments are not returned constants

In mode 10, bit `0x40` selects the single outgoing word as zero or `0x80000000`. The returned value is F-unknown-01’s result, not necessarily that argument widened to 64 bits.

In mode 8, with bit `0x40` clear and the later bit `0x10` set, read G-template-A, then G-payload, then test the later control value and read G-template-B. Transfer with zero and G-template-B.

### Overflow samples can differ

At either overflow construction site, suppose the first G-overflow read yields `0x12345678`.

- A false sign predicate returns high word `0x12345678`, low word zero, without a second template read.
- A true predicate followed by a second read of `0x23456789` returns high word `0xa3456789`, low word zero.

The true branch must not construct `0x92345678` from the first sample.

### Rounding and cancellation

At the cancellation-normalization exit with a negative adjusted signed exponent, return all-zero bits. That exit does not preserve a negative-zero sign or construct a gradual-underflow result.
