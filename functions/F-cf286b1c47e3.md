### Opening mutations and zero reread

Packed base is published at offset `0x54` from 256 times the final result’s low word. Shifted-base offset 9 is subsequently overwritten with the shifted base’s low byte.

Packed-base offset `0x14` then receives a zero word. A byte from packed-base offset 5 is saved before doubled-selector offset `0x14` receives a zero word. The saved unsigned byte is widened and stored as a word at packed-base offset `0x34`; packed-base offset `0x14` is then reread.

**[INFERENCE]** With successful ordinary-RAM accesses, modular 32-bit addressing, and no external mutation, this reread yields zero even if the doubled-selector store overlaps it: that intervening store contributes only zero bytes. The packed-base offsets `0x34` and `0x14` do not overlap.

The reread remains a distinct access even when its value is predictable. Observable-read storage does not justify omitting it.

Doubled-selector offset `0x14` receives another zero word. Two separate byte stores then publish the reread word’s low byte at doubled-selector offset `0x15`. Each affects one byte inside the word beginning at offset `0x14`.

### Aliasing at fixed address `0x54`

Assume wrapping arithmetic, ordinary writable memory, doubled selector `0x40`, packing result 1, and captured word `0x123`.

At the four-store boundary, all four word destinations equal `0x54`. An access observer must see values `0x04000000`, `0x00000246`, `0x00000123`, and `0x00000123`, in that order. Immediately after the fourth store, before subsequent byte publications, the word is `0x00000123`.

Moving the fixed-address store after the two captured-word stores would instead leave `0x00000246` at this boundary and is not acceptable.

### Capture and fresh-read boundaries
