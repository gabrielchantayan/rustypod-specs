### Quantity derivation and integer widths

The presented arithmetic uses a signed 32-bit boundary value and a source address converted to a signed 32-bit integer representation. Subtraction occurs before the right shift.

For a nonnegative, representable difference, shifting right by two produces the number of complete four-byte units in that difference. Any remainder of one, two, or three bytes does not increase the resulting quantity.

Consequently:

- A difference of zero produces zero.
- Differences from one through three produce zero.
- A difference of four produces one.
- A difference of fifteen produces three.
- A difference of sixteen produces four.

These facts do not establish that the dependency treats the result as a length, nor that it ignores any remaining bytes.

For negative differences, the supplied expression does not establish the execution model’s signed-right-shift rule. Under arithmetic right shift, the result corresponds to division by four rounded toward negative infinity, not generally truncation toward zero. For example, a difference of negative one would produce negative one under that rule.

The subtraction is not established to use a wider intermediate. If the mathematical difference is outside the signed 32-bit range, the supplied evidence alone does not justify a particular wrapped result or any normal-completion guarantee. No clamping or overflow check is visible.
