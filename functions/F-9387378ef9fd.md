### Widths and arithmetic

Bytes are 8 bits, halfwords 16 bits, and words 32 bits. Paired-result containers are represented as 64 bits.

A paired result’s low component comprises bits 0–31; its high component comprises bits 32–63. Unsigned whole-container shifts by 34, 36, and 44 correspond to shifting the high component right by two, four, and twelve. These relationships do not establish memory byte order.

Transformations include fixed left shifts through 31 positions, unsigned right shifts, multiplication, doubling, subtraction, and masking.

| Selection | Width consequence |
|---|---|
| Low 24 bits, shifted right by 19 | Five-bit result |
| Low 20 bits, shifted right by ten | Ten-bit result |
| Low 19 bits, shifted left by two | At most 21 occupied bits |
| Low 17 bits, shifted right by four | Thirteen-bit result |

Byte and halfword stores retain the low eight and sixteen bits respectively. Unsigned byte reads widened to words are zero-extended.

Signed-looking intermediates prevent a universal overflow rule from being established. Under a **conditional 32-bit wrapping model**, intermediate truncation matters: a left shift by 25 followed by eleven yields zero, not a retained 36-bit result. Multiplication by 33,554,432 leaves only seven input bits contributing to a 32-bit product.

A late right shift takes its count from the low byte of the working base. Counts can exceed 31. Signed-right-shift behavior and oversized-count handling are **documented_only**; count masking is not established.
