# F-33ca0546dba1

## Purpose

F-33ca0546dba1 replaces a single byte in caller-designated storage. The destination lies 16 bytes beyond an address basis supplied by the caller. The replacement preserves the supplied byte’s complete bit pattern.

The operation has no returned result. Its successful effect is the destination update; it does not report the previous value or indicate success through a separate channel.

The application-level role of the byte is not established. It must not be treated as a Boolean, status code, ownership marker, or other restricted value without additional evidence.

## Acceptance examples

The following are conditional requirements derived from static evidence, not executed tests. Successful examples assume valid address derivation, ordinary writable memory, sufficient lifetime, and no competing modifications.

1. **Complete replacement.** If the destination contains 0x27 and the replacement is 0xA6, successful completion leaves 0xA6. Combining the old and new patterns would not satisfy the contract.

2. **Zero remains meaningful.** If the destination contains 0xFF and the replacement is 0x00, successful completion leaves 0x00. Treating zero as “do not update” would violate the contract.

3. **No Boolean conversion.** A replacement of 0xFE leaves 0xFE. Substituting 0x01 would not preserve the supplied byte.

4. **Exact location and width.** Suppose valid ordinary storage at offsets 15, 16, and 17 initially contains 0x31, 0x42, and 0x53. Replacing with 0xA6 leaves those positions containing 0x31, 0xA6, and 0x53. The neighboring bytes receive no explicit writes.

6. **Sequential replacement.** Two successful calls to the same destination, supplying 0x12 and then 0xE4, leave 0xE4 when no intervening modification occurs.

7. **No preceding-byte access.** If address derivation and the destination write are valid, inaccessible locations before the destination do not themselves introduce an explicit failing access. The operation does not read or write offsets zero through 15.

8. **Arithmetic boundary.** If the positive displacement cannot be represented under the applicable signed arithmetic, no wrapped destination, successful return, or unchanged-memory outcome is required.

9. **Write protection.** If the derived destination denies the byte write, no particular error code, recovery procedure, or fault response is specified.
