# F-87d5d5c0a2b3

## Purpose

The supplied static representation describes a state-dependent routine that makes indirect calls, reads and writes dynamically addressed memory, and either constructs a 64-bit result or continues through a loop without a represented iteration bound.

No domain-level purpose is established. Adjacent stores resemble record construction, but their base can be a zero-extended byte rather than a validated object pointer. The evidence does not establish allocation, parsing, object initialization, or a numerical service.

One entry path ends at an explicitly reported invalid-instruction boundary. Other behavior depends on implicit entry state and unavailable dependencies. This contract describes the represented behavior, not a guarantee that every represented path is executable.

## Behavior

### Zero-flag entry path

If the incoming flag is zero, the routine reads a byte at the address formed by adding arguments 3 and 2, converts it through the represented character type to a halfword, and writes that halfword at argument 1 plus 60.

The read precedes the write, including when the locations overlap.

Control then reaches an explicitly truncated invalid-instruction boundary. No successful return, recovery action, or continuation beyond that boundary is established.

### Nonzero-flag entry and initial writes

If the incoming flag is nonzero, F-U01 is called through a target formed from the incoming return-link value plus `0xFBA`.

After F-U01 returns, the observable sequence is:

1. Read a byte at an incoming address plus 1 and zero-extend it into a pointer-valued word.
2. Read a word at F-U01’s returned address plus 100.
3. Perform the represented coprocessor operation using that word and incoming coprocessor operands.
4. Read a word at the address numerically equal to the preceding loaded word plus 20.
5. Call F-U02 with F-U01’s result and this last loaded word.

The pointer obtained from the byte is therefore numerically between 0 and 255. No base address is added to it, and no validity check precedes its use.

After F-U02 returns, writes occur in this order:

| Destination | Width and value |
|---|---|
| Byte-derived pointer plus the word loaded at F-U01’s result plus 100 | Byte: low byte of an incoming data value. |
| Byte-derived pointer | Word: F-U02’s result. |
| Byte-derived pointer plus 4 | Word: the pointer’s own numeric value. |
| Byte-derived pointer plus 8 | Word: another incoming data value. |
| Byte-derived pointer plus 12 | Word: the incoming value used for the first byte store. |

These destinations are not established as disjoint. Later writes can overwrite earlier bytes.

### Initial pair and branch selection

Before F-U03, the routine records a predicate: arithmetically shift F-U02’s result right by 9, interpret the result as unsigned, and compare it strictly greater than `0xFFFFFFBE`.

Under the stated 32-bit interpretation, this predicate is true exactly when F-U02’s signed result is between −33,280 and −1 inclusive.

F-U03 receives:

- F-U02’s result.
- Its arithmetic right shift by 9, plus 65.
- Argument 4 minus 60, arithmetically shifted right by 20.

F-U03 produces a low/high pair. The recorded predicate remains based on F-U02’s result, not on this pair.

If the predicate is false, the pair enters the common stage directly.

If it is true, the routine first stores the low halfword of the word previously loaded at F-U01’s result plus 100 at the returned low-word address plus 42. It then compares two Boolean conditions:

- Whether that previously loaded word is negative.
- Whether subtracting 47 from the returned high word indicates signed overflow.

Equality enters the common stage directly. Inequality enters the loop-processing stage with:

- **Working unsigned value:** F-U03’s low word logically shifted right by 25.
- **Working address:** F-U03’s high word.
- **Working data:** an incoming subtraction operand minus the previously loaded word.

This Boolean comparison is not an ordinary comparison of either operand with 47.

### Loop-processing stage

F-U04 receives the current working unsigned value, working address, and a pointer into represented stack storage. Its target is selected as specified above. Its permitted access extent through the stack pointer is unknown.

F-U05 is called next and produces a low/high pair. After both calls, the routine forms the working address plus 10 and stores the working data word at the original working address plus 62.

That store occurs before the early-return decision.

The early-return decision interprets the low-32-bit product of the incremented address and 65,536 as signed and tests whether it is negative. Under the bit-vector interpretation, this is equivalent to testing bit 15 of the incremented address.

If true, the routine returns with:

- Low word: F-U05’s low word doubled modulo \(2^{32}\).
- High word: the return-related global read at this point, minus 84 modulo \(2^{32}\).

The subsequent halfword store and calls are not reached.

If false, the routine stores F-U05’s low halfword at its high-word address plus 60, calls F-U06, calls F-U07, and stores the halfword value 38 at F-U07’s returned address plus 2.

It then derives:

- A **scaled word** by multiplying the incremented address by `0x10000000`, retaining 32 bits, then arithmetically shifting right by 9.
- A **combined word** by adding that scaled word to the incremented address, retaining 32 bits.

The mixing global is read before F-U08. F-U08 receives twice F-U07’s returned address and the incremented address multiplied by 65,536 minus 81, with 32-bit truncation.

After F-U08, the combined word is XORed with the previously read mixing value. Changes to the global during F-U08 do not replace that captured operand.

The scaled word plus 47 becomes the working data value. The XOR result is written into represented stack storage at offset `0x130`.

Next, the routine reads a byte at the XOR-result address plus 1 and calls F-U09 with:

- A fixed embedded address token.
- That byte.
- The current working data.
- An additional result channel from F-U08.

After F-U09, the working data word is stored at the XOR-result address plus 36. F-U10 is then called.

A replacement pair is formed with the XOR result as its low word and F-U10’s additional result plus 48 as its high word. That pair enters the common stage, selecting F-U11-B.

### Common stage and complete recurrence

The common stage receives either F-U03’s pair directly or the replacement pair constructed after loop processing.

Before its calls, it calculates:

- **Doubled low word:** the current pair’s low word shifted left by one, truncated to 32 bits.
- **Square:** the low-32-bit square of twice the current pair’s high word.

It then calls, in order:

1. F-U11, using the path-selected target, with the current pair’s low word plus an incoming address adjustment.
2. F-U12, with a word read from the argument-related global for that call.
3. F-U13, with no explicit arguments represented.

Only after F-U13 does the routine read the exit-control global.

If that global is zero, the routine returns. Its low word is F-U13’s additional result arithmetically shifted right by 9 and then doubled modulo \(2^{32}\). Its high word uses the return-related global read at return construction, minus 84.

If the exit-control global is nonzero, the routine stores the previously calculated square at twice the current pair’s high word plus 52. It then enters the loop-processing stage with the complete replacement state:

| Next state | Value |
|---|---|
| Working unsigned value | Current pair’s doubled low word, truncated to 32 bits. |
| Working data | Previously calculated square, truncated to 32 bits. |
| Working address | F-U13’s additional result channel. |

Consequently, the next F-U04 call uses target F-U04-B and receives the doubled low word as its first argument. After F-U05 returns, the following word store at the working address plus 62 uses the square. Neither value is retained from the preceding loop-processing stage.

There is no represented iteration limit or termination guarantee.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not executed tests. Required accesses and calls must be permitted by the actual environment.

1. **Zero flag and character extension.** A loaded byte of `0x80` yields a stored halfword of `0xFF80` under signed-character extension or `0x0080` under unsigned-character extension. The write precedes the invalid-instruction boundary; neither interpretation establishes a successful return.

2. **Byte-derived pointer.** A pointer-source byte of 7 makes the four word-store addresses 7, 11, 15, and 19. Adding an unstated object base would change the represented behavior.

3. **Initial-predicate boundaries.** F-U02 results of −33,280 and −1 make the predicate true. Results of −33,281 and zero make it false. Only the true path performs the halfword store at F-U03’s low-word address plus 42.

4. **Direct loop entry.** If the predicate is true and the Boolean comparison is unequal, the first loop-processing call selects F-U04-A. If processing continues into the common stage, that stage selects F-U11-B.

5. **Direct common entry.** If the predicate is false, or true with an equal Boolean comparison, the common stage selects F-U11-A. A nonzero exit-control result then sends execution to F-U04-B, not F-U04-A.

6. **Recurrence replaces prior state.** Suppose a common-stage pair has low word `0x80000003` and high word 5. Its doubled low word is 6 and its square is 100. If F-U13’s additional result is `0x1000` and the later exit-control read is nonzero, the square is stored at address 62. The next F-U04-B receives 6 and `0x1000`; after F-U05 returns, the word written at `0x103E` is 100, regardless of the preceding iteration’s working data.

7. **Early return ordering.** If the incremented working address has bit 15 set, the working-data store at the original address plus 62 still occurs. The following halfword store and F-U06 do not occur.

8. **Global changes during calls.** A zero exit-control value after F-U13 selects return even if it was nonzero beforehand. Conversely, a mixing-global change during F-U08 does not change the operand already captured for the subsequent XOR.

9. **Continued execution.** Nonzero exit control and a clear early-return bit on every reached iteration permit continued looping without a represented bound, unless a dependency or memory operation prevents continuation.
