# F-d34f94f591b3

## Purpose

F-d34f94f591b3 describes a parameterless entry that performs ordered indirect invocations and memory accesses, propagates and extracts byte-valued payloads, assigns a retained byte-valued expression to a global, and reaches a represented non-returning transfer.

The global’s declaration is absent. Its type, storage width, assignment conversion, and physical write footprint are **documented_only**. The byte width of the assigned expression does not establish a byte-wide destination or store.

No application-level purpose is established. Initialization, decoding, allocation, reset, and device-protocol interpretations are **documented_only**.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** access widths, data relationships, argument positions, and ordering explicitly represented in the supplied material.
- **[INFERENCE]:** conclusions requiring an additional, stated arithmetic or execution model.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior for which no executable requirement is invented.

Every effect is conditional on execution reaching it. Earlier dependencies need not return, and computed memory locations need not be valid.

## Behavior

### External boundaries and ordering

The visible path has no ordinary conditional branch or loop. Repeated-looking invocations are distinct occurrences; they are not evidence of interchangeable or pure dependencies.

F-000000000001 supplies the initial primary word result. An occurrence of F-000000000002 then receives that result scaled by four. Subsequent occurrences variously take no explicit arguments, discard results, forward results unchanged, transform results, or consume retained state.

Most indirect targets are selected from embedded control-location values with a displacement of 4,094. One exceptional target instead rounds its embedded value down to a four-byte boundary and adds 1,536. This exceptional invocation receives four times the preceding primary result and produces the result used for the exceptional early byte write.

The following ordering relationships are established:

- A producer completes before an invocation consumes its result.
- A direct write represented before an invocation precedes entry to that invocation.
- A fetched halfword is read before its consumer is entered.
- Older retained values remain the specified data sources despite newer primary results.
- The terminal transfer follows the remaining represented invocations.

These are represented sequencing relationships, not synchronization guarantees. Dependencies may subsequently change memory or global state. An invocation with no explicit arguments or unused results remains an external boundary with unknown effects.

### Argument and result relationships

A frequent relationship passes a primary word result scaled by four to a later dependency. Other boundaries use doubling, eightfold scaling, unchanged forwarding, duplicate arguments, or a new result paired with retained state.

One four-argument boundary has this positional contract:

| Argument position | Source |
|---|---|
| First | Producer result scaled by 16,384 |
| Second | Same scaled result |
| Third | Incoming signed word scaled by 1,024 |
| Fourth | Same value as the first and second arguments |

The next invocation consumes secondary post-call state from that boundary scaled by eight. Its source is not the boundary’s primary result.

Three dependency boundaries represent 64-bit results. At the first two consumers, the explicit argument order is low 32-bit portion, high 32-bit portion, high portion again. At the third, it is low portion followed by high portion once. The first two occur during incoming-payload propagation; the third occurs after the width-sensitive late shift.

Later boundaries forward secondary post-call words unchanged. Substituting a primary result at those boundaries changes the represented behavior.

### Incoming payload, zero writes, and self-derived payload

Initial byte writes propagate the incoming eight-bit payload. An ordinary destination is the producer result scaled by eight. Its associated consumer commonly receives two copies of that result scaled by 16,384.

Exceptions are significant:

- The exceptional aligned-target result supplies a destination scaled by two and duplicate arguments scaled by 4,096.
- Another incoming-payload write uses an eightfold destination but duplicate arguments scaled by four.
- The four-argument boundary follows an incoming-payload write at an eightfold destination.

After incoming-payload propagation, six separate writes store zero bytes. Each uses its own producer result scaled by eight as the destination. Associated consumers receive duplicate 16,384-scaled arguments.

These are six individual effects, not an established bulk clear. Destinations need not be contiguous, distinct, or persistently zero after intervening dependencies.

A later write stores the low eight bits of its own eightfold-scaled numeric destination. Its consumer receives two copies of the producer scaled by 16,384 and, third, the producer scaled by eight. Only the written payload is narrowed to eight bits; the third argument retains its represented word width.

### Retained payload and arithmetic history

An unsigned 32-bit producer establishes a retained payload: the low eight bits of that producer scaled by eight. Its low three bits are zero.

The initial write of this payload uses the unsigned producer scaled by sixteen as its destination. The associated consumer receives two copies of the unsigned producer scaled by 32,768.

The unsigned producer is retained separately for a later field extraction. New primary results do not replace it or the retained byte payload.

A different history supplies older arithmetic state:

- An earlier signed producer is scaled by 16,384 and retained.
- That retained word later supplies a destination scaled by 65,536.
- A further retained word is formed by scaling the older word by 32,768.
- The high byte of the further word later supplies the three-bit payload.

These relationships are subject to the arithmetic qualifications below.

### First read and global assignment

At the first explicit read boundary, one producer determines both:

- A byte destination scaled by 8,192, receiving the retained byte payload.
- A halfword source scaled by 4,096, plus 56 bytes.

The byte write precedes the halfword read. The consumer receives the 4,096-scaled word first and the fetched halfword second. The read is not established to read back the preceding write.

After further external interactions, the retained byte-valued expression is assigned to a named global. The next invocation has two explicit zero arguments.

**The assignment establishes the source expression and its position in the sequence, not the destination’s width.** No global declaration establishes its type, signedness, conversion rules, physical store width, or affected byte range. Those properties remain **documented_only**. In particular, acceptance must not require a one-byte write or preservation of adjacent storage based solely on the source expression’s width.

The assignment does not establish reset semantics. Subsequent dependencies are not known to preserve the assigned global state.

Six further writes propagate the retained byte payload to eightfold-scaled destinations before the payload changes. The last uses duplicate arguments scaled by four rather than the common 16,384.

### Field extraction and three-bit payload

The next distinct byte payload takes bits 19 through 26 of the retained unsigned producer, numbering from zero at the least significant bit. The represented mask and shift produce a wider intermediate, but the destination is one byte. No additional bytes are written by that extraction.

Later writes use a separate source: the high byte of the further retained arithmetic word. Moving that byte’s upper three bits into its low three positions yields a payload from zero through seven. This source is distinct from the unsigned producer used for the retained byte and eight-bit field.

The three-bit payload is reused across many external boundaries. Later, the retained high-byte state is itself replaced by its reduced value. Subsequent writes use that already reduced value without another five-position reduction.

### Word publication and later activity

A later producer determines an eightfold byte destination and a fourfold word value. The represented order is:

1. Write the reduced three-bit payload to the byte destination.
2. Write the fourfold word value through the retained pointer.
3. Invoke a dependency with three copies of that word value.

No non-aliasing condition is represented. The word write may overlap the preceding byte destination, another computed location, or global storage. If overlap occurs, the word write can replace the earlier byte effect. The global’s unresolved width prevents deriving a precise overlap range for it.

Publication is not completion. Later activity includes forwarding a new unchanged primary result twice, additional reduced-payload writes, secondary-state forwarding, and a paired invocation whose second argument is the previously published word value.

### Arithmetic and reachability

Scale descriptions express represented relationships, not unlimited-precision multiplication or guaranteed valid addresses.

Unsigned 32-bit shifts retain only the low 32 bits. Narrow byte payloads retain eight bits. Many other shifts and multiplications involve signed 32-bit words; their behavior for negative operands, overflow, or nonrepresentable shifted results requires an independently established execution interpretation.

**[INFERENCE]:** Under a wrapping 32-bit machine interpretation, scaled words retain only their low 32 bits. Large scales may therefore produce zero or repeated destinations even from nonzero inputs. Under that interpretation, the further retained word’s high-byte reduction depends only on the original signed producer’s low three bits.

One late expression shifts a represented signed 32-bit result left by two and then right by 32. Neither zero, sign replication, nor a masked count may be selected without additional evidence.
