# F-f66009d786a2

## Purpose

F-f66009d786a2 transforms three 32-bit unsigned inputs into one 32-bit unsigned result using normalization and two successive quotient-digit calculations.

**Inferred intent:** the first two inputs form the high and low words of a 64-bit unsigned dividend, and the third input is a 32-bit unsigned divisor. The arithmetic resembles division in base 65,536, producing two 16-bit quotient digits.

**Established behavior:** a zero third input returns `0xffffffff` immediately. Otherwise, the function calls a normalization dependency, optionally subtracts the third input from the first input once, normalizes the operands, and performs exactly two digit-calculation rounds if all dependencies return and all arithmetic operations complete.

The interpretation as ordinary unsigned division is conditional. It depends on dependency behavior, the externally read estimate-seed word, valid shift counts, and the input range. Those conditions are not established by the supplied material.

## Interface

### Inputs

All three arguments are 32-bit unsigned values:

| Argument | Contract name | Role |
|---|---|---|
| First | High word | Initial upper working word; inferred high half of a dividend |
| Second | Low word | Initial lower working word; inferred low half of a dividend |
| Third | Divisor | Zero-case selector and arithmetic divisor |

For discussing inferred division behavior, the composite dividend is:

\[
N = \text{High word}\times 2^{32}+\text{Low word}.
\]

This interpretation does not mean the implementation constructs a 64-bit value. The visible calculations operate on 32-bit words.

### Output

The return value is one 32-bit unsigned word.

- If the divisor is zero, the result is `0xffffffff`.
- Otherwise, the result combines the first round’s candidate shifted left by 16 bits with the second round’s candidate, using bitwise OR.
- Neither candidate is explicitly masked to 16 bits before this final combination. Consequently, describing the result as two independent 16-bit digits requires additional bounds on the candidates.

There is no remainder output and no explicit error-status output. The zero-divisor sentinel is indistinguishable from a legitimate result having the same bit pattern.

### Arithmetic model

Except for the shift-count qualification below, arithmetic is described as 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic:

- Addition, subtraction, and multiplication retain the low 32 bits.
- Comparisons involving the working words are unsigned.
- Right shifts of unsigned words are logical.
- Signed intermediate storage is used for some products and counters, but the working arithmetic consumes those products as 32-bit patterns in unsigned expressions. No signed ordering of a product controls a correction decision.

For shifts derived from the normalization result, the visible expressions retain only the low eight bits of the count. This is **not** evidence that an out-of-range count is reduced modulo 32 by the execution environment. A fully portable interpretation requires every executed shift count to lie between 0 and 31.

## Behavior

### 1. Zero-divisor handling

The divisor is checked before any dependency call or estimate-seed read.

If it is zero, the function returns `0xffffffff`. The high and low words do not affect this result.

The visible path performs no external write and invokes neither listed dependency.

### 2. Normalization preparation

For a nonzero divisor, the function first calls **F-f7e02acefeaa** with the original divisor. Its result determines the normalization distance.

Only after that call returns does the function compare the divisor with the high word:

- If the high word is at least the divisor, one divisor is subtracted from the high word.
- Otherwise, the high word is unchanged.

This is a single subtraction, not a loop or a general remainder operation. Inputs whose high word is at least twice the divisor are not reduced below the divisor by this step.

The normalization distance is the 32-bit result of subtracting the dependency result from 32. If this distance is zero, normalization shifts are skipped.

For a nonzero distance \(s\), the working words are transformed as follows:

- The new high word is the bitwise OR of the old high word shifted left by the low eight bits of \(s\), and the old low word shifted right by the low eight bits of \(32-s\).
- The divisor is shifted left by the low eight bits of \(s\).
- The low word is shifted left by the low eight bits of \(s\).

Each result is retained as a 32-bit word. The high-word combination uses the low word’s value from before its own shift.

**Inferred normalization convention:** if F-f7e02acefeaa returns the nonzero divisor’s bit length, its result lies in 1 through 32. The resulting distance lies in 0 through 31 and shifts the divisor’s highest set bit into bit 31. That convention is not established by the dependency’s implementation here.

### 3. Common digit-calculation round

The normalized divisor’s upper and lower 16-bit halves are used throughout both rounds. Call these halves \(A\) and \(B\), respectively.

Each round starts with a 32-bit read of an external **estimate-seed word**.

The function then compares \(A\) with the current high word’s upper 16 bits:

- If they are equal, the freshly read seed becomes the candidate.
- If they differ, **F-d7be9cfb4000** is called with the entire current high word as its first argument and \(A\) as its second argument. Its return value replaces the seed as the candidate.

Thus, the seed read occurs even on a path that subsequently replaces its value with a dependency result.

Let \(Q\) denote the candidate. The function forms two wrapping 32-bit products:

- \(P=AQ\), the upper-half product;
- \(T=BQ\), the lower-half product.

It then applies a repeated downward correction.

The correction continues precisely while both of these conditions hold:

1. The upper 16 bits of the wrapping difference between the current high word and \(P\) are zero.
2. The unsigned word formed by shifting that difference left 16 bits and OR-ing in the current low word’s upper 16 bits is less than \(T\).

Each correction decrements \(Q\) by one, subtracts \(B\) from \(T\), and subtracts \(A\) from \(P\). All three updates wrap at 32 bits.

There is no explicit iteration limit. Without suitable bounds on dependency results and the seed, termination is not established merely by the presence of this correction loop.

After this loop, the function constructs a subtraction quantity:

\[
C=P+(T\mathbin{\text{ shifted right }}16),
\]

with 32-bit wrapping addition.

It then compares the current low word against the low 32 bits of \(T\times 65{,}536\). If the low word is smaller, \(C\) is incremented by one, again with wrapping arithmetic. This incorporates the borrow from the lower-word subtraction.

A final correction follows:

- If the current high word is less than \(C\), the normalized divisor is added to the high word and \(Q\) is decremented once.
- Otherwise, neither value changes.

This final correction does not recompute \(P\), \(T\), or \(C\).

### 4. Transition between rounds

There are exactly two rounds.

After the first round:

1. The first candidate is shifted left by 16 bits and retained as the upper contribution to the return value.
2. The next high word is formed by OR-ing:
   - the wrapping difference between the current high word and \(C\), shifted left by 16 bits; and
   - the upper 16 bits of the wrapping difference between the current low word and \(T\times 65{,}536\).
3. The low word is shifted left by 16 bits.

The high-word calculation uses the current low word before that low word is shifted.

The second round repeats the seed read, optional dependency call, repeated candidate correction, borrow calculation, and final correction. Its normalized divisor and divisor halves are unchanged from the first round.

### 5. Return assembly

After the second round, the function returns the bitwise OR of:

- the first candidate shifted left by 16 bits; and
- the full second candidate.

The second candidate is not masked. If it exceeds `0xffff`, its upper bits can affect the portion otherwise associated with the first candidate.

The function does not denormalize or return a remainder. The final working high and low words are not exposed through the interface.
