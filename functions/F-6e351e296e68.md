### Quotient and remainder calculation

The calculation progressively removes descending multiples of 1,000 from a working remainder. Each successful comparison contributes a set bit to the quotient; each unsuccessful comparison contributes a clear bit.

For operands below 16,000, the final stage considers contributions corresponding to 8,000, 4,000, 2,000, and 1,000.

For operands from 16,000 through 255,999, it first considers contributions corresponding to 128,000, 64,000, 32,000, and 16,000, then performs the same final stage.

For larger operands, a scaled path handles groups of six quotient bits. Each group considers descending contributions at 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, and 4 times its current scale. Where further groups are needed, the scale is reduced by a factor of 64. The final group uses scale 1,000, followed by the remaining contributions of 2,000 and 1,000.

The reachable large-operand paths use:

| Initial scale | Six-bit groups | Scale progression |
|---:|---:|---|
| 64,000 | 2 | 64,000 → 1,000 |
| 4,096,000 | 3 | 4,096,000 → 64,000 → 1,000 |
| 262,144,000 | 4 | 262,144,000 → 4,096,000 → 64,000 → 1,000 |

### Widths and arithmetic interpretation

The operand, working remainder, scales, quotient accumulator, and carry checks operate at 32-bit width.

Some accumulator states are displayed as negative signed values. Their role is to control grouped extraction through 32-bit bit patterns and carry behavior; they are not negative operands or negative quotient results. A behavioral implementation must preserve the resulting unsigned division semantics rather than interpret these intermediates as ordinary unbounded signed arithmetic.

Subtractions are guarded by unsigned comparisons. A scale is subtracted only when the remaining operand is large enough for the corresponding weighted contribution. Thus the mathematically selected remainder updates do not underflow.

Some trial scale products would exceed 32 bits if evaluated indiscriminately. The visible comparisons prevent those oversized contributions from being selected. This matters especially on the path beginning at 262,144,000, where early high-weight contributions cannot fit within any permitted operand.

The final high word is the remainder after all selected contributions. The final low word is the assembled quotient.
