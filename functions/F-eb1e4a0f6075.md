# F-eb1e4a0f6075

## Purpose

The established behavior is a sequence of observations and mutations, not a recognizable application operation. The supplied evidence does not establish an object model, collection format, allocation protocol, or ownership transfer.

This contract describes static evidence only. Its acceptance examples are conditional obligations, not results from execution.

## Behavior

### Interpretation of memory and arithmetic

Offsets in this contract are byte offsets. Writes described as bytes, halfwords, and words affect 8, 16, and 32 bits respectively. Narrow payloads use the represented low bits; this does not permit widening the destination write.

Halfword observations used in calculations are signed 16-bit values widened for subsequent arithmetic. A halfword pattern of 0xffff therefore contributes −1, not 65535. Character observations are 8 bits, but whether they widen as signed or unsigned values is unresolved.

Byte order is not established. Neither is a universal rule that signed additions, multiplications, doubling, or left shifts wrap modulo 32 bits. Truncating a later payload does not establish that an earlier overflowing calculation has defined behavior.

The essential observation rules are:

- A captured value belongs to its observation time, not to the later contents of its source.
- A later explicit observation must not be replaced by an earlier capture.
- Repeated writes are separate effects, including writes of identical values.
- Reads and writes may overlap selection sources, traversed fields, or stack-associated retaining storage.
- No disjointness, atomicity, isolation, or concurrency semantics are established.

Consequently, neither a simultaneous-update interpretation nor a final-memory-image comparison is sufficient to characterize the function.

### Initial state publication

Before the first visible write, the entry region’s word at offset 0x14 is captured. This historical value is subsequently used both as a payload and as an address base.

The initial destination receives its own address at offset 0x54. Next, the displaced halfword destination receives the low 16 bits of that address. Only after this write is the halfword base’s word at offset 0x44 observed.

The entry region then receives its own address at offset 0x54 before the first selection publication.

These ordering requirements distinguish two overlap cases: the halfword write cannot change the already captured entry-region value, but it can change the subsequently observed word at the halfword base.

### Selection and publication protocol

There are eighteen successive selection boundaries. Each boundary uses its own external source twice: first to obtain the selected address, and subsequently to obtain the payload published into the current publication destination.

The two observations must not be collapsed into one. They refer to the same corresponding source storage, but the evidence does not establish an indivisible snapshot or unconditional equality between their observed values.

Each boundary has two ordered word publications:

1. The publication destination’s offset 0x44 receives the later external observation.
2. The selected region’s offset 0x14 receives a word captured earlier in the traversal.

The initial publication destination is the initial incoming destination; its captured-word payload comes from the initial halfword base. Later destinations and payloads depend on intervening traversal. Several destinations are widened character values, rather than ordinary pointer-field observations.

“Selected region” identifies the initial selection at a boundary, not necessarily every destination used afterward. The tenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth boundaries obtain replacement addresses for later effects. Those replacements also affect the source of the next captured offset-0x44 word.

### Early propagation

The early boundaries preserve three distinct kinds of history:

- The initial entry-region offset-0x14 capture.
- The first selected region’s offset-0x74 capture.
- The second selected region’s separate offset-0x74 capture.

The incoming payload is written to the first selected region at offset 0x24. The first offset-0x74 capture supplies the second selected region’s offset-0x24 write. The second capture remains available for later byte and word publications; it must not be substituted for the first capture.

The second selected region receives two separate writes at offset 0x14: the normal boundary publication, then the historical entry-region capture. Both writes are part of the required sequence.

Early byte effects distinguish address fragments from memory contents:

| Destination role | Established payload sources |
|---|---|
| Entry-region offset 0x11 | Doubled entry address, historical address fragments, and low bytes from offset-0x64 observations |

The historical region also receives a word at offset 0x54 from the second offset-0x74 capture.

Most early halfword writes combine the current selection with a signed halfword observed at the sum of the entry address and that selection. Their payloads come from separately observed words or addresses.

Two signed-halfword-derived offset-0x44 observations occur before working-region establishment. They feed different later accesses and remain distinct even when their calculated bases happen to match.

### Working-region establishment and active-region changes

At the eighth boundary, an offset-0x44 observation through a signed-halfword-derived base establishes a working-region value. It is stored in the stack-associated retaining word at offset 0x34.

The working region’s offset-0x04 word temporarily replaces the entry region’s offset-0x54 field. A later self-address write replaces that field again. The temporary write is not removable merely because it is later overwritten.

A separate capture from the eighth selected region’s offset 0x74 supplies two distinct writes to the working region’s offset 0x54. Storage addressed by that capture also receives the working-region value at offset 0x54.

The eighth boundary’s halfword destination is exceptional: it uses twice the entry address plus the selection, rather than the usual signed-halfword displacement.

Subsequent offset-0x14 observations establish changing active regions. Later self-address publications at offset 0x54 and byte writes at offset 0x11 follow those regions; they are not permanently attached to the entry region.

An offset-0x14 observation through the capture participates in another multiplication by the factor. The resulting base supplies an offset-0x74 word, retained at stack-associated offset 0x108. Multiplying that base by the factor again establishes a later product-derived base.

The observable tail includes repeated self-address writes at offset 0x54 of storage addressed by the signed factor. It also includes repeated writes at the final selected region’s offset 0x11: initially active-address fragments, then a historical fragment, and later a second payload without preceding visible initialization.

## Acceptance examples

These examples assume valid execution through the relevant prefix and no interference except where stated.

1. **Historical capture:** If the initial halfword write overlaps entry-region offset 0x14, later uses of the historical capture still use the pre-write observation.

2. **Later observation:** If that write overlaps the halfword base’s offset-0x44 word, its subsequent observation must account for the write, with byte interpretation dependent on the unresolved byte order.

3. **Publication collision:** If the two publication destinations coincide, the external payload is written first and the captured payload second. Matching only the final word is insufficient.

4. **Signed halfword:** A halfword pattern of 0xffff used additively with selection 0x2000 gives destination 0x1fff, provided the arithmetic and access are valid.

5. **Character widening:** A character pattern of 0xff used as a base for offset 0x44 gives 0x43 under signed widening or 0x143 under unsigned widening. The evidence does not choose between them.

6. **Replacement timing:** Changing the tenth boundary’s replacement address changes its halfword destination without changing the previously observed halfword.

7. **Replacement precedence:** Without subsequent overlap, the thirteenth selection’s later offset-0x44 observation yields its replacement value, not its displaced predecessor.

8. **Independent shifts:** With retaining value 0x1000 and no interference, the four-bit and eight-bit results are 0x10000 and 0x100000.

9. **Uninitialized payloads:** Neither unexplained byte payload is required to be zero or equal to its later initialization. Defined execution through either use remains unresolved.

10. **Call boundary:** Offset 0x60 has an explicit pre-call zero assignment; offset 0x08 receives its explicit zero only after F-unknown-01 returns.

11. **Fresh argument:** If F-unknown-01 changes the later external argument storage and returns, F-cdec09f5d432 receives the subsequently observed word.

12. **Interrupted execution:** If F-unknown-01 does not return, the post-call assignments and later dependencies are not reached. Earlier mutations have no established rollback.
