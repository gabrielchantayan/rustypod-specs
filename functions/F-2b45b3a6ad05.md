# F-2b45b3a6ad05

## Purpose

F-2b45b3a6ad05 repeatedly performs an ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, 32-bit value transformations, word transfers, and byte-selected writes. An iteration has no represented conditional exit. Its final derived values become inputs to the next iteration.

No application-level purpose is established. In particular, the evidence does not identify allocation, decoding, serialization, initialization, or object construction.

This contract separates three observable aspects:

- The dependency-invocation sequence, including invocations whose results are discarded.
- The explicit memory operations and the lifetime of values retained across invocations.
- The state handed to the next iteration.

Dependency invocations remain observable even when they produce no retained result. They may modify memory, affect subsequent behavior, fail, or not return.

## Interface

### Effective inputs

The represented formal interface has four 32-bit argument words, designated **a₁–a₄**. Their semantic types are unknown.

Two additional incoming machine-state words affect execution:

- **Link** determines the first dependency target through 32-bit addition of 4094.
- **Carry** supplies the first explicit word-store payload.

Carry is not visibly derived from a₁–a₄ on initial entry. The validity and origin of both additional inputs are unresolved.

Execution requires callable targets and accessible memory at the addresses described below. No represented checks establish these prerequisites.

### Outputs and completion

No normal return or return value is represented. Observable outputs comprise dependency invocations, explicit reads and writes, dependency effects, and abnormal control transfers.

The absence of a normal return does not establish that execution continues indefinitely: an invalid access, a nonreturning dependency, or an unrepresented nonlocal transfer may prevent continuation.

### Widths and contract notation

All named scalar values are 32-bit words unless explicitly identified as 64-bit results or byte selectors.

- **W(x)** retains the low 32 bits.
- **B(x)** retains the low eight bits.
- **s(x) = W(4x)**.
- **p(x) = W(4x + 20)**.
- **q(x) = W(16x + 20)**.
- **low(x)** and **high(x)** denote the low and high 32-bit halves of a captured 64-bit result.
- Left shifts retain the low 32 bits; right shifts are unsigned logical shifts.

Address arithmetic and multiplication use this 32-bit bit-pattern interpretation. It describes the represented dataflow, not portable signed-arithmetic semantics.

A word access transfers 32 bits. A selector read transfers one byte and zero-extends it before address calculation. Byte order, alignment requirements, and unaligned-access behavior are unknown.

Letters such as A, N, and AB below identify retained contract values, not source variables or memory objects.

### Direct byte clearing

Immediately after F becomes available, execution writes a zero byte at W(base + 17), then a zero byte at W(base + 1).

The same ordered pair occurs immediately after G becomes available.

These are two distinct clearing events. They do not establish that a later selector read returns zero: dependency invocations intervene and may modify the storage.

### Lookup acquisition

After H becomes available:

1. The high-bit word transfer at q(H) occurs.
2. A 32-bit lookup value is read from W(((F AND 0x3fffffff) ≫ 20) + 20).

The lookup’s starting address ranges from 20 through 1043. There is no represented multiplication by four or alignment adjustment.

The captured lookup, rather than a later memory read, supplies the transformed argument to F-40ba. If its storage overlaps the preceding word store, the read observes storage after that store, subject to the environment’s memory semantics.

### Split results and secondary machine state

N and V are captured 64-bit results. Their low halves supply n and v respectively; their high halves are passed to later dependency entries.

Two additional 64-bit captures, **Z₁** and **Z₂**, are forwarded immediately as ordered low-half and high-half arguments within their respective dependency families. Z₁ is independent of V.

A distinct invocation exposes a secondary machine-state word **ρ**, while its primary result is discarded. The next invocation uses ρ as its second argument. The evidence does not establish that ρ is an ordinary return value or a valid independent ABI result.

The first argument paired with ρ is W(payload ≪ 28). Since payload already includes a 14-bit left shift, this argument is always zero under the 32-bit model. The invocation itself must nevertheless remain observable.

## Dependencies

### Opaque invocation families

Each identifier below names a family of opaque invocation-site IDs. For example, F-a701.0 and F-a701.1 identify separate sites, not proven distinct callees. No equivalence between resolved targets is established.

“No explicit arguments” describes only the supplied representation; it does not exclude additional register, stack, or memory inputs.

Five templates define the protocol:

- **E5:** Site .0 receives the listed entry arguments. Sites .1, .2, and .3 have no explicit arguments and retain no primary result. Site .4 has no explicit arguments and supplies the listed terminal result.
- **R7:** Site .0 receives the entry arguments. Site .1 has no explicit arguments and captures forwarding word f. Site .2 receives f followed by any listed extra argument. Sites .3, .4, and .5 have no explicit arguments and retain no primary result. Site .6 supplies the terminal result with no explicit arguments.
- **S7:** Sites .0–.2 behave as in R7. Site .3 captures the listed 64-bit split result with no explicit arguments. Site .4 receives its low and high halves, in that order. Site .5 has no explicit arguments and no retained primary result. Site .6 supplies the terminal result with no explicit arguments.
- **U7:** Site .0 receives the entry arguments. Site .1 has no explicit arguments; its primary result is discarded and secondary word ρ is represented. Site .2 receives W(payload ≪ 28), then ρ. Sites .3–.5 have no explicit arguments and no retained primary result. Site .6 supplies the terminal result with no explicit arguments.
- **D2:** Site .0 receives the entry arguments. Site .1 has no explicit arguments and supplies the terminal result.

Unless expressly captured, invocation results are not retained by this contract.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static deductions, not executed tests.

1. **Invocation preservation.** F-a701 requires five separate invocations. Its four-argument entry precedes three no-explicit-argument invocations and the invocation producing A. Omitting a discarded-result invocation changes the contract.

2. **Initial transfer.** If A is 3 and Carry is 0x11223344, the first explicit word store starts at address 32 and stores 0x11223344. The following family’s entry receives 12.

3. **Triplicate arguments.** If B is 5, the associated word store writes 20 at address 100. F-c943.0 then receives three arguments, each 20.

4. **Captured versus fresh selector.** If captured-selector is 200, the byte write associated with M targets 217 and the later selected word store starts at 220. Intervening changes to the original selector storage do not redirect that word store. The selector read associated with N remains fresh.

5. **Snapshot timing.** If a dependency changes the global after snapshot capture, the byte payload associated with N still uses the earlier snapshot. If the selected word store overlaps the global, capture occurs after that store.

6. **Byte truncation.** If L is 0x12345678, the byte associated with L is 0xe0, while F-62dc.0 receives 0x48d159e0.

7. **Unaligned lookup possibility.** If F is 0xffffffff, lookup starts at address 1043. The address is not rounded; whether that access succeeds is environmental.

8. **Split-result provenance.** F-ea54.4 receives the halves captured by F-ea54.3, not the halves of V. Its argument order is low half, then high half.

9. **Derived zero without call removal.** For every Y, F-2ea8.2 receives zero as its first argument. Its second remains ρ, and the invocation remains required.

10. **Loop handoff.** If AB is 1 and AD is 2, the final word store writes 8 at address 16404. The next iteration’s formal arguments are 4096, 4096, 16384, and 8; Carry is 8 and Link denotes K-cont.

## Evidence

Established static evidence consists of four formal argument words, additional incoming machine state, 146 ordered indirect-invocation sites, explicit byte and word accesses, four captured 64-bit results, one global-word read, and unconditional iteration.

The supplied representation reports removed unreachable material and overlapping global symbols. Those reports do not establish behavior for omitted paths or a recoverable data structure.
