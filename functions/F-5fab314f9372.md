## Behavior

Calls are statically ordered; no explicit loop or conditional branch is represented in this phase. Between zero and four argument words are explicitly shown at individual call sites. A call with no represented arguments may still depend on implicit machine state.

Dependency dataflow includes:

- Primary results shifted left by eight.

- Secondary machine-state outputs used independently of paired results.

For a 64-bit result, the low word comprises bits 0–31 and the high word bits 32–63. Unsigned right shifts of the full result by 34, 36, or 44 correspond to the high word shifted right by 2, 4, or 12.

Masks select fields of several widths, including 11–14, 16–22, and 24 bits. Selection and shifting order matters. Unsigned right shifts zero-fill; narrowing can discard information permanently.

**[INFERENCE]** Under a 32-bit bit-vector model, additions, multiplications, and left shifts retain only the low 32 bits. Successive shifts can therefore discard every original bit. Unbounded arithmetic is not an equivalent model.

Some intermediate operations are represented with signed types. Signed overflow, signed left shifts, negative signed right shifts, and unsupported variable-shift counts remain **documented_only** without execution-model evidence.

### Initial direct-update region

For this description:

- **Large base** means a retained result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Shifted value** means a different retained result shifted left by eight.
- **Address value** means a retained result shifted left by 25.
- **Compact quantity** means a result shifted left by eight, restricted to its low fifteen bits, then shifted right by six.
- **Compact base** means twice the compact quantity.

**[INFERENCE]** Under the 32-bit bit-vector model, the compact quantity is a multiple of four from 0 through 508; the compact base ranges from 0 through 1016. These bounds do not validate any address.

The first memory effects preserve this order:

- The last paired result’s high word, used as a base, receives the address value at offset 0x14.
- The address value’s offset-9 byte receives the low byte of that high word.
- The last paired result’s low word, scaled by 256, receives the large base at offset 0x54.
- The address value’s offset-9 byte is overwritten with the low byte of the address value itself.

The function then clears the large base’s offset-0x14 word, reads its offset-5 byte, clears the compact base’s offset-0x14 word, and stores the zero-extended byte at the large base’s offset 0x34.

Only afterward does it reread the large base’s offset-0x14 word. The reread is not a license to substitute zero: intervening accesses may overlap it, and the memory environment may make separate accesses significant.

The compact base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared again. Two consecutive byte stores then write the low byte of the reread word at the compact base’s offset 0x15.

### Early fixed globals and address propagation

The next fixed-global access is the unresolved-width read associated with 0x05. Its converted 32-bit value becomes a base for offset-0x15 and offset-0x54 accesses.

After that read, the function updates the byte associated with 0x01 using the low byte of the compact base. It then writes the compact quantity’s low byte at the converted base’s offset 0x15.

The large base’s offset-0x11 byte receives the shifted value’s low byte. A calculated-address word store follows, using the earlier offset-0x14 reread as a base and writing the shifted value at offset 0x54. The offset-0x11 byte is then cleared.

The converted global-derived base’s offset-0x54 word is read and retained, then overwritten with the earlier offset-0x14 reread. Call the retained pre-overwrite word the **propagated word**.

Further operations propagate loaded words through offset-0x54, offset-0x64, and offset-0x74 accesses, interleaved with byte writes and self-derived stores. Repeated stores remain distinct represented effects.

### Same-base retained transfer

### Overwrites and interruption
