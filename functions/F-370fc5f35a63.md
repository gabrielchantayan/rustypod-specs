# F-370fc5f35a63

## Purpose

F-370fc5f35a63 performs an ordered series of unresolved indirect invocations, derives 32-bit values from incoming execution state and invocation results, modifies byte-addressed storage, and makes a final indirect invocation with four displayed arguments.

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated offset-based accesses suggest relationships between stored values, but do not establish object types, collection semantics, or ownership. Constants that coincide with character encodings do not establish text output.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** visible widths, value dependencies, accesses, and ordering in the supplied representation.
- **Inferred intent:** possible interpretations that impose no acceptance requirement.
- **documented_only:** behavior not resolved or not fully enumerated by this contract.

All behavioral requirements are conditional on the supplied representation accurately describing reachable execution. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Invocation preparation and retained values

The preparation region is a long straight-line invocation sequence. The first destination is derived from incoming execution context plus displacement 0xFFE. Later destinations use embedded anchors plus the same displacement. Their identities are unresolved.

Invocations with no displayed arguments alternate with invocations having one through four displayed arguments, including explicit zeros. Some results feed later arguments; others have no explicitly consumed result. No visible success/failure branch selects whether the next invocation occurs.

An unconsumed result does not make an invocation removable. Unknown destinations may modify memory or execution state, fault, or fail to return.

Established argument transformations include:

- The incoming state word shifted right by 15 and left by 13.
- Its low 19 bits shifted right by two.
- Its low 14 bits shifted left by three.
- Low result halves shifted left by eight, paired with high result halves.
- Extraction of bits 8–23, yielding at most 65,535.
- Extraction of bits 12–23, yielding at most 4,095.
- Extraction of bits 11–17 from a saved high result half, yielding at most 127.
- Extraction of bits 4–16 from a saved word, yielding at most 8,191.

A value first shifted left by 13 is later shifted left by 30. Under the stated 32-bit interpretation, the latter result is zero, not a selection of the original high bits.

Several argument sources are explicitly set to zero and subsequently shifted; those derived local values remain zero. This does not imply that intervening invocations preserve globals.

Saved values can remain live across many invocations. Later consumers use those saved quantities, not substituted fresh results or memory reads. Exact invocation-site enumeration and unresolved secondary-register semantics are **documented_only**.

### Opening storage mutation and zero readback

The following descriptive labels identify values, not object types:

- **Primary base:** a returned word multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Narrow index:** a returned word shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, then shifted right by six.
- **Small base:** twice the narrow index.
- **Shifted base:** a saved low result half shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled saved value:** another saved result word shifted left by eight.
- **Final result pair:** the low and high halves of the last combined result before direct stores.

The narrow index is a multiple of four between zero and 508. The small base is a multiple of eight between zero and 1,016. Primary and shifted bases are multiples of \(2^{25}\); their low bytes are zero.

The opening publication order is significant:

1. The shifted base is stored as a word at offset 0x14 from the final high result half.
2. The low byte of that high half is stored at shifted-base offset 0x09.
3. The primary base is stored as a word at offset 0x54 from the final low result half multiplied by 256.
4. The byte at shifted-base offset 0x09 is overwritten with the low byte of the shifted base, therefore zero under the stated interpretation.

The primary-base word at offset 0x14 is then cleared. A byte is read at primary-base offset 0x05, the small-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared, and the captured byte is written, zero-extended, at primary-base offset 0x34. The primary-base word at offset 0x14 is subsequently read back.

**Ordinary stable memory yields zero for this readback.** The primary-base and small-base four-byte accesses at offset 0x14 cannot partially overlap under the stated arithmetic. They coincide only when both bases are zero, and both preceding stores then write zero to the same word. The primary-base store at offset 0x34 cannot overlap its offset-0x14 word.

Device-backed storage, externally changing storage, or a different target arithmetic model remain separate uncertainties. The read remains an observable access even where ordinary-memory reasoning determines its value.

Afterward, the small-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared again, and two byte stores to its offset 0x15 use the low byte of the captured readback.

### Fixed-address input and linked storage updates

After those two byte stores, the function reads the unsigned byte at **fixed address 0x05**. It then writes the low byte of the small base to **fixed address 0x01**. The read precedes the write; it must not be moved after it.

The captured fixed-address byte becomes an address component for accesses at offsets 0x15 and 0x54. Elsewhere, bytes loaded at calculated offset 0x05 are used numerically: adding 0x74 to such a byte selects a word address between 0x74 and 0x173. This is not an established array or pointer type.

The linked-update region:

- Loads words at offsets including 0x04, 0x14, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.
- Uses loaded words as later values and bases without validation.
- Produces twice-, fourfold-, and eightfold-scaled quantities.
- Repeatedly overwrites words at offsets 0x14 and 0x54.
- Stores low-byte portions of address-like quantities at offsets 0x09, 0x0D, 0x11, and 0x15.
- Writes a doubled loaded word to fixed address 0x54 and later writes the small base’s low byte to fixed address 0x15.

Repeated calculated reads remain distinct. In particular, a word at small-base offset 0x74 occurs twice within one nested load/store expression. The representation does not establish the evaluation order of those two reads or that they return the same value.

For describing subsequent regions:

- **Linked word** denotes the earlier word loaded through the captured fixed-address byte’s offset-0x54 location.
- **Fourfold base** denotes four times the linked word.
- **Adjusted quantity** denotes the fourfold base minus 0x96.
- **Adjusted base** denotes four times the adjusted quantity.

These labels do not establish allocation boundaries or disjointness.

### Constant writes and repeated publication

A later loaded word supplies a **constant-store base**. The function writes 0x3A:

- As a byte at four times that base.
- As a halfword at eight times that base.
- As a word at offset 0x20 from four times that base.

At adjusted-base offset 0x20, the observable word-write sequence is:

- 0x20.
- 0x2F.
- An unsigned byte freshly read from adjusted-base offset 0x04, widened to a word.
- 0x20 again.

Neither the intermediate stores nor the intervening read may be collapsed into the last store.

The function next **loads the separate global storage base before writing the word 0x20 to fixed address 0x60**. The loaded base is retained for later accesses. The fixed-address write does not cause an implied reload. This ordering matters because fixed and calculated storage are not established as disjoint.

The repeated region uses:

- The retained global base.
- The adjusted base.
- Eight times the adjusted quantity.
- Four times the constant-store base as a stored value.

Each repeated group has the following observable store order:

| Position | Width and destination | Value |
|---|---|---|
| First | Word, offset 0x54 from the group’s eightfold base | Four times the constant-store base |
| Second | Byte, global-base offset 0x19 | Low byte of adjusted base |
| Third | Byte, adjusted-base offset 0x11 | Low byte of global base |
| Fourth | Word, global-base offset 0x34 | The group’s eightfold base |

The initial nearby group uses eight times the linked word; subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted quantity. Equal numeric addresses in some executions do not erase this distinction.

Many groups are explicitly present. Their exact multiplicity is **documented_only** here; this contract does not specify an invented count or reinterpret the sequence as traversal over different objects.

### Final captures, fixed-address ordering, and fresh reads

For this region, **exchange base** means twice the constant-store base.

The function first captures the word at **fixed address 0xA9**, then captures a word at offset 0x74 from four times the constant-store base. Only afterward does it write the byte at **fixed address 0x3A**. That byte is the low-byte result of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30.

Next it captures:

- A word at exchange-base offset 0x14, retained as the third final argument.
- A word at adjusted-base offset 0x04, later published at global-base offset 0x74.

Calculated byte and word stores follow, including a byte value 0x6C at adjusted-base offset 0x08 and a self-derived word at exchange-base offset 0x54.

The fixed-address byte at **0x67** is then written with the low byte of the previously captured word from 0xA9. Its subsequent consumers are fresh reads:

- **First read:** after a word load from exchange-base offset 0x44, the byte currently at 0x67 is read and written to fixed address **0x21**.
- **Second read:** after intervening calculated stores, the byte currently at 0x67 is read for a store at adjusted-base offset **0x09**.
- **Third read:** after further calculated stores and a word load from adjusted-base offset 0x54, the byte currently at 0x67 is read again for another store at adjusted-base offset **0x09**.

These are not unconditional reuses of the byte originally written to 0x67.

The interval before the second read includes stores at adjusted-base offset 0x10, global-base offsets 0x54, 0x74, and 0x44, and exchange-base offset 0x01. The byte store at adjusted-base offset 0x10 precedes a word store beginning at that same offset, sourced from a word read at adjusted-base offset 0x40.

Between the second and third reads, the function writes a byte at exchange-base offset 0x09 and writes the captured 0xA9 word at adjusted-base offset 0x44. These calculated stores may alias 0x67. The word load from adjusted-base offset 0x54 also remains an observable access.

One exchange-base byte store uses a signed right-shift expression. Its count is the low eight bits of the adjusted base. Counts outside 0–31 are not visibly rejected or normalized. Exact target behavior is **documented_only**; a modulo-32 count rule must not be assumed.

### Final publication and invocation

After the fresh-read sequence, additional stores exchange captured words and low-byte portions among the adjusted base, exchange base, and a destination loaded from adjusted-base offset 0x54.

Established dependencies include:

- The earlier captured exchange-base offset-0x14 word is restored at exchange-base offset 0x54 and retained for the final invocation.
- A word is freshly captured from adjusted-base offset 0x14 before its low byte is published elsewhere.
- A word loaded from exchange-base offset 0x04 supplies a later base.
- The halfword 0x65 is written at twice that later base.
- The second final argument is loaded from that later base’s offset 0x64.
- A separate loaded destination receives the word 100 at offset 0x54.
- The fourth final argument is then captured from the later base’s offset 0x74.

After capturing the fourth argument, the function loads another destination from exchange-base offset 0x64, performs a byte store at exchange-base offset 0x09, publishes the saved second argument at that destination’s offset 0x54, and stores that destination as a word at twice the second argument’s numeric value.

The final invocation receives:

| Position | Value |
|---|---|
| First | Constant 0x72 |
| Second | Saved word from the later base’s offset 0x64 |
| Third | Earlier captured exchange-base offset-0x14 word |
| Fourth | Saved word from the later base’s offset 0x74 |

Intervening stores do not imply fresh argument reads.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Shift truncation.** Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, a left shift by 13 followed by a left shift by 30 yields zero. Retaining wider intermediate bits would not satisfy that interpretation.

2. **Bounded address component.** Every narrow index is divisible by four and at most 508; every small base is divisible by eight and at most 1,016.

3. **Stable-memory readback.** With ordinary stable memory, the opening primary-base offset-0x14 read returns zero. If primary and small bases are both zero, both clears address the same word and still leave zero. Partial overlap is not a supported alternative.

4. **Device-backed readback.** A device-backed location may return a value other than the last stored zero. Its read must still occur; concrete device behavior is **documented_only**.

5. **Global capture before fixed write.** If the word write at 0x60 changes storage that also contains the separate global base, later accesses still use the base captured before that write.

6. **Fixed-address input ordering.** The read at 0x05 occurs before the write at 0x01. The final-region word read at 0xA9 occurs before the byte write at 0x3A.

7. **Fresh 0x67 dependency.** If an intervening calculated store changes byte 0x67 between its second and third reads, the two adjusted-base offset-0x09 stores use their respective read values, not one cached byte.

8. **Repeated constant stores.** An observing backend sees 0x20, 0x2F, the widened freshly read byte, and 0x20 at adjusted-base offset 0x20, in that order.

9. **Saved final argument.** A store after the fourth argument’s capture may change its original location without changing the saved argument passed to the final destination.

10. **Interrupted execution.** An inaccessible derived address or nonreturning dependency need not produce a graceful status. Later operations are not required to occur after execution stops.
