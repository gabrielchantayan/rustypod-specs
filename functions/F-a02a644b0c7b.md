## Behavior

### Indirect invocation prefix

The first target is derived by adding 0xffe to the incoming control-link value. Later targets repeatedly use that displacement from embedded control references.

The prefix is represented as straight-line execution, not a conditional branch or data-dependent loop. It includes calls with discarded results, calls consuming primary or secondary result channels, explicit zero arguments, and arguments derived from results retained across intervening calls.

Argument transformations include:

- Low-word left shifts, commonly by eight.
- Separately extracted high words, unchanged or shifted.
- Multiple differently shifted forms of one retained result.
- Masked unsigned extraction and logical right shifts.
- Full-width logical right shifts of paired results by 34, 36, and 44.

Discarded results do not make their producing invocations dispensable. Dependency purity, global preservation, failure conventions, and memory-management behavior are unknown.

This document does not enumerate every prefix invocation site or its complete argument tuple; that site-by-site coverage is **documented_only**. It does establish the represented invocation mechanism and width-sensitive result handling, without asserting that the invocation family resolves to one dependency.

### Arithmetic constraints

Operation width and order matter. Shifting a low 32-bit portion is not interchangeable with shifting the complete 64-bit result. Extracting the high portion before a shift is likewise distinct. A logical 64-bit right shift by 44 retains at most 20 bits.

Masks include 24-, 22-, 20-, and 19-bit selections. Examples include a 20-bit mask followed by a right shift of ten and then a left shift of five or six; 22-bit masks preceding left shifts of three, seven, or eight; and a 19-bit mask preceding a left shift of two.

Under a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector model**, word-sized shifts, multiplication, subtraction, and address calculations retain their low 32 bits. A subsequent right shift cannot recover bits discarded by an earlier left shift. Retained zeros remain zero through represented shifts until replaced.

That model is not an independently established machine guarantee. Signed overflow, signed right shifts, address wrapping, and oversized variable shifts remain **documented_only**.

### Opening mutations and retained quantities

The following descriptive roles identify values, not objects:

- **Flag base:** a dependency-derived value shifted left by 25.
- **Packed base:** another dependency result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Retained shifted word:** an earlier result shifted left by eight.
- **Compact index:** a later result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, then logically shifted right by six.
- **Compact base:** twice the compact index.
- **Final pair:** the final paired result before direct mutation.

A word containing the flag base is stored at offset 0x14 from the final pair’s high word. The high word’s low byte is written at flag-base offset 0x09. A word containing the packed base is stored at offset 0x54 from 256 times the final pair’s low word. The flag-base byte is then overwritten with the flag base’s own low byte.

Packed-base offset 0x14 is cleared before an explicit unsigned-byte capture from packed-base offset 0x05. Compact-base offset 0x14 is cleared, and the captured byte is stored as a zero-extended word at packed-base offset 0x34.

The word at packed-base offset 0x14 is then captured. Another compact-base clear precedes two separate byte stores of that capture’s low byte at compact-base offset 0x15. Aliasing can make the captured word differ from an assumed zero; the intervening accesses must not be ignored.

Under the conditional word-arithmetic model, the compact index is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. The compact base is therefore a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016. Neither range establishes memory accessibility.

### Converted-global addressing and linked mutations

After the duplicate compact-base byte stores, the named global associated with 0x05 is converted to unsigned 32 bits. **Converted-global base** denotes that captured value.

The next named-global assignment, at 0x01, receives the compact base explicitly narrowed to one byte. This occurs before the byte store of the compact index’s low byte at converted-global-base offset 0x15.

Packed-base offset 0x11 receives the retained shifted word’s low byte. The retained shifted word is also stored as a word at offset 0x54 from the earlier packed-base offset-0x14 capture. Packed-base offset 0x11 is subsequently cleared.

The old word at converted-global-base offset 0x54 is captured as the **retained link**, before that field is replaced with the earlier packed-base offset-0x14 capture. Subsequent scaling uses the retained link, not a byte-truncated version of the global.

This region traverses packed-base offset 0x74 and compact-base offset 0x74, writes word fields at offsets 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, and 0x54, and interleaves byte mutations. A self-referential-looking update contains two distinct reads of compact-base offset 0x74: one supplies the destination base, the other the stored value. Their within-expression evaluation order is unresolved.

Two further named-global assignments have specific state effects and positions:

| Global association | Assigned value | Ordering boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 0x54 | Retained link shifted left by one | After compact-base offset 0x14 receives the dependency-derived value represented as the packed-base producer shifted left by 26; before that field receives the retained link twice |
| 0x15 | Compact base explicitly narrowed to one byte | After two byte stores of four times the retained link’s low byte at compact-base offset 0x15; before the next retained-shifted-word store at four-times-link offset 0x54 |

Assigned-value narrowing remains separate from each global’s unknown storage width.

Four times the retained link becomes a frequently reused base. Compact-base offset 0x34 receives that scaled link. A captured compact-base offset-0x74 word is doubled and used as another mutation base. Eight times the retained link supplies further word and byte values.

An initial explicit unsigned-byte load from four-times-link offset 0x05 supplies the base for a word lookup at offset 0x74. That lookup’s result becomes an **initial lookup base**, used for later offset-0x44 stores. Additional traversal uses four-times-link offset 0x64.

A later, distinct reload sequence must preserve both byte captures:

- A new address-bearing word is loaded from four-times-link offset 0x04.
- After intervening byte and word mutations, an unsigned byte is captured from offset 0x05 of that newly loaded base. This **first capture** is zero-extended and stored as a word at initial-lookup-base offset 0x44.
- Further mutations affect four-times-link offset 0x14 and the doubled traversal base’s offsets 0x15 and 0x44.
- The same offset-0x05 byte field is read again. This **second capture**, not the first, supplies the base for the later word lookup at offset 0x74.

The second lookup yields the **selected word**. A changed byte between captures can therefore change the selected word without changing the already completed zero-extended store.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional review criteria, not executed tests.

### Converted-global addressing

If the conversion of the global at 0x05 produces 0x1234, its offset-0x54 word address is 0x1288 under nonwrapping address addition. Truncating the converted base to 0x34 and using 0x88 is unacceptable. Whether the actual global declaration permits that input remains unresolved.

### Distinct byte captures

Suppose the first explicit byte capture from the newly loaded base’s offset 0x05 produces 0x12. Initial-lookup-base offset 0x44 receives word value 0x00000012.

If an intervening mutation changes that byte to 0xfe, the second capture supplies word-lookup address 0x172 under ordinary addition. Using address 0x86 from the first capture would violate the reload ordering.

### Fixed-global assignment values

Under 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, compact base 0x3f8 supplies one-byte-converted value 0xf8 to the globals at 0x01 and 0x15, at their distinct ordering boundaries.

A retained link of 0x80000003 supplies 0x00000006 to the global at 0x54 after its left shift by one. These assigned values do not establish destination storage widths.

### Width-sensitive arithmetic

For a paired result whose low word is 1 and high word is 2, the low word shifted by eight, unchanged high word, and high word shifted by six are 256, 2, and 128.

Masking 0x000fffff to 20 bits, logically shifting right by ten, then left by six produces 65472. A 64-bit shift by 44 must not be replaced with a 32-bit shift using a reduced count.

### Aliasing and access extent

An explicit byte-pointer store derived from 0x123456ab writes 0xab without authorizing changes to three surrounding bytes. A 16-bit store of 0x65 is not interchangeable with that byte store.

By contrast, a named-global assignment with a one-byte-converted value cannot be assumed to affect exactly one byte. If its actual storage overlaps a later capture, acceptance depends on that declaration and the target layout.

### Publication and failure

Where individual writes are observable, collapsing repeated publication groups into one group is unacceptable despite equal final contents.

An invalid target or inaccessible address has no established graceful-error, retry, or rollback behavior.
