# F-b56563617676

## Purpose

F-b56563617676 accepts five 32-bit arguments and returns a 32-bit result. Global values control a dispatch loop whose operations activate arguments, obtain dependency-produced data, update two object fields, transform working words, invoke dependencies, and select a return value.

**Established static behavior:** the supplied body establishes the operations and their ordering, including distinct reads of the same global location. It does not establish the global contents needed to determine a normal route, termination, or dependency-call counts.

**Inferred intent:** some routes resemble acquisition of object-related data, preparation of temporary outputs, processing, and cleanup. These interpretations do not establish success conventions or ownership.

## Behavior

### Notation and arithmetic

The following are specification labels, not implementation identifiers:

- **S:** the first cached global value.
- **P:** the second cached global value.
- **N:** the signed negation of a later, separate read from the location supplying P.
- **R:** a working word also used to derive the stored result.
- **A, B, C:** three other independent working words.

R, A, B, and C are unsigned 32-bit words, initially zero. Changing one does not implicitly change the others.

“Complement” means complementing all 32 bits. “High bit” means extracting bit 31 as zero or one. Retaining a full complement is observably different from retaining only its high bit.

Unsigned arithmetic is modulo \(2^{32}\). Signed operands and intermediate expressions require separate treatment:

- Negating the global read used for N may exceed the signed 32-bit range.
- Products containing only signed operands can overflow before assignment to an unsigned working word.
- Signed dispatch subtraction, successor adjustments, active-input scaling, object-address additions, and several dependency-argument products can also overflow.
- Converting a high-bit-set unsigned word to a signed destination requires the target’s conversion behavior.

Consequently, product descriptions below preserve operand grouping where it affects signed intermediates. They do not assert that all arithmetic is portable wrapping arithmetic.

### Entry and dispatch

Entry reads S and then P. It saves arguments 1–4 and initializes local state. It then separately reads P’s global location and negates that value to obtain N. The initial dispatch word is read afterward.

N therefore need not equal the negation of cached P.

At each dispatch, the routine reads a global **dispatch base** and subtracts it from the current dispatch word. Selectors 0–16 have explicit operations, except that selector 6 shares its operation with every unmatched selector.

A successor source denotes a global location, not a known selector value. Unless noted otherwise, different selectors have distinct successor locations. Selectors 11 and 12 share one. Selectors 13 and 16 use the dispatch-base location as their successor source.

The base is read again on every dispatch. Loading a dispatch word from that location does not guarantee that the next subtraction selects zero.

Dependencies are not assumed to preserve globals. Cached S, P, and N remain cached, while subsequent global reads can observe changed contents.

### Selector 1: ordered input activation

Selector 1 performs these operations in this order:

1. Sets the first array index to zero.
2. Sets the second array index to one.
3. Activates argument 4 from its saved copy.
4. Activates argument 3 from its saved copy.
5. Activates argument 5.
6. Reads its successor global into the next dispatch word.
7. Activates argument 1 from its saved copy.
8. Activates argument 2 from its saved copy.

Thus, at the successor-global read, arguments 4, 3, and 5 have been activated, but active arguments 1 and 2 still have their preceding values. On the first activation those preceding values are zero; on later visits they may differ.

Both remaining activations complete before the next dispatch-base read. Revisiting this selector restores active argument 2 from the original argument rather than retaining prior scaling.

### Selector 2: fourth-input selection

Selector 2 contains two references to its successor-global location:

- One supplies a value adjusted by +5.
- The other supplies an unadjusted value.

If active argument 4 is zero, the adjusted alternative contributes to the next dispatch word. Otherwise, the unadjusted alternative contributes.

Both global references occur; selection is arithmetic, not a short-circuit conditional read. Their relative evaluation order is not established by the supplied expression, and their observed values need not agree. The +5 adjustment is signed arithmetic.

### Working-state operations

|---|---|---|

| 5 | Multiplies signed S by signed N, then by B; assigns the resulting word to A and R. B is unchanged. | Its successor global. |

Selector 8 returns the stored result immediately, with no successor read.

### Two-read conditional transitions

Selectors 3, 7, 9, 14, and 15 each read their respective successor-global location twice. The first read supplies an adjusted alternative; the second supplies an unadjusted alternative.

Their common selection rule is:

- Routing bit zero selects the adjusted first value.
- Routing bit one selects the unadjusted second value.

Both reads occur regardless of the routing bit. Their values need not match. The final arithmetic selection uses the extracted unsigned bit.

**Selector 3**

Using the previous A:

- B becomes N multiplied by that A.
- The routing bit is the high bit of the complement of the OR of B and P multiplied by that previous A.
- R becomes S multiplied by B.

The routine then reads its successor global and adjusts the value by −4. It next multiplies active argument 2 by S, reads the successor global again, and copies R into A before selecting the next dispatch word.

This selector changes B, R, A, and active argument 2. It does not retain the full predicate complement in R.

**Selector 7**

The routine first reads object offset 0x28 and multiplies the signed field value by S. It then reads its successor global for the −6 alternative.

R becomes the full complement of the OR of N times the field product and P times that product. Its high bit supplies routing. The routine then performs the second successor read and resets A to zero before completing selection.

B and C are unchanged. R retains the entire complemented word, not just the routing bit.

**Selector 14**

B becomes N multiplied by C. R becomes the full complement of the OR of B and P multiplied by C.

After extracting R’s high bit, the routine reads its successor global for the +9 alternative and then reads the same location for the unadjusted alternative. A and C are unchanged.

Selectors 9 and 15 use the same two-read selection mechanism after dependency calls, as described below.

### Selector 9: array acquisition

The routine resets the array-related output slot to zero and calls F-5e93937f0cfc with:

1. The local array address.
2. Active argument 4.
3. The output-slot address.

After normal return, C becomes S multiplied by the dependency return. This multiplication has signed operands before assignment to C.

The routine then reads its successor global for the −2 alternative. R becomes the full complement of the OR of:

- N multiplied by S, then by the current output-slot value.
- P multiplied by S, then by the current output-slot value.

These predicate products have signed operands. R’s high bit supplies routing. The routine then performs the second successor read.

A and B are unchanged. Neither the output slot nor the return value has an established count, Boolean, or error meaning. No array-capacity argument is supplied, and the routine does not verify dependency write bounds or initialized elements.

### Selector 4: ordered object writes

The routine reads the array element selected by the first index, multiplies N by S and then by that element, and writes the four-byte result to active-object offset 0x28.

It then performs the corresponding array read and multiplication using the second index and writes to offset 0x24. Only after both writes does it read its successor global.

All operands in these products are signed. After activation, the selected indices are zero and one; before activation, both are zero.

There is no bounds check, null check, or rollback. The first write can become observable before a failure during the second operation. No established routing guarantee requires selector 9 to initialize the array before selector 4 reads it.

### Selector 15: temporary-output preparation

The routine resets the first temporary-output slot to zero, then resets the second to zero. It reads object offset 0x28 and calls F-57598f835995 with:

1. The first output-slot address.
2. Active argument 4.
3. The second output-slot address.
4. N multiplied by S, then by the signed object-field value.

After normal return, C becomes N multiplied by S, then by the dependency return. These products have signed operands before assignment.

The routine then reads its successor global for the −1 alternative. Its routing bit is the high bit of the complement of the OR of C and P multiplied by S and then by the dependency return.

R becomes S multiplied by C. The routine reads the successor global again and copies R into B before completing selection.

C therefore holds one transformed return, while R and B include an additional multiplication by S. A is unchanged. The full predicate complement is not retained in R.

The two output values have no established semantic type or ownership meaning.

### Selector 6 and unmatched selectors: shared dependency operation

The routine calls F-2f10c1690f2f with:

| Position | Value |
|---|---|
| 1 | Active object address. |
| 2 | Current first temporary-output value. |
| 3 | A freshly read global factor multiplied by active argument 5. |
| 4 | Active argument 3. |

The factor read occurs before the call. Relative ordering among argument evaluations is otherwise not established. The factor product and sixth-argument product have signed operands.

The two calls are not guaranteed to receive identical output-slot values if a dependency can alter the slot through a retained address.

After the second call returns, B becomes S multiplied by N and then by the saved return of the first call. This is an all-signed product before assignment to B. R receives B, and only then is the shared successor global read.

The second dependency’s return is unused. A and C are unchanged.

## Side effects

Dependency effects on memory, globals, resources, and subsequent reads are unknown. In particular, the first shared-operation call may change state observed by the second call or later dispatch.

The array and output slots have invocation-local lifetime. Passing their addresses establishes neither safe retention beyond that lifetime nor a promise that dependencies do not retain them. No direct allocation or deallocation is established.

F-5e3309c9d083 is cleanup-like only by inference. The routine does not establish that each prepared value reaches it, reaches it once, or is released.

Path-dependent preconditions include accessible object memory, initialized array elements before reads, valid address arithmetic and alignment, bounded dependency writes, and unknown dependency-specific requirements. These are not checked. There is no explicit recovery path for invalid memory access, exceptions, or dependencies that do not return normally.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Numerical examples assume the stated arithmetic and conversion behavior.

1. **Immediate return.** If the initial dispatch subtraction selects 8, the returned result is zero. Entry and dispatch global reads still occur, but no dependency call or object access occurs.

2. **Activation order.** On selector 1, the index resets precede activation of arguments 4, 3, and 5, in that order. The successor-global read follows those activations and precedes activation of arguments 1 and 2. On a first visit, the active object and active argument 2 are still zero at that read.

3. **Repeated activation.** If selector 3 has scaled active argument 2, a later selector 1 restores the original argument 2 only after its successor-global read. The following dispatch uses the restored value.

4. **Field-write order.** After activation, with valid memory and initialized elements 7 and 11, S = 1 and N = −1 cause selector 4 to write −7 at offset 0x28 before −11 at offset 0x24.

5. **Independent working words.** With S = 1, N = −1, R = 2, A = 3, and B = 5, selector 0 sets A and R to the word representing −2. Selector 5 instead sets them to the word representing −5. Selector 11 instead changes only R to the word representing −3.

6. **Retained complement.** With S = P = 1, N = −1, and field 0x28 equal to zero, selector 7 retains R = 0xFFFFFFFF and routes using bit one. If selector 10 follows without changing R, its stored result is 1 under 32-bit word arithmetic.

7. **Distinct successor observations.** If selector 14’s successive reads observe 100 and 200, routing bit zero produces dispatch word 109; routing bit one produces 200. Both reads occur. The next selector also depends on the next base read.

8. **Post-call reads.** Selector 9 reads its successor location only after F-5e93937f0cfc returns. Selector 15 likewise waits for F-57598f835995. Dependency changes to those globals can affect routing.

9. **Shared-call sequence.** On normal returns, F-2f10c1690f2f precedes F-5e3309c9d083, and both precede the successor read. A zero first output does not suppress either call.

10. **Activation bypass and base changes.** An object access before selector 1 uses the zero active object address, not argument 1. Separately, selectors 13 and 16 do not guarantee next selector zero when the dispatch base changes between its two reads.

## Uncertainties

The following remain **documented_only**:

- Actual global contents, reachable routes, termination, repeated visits, and call counts.
- Stability of globals between reads.
- Object-field meanings and argument/output semantics.
- Dependency mutation, ownership, retention, release, and error contracts.
- Behavior after invalid accesses or abnormal dependency completion.
- Target handling of signed overflow, address arithmetic, and unsigned-to-signed conversions.

Under P = 1, N = −1, and 32-bit word arithmetic, the high bit of the complement of the OR of N times a word and P times that word acts as a zero test. This conditional property does not establish actual global values or a success convention.

Missing global contents do not erase established ordering or independent working-state relationships. In particular, selector 1’s successor read occurs between activation of arguments 5 and 1, not after activation of all five inputs.
