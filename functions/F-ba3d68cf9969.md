# F-ba3d68cf9969

## Purpose

Its established behavior is the order of these operations, the relationships between their target-selection reads, and the arguments explicitly supplied to each target. Opening and closing targets are selected from a callback slot at byte offset 48. The central target is selected from a slot at byte offset 24.

The opening and closing selections are independent. They occur at different times and can produce different targets, including one null selection and one non-null selection.

The constants zero and one suggest distinct phases, but their application-level meaning is not established. Locking, resource acquisition, notification, and cleanup are possible interpretations, not contractual behavior.

- The saved base determines the addresses of the later central and closing slots.

If it is null, the opening invocation is skipped. Central processing still follows.

If it is non-null, the target receives these five represented arguments, in order:

1. The first declared 32-bit input.
2. Numeric zero.
3. The selected opening target pointer itself.
4. The fourth declared 32-bit input.
5. The unrecovered 32-bit register-associated value.

After the opening invocation is skipped or returns normally, the function loads the central target from byte offset 24 of the saved base.

This is a fresh selection. The opening pointer is not reused.

The first and fourth declared inputs, the opening self-pointer argument, and the additional 32-bit register-associated argument are not explicitly supplied to the closing target.

The opening and closing argument lists therefore differ. The representation does not establish one verified callback prototype that explains both uses. Selected targets must be compatible with their actual invocations for normal execution.

An opening invocation does not guarantee a closing invocation. Conversely, a closing invocation does not require an opening invocation.
