# F-c1964541c3bb

### Widths and arithmetic

The representation uses 8-, 16-, 32-, and 64-bit quantities. Unless otherwise qualified, numerical acceptance examples use a **32-bit bit-vector interpretation**:

- Left shifts and multiplication retain the low 32 bits.
- Addition and subtraction wrap modulo 2³².
- Unsigned right shifts introduce zeros.
- Byte stores retain eight bits; halfword stores retain sixteen bits.
- An unsigned byte converted to a word yields a value from 0 through 255.
- A represented 64-bit result has distinct low and high 32-bit portions.

Field masks include widths of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 14, 13, 12, and 11 bits, with smaller masks also present. Masking after a truncating shift need not equal masking the original value.

Unsigned 64-bit right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 positions occur before conversion to 32 bits. They must not be replaced by shifts of an already truncated low word.

Successive 32-bit left shifts do not gain a wider intermediate. Under the bit-vector interpretation, a cumulative displacement of at least 32 removes all original bits.

These rules define conditional numerical interpretations, not established instruction semantics for every signed expression. Signed overflow, signed left shift, pointer arithmetic, and the late variable-count signed right shift remain **documented_only** where instruction-level clarification is required.

No saturation, range checking, or bounds enforcement is represented.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static expectations, not executed tests.

1. **Entry truncation.** For third-input pattern 0x08000001, its five-position 32-bit left shift is 0x00000020. F-unknown-entry receives the original pattern third and the truncated pattern fourth.

2. **Separated result portions.** For an applicable result with low portion 0x12345678 and high portion 0x9ABCDEF0, the eight-position low-word shift produces 0x34567800. Forwarding the separate high portion preserves 0x9ABCDEF0.

3. **Mask placement.** Selecting the low 24 bits of 0xFF123456 and shifting six positions produces 0x048D1580. An unmasked shift is not equivalent.

4. **Initial zero reload.** With ordinary stable memory and the stated address interpretation, the initial word reload at the shifted base plus 0x14 returns zero. The intervening zero store cannot make it nonzero, and the word store at plus 0x34 is disjoint.

5. **Repeated accesses.** Both initial consecutive byte stores remain represented accesses even when both store zero. Likewise, repeated association groups are not reduced to their final memory state.

6. **Disjoint numeric-store read.** If the derived association base is 0x1008, the byte read is at 0x100C and the preceding word writes cover 0x1028–0x102B. With stable ordinary memory, changing those word values does not change the byte read.

7. **Wraparound remains disjoint.** A captured association value of 0x24 yields adjusted value 0xFFFFFFFA and derived base 0xFFFFFFE8. The relevant byte read is at 0xFFFFFFEC; the word writes cover 0x00000008–0x0000000B. Wraparound does not create overlap.

8. **Global read before fixed write.** The retained association base is captured before the word store at 0x60. Obtaining it only after that store does not preserve the represented ordering.

9. **Fresh fixed-byte reads.** If an intervening derived write changes 0x67 before a later plus-0x09 store, that store uses its own read of 0x67, not a cached byte from the earlier 0xA9 capture.

10. **Numeric width.** The halfword store of 0x003A is one 16-bit access. Its byte representation depends on endianness; two character emissions are unsupported.

11. **Failure boundary.** An invalid indirect target has no established error return. Neither silent success nor guaranteed persistence of preceding writes is supported.

12. **Terminal boundary.** Reaching F-unknown-terminal requires first displayed argument 0x72. Normal completion remains conditional on that dependency returning.
