# F-e425e1b25b69

## Purpose

F-e425e1b25b69 specifies an ordered mutation of potentially overlapping memory, followed by external control transfers. No application-level purpose is established.

Its defining behavior is the distinction between **retained observations** and **fresh observations**. A retained value continues to supply later effects even when its original storage changes. Conversely, revisiting an address can produce a different value because intervening writes may overlap it. An implementation that snapshots all inputs, merges repeated writes, or caches repeated reads would not generally satisfy this contract.

Two byte writes consume values without visible preceding definitions. Their payloads are unresolved; they are not specified as zero, arbitrary selectable bytes, or values established later. Consequently, the supplied representation does not establish a fully defined execution through the entire operation.

## Behavior

### Observation and ordering contract

The descriptions below identify dependencies and observable ordering boundaries, not source statements or an implementation recipe.

A retained address preserves the address value, not the contents found there. Repeated observations remain distinct even when their addresses coincide. Writes likewise remain distinct when intervening accesses could expose their effects.

Twelve global observations select addresses during the mutation phase. Where an address selection and a replacement payload come from the same global source, they are separate acquisitions. Equality between them is not guaranteed if that source changes.

The active mutation address changes five times:

- The first two replacements come from selected-address offset 0x14.
- The third comes from the preceding mutation address’s offset 0x14.
- The last two come from offset 0x14 relative to widened signed-halfword values.

These constraints establish neither atomicity nor synchronization. Ordering within a compound expression is not strengthened beyond its represented dependencies.

### Opening effects and stable payloads

The opening retained word is observed at initial-observation-address offset 0x14. Its low halfword supplies the write addressed by the third argument plus the initial observation address. The fourth argument’s byte write at initial-mutation-address offset 0x11 precedes that halfword write.

The observation at initial-observation-address offset 0x44 occurs after both writes. Thus, overlap can affect this later observation without changing the already retained opening payload.

The opening region also has these distinct effects:

- Initial-mutation-address offset 0x54 receives the initial mutation address itself.
- The opening retained word identifies an offset-0x44 replacement destination.
- The later observation-address word is forwarded to a globally selected address’s offset 0x14.
- Initial-mutation-address offset 0x11 receives the low byte of the doubled initial mutation address.

The first argument’s offset-0x64 word is observed once. Its retained low byte supplies two later writes at initial-mutation-address offset 0x11. These are not fresh reads of the argument-relative location.

Between those two consumers, the operation observes mutation-address offset 0x44 and a sum-addressed signed halfword, writes the initial mutation address to offset 0x54 of another retained address, and performs an indirect offset-0x44 replacement. Overlap with these effects does not refresh the retained argument-derived byte.

### Preliminary address, anchor, and reciprocal value

Two separate acquisitions obtain words at offset 0x44 relative to widened, sum-addressed signed halfwords. Call their results the **preliminary address** and the **anchor**. Mutations intervene; the acquisitions are not interchangeable.

Preliminary-address offset 0x64 supplies a word whose low byte is written to initial-mutation-address offset 0x11.

The anchor supports the following observation roles:

| Location | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Offset 0x04 | One retained word, used both whole and as a low byte. |
| Offset 0x64 | Six separate word observations: a byte consumer, a full-word consumer, then four byte consumers. |
| Offset 0x74 | One late retained word. |

After the anchor-offset-0x04 observation, a word from selected-address offset 0x74 becomes the retained **reciprocal value**. Initial-mutation-address offset 0x54 receives the anchor-derived word, then receives the initial mutation address before the retained word’s low byte is consumed at offset 0x11.

Anchor offset 0x54 receives the reciprocal value twice. Reciprocal-value offset 0x54 separately receives the anchor. Selected-address offset 0x1D receives the reciprocal value’s low byte. These effects do not guarantee a lasting reciprocal relationship: later or overlapping writes can destroy it.

A separate halfword destination combines a selected address with twice the initial mutation address. Its payload is the low halfword of an earlier selected-address-offset-0x14 observation.

### Replacement visibility and traversal provenance

The third byte-derived region imposes an important visibility boundary. Byte writes at anchor offset 0x01 and selected-address offset 0x0D precede installation of the retained replacement value at selected-address offset 0x44. The byte-derived offset-0x0D write and associated halfword write follow that installation.

Only after those later writes is selected-address offset 0x44 observed again. Therefore:

- Earlier overlapping narrow writes may be overwritten by replacement installation.
- Later overlapping narrow writes may alter the freshly observed word.
- That fresh observation is not necessarily equal to the retained replacement value.

Three subsequent halfword destinations depend on different traversals. One incorporates a word reached through offset 0x14; two incorporate separately acquired offset-0x74 words. The traversals remain independent even if they ultimately select equal addresses.

The third anchor-offset-0x64 observation supplies an offset-0x11 byte before the third mutation-address transition. The fourth is acquired before that transition but supplies the corresponding byte at the new mutation address afterward.

Another redirection writes a selected address’s low byte at retained-word-relative offset 0x15, then installs that retained word at selected-address offset 0x44. This installation uses the retained word, not the retained replacement value.
