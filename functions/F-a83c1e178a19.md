# F-a83c1e178a19

## Purpose

F-a83c1e178a19 computes and directly stores nine 32-bit coefficient words in a strided destination, then clears one byte at destination offset 64. Its calculations depend on three signed components, an opaque 32-bit parameter, dependency returns, and one explicitly read global configuration value.

The coefficient relationships and scale constant 65,536 suggest a fixed-point axis-angle rotation construction. This is **inferred intent**, not a guaranteed mathematical interpretation. The available evidence does not establish normalization, angle units, trigonometric semantics, orthogonality, or determinant.

The contract distinguishes three things:

- Arithmetic expression results.
- Values written by this function’s direct store events.
- Destination contents remaining after dependencies and other actors have executed.

These are not interchangeable. A dependency executes between the first and second coefficient stores and may modify the first stored word through an independently available alias. Some unsigned expression results also require conversion before assignment to signed destination words; the target behavior of out-of-range conversions is not established.

## Behavior

### Arithmetic interpretation

The repeated product extraction can be described independently of the surrounding coefficient formulas.

For signed 32-bit operands \(a\) and \(b\), define \(Q(a,b)\) as the 32-bit word consisting of bits 16 through 47 of their exact signed 64-bit product. Its unsigned numerical value is:

\[
U(Q(a,b))=
\left\lfloor\frac{ab}{65{,}536}\right\rfloor
\bmod 2^{32},
\]

where \(U\) denotes interpretation as an unsigned 32-bit word.

When an extracted word subsequently becomes a signed multiplier, the formulas below use its signed 32-bit interpretation. This distinction matters: the extracted word is not an unrestricted mathematical integer carried unchanged through later products.

Every signed 32-by-32-bit product fits in signed 64 bits. Extraction nevertheless discards both fractional information and any product information above bit 47. There is no saturation or rounding to nearest. For a negative product with a fractional part, the extraction corresponds to rounding downward before reduction to 32 bits, not truncating division toward zero.

Nested extractions must be retained. For example, extracting a two-factor product before multiplying by a third factor is not generally equivalent to one combined three-factor calculation.

Arithmetic outside those products has two distinct treatments:

- The component-return sum, the subtraction from 65,536, and the first coefficient addition are signed 32-bit operations.
- The remaining eight final coefficient expressions use unsigned 32-bit arithmetic because their extracted-word operands are unsigned. Their expression results are reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).

Signed overflow behavior is **documented_only**. In particular, the component-return sum includes an intermediate addition; a representable final mathematical sum does not make an overflowing intermediate addition valid.

Unsigned expression results and signed destination values must remain separate. An unsigned result exceeding 2,147,483,647 is assigned to a signed destination word, but the target’s out-of-range conversion behavior has not been independently established. Consequently, an unsigned result such as 4,294,901,760 does not, by itself, establish a signed stored value of −65,536.
