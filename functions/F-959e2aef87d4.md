### Arithmetic boundaries

**Established static evidence:** some aggregate extractions shift the unsigned 64-bit result before narrowing. Right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 bits therefore select the original high half shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 bits, respectively. They are not oversized shifts of an already narrowed 32-bit operand.

**[INFERENCE]:** with 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, left shifts, multiplication, and address arithmetic retain their low 32 bits. A shift followed by another shift cannot recover bits discarded by the first operation. Some chained shifts consequently become zero regardless of the original input.

This interpretation must not be silently generalized to every signed-looking expression. Exact signed overflow, negative-value right shifts, and machine address calculation remain **documented_only** without verified instruction semantics.

A late byte store uses a right shift whose count is the low eight bits of a retained address-like value. The count can therefore be between 0 and 255. Counts of 32 or greater must not be silently reduced modulo 32 or assigned another architecture’s behavior.
