## Behavior

The following phases describe the established access order. A captured word retains the value obtained at its stated read point; subsequent changes to its source storage do not themselves change that captured value.

### Initial write and thirteen repeated groups

The first memory operation stores the secondary input as a word at B plus `0x34`.

It is followed by **exactly thirteen occurrences** of the same ordered four-write group, counting the first group immediately after that initial store. Each group performs:

1. A word store of the primary input at twice A plus `0x54`.
2. A byte store of the low byte of A at B plus `0x19`.
3. A byte store of the low byte of B at A plus `0x11`.
4. A word store of twice A at B plus `0x34`.

Thus, this prefix contains **53 memory writes**: the initial secondary-input store and 52 writes across the thirteen groups. There are no intervening memory reads.

For each of the four group destinations, the corresponding write occurs thirteen times. B plus `0x34` receives fourteen word stores in total: the secondary input first, then twice A thirteen times.

The repetition is fixed, not conditional or a demonstrated retry mechanism. An equivalent observable implementation cannot collapse it to final assignments where write history matters, including device-backed storage or an access observer.

### Early snapshots and fixed-address publication

After all thirteen groups, the operation captures, in order:

1. A word beginning at fixed address `0xA9`.
2. A word at the primary input plus `0x74`.

It then stores a byte at fixed address `0x3A`. Under the apparent byte model, its value is twice the low byte of A plus `0x30`, reduced to eight bits. No digit-range check or validation branch is visible.

Two more word snapshots follow:

1. The word at C plus `0x14`.
2. The word at A plus `0x04`.

The next writes, in order, are:

| Destination | Width | Value |
|---|---|---|
| C plus `0x0D` | Byte | Low byte of A |
| A plus `0x08` | Byte | `0x6C` |
| A plus `0x0D` | Byte | Low byte of B |
| A plus `0x09` | Byte | Low byte of B |
| C plus `0x54` | Word | C |
| Fixed address `0x67` | Byte | Low byte of the earlier word captured at `0xA9` |

A word is then read at C plus `0x44`. This contract calls its captured value the **first derived value**.

After that word read, the current byte at fixed address `0x67` is read and stored at fixed address `0x21`. This is a read from storage at that point, not merely another use of the earlier `0xA9` snapshot.

### First derived value and replacement writes

The low byte of the first derived value is stored at A plus `0x10`. The complete first derived value is then stored as a word at B plus `0x54`.

Next, C plus `0x01` receives a byte derived from a signed right shift of the first derived value. The shift count is A masked to its low eight bits. The stored byte is the low-byte conversion of that shift result.

The signed-shift result for negative operands and the behavior for counts outside the operand width are unresolved. No additional masking to five bits is visible.

The word at B plus `0x54` is then overwritten with A.

A word is read at A plus `0x40`. Before that captured word is stored elsewhere, the earlier snapshot from A plus `0x04` is written as a word at B plus `0x74`. The captured word from A plus `0x40` is then written at A plus `0x10`.

That last word store covers the earlier byte locations A plus `0x10` and A plus `0x11`. Their previous byte values are therefore not final-state guarantees, even when the context regions are otherwise disjoint.

The following accesses occur next:

- C is stored as a word at B plus `0x44`.
- The current byte at fixed address `0x67` is read and stored at A plus `0x09`.
- The low byte of A is stored at C plus `0x09`.
- The original word snapshot from fixed address `0xA9` is stored at A plus `0x44`.

### Second derived address

A word is read at A plus `0x54`. Its captured value is the **second derived address**.

This read is distinct from every prefix store at twice A plus `0x54`. The contract does not treat those address expressions as interchangeable.

After this read, the current byte at fixed address `0x67` is read again and stored at A plus `0x09`. The two later copies from `0x67` are separate reads at different points in the access sequence.

The subsequent accesses are:

| 1 | Store the low byte of the primary-input-plus-`0x74` snapshot at the second derived address plus `0x0D`. |

The write at C plus `0x54` replaces the earlier C-valued word there, subject to intervening alias effects. Likewise, A plus `0x44` is explicitly assigned first the `0xA9` snapshot and later the second derived address.

### Final derived-address chain

A word is read at C plus `0x04`. Its captured value is the **third derived value**, interpreted as a signed integer by the subsequent arithmetic.

A halfword with numeric value **`0x0065`** is then stored at the address equal to **twice the third derived value**.

These operands have separate roles:

- The third derived value supplies the destination through signed doubling.
- `0x0065` is only the value stored.
- The destination is not twice `0x0065` unless the third derived value happens to equal `0x0065`.

Signed overflow in that destination calculation remains unresolved.

Next, a word is read at the third derived value plus `0x64`. Its captured value is the **fourth derived value**.

Two stores follow:

1. The low byte of the earlier A-plus-`0x14` snapshot is written at the second derived address plus `0x11`.
2. Word value `0x00000064` is written at the second derived address plus `0x54`.

The operation then captures:

1. A word at the third derived value plus `0x74`.
2. A word at C plus `0x64`, called the **fifth derived value**.
