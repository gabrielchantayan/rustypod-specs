## Behavior

### Execution and numeric interpretation

The visible body consists of a long straight-line indirect-call region, a direct-memory mutation region, and the final transfer. No explicit conditional branch or loop is visible. Dependencies may contain either.

Values have represented widths of 8, 16, 32, and 64 bits. Byte stores retain eight bits; halfword stores retain sixteen; word stores retain thirty-two. Byte reads used numerically are zero-extended.

Paired dependency results are repeatedly separated into low and high 32-bit words. Unsigned shifts of a full 64-bit result must occur before narrowing: a right shift by 36 extracts the high word shifted logically right by 4, not a shifted low word.

The transformations include masks selecting fields of 11 through 14 bits, 16 through 22 bits, and 24 bits; logical right shifts; repeated low-word left shifts by 8; and shifts of high words.

**Conditional arithmetic model:** conclusions below involving wraparound assume 32-bit bit-vector arithmetic, with truncation after each represented 32-bit operation. Signed overflowing shifts and multiplication do not establish portable executable semantics. Their actual machine interpretation remains `documented_only`.

Intermediate truncation is material. For example, a 32-bit value shifted left by 25 and then by 11 becomes zero under this model, even though an unbounded intermediate might not.

### Initial writes and the early fixed-storage base

The initial mutations publish the packed base as a word at final-pair high word plus `0x14`. Packed base plus `0x09` first receives the low byte of that high word. Between this byte write and its overwrite with the packed base’s low byte, a word containing the scaled base is written at final-pair low word multiplied by 256, plus `0x54`.

Scaled base plus `0x14` receives a zero word before the byte at scaled base plus `0x05` is read. That byte is widened for a word store at scaled base plus `0x34`. A zero-word store at index base plus `0x14` precedes that widened store. The word at scaled base plus `0x14` is then captured, followed by another zero-word store at index base plus `0x14` and two byte writes of the captured word’s low byte at index base plus `0x15`.

The capture must not simply be replaced with zero: intervening computed destinations may alias its source.

**The early fixed-storage base is the zero-extended byte read at address `0x05`.** This read occurs after those two byte writes. Its captured value is retained as a base; subsequent accesses through it are not fresh reads of address `0x05`.

The following temporal relationships are established:

- Fixed byte address `0x01` receives the index base’s low byte immediately after the fixed-base capture.
- The captured fixed base plus `0x15` receives the narrow index’s low byte.
- Scaled base plus `0x11` receives the shifted payload’s low byte; the earlier captured word plus `0x54` receives the full shifted payload; scaled base plus `0x11` is then overwritten with zero.
- A word at captured fixed base plus `0x54` is captured as the **old word**, before that location is overwritten with the earlier captured word.
- Index base plus `0x34` subsequently receives the narrow index as a word.

Thus the old word depends on storage observed after the fixed `0x01` write and intervening computed stores, not on an entry snapshot.

### Old-word publication and further dependent accesses

A word loaded from scaled base plus `0x74` becomes another base. Its `0x54` destination receives the scale source shifted left by 26, and later the shifted payload. Between these publications, repeated reads of index base plus `0x74` participate in a word publication through the loaded value. Those repeated reads must not automatically be collapsed.

The old word is published twice at old word plus `0x24`; its low byte is likewise published twice at the loaded base plus `0x09`, with an intervening word store at that base plus `0x54`.

The fixed word write at address `0x54` has a specific value and placement: **it stores the old word shifted left by one**, after index base plus `0x14` receives the scale source shifted left by 26, and before two word publications of the old word at index base plus `0x14`.

Define the **quadrupled old base** as four times the old word. After those two publications, index base plus `0x15` receives its low byte twice. **Fixed byte address `0x15` then receives the index base’s low byte.** This precedes the next word write at quadrupled old base plus `0x54` and the word write of the quadrupled old base at index base plus `0x34`.

Further accesses use loaded words and widened bytes as address bases without validation. Important relationships include:

- A fresh word at index base plus `0x74`, doubled, supplies subsequent stored values and destinations.
- A byte at quadrupled old base plus `0x05`, widened and offset by `0x74`, selects another word-valued base.
- Word stores at offsets `0x14`, `0x24`, `0x44`, and `0x54` interleave with byte stores at offsets `0x09`, `0x0d`, and `0x15`.
- A word read at quadrupled old base plus `0x04` selects a base whose byte at `0x05` is read twice, with intervening writes. The second byte read supplies the address for a word read at byte value plus `0x74`.

Call that last loaded word the **constant-region base**. Twice it becomes the **tail base**, retained into the final mutation region.

These accesses do not establish a record schema. In particular, a byte offset within a word-sized destination may overlap that destination, and low fixed storage may overlap any computed access.

**Fixed byte address `0x3a` next receives the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted source plus `0x30`.** The arithmetic-derived value is not the earlier constant `0x3a`.

The captured word from tail base plus `0x44` supplies a byte at adjusted base plus `0x10`, a word at publication base plus `0x54`, and a signed-right-shift-derived byte at tail base plus `0x01`. - The first follows the word write at publication base plus `0x44`.
- Between the first and second, tail base plus `0x09` receives the adjusted base’s low byte, adjusted base plus `0x44` receives the full fixed-word snapshot, and a word at adjusted base plus `0x54` is captured as the **late linked base**.

### Completion and final transfer

The late tag’s low byte is published at late linked base plus `0x0d`, followed by restoration of the saved tail word at tail base plus `0x54`. A word at adjusted base plus `0x14` is captured before further byte publications at tail base plus `0x11` and adjusted base plus `0x09`; adjusted base plus `0x44` then receives the late linked base.

A word at tail base plus `0x04` supplies the **final loaded base**. Twice that base receives a 16-bit `0x65`. The word at final loaded base plus `0x64` is captured for the final transfer’s second argument.

Before the remaining final reads, late linked base plus `0x11` receives the low byte captured from adjusted base plus `0x14`, and late linked base plus `0x54` receives decimal 100.

The word at final loaded base plus `0x74` becomes the fourth argument. A further word read at tail base plus `0x64` selects a destination whose `0x54` receives the second argument. Between that read and publication, tail base plus `0x09` receives the final loaded base’s low byte. Twice the second argument then receives the selected destination as a word.

The final transfer follows these mutations, with arguments `0x72`, the second-argument capture, the saved tail word, and the fourth-argument capture. No explicit cleanup follows.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Index boundary.** A narrow-index source of `0x7f` produces narrow index 508 and index base 1016. A source of `0x80` produces zero for both, under 32-bit arithmetic.

2. **Fixed index-byte publications.** With index base 1016, the direct writes at fixed `0x01` and later fixed `0x15` each carry `0xf8`. Their equal values do not make them simultaneous or eliminate intervening accesses.

3. **Captured old word.** If the captured old word is `0x80000003`, the fixed word publication at `0x54` is `0x00000006` under 32-bit truncation. A later aliasing overwrite of the original capture location does not change the captured value.

4. **Publication-base timing.** If a preceding computed write changes fixed word storage at `0x087fbbbc`, the subsequent publication-base capture observes that changed storage. An entry-time snapshot is insufficient.

5. **Snapshot versus fresh byte.** If the fixed-word snapshot is `0x123456ab`, its direct byte publication at `0x67` carries `0xab`. If an intervening computed store later changes byte `0x67` to `0x42`, the following fresh read for adjusted base plus `0x09` supplies `0x42`; the captured full snapshot remains `0x123456ab`.

6. **Fixed arithmetic byte.** If the adjusted source is 1, the direct byte write at `0x3a` carries `0x38`, not `0x3a`.

7. **Partial execution.** A nonreturning dependency imposes no requirement for later direct stores or the final transfer. Earlier effects are not promised to roll back.

8. **Final arguments.** If execution completes the mutation region, the third argument is the saved tail word, not a fresh read of tail base plus `0x14`.
