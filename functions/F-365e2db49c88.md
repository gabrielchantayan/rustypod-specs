# F-365e2db49c88

## Purpose

F-365e2db49c88 has an externally observable boundary consisting of ordered memory accesses and interactions with unresolved dependencies. It distributes an incoming byte and several retained or derived values to numerically selected destinations, reports information to dependencies, replaces a word through a retained pointer, and invokes an execution target supplied by an endpoint dependency.

The supplied representation establishes the following direct-access inventory:

- 62 eight-bit writes.
- Two sixteen-bit reads.
- One assignment to designated global storage whose declared width is unavailable.
- One thirty-two-bit write through a retained pointer.

Dependency effects are additional and unresolved. These accesses do not establish a contiguous buffer operation, object initialization, subsystem reset, transaction, or successful application protocol.

This contract describes conditional behavioral obligations at represented boundaries. It does not establish that a complete invocation is defined or executable in any particular environment.

Equal widths do not make these channels interchangeable.

There is no established success result, error result, returned allocation, or ordinary error-reporting interface.

### Storage and execution preconditions

Every reached byte destination must support a one-byte write. Each observation region must support a two-byte read. The original pointer retained during the final payload family must still designate live, writable four-byte storage when the word replacement is reached.

The representation supplies numerical address relationships, not a containing data structure. It establishes no array length, record size, allocation boundary, capacity, or separation between regions. Destinations may coincide or overlap each other, the observation regions, the designated global storage, or the retained referent.

Pointer width, conversion rules, alignment requirements, and numerical address mappings are **documented_only**. A selected numerical address is not necessarily accessible storage.

Every reached indirect interaction also requires an executable target compatible with the argument and result channels actually used. Target arithmetic alone does not establish that compatibility.

### Width-sensitive numerical relationships

An **ordinary byte event** uses a fresh signed thirty-two-bit primary selector. Its destination has eight times the selector’s numerical value. Its first following dependency interaction receives two equal arguments, each 16,384 times that selector.

These relationships originate in signed thirty-two-bit left shifts of three and fourteen positions respectively. They are conditional numerical descriptions, not permission to substitute unsigned arithmetic, widen intermediate values, or assume wraparound.

Except where explicitly unsigned, the other scaled relationships below likewise retain their represented signed thirty-two-bit arithmetic constraints. Signed multiplication and signed left shift are not interchangeable for all operands. Signed thirty-two-bit values range from −2,147,483,648 through 2,147,483,647.

Unsigned descriptor scaling retains thirty-two bits. Pointer conversion, character conversion, displacement addition, signed overflow, and the exceptional full-width right shift require separate treatment; none is resolved by the ordinary-event convention.

### Incoming-payload family

All twenty writes use the original incoming payload. Dependency results obtained between these writes do not replace that payload.

Ordinary events apply except for the following boundary requirements:

- **Event 4:** An exceptional producer receives an earlier signed primary result scaled by a two-position left shift. The producer’s own primary result selects a destination at twice its numerical value. The following equal pair uses that producer result scaled by a twelve-position signed left shift.
- **Event 5:** The following interaction has four explicit arguments. The first, second, and fourth are the ordinary 16,384-fold value. The third is the incoming auxiliary quantity scaled by a ten-position signed left shift. The next interaction consumes the four-argument recipient’s secondary signed result scaled by a three-position left shift, rather than its primary result.
- **Events 6–8 and 11–16:** The ordinary equal-pair recipient supplies a newer signed primary result. The immediately following interaction receives that newer result scaled by a two-position left shift.
- **Event 20:** The following equal arguments are signed thirty-two-bit products of the event’s selector and four, not the ordinary 16,384-fold value.

The equal-pair value associated with event 16 remains retained independently of the recipient’s result. Below, this value is the **retained scale**.

Two independent sixty-four-bit results are communicated during the intervals between events 17–18 and 18–19. In each case, a recipient receives three thirty-two-bit numerical portions in low–high–high order. The low portion undergoes signed thirty-two-bit conversion; the high portion’s signedness is not established. The repeated high portion is part of the interface, not an optional duplicate.

These portions describe numerical significance, not memory byte order.

### Zero and character families

The six zero events are ordinary byte events storing zero. They remain six distinct writes even if destinations coincide. They do not establish that any contiguous region is cleared.

The address-character event has an ordinary destination. Its stored content is a character conversion of the destination’s full numerical value. The next dependency receives the ordinary equal pair followed by that full destination value as a third argument. Narrowing the stored character does not narrow the third argument.

Character signedness, representation, and out-of-range conversion are **documented_only**. Unconditional reduction of every character conversion input modulo 256 is not established.

The later descriptor-character event uses a separate retained field described below. It also has an ordinary destination, but its following equal pair consists of signed products of its selector and four.

### Descriptor-derived content and addressing

An unsigned thirty-two-bit primary result obtained immediately before the first descriptor-payload event is retained as the **descriptor**.

All thirteen events store the low eight bits of the descriptor’s unsigned thirty-two-bit eightfold scaling. Consequently, the stored byte’s lowest three bits are zero.

The descriptor-character conversion input comprises descriptor bits 19–28, numbering the least significant bit zero. This is a ten-bit quantity ranging from zero through 1,023. Descriptor bits above bit 28 do not contribute.

Before descriptor-payload event 2, the retained scale is multiplied by 32,768 using signed thirty-two-bit arithmetic. The result is the **retained product**, independent of newer dependency results.

### Second halfword observation and word replacement

Immediately after product-field event 15, a fresh sixteen-bit read occurs without an intervening dependency invocation.

Its numerical address is 32 bytes beyond the signed thirty-two-bit product of 128 and the retained pointer’s signed numerical conversion. This is not the original pointer simply displaced by 32 bytes.

The next dependency receives only the observed halfword as an explicit argument. Argument-channel extension remains **documented_only**.

At event 17, the replacement value is the signed thirty-two-bit product of that event’s selector and four. The byte write occurs before complete thirty-two-bit replacement through the original retained pointer. No dependency invocation separates these two storage effects, and no direct read of the previous word is represented.

The next dependency receives three copies of the replacement value. If the byte destination overlaps the referent, the word replacement is the later direct effect. Resulting bytes depend on representation and byte order.

Interval 17–18 additionally communicates two copies of a newer primary thirty-two-bit result. Interval 18–19 communicates a newer signed primary result scaled by a two-position left shift alongside the retained replacement value. That retained argument is not a reread of the referent.

### Conditional arithmetic constraint

Under semantics requiring nonnegative signed left-shift operands and signed-representable results, the retained scale is a nonnegative multiple of 16,384. Its later twenty-seven-position left shift permits a value no greater than fifteen. Together, these constraints force the retained scale, retained product, and product-field payload to zero.

This is a conditional deduction, not proof of a valid complete invocation. It does not establish accessibility of numerical address zero, executable targets, or a meaning for the full-width right shift. Undefined arithmetic under the governing semantics prevents unconditional whole-invocation guarantees, including guarantees about earlier effects.

## Side effects

No direct allocation, release, ownership transfer, or lifetime extension is established. Retaining the pointer does not retain its allocation. Dependencies must not be assumed either to preserve lifetimes or to release memory.

Dependencies may inspect or change selected destinations, observation regions, global storage, the retained referent, or unrelated state. The direct-access inventory therefore does not bound total memory effects.

Access widths do not establish atomicity, volatility, synchronization, transactionality, or concurrent visibility. Represented ordering is not a hardware memory-ordering guarantee.

No validation, retry, rollback, cleanup, or recovery protocol is represented. Invalid storage, incompatible targets, arithmetic violations, faults, or dependencies that do not continue have no established error-handling path. Fault behavior and partial-effect guarantees are **documented_only**.

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests. Each assumes local reachability, valid storage and executable targets, continuing dependencies, and arithmetic semantics supporting the stated values.

6. **Wide-result multiplicity.** Numerical portions hexadecimal 11223344 and 55667788 require low–high–high communication in either early wide-result interval, but low–high communication in the third interval.

7. **Retained secondary selection.** Secondary word three selects destination 6,144 and equal arguments 12,582,912 at product-field event 11, independently of newer primary results.

8. **Original pointer and retained replacement.** Pointer numerical conversion four gives second-observation address 544.
