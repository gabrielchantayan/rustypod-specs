# F-c975530d0297

## Purpose

F-c975530d0297 obtains an allocation-like address and updates a caller-supplied record through one of three paths:

- A nonzero predicate result leads to replacement of both four-word descriptors with equal contents.
- A zero predicate result, with the first slot different from the recorded region base, leads to use of the immediately preceding slot and replacement of the first descriptor.
- A zero predicate result, with the first slot equal to the recorded region base, leads to allocation of another region, transfer-like and release-like calls, and replacement of both descriptors.

A segmented container with a map of allocation addresses is a plausible interpretation. That is **inferred intent**, not an established container type, allocation contract, or ownership model.

The established behavior consists of dependency calls, address calculations, record reads, and ordered writes. There is no explicit return value or error-status path.

## Interface

### Input and layout

The sole input is an address identifying a mutable record. The supplied representation uses eleven consecutive 32-bit words, requiring at least 44 accessible bytes.

The names below identify locations and visible uses, not externally established types.

|---|---|---|
| 0–15 | First descriptor | Four words replaced from temporary output |
| 12 | First slot | Fourth word of the first descriptor; also interpreted as an address |
| 16–31 | Second descriptor | Four words replaced on selected paths |

The overlapping descriptions of descriptor words are intentional.

The function also supplies dependencies with:

- A temporary four-word descriptor-output area.
- A separate four-byte scratch area.

Neither area is explicitly initialized before its first dependency call. The descriptor-output area is reused for successive construction calls. Neither temporary address is explicitly returned, but whether a dependency retains either address is unknown.

### Outputs and preconditions

There is no explicit scalar result. Observable results include record changes, indirect slot writes, dependency calls, and any effects those dependencies produce.

Successful execution requires:

- Readable and writable record storage for all reached accesses.
- Valid storage for each reached indirect access.
- Dependency results suitable for the subsequent operations.
- Arithmetic within the defined domain of the actual expression types.
- Four usable output words from each descriptor-construction call.

These requirements are not checked.

The record is not established to be isolated from dependencies or indirect slot writes. If aliases permit changes to the record, later reads can observe those changes. Claims that other fields remain unchanged require suitable nonaliasing and dependency-behavior assumptions.

## Behavior

### Widths, conversions, and arithmetic

The supplied representation uses 32-bit stored words and signed and unsigned 32-bit integer quantities. Addresses are represented through these word-sized quantities. It does not establish portable pointer validity merely by computing an integer address.

The expansion-distance calculation has an important unresolved type dependency: the signed destination receiving a result does **not** determine the signedness of the preceding subtraction or right shift. The underlying signedness of the record-word type is not defined in the supplied excerpt.

Let \(S\) denote the first-slot value retained for the branch comparison, and \(T\) the second-slot word read for the distance calculation. Let \(d\) denote the signed destination value after evaluating the word subtraction and right shift by two.

Two interpretations must remain distinct:

- **If record words are unsigned 32-bit integers:** usual arithmetic conversions make the subtraction unsigned. Its result is reduced modulo \(2^{32}\), and the right shift is logical. Under that interpretation,
  \[
  d=\left\lfloor\frac{(T-S)\bmod 2^{32}}{4}\right\rfloor.
  \]
  The shifted result is at most \(2^{30}-1\), so its assignment to a signed 32-bit destination is representable. Converting the first-slot word to the earlier signed holding value can nevertheless require implementation-specific behavior for words above the signed maximum; this formula presumes preservation of its 32-bit address representation.

- **If record words are signed 32-bit integers:** the subtraction is signed, subject to signed-overflow restrictions. Right shifting a negative signed result is not established here to be arithmetic or portable. A nonnegative, representable subtraction gives the ordinary quotient rounded down.

Other possible definitions of the unspecified word type require their own conversion analysis. Neither signed subtraction nor arithmetic right shift is an unconditional contract.

After \(d\) is stored in its signed destination, expansion calculates:

\[
k=d+1,\qquad q=2k,\qquad o=q\mathbin{\&}\texttt{0xFFFFFFFC}.
\]

The addition producing \(k\), multiplication producing \(2k\), and allocation-size multiplication producing \(8k\) use signed integer operands in the supplied representation. The \(2k\) result is subsequently stored as unsigned. That later conversion does not make an overflowing signed multiplication defined. The later multiplication \(4d\) is also signed before participating in its surrounding address expression.

The mask makes \(o\) a multiple-of-four **byte offset**, not a slot count.

Additional arithmetic distinctions are relevant:

- The common-prefix allocation size is obtained by left shifting a signed count result by two. Negative operands or an unrepresentable signed result are outside a guaranteed portable domain.
- Address calculations using a signed allocation address and a signed count multiplied by four can also encounter signed-overflow restrictions.
- The nonzero-predicate path explicitly halves unsigned 32-bit quantities using logical right shifts. Its unsigned multiplications and additions follow unsigned arithmetic rules where those types govern the expression.
- Unsigned arithmetic wraparound does not establish the validity of the resulting memory address.

The ordinary nonnegative, nonoverflowing examples below avoid the unresolved distance cases.

### Common prefix

Every invocation performs these calls in order:

1. F-dc364b41daca returns a count-like value.
2. F-871e64ddc6de receives that value shifted left by two and a second argument of zero.
3. Its returned word is retained as the new allocation address.
4. F-3538c6888938 receives the record address.

The allocation-like call therefore occurs before the predicate call and before inspection of the slot/base relationship. No explicit null or success check follows it.

A zero predicate selects a comparison between the first slot and region base. A nonzero predicate selects descriptor establishment.

Every later F-dc364b41daca invocation is a distinct call. Equal results, stable configuration, and absence of side effects are not guaranteed.

### Zero predicate: preceding-slot path

After a zero predicate result, the first-slot value is read into a signed holding value and compared with the region-base word, under the applicable conversion rules.

If they differ:

1. The first-slot field is written with the retained slot address minus four bytes.
2. The common-prefix allocation address is written to that calculated location.
3. F-dc364b41daca is called again.
4. F-5bb1b38eb3e5 receives the temporary descriptor-output area, the allocation address plus four times the latest count result, and the first-slot field read for this call.
5. The four output words replace the first descriptor at offsets 0, 4, 8, and 12, in that order.

The provisional first-slot write precedes both the indirect slot write and the later count call. Descriptor replacement subsequently overwrites the first-slot field with the constructor’s fourth output word. Consequently, the final first slot is not independently guaranteed to remain the decremented address.

No explicit field writes target the second descriptor, region base, region count, or offset 32 on this path. Dependencies and aliased indirect writes remain separate possible sources of changes.

The branch tests inequality only. It does not prove that a preceding slot exists or that subtracting four yields valid storage.

### Zero predicate: expansion path

If the first slot equals the region base under the comparison’s conversion rules, the function calculates \(d\), \(k\), and \(q\) as described above. It then performs these operations:

1. F-871e64ddc6de receives \(8k\) and zero. Its return is retained as the new region address.
2. The offset \(o\) is formed from \(q\).
3. F-8a47fbc36db6 receives:
   - The current first-slot field.
   - The current second-slot field plus four bytes.
   - The retained new region address plus \(o+4\) bytes.
4. F-ff1bdc376dcd receives the current region-base field, current region-count field, and zero.
5. The region-base field is written with the retained new region address.
6. The common-prefix allocation address is written at the retained new region address plus \(o\).
7. F-dc364b41daca is called.
8. F-5bb1b38eb3e5 receives:
   - The temporary descriptor-output area.
   - The common-prefix allocation address plus four times the latest count result.
   - The current region-base field plus \(o\).
9. The four output words replace the first descriptor in ascending field order.
10. F-5bb1b38eb3e5 receives:
    - The same temporary output area.
    - The current second-position field.
    - The current region-base field plus \(o+4d+4\).
11. The four output words replace the second descriptor in ascending field order.
12. The region-count field is written with \(q\).

Observable ordering constraints include:

- The transfer-like call occurs before the release-like call.
- Both occur before the explicit region-base replacement.
- The allocation address is written one four-byte slot before the transfer-like destination.
- The slot write uses the retained new region address; subsequent constructor arguments reread the region-base field.
- The second constructor call occurs after all first-descriptor writes.
- The region-count replacement occurs after both descriptor replacements.

The transfer-like arguments resemble a half-open source range and destination address. Range semantics, element size, copying direction, and overlap behavior are not established by this caller.

### Nonzero predicate: descriptor establishment

If the predicate returns nonzero:

1. F-dc364b41daca is called.
2. F-54841aae8aad receives the four-byte scratch area, that count result, and the current region-base field.
3. Its return value replaces the region-base field.
4. F-dc364b41daca is called again.
5. That result, held as unsigned, replaces the region-count field.
6. The common-prefix allocation address is written at the current region base plus four times the unsigned half of that retained count result.
7. F-dc364b41daca is called a third time after the predicate.
8. F-5bb1b38eb3e5 receives:
   - The temporary descriptor-output area.
   - The common-prefix allocation address plus four times the unsigned half of the latest count result, plus four bytes.
   - The current region-base field plus four times the unsigned half of the current region-count field.
9. The four output words replace the first descriptor in ascending field order.
10. The first descriptor is read and copied into the second descriptor, one corresponding word at a time in ascending order.

With ordinary nonoverlapping storage and no external interference, the two descriptors are equal after this path.

The three post-predicate count results have distinct uses: the first is passed to F-54841aae8aad, the second supplies the stored region count and slot-write midpoint, and the third supplies the allocation-relative position for descriptor construction. The third does not replace the stored region count.

### Ordering limits

The sequences above describe ordering between operations. They do not impose an evaluation order among separate argument expressions within one call.

Dependencies are not assumed to preserve shared state. A retained value and a record field reread after a dependency call can differ.

## Side effects

Every normally completed path explicitly replaces the first descriptor and performs an indirect write of the common-prefix allocation address.

Additional explicit field writes are:

| Path | Additional writes |
|---|---|
| Preceding slot | Provisional first-slot decrement |
| Expansion | Region base, second descriptor, region count |
| Nonzero predicate | Region base, region count, second descriptor |

There is no explicit field access at offset 32. This is not an unconditional preservation guarantee: an indirect slot write or dependency could reach that storage through an alias.

The common-prefix allocation-like result is stored into a slot on every completed path. This function contains no explicit release-like call for that result.

Expansion obtains a second allocation-like result and calls F-ff1bdc376dcd with the region information read at that point. Replacing and releasing an old map is inferred intent only. Storing or passing an address does not establish ownership transfer.

There is no explicit allocation-failure branch, bounds validation, rollback, exception recovery, or error-status return. A dependency failure or invalid operation can occur after earlier effects have become visible. The function provides no established transactional guarantee.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static expectations, not executed tests. Assume valid aligned storage, representable arithmetic, nonaliasing between slot storage and the record, normally returning dependencies, and no dependency mutation beyond stated outputs.

### Preceding slot

Given a zero predicate, first slot \(S\), and a different region base:

- The first-slot field is provisionally written with \(S-4\).
- The allocation address is stored at \(S-4\).
- Descriptor construction receives that slot and the allocation address plus four times its immediately preceding count result.
- If construction produces four words \(a,b,c,e\), the first descriptor finishes with those words.
- The other descriptor, region base, and region count receive no explicit field writes.

### Expansion with four existing positions

Given a zero predicate, equal first slot and region base, and a second slot twelve bytes later, assume values for which the subtraction is representable under the applicable type interpretation.

Then:

- \(d=3\), \(k=4\), \(q=8\), and \(o=8\).
- The expansion allocation-like call receives 32 and zero.
- If its result is \(M\), the transfer-like destination is \(M+12\).
- The common-prefix allocation address is stored at \(M+8\).
- First-descriptor construction receives slot \(M+8\).
- Second-descriptor construction receives slot \(M+24\).
- The final region-count write stores 8.

These outcomes do not establish what the transfer dependency copies.

### Nonzero predicate with unequal counts

Suppose the common-prefix count is 8 and the three subsequent counts are 10, 12, and 14.

Then:

- The initial allocation-like call receives 32 and zero.
- F-54841aae8aad receives count 10.
- The stored region count becomes 12.
- The allocation address is written 24 bytes after the replacement region base.
- Descriptor construction receives a position 32 bytes after the allocation address.
- Its slot argument is 24 bytes after the region base.
- The constructed first descriptor is copied to the second.

### Distance type dependency

For hypothetical 32-bit address words \(S=4096\) and \(T=4092\):

- Under the unsigned-word interpretation, subtraction yields \(2^{32}-4\), and logical shifting yields \(d=1073741823\).
- The subsequent signed multiplication producing \(2(d+1)\) is not representable as a signed 32-bit integer. The function therefore has no guaranteed successful expansion outcome for these inputs under that interpretation.
- Under a signed-word interpretation, subtraction yields \(-4\), but the supplied material does not establish the result of right shifting that negative value. No alternative completed outcome is promised.

This example distinguishes expression types; it does not validate reversed slot order.
