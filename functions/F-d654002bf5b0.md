## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static expectations, not executed tests.

1. **Low-byte restriction.** If the incoming register is `0x123456AB`, **F-u001** receives `0x2AC00000` as its fourth argument. Changing only the incoming register’s upper 24 bits does not change that argument.

2. **Initial word shifts.** If the word used in the early four-argument call is one, that call receives one, `0x00020000`, zero, and `0x08000000`.

3. **Discarded high bits.** If the first relevant 64-bit result’s high half is `0x00000010`, its later net left shift by 28 produces zero. A high half of one produces `0x10000000`.

4. **Signed recovery boundary.** If the word subjected to left shift by five and arithmetic right shift by five is `0x04000000`, the resulting argument is `0xFC000000`, not `0x04000000`.

5. **Final address independence from low bits.** If two **F-u003** results differ only in bits 0 through 51, they produce the same final destination. Their low halves can nevertheless cause different effects in the intervening dependency call.

6. **Zero destination.** If the top 12 result bits are zero, the represented write targets address zero. The function does not substitute another destination.

7. **Alignment is not guaranteed.** If those top 12 bits equal one, the destination is address one. Successful completion therefore depends on the environment’s unaligned-write behavior.

8. **Maximum destination.** If the top 12 bits are all set, the destination is 5,118. The stored value remains `0x00CD`.

9. **Interrupted execution.** If any earlier dependency does not return, later transfers and the final store are not reached. No recovery path is established.
