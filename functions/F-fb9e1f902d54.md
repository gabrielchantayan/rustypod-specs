1. Reads the 32-bit field at input-object offset 12.
2. Passes that field value to F-f50757674697.
3. Compares the dependency’s 32-bit result with signed −1.

If the result is −1, the function returns zero immediately. It does not directly read the byte at offset 16 and does not perform its own output store.

Consequently, if that dependency changes the byte through shared state or an alias, the later read observes the value available at that time; no earlier snapshot is established.

If the byte equals exactly 1, the acquired value’s low 16 bits are retained unchanged.

If the byte has any other value, the two bytes of those low 16 bits are exchanged. A zero marker does not receive special treatment relative to other non-1 markers.

For clarity, suppose the low 16 bits consist of a high byte and a low byte:

- Marker equal to 1: the result retains the high byte followed by the low byte in its numerical bit positions.
- Marker not equal to 1: the original low byte becomes bits 15–8, and the original high byte becomes bits 7–0.

Bits 31–16 of the dependency result do not contribute to the stored value. They matter only to the earlier full-width failure comparison.

The shift used to obtain the second byte is an unsigned right shift of the 32-bit result by eight positions, followed by selection of one byte. Thus, negative results other than −1 are not rejected and do not introduce sign-extension bits into the final two-byte value.
