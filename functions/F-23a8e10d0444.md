### Widths and arithmetic

Most values and derived addresses are represented at 32 bits. Narrow writes retain the destination width: eight or sixteen bits. Byte reads used as integer values or address inputs are zero-extended, giving a value from 0 through 255.

The representation includes:

- Left shifts and multiplication used both for data and addresses.
- Masks applied before or after shifts.
- Logical right shifts of unsigned values.
- Unsigned extraction from represented 64-bit results at shifts of 34, 36, and 44 bits.
- A late signed right shift with a count taken from the low eight bits of an address-like value.

Mask placement is significant. Masking an original result is not generally equivalent to masking a previously shifted result.

Under a **conditional 32-bit bit-vector interpretation**, left shifts and multiplication retain the low 32 bits. Consequently, a value shifted left by twenty-five bits and then seven bits becomes zero; a value shifted left by twenty-eight bits twice also becomes zero. Repeated shifts of an explicitly initialized zero remain zero.

Those consequences do not establish signed-overflow or address-conversion semantics for actual execution. Overflow, address wrapping, negative signed shifts, and the late right-shift operation remain **documented_only** where target semantics are required. In particular, the late shift count can range from 0 through 255; counts of 32 or more must not be silently reduced modulo 32.
