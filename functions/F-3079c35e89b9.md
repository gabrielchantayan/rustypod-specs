# F-3079c35e89b9

## Purpose

F-3079c35e89b9 executes a long, ordered sequence of indirect dependency invocations, transforms incoming execution-context values and dependency results, and performs mixed-width memory updates before a final unresolved invocation.

No application-level purpose is established. Repeated offsets, self-referential stores, and character-like constants do not establish a formatter, allocator, serializer, or container operation.

This contract distinguishes **established static evidence**, **inferred intent**, and **`documented_only` unknowns**. Requirements concerning arithmetic are conditional on the width interpretation stated below. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Ordered dependency phase

The initial phase contains a lengthy fixed sequence of indirect invocations without a represented conditional branch, retry, or runtime-controlled loop.

The first destination is derived from incoming control context with displacement 0xFFE. Its two explicit arguments are equal: an incoming word shifted left seven bits. Subsequent destinations are predominantly derived from embedded control-location values with the same displacement. Distinct sites are not evidence of distinct dependency identities, nor of equivalent behavior.

Later arguments include primary results shifted left eight bits; another incoming word shifted three, nine, or ten bits; high words of combined results; saved earlier values; masks and scales; literal zeros; and secondary-channel values. Calls have zero through four explicit arguments. An empty apparent argument list does not establish absence of ambient inputs.

Some results are retained across intervening invocations. Others are split and consumed independently. No dependency status is explicitly tested, and no invocation may be removed merely because its explicit arguments are zero.

Important width-sensitive transformations include:

- A 20-bit mask followed by a ten-bit logical right shift selects bits 10–19, yielding 0–1,023.
- A 24-bit mask discards the top byte before later extraction or scaling.
- A 22-bit mask followed by a seven- or eight-bit left shift produces bounded bit fields.
- Logical shifts of represented 64-bit results by 34, 36, or 44 correspond to the high word shifted right two, four, or twelve bits.
- A 32-bit left shift by eight followed by logical right shift by eight retains only the original low 24 bits.
- Successive left shifts can erase all original bits. They are not unbounded-precision scaling.

Several argument streams begin at explicit zero assignments and remain zero through subsequent transformations under this model. The calls surrounding those transformations still occur.

### Initial direct-memory region

The following descriptive names identify values, not typed objects:

- **Shifted word:** a dependency result shifted left 25 bits.
- **Multiplied base:** another result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Small index:** a result shifted left eight, masked to 15 bits, then logically shifted right six.
- **Doubled index:** twice the small index.
- **Saved shifted result:** an earlier result already shifted left eight bits.

The small index is a multiple of four from 0 through 508; its doubled value is 0–1,016.

After the last combined dependency result, the initial direct effects occur in this order:

1. The shifted word is stored as a word at the result’s high word plus 0x14.
2. The low byte of that high word is written at the shifted word plus 9.
3. The multiplied base is stored at the result’s low word multiplied by 256, plus 0x54.
4. The earlier offset-9 byte is overwritten with the shifted word’s low byte, which is zero under the width model.
5. The word at multiplied base plus 0x14 is cleared; the byte at multiplied base plus 5 is then read.
6. The word at doubled index plus 0x14 is cleared, followed by a word store of the zero-extended byte at multiplied base plus 0x34.
7. The word at multiplied base plus 0x14 is read.
8. The doubled-index word is cleared again, followed by two byte stores of the last read word’s low byte at doubled index plus 0x15.

The read following the clear is not unconditionally zero: intervening stores can alias its bytes.

### Fixed-location effects among interlinked updates

The next region intermixes fixed locations and computed destinations. Their relative positions are part of the contract.

After the duplicate doubled-index byte writes, the byte at fixed address 0x05 is read and zero-extended as a base. **The low byte of the doubled index is then written to fixed address 0x01**, before writing the small index’s low byte at that byte-derived base plus 0x15.

A byte at multiplied base plus 0x11 temporarily receives the saved shifted result’s low byte. A word store at the previously read word plus 0x54 intervenes before that offset-0x11 byte is cleared. Under the width model, the temporary byte is also zero, but both accesses remain represented.

The word at byte-derived base plus 0x54 is captured before replacement. Call this captured word the **retained link**. It is not an entry-time snapshot. Subsequent work publishes the small index at doubled index plus 0x34 and follows word-sized links at offset 0x74.

Stores through those links include a result scaled by 2²⁶, the saved shifted result, and self-referential values. The retained link is stored twice at its own offset 0x24, with corresponding repeated byte writes at another loaded link’s offset 9. Multiple represented offset-0x74 loads must not be merged without evidence that their read effects and observations are equivalent.

Two further fixed-location writes have precise positions:

- After the word at doubled index plus 0x14 receives the 2²⁶-scaled result, **a 32-bit store at fixed address 0x54 receives twice the retained link**. Only afterward does the doubled-index offset-0x14 word receive the retained link, twice.
- After two byte writes of the low byte of four times the retained link at doubled index plus 0x15, **fixed address 0x15 receives the doubled index’s low byte**. This precedes the next computed offset-0x54 store and publication of four times the retained link at doubled index plus 0x34.

Thus, fixed 0x54 is not merely a recurring field offset, and fixed 0x15 is distinct from computed offset-0x15 destinations.

### Further link manipulation

The function derives fourfold and eightfold versions of the retained link. It doubles a word loaded at doubled index plus 0x74 and writes that doubled link through offset-0x44 destinations.

Further word reads use zero-extended bytes from offset 5 as address bases, followed by offset 0x74. The region also includes:

- Self-referential word stores at offsets 0x14 and 0x24.
- Byte publications at offsets 9, 0x0D, and 0x15.
- Word reads at offsets 4 and 0x64.
- Repeated byte reads at offset 5 separated by writes.
- Word overwrites at offsets 0x14, 0x44, and 0x54.

A later offset-5 byte and its following offset-0x74 word read determine a new link. This contract calls that observed word the **late link**, and twice it the **late doubled link**. These are descriptive arithmetic roles, not ownership or object-type claims.

Every re-read observes the state at its own position. A previously observed link cannot replace a later read solely because the address expression looks similar.

### Constant stores and repeated publication

Using the late link, the function writes 0x3A as a byte at four times that link, as a 16-bit value at eight times that link, and as a word at four times that link plus 0x20.

Define the **adjusted value** as four times the retained link minus 0x96, and the **scaled adjusted base** as four times the adjusted value. At scaled adjusted base plus 0x20, successive word writes publish 0x20, 0x2F, the zero-extended byte read from scaled adjusted base plus 4, and 0x20 again. That byte read occurs after the first two word stores.

The fixed-location word supplying the **publication base is captured next, before the word at fixed address 0x60 is assigned 0x20**. Publication uses the captured base, not a later reload. This ordering remains significant without a disjointness guarantee.

A long finite sequence then repeats four-access publication groups:

- A word publishes four times the late link at a scaled destination plus 0x54.
- A byte publishes the scaled adjusted base’s low byte at captured publication base plus 0x19.
- A byte publishes the captured publication base’s low byte at scaled adjusted base plus 0x11.
- A word publishes a scaled value at captured publication base plus 0x34.

The first group uses eight times the retained link for both its first destination base and final published word. Later groups use eight times the adjusted value instead. The byte values and captured publication base remain saved values throughout; aliasing does not cause implicit reloads.

The exact repetition count remains `documented_only` in this contract. The supplied representation contains concrete repetitions, not a runtime loop.

### Final fixed-byte computation and saved reads

After the publication sequence, the function captures the 32-bit word at fixed address **0xA9**, then reads the word at four times the late link plus 0x74.

**Only after those two reads, fixed address 0x3A receives the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30.** This is a computed byte value, not a store of the constant 0x3A.

The function then captures the words at late doubled link plus 0x14 and scaled adjusted base plus 4. The fixed 0x3A write therefore precedes both saved reads. Aliasing can change what those reads observe.

Next, byte writes publish saved low bytes at late doubled link plus 0x0D and scaled adjusted base plus 0x0D and 9; scaled adjusted base plus 8 receives 0x6C. A word at late doubled link plus 0x54 receives the late doubled link itself.

### Final byte flow, mutations, and transfer

The low byte of the previously captured 0xA9 word is written to fixed address **0x67**. A computed word read at late doubled link plus 0x44 intervenes before fixed 0x67 is read for copying to fixed **0x21**. The copy uses that read, not direct reuse of the captured 0xA9 byte.

The word just read from offset 0x44 supplies a byte at scaled adjusted base plus 0x10 and a word at captured publication base plus 0x54. Its signed right-shifted value supplies a byte at late doubled link plus 1; the shift count is the low eight bits of the scaled adjusted base. Counts of 32 or greater and signed-shift details remain unresolved. The publication-base offset-0x54 word is subsequently overwritten with the scaled adjusted base.

A word read at scaled adjusted base plus 0x40 is later stored at that base plus 0x10. Between those accesses, captured publication base plus 0x74 receives the saved offset-4 word. Captured publication base plus 0x44 then receives the late doubled link.

**Fixed 0x67 is read again** for a byte store at scaled adjusted base plus 9. Following a byte write at late doubled link plus 9 and publication of the captured 0xA9 word at scaled adjusted base plus 0x44, the word at scaled adjusted base plus 0x54 is loaded. **Fixed 0x67 is then read yet again** for another store at scaled adjusted base plus 9.

These later reads are not substitutes for the earlier 0x67 read. Intervening computed stores may overlap 0x67; read-sensitive memory effects are also unspecified.

The remaining updates use the newly loaded offset-0x54 word as a destination base, restore the saved late-doubled-link offset-0x14 word at its offset 0x54, and consume a fresh word from scaled adjusted base plus 0x14. Further byte stores overwrite scaled adjusted base plus 9 and publish at offsets 0x0D and 0x11; its offset-0x44 word is also overwritten.

Finally, a word loaded at late doubled link plus 4 supplies:

- A doubled destination receiving the 16-bit value 0x65.
- Subsequent word reads at offsets 0x64 and 0x74.

A computed offset-0x54 destination receives decimal 100. A later word read at late doubled link plus 0x64 supplies another destination base. That base’s offset 0x54 receives the previously loaded offset-0x64 word, and a word store at twice that previously loaded word receives the destination base.

The final unresolved invocation has four explicit arguments: 0x72, that previously loaded offset-0x64 word, the saved late-doubled-link offset-0x14 word, and the final offset-0x74 word. A return is represented only after this invocation returns normally.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

- **Intermediate byte overwrite:** If the final combined result’s high word ends in 0xAB, the initial shifted-word offset-9 destination receives 0xAB before receiving zero under the width model. A final-state-only check misses an access.
- **Small-index boundary:** If the relevant result’s low seven bits are set, the small index is 508 and its doubled base is 1,016. The clear is at that base plus 0x14, not at an assumed heap object.
- **Zero extension:** An offset-5 byte observed as 0xFE produces the corresponding word publication of 254, not a negative sign extension.
- **Publication-base capture:** If the fixed publication-base read observes one value and the subsequent fixed 0x60 store alters overlapping storage, publication still uses the captured value. Moving the read after the store is incompatible.
- **Fixed scaled effects:** If the retained link is 0x12345678, the fixed 0x54 word receives 0x2468ACF0 under the width model. If the small index is 508, fixed 0x01 and fixed 0x15 each receive 0xF8 at their separate specified positions.
- **Computed fixed byte:** The fixed 0x3A byte receives the low byte of eight times the adjusted value plus 0x30, after the 0xA9 and following computed offset-0x74 reads but before the two saved reads.
- **Fixed-byte re-observation:** If 0x67 initially receives 0xAB, later computed stores that overlap it can change the bytes observed by later explicit reads. Acceptance must not force every subsequent publication to remain 0xAB.
- **Nonreturning final invocation:** If the final dependency does not return, the trailing return is not reached.
