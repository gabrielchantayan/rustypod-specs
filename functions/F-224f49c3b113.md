# F-224f49c3b113

It does not establish object initialization, text formatting, collection management, or ownership transfer. ## Behavior

### Initial association and repeated stores

The shared base is captured before any visible memory write. Later stores do not trigger another load of that base in the supplied representation, even if they overlap its global source.

The initial association has this observable order:

1. The initial destination’s word at offset `0x54` receives the payload.
2. The shared-base byte at offset `0x19` receives the primary context’s low eight bits.
3. The primary-context byte at offset `0x11` receives the captured shared base’s low eight bits.
4. The shared-base word at offset `0x34` receives the initial destination.

An explicitly repeated, finite store sequence follows. Each repetition writes, in order:

| Destination | Width | Stored value |
|---|---:|---|
| Twice the primary context, plus `0x54` | 32 bits | Payload |
| Shared base plus `0x19` | 8 bits | Primary context’s low byte |
| Primary context plus `0x11` | 8 bits | Captured shared base’s low byte |
| Shared base plus `0x34` | 32 bits | Twice the primary context |

The primary-context doubling is unsigned 32-bit arithmetic, with the numeric result reduced modulo \(2^{32}\). The associated unsigned address arithmetic must not silently become wider host arithmetic.

The repetition is unconditional in the supplied representation. Replacing it with one group of stores is not justified merely because ordinary memory might finish with equal bytes. Write-sensitive storage, aliases, and external observation can distinguish the traces.

The exact repetition cardinality is not certified by this contract and remains **documented_only**. Consequently, this contract establishes the repeated pattern and its ordering but is insufficient by itself for exact write-count acceptance.

### Early captures and absolute-address effects

After the repeated section, the function captures a word from absolute address `0xA9`, then a word from payload plus `0x74`.

The byte at absolute address `0x3A` then receives the low eight bits of twice the primary context’s low byte plus `0x30`. This is a byte-arithmetic effect, not a guarantee of decimal formatting or printable output.

Next, a word is captured from secondary context plus `0x14`, followed by a word from primary context plus `0x04`. These captured values remain distinct from subsequent contents of their source locations.

The next writes occur in this order:

- Secondary context plus `0x0D` receives the primary context’s low byte.
- Primary context plus `0x08` receives `0x6C`.
- Primary context plus `0x0D`, then plus `0x09`, receive the shared base’s low byte.
- Secondary context plus `0x54` receives the complete secondary-context value.

The low byte of the word previously captured from absolute address `0xA9` is then stored at absolute address `0x67`. Only after this store is a word read from secondary context plus `0x44`.

The byte at absolute address `0x67` is subsequently read and copied to absolute address `0x21`. This is a fresh memory read, not a requirement to reuse the earlier captured low byte. The intervening secondary-context word read remains part of the ordering.

### Context updates and overlapping writes

The captured word from secondary context plus `0x44` supplies three successive stores:

- Its low byte goes to primary context plus `0x10`.
- Its complete word goes to shared base plus `0x54`.
- The low byte of its right-shifted result goes to secondary context plus `0x01`.

The shift count is the primary context’s low eight bits, so its possible numeric range is `0` through `255`. The shifted operand is represented as signed. A nonnegative operand and a count from `0` through `31` support the ordinary 32-bit right-shift interpretation. Negative operands and counts of `32` or greater remain **documented_only**; masked-count machine behavior is not established.

The shared-base word at `0x54` is then overwritten with the complete primary-context value.

The following capture and stores are ordered:

- A word is captured from primary context plus `0x40`.
- The earlier word captured from primary context plus `0x04` is stored at shared base plus `0x74`.
- The newly captured word from primary context plus `0x40` is stored at primary context plus `0x10`.
- The complete secondary-context value is stored at shared base plus `0x44`.

The word store beginning at primary context plus `0x10` covers offsets `0x10` through `0x13`. It therefore overlaps both the earlier byte store at `0x10` and the repeated byte stores at `0x11`.

This is a **write-after-write overlap**. There is no represented word read beginning at primary context plus `0x10`, and no established dependency in which the earlier byte at `0x11` supplies the later word’s value. That value was captured from offset `0x40`. Other address aliases can still affect that capture according to their actual locations and ordering.

Next, primary context plus `0x09` receives a fresh byte read from absolute address `0x67`; secondary context plus `0x09` receives the primary context’s low byte; and primary context plus `0x44` receives the word captured earlier from absolute address `0xA9`.

Only after those stores is a word read from primary context plus `0x54`. Its captured value supplies the **linked destination**.

Primary context plus `0x09` is refreshed again from a fresh read of absolute address `0x67`. The linked destination’s byte at `0x0D` then receives the low byte of the earlier payload-relative word. Secondary context plus `0x54` receives the word captured earlier from secondary context plus `0x14`, replacing the earlier self-reference unless aliasing has already changed that storage.

### Terminal linking and invocation

A word is captured from primary context plus `0x14`. The next stores place:

- The earlier payload-relative word’s low byte at secondary context plus `0x11`.
- The linked destination’s low byte at primary context plus `0x09`.
- The complete linked destination at primary context plus `0x44`.

A word read from secondary context plus `0x04` supplies the **terminal base**. A halfword value of `0x0065` is written at twice that terminal base.

Only after that halfword store is a word read from terminal base plus `0x64`, supplying the **terminal link**. If the halfword destination overlaps this word’s storage, the overlap can affect the captured terminal link.

The linked destination then receives the low byte of the word captured from primary context plus `0x14` at offset `0x11`, followed by the word value `0x00000064` at offset `0x54`.

The final capture-and-store sequence is:

- Capture a word from terminal base plus `0x74`.
- Read a word from secondary context plus `0x64`, supplying the **final destination**.
- Store the terminal base’s low byte at secondary context plus `0x09`.
- Store the terminal link at final destination plus `0x54`.
- Store the final destination as a word at twice the terminal link.

Unlike the unsigned primary-context doubling, the terminal-base and terminal-link doublings are represented using signed quantities. Their overflow behavior is not established as portable modulo-\(2^{32}\) arithmetic. Address-overflow cases remain **documented_only**.

| 3 | Word captured earlier from secondary context plus `0x14` |

## Side effects

### Memory and aliasing

The function writes to explicit-input-relative, implicit-context-relative, global-relative, absolute, and dynamically linked addresses.

Access order governs aliasing. In particular:

- The word store at primary context plus `0x10` overlaps earlier byte stores at `0x10` and `0x11`; it does not read those destinations to construct its value.
- A store to absolute address `0x67` can overlap another addressed region.
- Later reads of absolute address `0x67` must observe the then-current memory under the applicable memory model.
- The halfword store at twice the terminal base precedes, and can overlap, the terminal-link read.
- Writes through the linked destination can affect later reads through the secondary context or terminal base if their storage overlaps.
- The final store at twice the terminal link occurs after the store at final destination plus `0x54`; overlapping bytes follow that order.

No locking, isolation, atomicity, rollback, or transaction boundary is established. A failure can leave earlier writes visible.

Endianness is unresolved. Predicting individual bytes after multibyte stores requires a platform byte-order assumption. Access widths and represented ordering do not alone establish a hardware bus trace or concurrent-observer guarantees.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional static deductions, not executed tests.

### Ordinary disjoint storage

Assume all accesses are valid ordinary memory; all regions and doubled-address destinations are disjoint except for the explicitly described same-region overlaps; no external mutation occurs; and the shift and address calculations have defined behavior.

Immediately before the indirect invocation:

- Initial destination plus `0x54` contains the payload.
- Twice the primary context plus `0x54` contains the payload.
- Shared base plus `0x34` contains twice the primary context.
- Shared base plus `0x54` contains the primary context rather than its earlier temporary word.
- Secondary context plus `0x54` contains the word captured from its offset `0x14`.
- Linked destination plus `0x54` contains `0x00000064`.
- Primary context plus `0x10` contains the word captured from primary context plus `0x40`.

These conditions do not extend past the unknown dependency’s invocation without additional preservation evidence.

### Overlapping writes, not an overlapping read

Assume little-endian ordinary memory, no external mutation, and no other aliases affecting primary-context offsets `0x10` through `0x13`.

Suppose the earlier byte stores place `0x99` at offset `0x10` and `0xAB` at offset `0x11`, and the later word capture from offset `0x40` yields `0x11223344`.

After that captured word is stored at offset `0x10`, offsets `0x10` through `0x13` contain `0x44`, `0x33`, `0x22`, and `0x11`, respectively. The earlier `0x99` and `0xAB` are overwritten. They are not inputs to a word read at offset `0x10`; no such read is represented.

### Byte truncation

If the primary context’s low byte is `0xE1`, the byte stored at absolute address `0x3A` is `0xF2`: twice `225` plus `48` is `498`, whose low eight bits are `242`.

If the captured shared base ends in `0xAB`, its repeated byte stores to primary context plus `0x11` store `0xAB`, not the complete shared-base value.

### Shift within the established range

If the word captured from secondary context plus `0x44` is `0x00000100` and the primary context’s low byte is `4`, the shifted byte stored at secondary context plus `0x01` is `0x10`.

This example establishes nothing about negative operands or shift counts of `32` or greater.

### Capture before later mutation

If the early read from secondary context plus `0x14` yields `0x11223344`, that captured word supplies both the later secondary-context store at `0x54` and the third indirect argument. A subsequent change to the source location does not by itself alter the captured word.

### Trace-sensitive storage and faults

If the repeated destinations are write-sensitive, a single four-store group is not an acceptable replacement merely because final bytes match.

If an early access fails, the contract does not require invocation of the indirect dependency or reversal of preceding writes. Actual fault delivery remains platform-dependent.
