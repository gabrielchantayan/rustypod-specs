# F-91d38c44e415

## Purpose

F-91d38c44e415 performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, derives address-like quantities from dependency results and ambient machine state, reads and modifies fixed and computed memory locations, and finishes with an unresolved indirect transfer.

The supplied static body does not establish an application-level purpose. Constants resembling characters and recurring memory offsets do not establish formatting, allocation, object initialization, or collection management.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** represented invocations, widths, value retention, memory accesses, and ordering.

- **documented_only:** unresolved semantics or explicitly identified coverage limits.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Widths and arithmetic

The representation distinguishes:

- 8-bit byte accesses.
- 16-bit halfword accesses.
- 32-bit word accesses and address-like quantities.
- Some 64-bit dependency results.

For a represented 64-bit result, its low component contains bits 0–31 and its high component bits 32–63. This decomposition does not establish memory byte order.

Byte stores retain the low eight bits of their source. Explicit unsigned-byte reads widen to values from 0 through 255. Halfword stores of 0x3A and 0x65 write 0x003A and 0x0065; substituting byte stores changes their footprints.

Arithmetic deductions below are conditional on ordinary non-overflowing arithmetic or, where stated, truncating 32-bit arithmetic. The representation alone does not settle signed overflow, address wrapping, signed right shifts, or oversized shift counts.

### Derived bases and initial mutation

Immediately before explicit mutation, retained quantities include:

- A dependency result multiplied by 0x02000000: the **heavily scaled base**.
- Another result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, and shifted right by six: the **small derived base**.
- An earlier result shifted left by 25.
- The low and high components of the final represented 64-bit dependency result.
- A separate retained result shifted left by eight.

These labels describe value relationships, not object types.

Under truncating 32-bit arithmetic, the small derived base ranges from 0 through 508 in multiples of four. Its doubled value ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

The initial mutations establish potentially overlapping relationships. The final result’s high component supplies the base for an offset-0x14 word store containing the earlier left-25-shifted quantity. A byte containing the high component’s low fragment is then written at offset 0x09 of that earlier quantity. A word containing the heavily scaled base is stored at offset 0x54 of the final low component multiplied by 256. Another byte store to the same offset-0x09 destination follows, using the earlier left-25-shifted quantity’s low byte.

The heavily scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared. Its offset-0x05 byte is read unsigned. The doubled small derived base’s offset-0x14 word is then cleared, and the captured byte is widened and stored as a word at offset 0x34 of the heavily scaled base. Only afterward is the heavily scaled base’s offset-0x14 word read back.

**Under ordinary sequential writable-memory semantics, that read-back is zero.** Between its clear and read-back:

- The intervening clear writes only zeros, even if its footprint overlaps.
- The offset-0x34 word store cannot overlap the same base’s offset-0x14 word. Their starting addresses differ by 0x20, including under ordinary modulo-32-bit addressing.
- No dependency invocation intervenes.

External mutation or nonordinary memory-access effects can invalidate that deduction; those possibilities remain **documented_only**.

Following the read-back, another clear at offset 0x14 of the doubled small derived base precedes two byte stores at its offset 0x15. Both use the retained read-back’s low byte and therefore write zero under the ordinary semantics above. These byte stores overlap their destination’s offset-0x14 word footprint, but occur too late to affect the already captured read-back.

Next, an unsigned byte read from fixed address 0x05 becomes an address-like base. The doubled small derived base’s low byte is written to fixed address 0x01. Further updates involve offsets 0x11, 0x15, 0x34, and 0x54.

The retained read-back supplies the base for an offset-0x54 word store. Consequently, under ordinary semantics this store targets absolute address 0x54. This does not establish that low memory is valid storage.

A word at offset 0x54 of the fixed-byte-derived base is captured before replacement. This **retained relationship word** supplies later scaled values and destinations.

### Relationship updates and fixed-address mutation

Subsequent updates follow offset-0x74 word relationships, repeat self-referential offset-0x24 stores, and publish doubled, quadrupled, or octupled forms of retained quantities. A doubled form of the retained relationship word is stored as a word at fixed address 0x54.

A later pair of byte stores targets offset 0x15 of the doubled small derived base. Both contain the low byte of four times the retained relationship word.

**After this pair, a separate byte store to fixed address 0x15 writes the doubled small derived base’s low byte.** It precedes the following offset-0x54 word store based on four times the retained relationship word.

This fixed-address store is distinct from the earlier fixed-address-0x01 store and both computed offset-0x15 pairs. Neither repeated values nor matching offsets make these mutations interchangeable.

Further relationship updates include:

- Word loads through loaded relationships.
- Byte fragments beside word fields.
- Mutation through a base loaded from offset 0x64.
- Repeated unsigned-byte reads from offset 0x05.
- Offset-0x74 word loads whose base is an unsigned byte.

A byte-derived base lies in 0–255. Its offset-0x74 access therefore targets low memory, not necessarily the storage from which the byte originated.

### Constants and publication

A later loaded quantity supplies three destinations for 0x3A:

- A byte at four times the quantity.
- A halfword at eight times the quantity.
- A word at offset 0x20 from its four-times-scaled address.

Call the four-times-scaled address the **constant-group base**.

Separately, the **reduced quantity** is four times the retained relationship word minus 0x96. The **separate computed base** is four times this reduced quantity.

Four word stores target offset 0x20 of the separate computed base. Their values, in order, are 0x20, 0x2F, an unsigned byte captured from offset 0x04 of that base, and 0x20. The byte read occurs after the second store and before the third.

Under ordinary non-overflowing or truncating 32-bit arithmetic, the separate computed base is congruent to 8 modulo 16. Its offset-0x04 word therefore begins at an address congruent to 12 modulo 16. In particular, that word cannot begin at 0x38 or overlap fixed address 0x3A.

After the final 0x20 store, the function captures the global address-like word. **Only then** does it store word value 0x20 at fixed address 0x60. Later publication uses the captured global value without a represented reload.

Each publication group has this access order:

1. An offset-0x54 word of a scaled relationship base receives the constant-group base.
2. An offset-0x19 byte of the captured global base receives the separate computed base’s low byte.
3. An offset-0x11 byte of the separate computed base receives the captured global base’s low byte.
4. An offset-0x34 word of the captured global base receives the scaled relationship base.

The first group uses eight times the retained relationship word as its relationship base. Subsequent groups use eight times the reduced quantity.

The body contains a finite repeated sequence, not a data-dependent loop. Exact publication multiplicity is **documented_only** as a coverage limit here. Repetition does not authorize replacement with one final-state update. Publication does not reload the global cell even if stores alias that cell.

| Order | Access |
|---|---|
| First | Capture the word at fixed address 0xA9 |
| Second | Capture the offset-0x74 word of the constant-group base |
| Third | Write a computed byte to fixed address 0x3A |
| Fourth | Capture the offset-0x14 word of the retained doubled relationship base |
| Fifth | Capture the offset-0x04 word of the separate computed base |

The computed byte is the low-byte result of eight times the reduced quantity plus 0x30, under ordinary truncating-byte interpretation.

The first two captures retain pre-write values. The latter two are ordered after the write. The offset-0x14 capture supplies the terminal dependency’s third argument. The offset-0x04 capture is also post-write, but its arithmetic address constraints exclude overlap with 0x3A under the ordinary addressing models described above.

Following these captures, the function writes address fragments at computed offsets 0x09 and 0x0D, byte 0x6C at offset 0x08 of the separate computed base, and further word relationships.

The low byte of the captured fixed-address-0xA9 word is written to fixed address 0x67. A subsequent read of 0x67 supplies the byte written to fixed address 0x21. Additional later reads of 0x67 supply computed destinations. These reads are not interchangeable with the original captured byte: intervening aliasing or external mutation may change their results.

## Side effects

Fixed and computed addresses are read or modified. Access width, order, and retained-value identity matter wherever aliasing or observation distinguishes them.

Key constraints:

- Repeated stores are not automatically redundant.
- Byte stores may change later overlapping word reads.
- Word stores may change later byte-derived addresses.
- A captured value does not change when its source memory changes.
- The initial clear/read-back interval yields zero under ordinary sequential writable-memory semantics; later byte stores do not explain a nonzero capture.
- The fixed-address-0x15 byte store is a separate ordered mutation.
- The fixed-address-0x3A write separates two earlier captures from two later captures.
- Global capture precedes the fixed-address-0x60 word store and is reused without reload.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Third-input selection.** Under truncating 32-bit shifts, third-input values 0, 1, 2, and 3 contribute 0x00000000, 0x40000000, 0x80000000, and 0xC0000000. Higher bits do not contribute.

2. **Unsigned extraction.** Ambient value 0xFFFFFFFF yields 0x0001FFFF for the low-19-bit extraction shifted right by two, and 0x0001FFF8 for the low-14-bit extraction shifted left by three.

3. **Small-base width.** If the relevant result’s low seven bits are all set, the small derived base is 508 and its doubled value is 1016.

4. **Initial read-back.** With ordinary sequential writable memory and no external mutation, the heavily scaled base’s cleared offset-0x14 word reads back as zero. Both following computed offset-0x15 byte stores write zero. Even overlap by the intervening zero clear does not produce a nonzero capture.

5. **Fixed-address-0x15 mutation.** For a doubled small derived base of 1016, the fixed-address byte store writes 0xF8. It follows the later computed byte-store pair and precedes the next computed offset-0x54 word store. That pair need not contain 0xF8.

6. **Ordered overwrite and global retention.** With ordinary memory and no relevant interference, the four-store offset-0x20 sequence finishes at 0x20. Its intermediate stores and byte read remain observable. If the subsequent global capture returns 0x1000, publication uses 0x1000 even if the fixed-address-0x60 store changes the global cell.

7. **Post-write capture without impossible overlap.** If the retained relationship word is 0x30, the reduced quantity is 0x2A and the separate computed base is 0xA8. Its post-0x3A offset-0x04 word begins at 0xAC, not 0x38. More generally, that word’s start is congruent to 12 modulo 16 and cannot overlap 0x3A under the stated arithmetic models. Its ordering after the write remains required.

8. **Terminal retained argument.** If the post-0x3A offset-0x14 capture obtains a particular word, later changes to that source do not replace F-U02’s third argument.

9. **Partial failure.** If a store completes and the next access faults, no represented rollback restores the completed store.

## Evidence
