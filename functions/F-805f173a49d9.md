# F-805f173a49d9

## Purpose

F-805f173a49d9 performs an ordered sequence of indirect invocations, derives fields and addresses from invocation results and implicit entry state, modifies memory through those addresses, and reaches a terminal indirect invocation.

The supplied static representation does not establish a business purpose. Character-like constants, repeated stores, and pointer-like values do not establish formatting, allocation, parsing, initialization, or container maintenance.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** displayed operations, widths, dependencies, and access ordering.
- **Inferred intent:** possible semantic interpretations, not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** unresolved behavior or details not fully specified here.

All behavior is conditional on execution reaching the relevant operation. Valid addresses, successful dependency invocations, and normal completion are not guaranteed.

4. A late-loaded 32-bit value from another loaded base plus 0x74.

## Behavior

### Indirect invocation phase

The first target depends on entry state. Later targets are expressed using continuation-related or code-location-derived calculations.

Invocation order is observable. Some sites have no displayed arguments; others have one through four. Absence of displayed arguments does not establish absence of implicit inputs. Unused primary results do not make invocations removable or side-effect-free.

Established transformations include:

- Logical right shift of the entry-state value by twelve, followed by a left shift by seven for two arguments at a later site.
- Selection of the low eleven bits of an early result, followed by a left shift by ten.
- Selection of low-24-bit fields followed by right shifts of four, eight, twelve, or nineteen at different stages.
- Selection of bits 11 through 17 from an upper result half.
- Selection of bits 4 through 16 from another upper result half.
- Left shifts of primary results or selected fields, including shifts by fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, twenty-two, twenty-three, twenty-four, and twenty-five.
- Passing a lower result half shifted left by eight alongside an unmodified upper half.
- Passing additional post-invocation state whose formal provenance is unresolved.

Several values are explicitly zeroed before later shifts or masks. Their derived values remain zero under ordinary fixed-width arithmetic, but intervening invocations remain observable.

A left shift by thirty-one followed by a left shift by thirty leaves no surviving bits under low-32-bit semantics. It must not be treated as a mathematical 61-bit result.

### Initial direct mutations

After the invocation-heavy phase, direct memory mutation proceeds without another visibly represented dependency invocation until the terminal transfer.

The first four stores, in order, are:

- A word at the final paired result’s upper-half-derived base plus 0x14, containing an earlier result shifted left by twenty-five.
- A byte at that shifted-result-derived base plus 9, containing the low byte of the paired result’s upper half.
- A word at 256 times the paired result’s lower half plus 0x54, containing another result scaled by 0x02000000.
- A second byte at the same base plus 9, containing the low byte of the earlier shifted result.

The intervening word store prevents treating the two byte stores as a single final-state assignment.

A compact address component is formed by shifting a result left by eight, retaining bits selected by 0x7FFF, and shifting right by six. Under low-32-bit semantics, its possible values are multiples of four from zero through 508. Doubling it produces multiples of eight from zero through 1016.

### Linked and overlapping mutations

The next region interleaves byte reads, word reads, word writes, and byte writes. Recurring offsets include 4, 5, 9, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x14, 0x15, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.

These offsets do not establish a structure layout. Loaded values are variously treated as payloads, arithmetic operands, and addresses.

Important ordering constraints include:

- A word at a scaled base plus 0x14 is cleared before a later read of that location.
- A byte from that base plus 5 is saved, then written as a zero-extended word at offset 0x34.
- Another offset-0x14 word is cleared repeatedly; later offset-0x15 byte stores overlap its range.
- The byte at fixed address 0x05 is read after preceding calculated-address writes.
- A word at a byte-derived base plus 0x54 is saved before replacement.
- Offset-0x74 locations are used to obtain further addresses, including repeated accesses rather than universal reuse of an earlier load.
- Self-address-like word stores and low-byte link-like stores recur.

Intervening aliases can change a location after it is cleared. A later read from a previously cleared word is therefore not unconditionally zero.

Fixed-address writes in this region include a byte at 0x01, a byte at 0x15, and a word at 0x54. Fixed and calculated locations are not assumed disjoint.

### Literal stores and repeated updates

A later region stores 0x3A as a byte, as a halfword, and as a word at another calculated location plus 0x20.

A separate offset-0x20 word receives, in order, 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from the corresponding base plus 4, and 0x20 again. The intervening read is observable and alias-sensitive.

An adjusted value is obtained by subtracting 0x96 from a fourfold-scaled value. Four times this adjusted value becomes the **calculated base** used below. A global pointer-like load supplies the **global-derived base**. An earlier doubled linked value supplies the **linked base**. These names describe value provenance, not object types or ownership.

After the global-derived base is loaded, a fixed-address word at 0x60 receives 0x20. A long, finite sequence of explicit updates then repeatedly combines:

- A word store at a scaled address plus 0x54.
- A byte store at the global-derived base plus 0x19.
- A byte store at the calculated base plus 0x11.
- A word store at the global-derived base plus 0x34.

The first update uses an earlier scaled value for its scaled destination and final word payload. Subsequent updates use eight times the adjusted value.

These are explicit repetitions, not evidence of a runtime loop or variable iteration count. Their exact multiplicity is **documented_only** in this contract. Their repeated store events and internal ordering are not reducible to a final memory image.

### Final-region saved values

Before the three fixed-address reads detailed below, the function:

- Saves the word at fixed address 0xA9.
- Saves a word at offset 0x74 of another scaled base.
- Writes a byte at fixed address 0x3A, derived from the adjusted arithmetic value scaled by eight and increased by 0x30.
- Saves the word at the linked base plus 0x14.
- Saves the word at the calculated base plus 4.

It then writes the low byte of the calculated base at the linked base plus 0x0D; writes 0x6C at the calculated base plus 8; writes the low byte of the global-derived base at calculated-base offsets 0x0D and 9; and stores the linked base as a word at its own offset 0x54.

The low byte of the saved 0xA9 word is then written to fixed address 0x67.

### Three distinct reads of fixed address 0x67

There are **three separate byte reads of 0x67**. Each supplies its own later byte store. None may be replaced unconditionally by the byte initially written to 0x67 or by a previous read.

**First read: supplies fixed address 0x21.** After writing 0x67, the function reads a word from the linked base plus 0x44. It then reads the byte currently at 0x67 and writes that byte to 0x21. The intervening word read does not itself write memory, but the read of 0x67 remains a distinct access rather than direct reuse of the saved 0xA9 value.

**Between the first and second reads**, the following access groups occur in the displayed order:

1. The low byte of the loaded linked-offset-0x44 word is written at calculated-base offset 0x10. That loaded word is also written at global-derived offset 0x54. A shifted byte derived from it is written at linked-base offset 1. The word at global-derived offset 0x54 is then replaced with the calculated base.
2. A word is read from calculated-base offset 0x40. The previously saved calculated-offset-4 word is written at global-derived offset 0x74. The newly read offset-0x40 word is written at calculated-base offset 0x10.
3. The linked base is written as a word at global-derived offset 0x44.

**Second read: supplies the first later calculated-offset-9 store.** Only after these groups does the function read 0x67 again and write that newly read byte at the calculated base plus 9.

**Between the second and third reads**, the function writes the low byte of the calculated base at linked-base offset 9, writes the saved 0xA9 word at calculated-base offset 0x44, and reads a word from calculated-base offset 0x54.

**Third read: supplies the second later calculated-offset-9 store.** After that word read, the function reads 0x67 again and writes the newly read byte at the calculated base plus 9.

Calculated-address writes in either interval may alias 0x67. The second and third reads therefore need not match the first read, each other, or the original extracted byte. The calculated-offset-9 destination also participates in aliasing; its writes are not assumed independent of subsequent reads.

The shifted-byte store in the first interval uses a signed right shift whose count is the low eight bits of the calculated base. Oversized counts and negative operands require unresolved platform semantics.

### Remaining mutations and terminal invocation

After the third read-and-store pair, the word previously loaded from calculated-base offset 0x54 acts as another address base. The function:

- Writes the low byte of the earlier saved offset-0x74 word at that base plus 0x0D.
- Restores the saved linked-offset-0x14 word at linked-base offset 0x54.
- Reads calculated-base offset 0x14.
- Writes the saved offset-0x74 value’s low byte at linked-base offset 0x11.
- Overwrites calculated-base offset 9 with the low byte of the loaded address base, and writes that base as a word at calculated-base offset 0x44.

Thus, the two stores supplied by later reads of 0x67 are not necessarily the final writes to calculated-base offset 9.

A word read from linked-base offset 4 supplies another base. A halfword containing 0x65 is stored at twice that loaded value. A word from its offset 0x64 is saved for the terminal invocation.

Further effects include a byte store using the saved calculated-offset-0x14 value, a word store of decimal 100 at offset 0x54, a word read at the newer base plus 0x74, and a word read at linked-base offset 0x64. The closing stores include a byte at linked-base offset 9, a word at the latter loaded base plus 0x54, and a word at twice the saved terminal-argument value.

The terminal invocation follows these mutations. A return is displayed afterward, but the target’s return, termination, or transfer behavior is unknown.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional requirements, not executed tests.

### Field extraction

For an early result of 0xFFFFFFFF, retaining the low eleven bits and shifting left by ten produces 0x001FFC00.

For a low-24-bit extraction input of 0xFFFFFFFF, shifting the masked value right by nineteen produces 31.

### Compact component

Under low-32-bit shift semantics, input 0x7F produces 508 in the compact extraction and 1016 when doubled. Input 0x80 produces zero. The extraction is not an unconstrained nine-bit value.

### Stable 0x67 storage

If ordinary memory is used and no intervening write aliases 0x67, all three reads return the low byte initially copied from the saved 0xA9 word.

Acceptance still requires three reads: one supplying 0x21 and two separately supplying calculated-base offset 9.

### Changed value before the second read

If an intervening write between the first and second reads aliases 0x67 and changes its byte, the second read observes the changed byte under ordinary sequential memory semantics. Reusing the first read’s byte is not equivalent.

### Changed value before the third read

If the linked-base-offset-9 store between the second and third reads aliases 0x67 and changes its byte, the third read supplies that new byte to calculated-base offset 9, absent another intervening change. Reusing the second read is not equivalent.

### Cleared-word readback

A cleared word reads as zero only if no intervening access changes any of its bytes. An overlapping byte store invalidates an unconditional zero-readback expectation.

### Fault or non-return

An unmapped reached address has no specified recovery behavior. Earlier successful stores need not be undone.

If F-805f173a49d9-D02 does not return, this function is not required to return.
