# F-72402a26a8ac

## Purpose

F-72402a26a8ac performs an ordered chain of indirect invocations, transforms values obtained from execution state and call results, mutates memory through fixed and computed addresses, and finishes with an unresolved four-argument indirect invocation.

An application-level purpose is not established. Repeated offsets do not establish a container layout; character-like constants do not establish formatting or textual output.

This is a **source-facing, static-only behavioral contract**. “Established” means visible in the supplied representation, subject to its unresolved calling convention and control-flow interpretation. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Indirect invocations and value preparation

The first invocation targets incoming link/control state plus 0xFFE. Its two displayed arguments are equal: the incoming word shifted left by seven.

Subsequent targets are generally constructed from embedded control-location values plus 0xFFE. These are distinct call sites; a shared displacement does not establish a shared dependency. Calls with no displayed arguments still have observable ordering and unknown effects.

Arguments consume preceding primary and secondary results, saved values, explicit zeros, and halves of captured 64-bit results. Preparation includes left shifts, unsigned right shifts, low-bit masking, extraction of bits 10–19, and 64-bit right shifts by 34, 36, and 44. Masks retain differing widths, including 11–24 bits at different sites. These operations do not establish an encoding or protocol.

Explicitly zeroed saved values remain zero through their displayed shifts. That fact says nothing about intervening callees or their secondary results. Calls cannot be removed merely because their displayed primary result is unused.

There is no explicit branch around the displayed chain. Reaching direct mutation depends on every preceding invocation returning. Callees may change globals or memory later read by this routine; no preservation guarantee is supplied.

### Opening memory mutations

The following descriptive names identify saved values, not objects:

- **Final low/high words:** halves of the last captured paired result.
- **Packed base:** an earlier result-derived word shifted left by 25.
- **Shifted base:** another earlier primary result multiplied by \(2^{25}\).
- **Small component:** an earlier result shifted left by eight, masked to fifteen bits, then logically shifted right by six.
- **Small base:** twice the small component.
- **Saved argument:** an earlier primary result already shifted left by eight.
- **Original shifted result:** the primary result used to form the shifted base.

The first word store places the packed base at final high word plus 0x14. A byte at packed base plus 9 then receives the final high word’s low byte. A word at 256 times final low word plus 0x54 receives the shifted base, after which packed base plus 9 receives the packed base’s low byte.

The routine clears shifted base plus 0x14, reads the byte at shifted base plus 5, clears small base plus 0x14, and publishes the zero-extended byte as a word at shifted base plus 0x34. It then reads shifted base plus 0x14, retaining that word as the **cleared-field sample**. Another clear of small base plus 0x14 precedes two consecutive byte stores of the sample at small base plus 0x15.

These are separate, mixed-width effects. Even the cleared-field sample is not unconditionally zero: memory observability and aliasing remain relevant.

### Fixed-byte selection and retained-word replacement

Next, the byte at fixed address **0x05** is read and saved as the **fixed-byte base**. The byte at **0x01** receives the small base’s low byte. The selected base plus 0x15 receives the small component’s low byte.

The saved argument is written first as a byte at shifted base plus 0x11 and then as a word at cleared-field sample plus 0x54. Shifted base plus 0x11 is subsequently cleared.

Only then is the word at fixed-byte base plus 0x54 read and retained as the **retained word**. That same location is replaced with the cleared-field sample. The retained value is therefore the pre-replacement sample, not a later reload. Small base plus 0x34 receives the small component.

A word read from shifted base plus 0x74 supplies a **first linked base**. Its offset-0x54 word receives the original shifted result shifted left by 26. Two displayed reads of small base plus 0x74 supply the destination base and stored value for another offset-0x54 write. Their relative evaluation order is unresolved; they must not be silently collapsed into one read.

The retained word then drives a self-valued word store at its own offset 0x24 and a byte store at first linked base plus 9. First linked base plus 0x54 is replaced with the saved argument. The self-valued store and byte store are repeated.

Small base plus 0x14 next receives the original shifted result shifted left by 26. Fixed word storage at **0x54** receives twice the retained word. Small base plus 0x14 then receives the retained word twice.

### Dependent links and repeated byte sampling

Define **quadrupled base** as four times the retained word and **eightfold value** as eight times it.

Two byte stores at small base plus 0x15 publish the quadrupled base’s low byte. Fixed byte **0x15** then receives the small base’s low byte. Quadrupled base plus 0x54 receives the saved argument, and small base plus 0x34 receives the quadrupled base.

A fresh word read from small base plus 0x74, doubled, becomes the **doubled link**. Quadrupled base plus 0x54 is replaced with the quadrupled base itself. Its offset-0x15 byte receives the eightfold value, and retained word plus 0x44 receives the doubled link.

A byte read at quadrupled base plus 5 selects a word-read address by adding 0x74. That word becomes the **byte-selected link**, whose offset-0x44 word receives the doubled link. This selection uses an eight-bit value, not a word-sized pointer field.

Quadrupled base then receives its own low byte at offset 0x15, the byte-selected link’s low byte at 0x0D, its own word value at 0x14, and its own word value at 0x54. A word loaded from its offset 0x64 supplies a **nested base**; nested base plus 0x0D receives the byte-selected link’s low byte, and nested base plus 0x14 receives the nested base itself.

A word read at quadrupled base plus 4 supplies the **sample base**. Doubled link plus 9 receives the byte-selected link’s low byte, and doubled link plus 0x44 receives the eightfold value.

The first byte sample at sample base plus 5 is followed by stores to quadrupled base plus 9, byte-selected link plus 0x44, quadrupled base plus 0x14, doubled link plus 0x15, and doubled link plus 0x44. Their values are, respectively, the doubled link’s low byte, the zero-extended sample, the eightfold value, the sample base’s low byte, and the quadrupled base.

The byte at sample base plus 5 is then **read again**. Intervening stores may have changed it. Quadrupled base plus 0x15 receives the eightfold value’s low byte before a word is read at this second byte sample plus 0x74. Call that word the **selected word**, and twice it the **dependent base**.

Subsequent stores publish the sample base’s low byte at quadrupled base plus 0x15, the quadrupled base at its own offset 0x54, the dependent base’s low byte at quadrupled base plus 9, and the eightfold value at quadrupled base plus 0x14.

### Literal stores and repeated association

Four times the selected word becomes the **scaled selected base**. The routine writes:

- Byte 0x3A at the scaled selected base.
- Halfword 0x003A at eight times the selected word.
- Word 0x0000003A at scaled selected base plus 0x20.

Between the first and second of these stores, the quadrupled base is reduced by 0x96 to form the **adjusted base**. Four times that adjusted base becomes the **working base**.

Working base plus 0x20 receives consecutive word values 0x20 and 0x2F, then a zero-extended byte read from working base plus 4, then 0x20 again.

The **owner base** is loaded as a word from fixed storage **0x087FBBBC**. Fixed word **0x60** then receives 0x20.

An initial association writes the scaled selected base at eight times retained word plus 0x54, the working base’s low byte at owner base plus 0x19, the owner base’s low byte at working base plus 0x11, and eight times retained word at owner base plus 0x34.

Subsequent explicit repetitions preserve that four-store order but use eight times the **adjusted base** for the offset-0x54 destination and offset-0x34 value. The owner base and stored byte values remain saved values; they are not reloaded during this region. Exact repetition multiplicity remains **documented_only** in this contract. The sequence must not be reduced to final memory contents.

### Fixed-storage samples and late field replacement

After the repeated association region, the word at fixed address **0xA9** is saved as the **fixed-word sample**. A word is then read from scaled selected base plus 0x74 as the **saved linked word**.

Fixed byte **0x3A** receives the low eight bits of eight times adjusted base plus 0x30. The routine next saves the word at dependent base plus 0x14 as the **saved dependent field**, then the word at working base plus 4 as the **saved working field**.

Dependent base plus 0x0D receives the working base’s low byte. Working base receives byte 0x6C at offset 8 and the owner base’s low byte at offsets 0x0D and 9. Dependent base plus 0x54 receives the dependent base itself.

Fixed byte **0x67** receives the fixed-word sample’s low byte. A word is then loaded from dependent base plus 0x44 as the **late linked value**. Fixed byte **0x21** receives a byte read from **0x67**.

Working base plus 0x10 receives the late linked value’s low byte, and owner base plus 0x54 receives the full late linked value. Dependent base plus 1 receives the low byte of that value after a signed right shift by the working base’s low byte. Owner base plus 0x54 is then replaced with the working base.

A word read at working base plus 0x40 is saved. Owner base plus 0x74 receives the saved working field; working base plus 0x10 receives the newly saved offset-0x40 word, replacing the earlier byte-containing region with a word store. Owner base plus 0x44 receives the dependent base.

Working base plus 9 next receives a **fresh byte read from 0x67**. Dependent base plus 9 receives the working base’s low byte, and working base plus 0x44 receives the saved fixed-word sample.

A word read from working base plus 0x54 is retained as the **late destination**. Working base plus 9 then receives another **fresh byte read from 0x67**. Neither read may be replaced with the byte originally assigned there: intervening computed writes can alias 0x67. Conversely, the fixed-word sample remains the earlier saved word from 0xA9.

Late destination plus 0x0D receives the saved linked word’s low byte. Dependent base plus 0x54 is restored to the saved dependent field. The word at working base plus 0x14 is then sampled as the **late field sample**. Dependent base plus 0x11 receives the saved linked word’s low byte; working base plus 9 and plus 0x44 receive, respectively, the low byte and full word of the late destination.

### Final argument production

A word read at dependent base plus 4 supplies the **final base**. Before any final-base offset-0x64 read, a halfword **0x0065** is written at twice the final base.

The word at final base plus **0x64** is then saved as the **final data word**. Next, late destination plus **0x11** receives the late field sample’s low byte, and late destination plus **0x54** receives word value **100**, decimal, equivalent to 0x64.

Only after those writes is the word at final base plus **0x74** saved as the **final linked word**. Another word read, at dependent base plus **0x64**, supplies the **final destination**. The intervening writes may alias either read and therefore affect the final invocation.

Dependent base plus 9 receives the final base’s low byte. Final destination plus 0x54 receives the saved final data word. A word at twice the final data word receives the final destination.

The final indirect invocation receives four displayed arguments, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The saved final data word.
3. The saved dependent field.
4. The saved final linked word.

These arguments are saved samples; the two final word stores do not trigger argument reloads.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional review examples, not executed tests.

- **Initial arguments:** With incoming word 1 and 32-bit shift semantics, both first-call arguments are 128.
- **Small-base boundary:** Under bit-vector arithmetic, the small component retains only the originating result’s lowest seven bits, shifted left by two. The small base therefore ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.
- **Retained replacement:** If fixed-byte base plus 0x54 contains 0x12345678 when sampled, the retained word is 0x12345678 even after that location is replaced. Later accesses through the retained word use that saved value.
- **Repeated offset-5 sampling:** If an intervening store changes sample base plus 5, the second byte sample—and its dependent word-read address—must reflect the changed byte.
- **Fixed-byte aliasing:** If a computed store changes 0x67 between its initial assignment and a later read, the later publication to working base plus 9 uses the newly read byte. The saved 0xA9 word is not recomputed.
- **Final halfword aliasing:** If twice final base overlaps final base plus 0x64, the final data word is read after the 0x0065 halfword store and may include its effect.
- **Final intervening stores:** If late destination plus 0x54 overlaps final base plus 0x74, the final linked word is sampled after the word-100 store, not before it.
- **Failure:** If a dependent destination is inaccessible, no success result or rollback may be promised.
