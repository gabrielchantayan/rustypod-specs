### Widths and addressing

A word is 32 bits; a halfword is 16 bits; a byte is 8 bits. A low-byte store retains only the least significant eight bits of its source.

For an index, its **scaled slot** is the address `index × 4 + 20`. Its **scaled value** is `index × 4`.

A **selected-byte operation** first reads an unsigned selector byte, then accesses address `selector value + 17`. The destination is therefore between 17 and 272, inclusive—not an offset from the selector’s address.

Unsigned word shifts right by 22 and 23 yield ranges 0–1023 and 0–511. Where the representation uses signed integers for multiplication, left shifting, or address construction, overflow semantics are not established. The formulas below preserve those distinctions; they do not impose universal wraparound.

### 4. High-bit publication and forced zeros

The latest scaled index is passed onward, followed by a four-call group.

The forwarding call and a four-call group follow. The field extraction is stored in the returned index’s scaled slot. That scaled index is passed onward, followed by a four-call group leaving another secondary value, the **masked-read selector**.

A zero word is stored in the masked-read selector’s scaled slot. That selector is passed onward. A forwarding pair and a four-call group yield another index; `global extraction >> 23`, also zero, is stored in its scaled slot.

The function reads a word at:

`((masked-read selector & 0x1fffffff) >> 19) + 20`

It passes the latest scaled index onward and executes a four-call group. The word just read is then shifted right by 22 to produce the **selected extraction**.

Next, the function reads the byte at address 1 and writes the low byte of the selected extraction to that byte’s selected destination. This is a value store, not necessarily a clear. The selector address is 1 because its underlying saved offset is the forced-zero value.

## Acceptance examples

These examples are conditional consequences of static operations, not observed executions. Arithmetic examples require supported addresses and applicable arithmetic semantics.

1. **Initial narrowing.** If the initial selector byte is 5, its destination is address 22. A byte result with bit pattern `0x43`, shifted left by two and narrowed to a byte, produces `0x0c`.

2. **Different address formulas.** A first secondary selector of 3 gives a scaled-slot store at address 32, followed by a word read at address 23. These accesses must not be merged.

3. **Forced zero.** For every captured unsigned G-A word, shifting right by 23 twice yields zero. Later changes to G-A cannot change this already-derived value.

4. **Extraction narrowing.** A field word of `0xffc00000` shifted right by 22 yields 1023. Its byte store writes `0xff`.

5. **Three address-1 reads.** If the first address-1 read yields 5 and the selected extraction is `0x123`, address 22 receives `0x23`. If the second read yields 6, address 23 is cleared. If the third read yields 7, address 24 is cleared and the saved selector becomes 7. The later field copy uses addresses 123 and 8 even if address 1 changes again.

6. **Fresh field read.** If consecutive reads at `repeated-copy base + 116` yield `0x12345678` and `0xaabbccdd`, the corresponding byte writes produce `0x78` and `0xdd`.

7. **Terminal forwarding.** If F-T002 returns `0x12345678`, F-T003 receives that word as its displayed argument. The contract does not substitute F-T001’s result or the later byte result.

8. **Final publication.** If G-C contributes `0x123456` and F-T004 supplies byte `0xab`, G-B receives `0x123456ab`. This occurs after the pointer-based byte clear and before the halfword clear.

9. **Unsupported final target.** If address zero is not callable, ordinary return is not established. The terminal byte clear, G-B publication, and halfword clear already precede the attempted call.
