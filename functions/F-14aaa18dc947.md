# F-14aaa18dc947

## Purpose

The established behavior is a straight-line sequence of 96 indirect-call occurrences interleaved with five byte reads, five byte writes, and fourteen 32-bit writes. Dependency results supply later call arguments, address components, and stored values.

No application-level purpose is established. Initialization, serialization, allocation, and resource release are not supported conclusions.

The supplied recovery ends at an invalid-instruction boundary. It does not establish normal completion or a return value. This contract describes the visible execution prefix, conditional on each preceding call returning and each preceding operation succeeding.

### Widths and arithmetic

A **byte** is 8 bits; a **word** is 32 bits. The result consumed from F-D026 is 64 bits, divided into low and high 32-bit portions.

The following descriptive values are used:

- **W:** R shifted right logically by 3.
- **H:** R shifted right logically by 6.
- **B:** the initial unsigned byte selector.
- **Q:** a retained scaled result from F-D050.
- **U:** the unsigned 32-bit result from F-D072.
- **V:** the unsigned 32-bit result from F-D084.
- **T:** the later selector-address base derived from W.

Byte stores retain the low eight bits of their described values. Right shifts of R, S, U, and V are logical.

Most other scaled dependency results are recovered as signed 32-bit integers. Multiplication, left shift, and address addition involving those results must not silently be promoted into arbitrary-precision arithmetic or uniformly assigned wrapping semantics. Signed overflow and negative signed left shifts remain unresolved.

Two unsigned calculations are more definite within the recovered type model:

- W multiplied by `0x80000` is a 32-bit unsigned multiplication, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\), before assignment into the signed storage used for T.
- The final selector-read expression, eight times V plus one, uses unsigned 32-bit arithmetic and is reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).

Conversion of the first result into signed storage, pointer interpretation, and the correspondence between recovered expressions and actual machine address semantics remain separate uncertainties.
