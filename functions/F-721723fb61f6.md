### Widths and arithmetic

A 32-bit integer-word interpretation is strongly supported by unsigned right shifts of 31 positions used to extract branch bits. Under that interpretation:

- An unsigned word has 32 bits; unsigned arithmetic retains the low 32 bits.
- Unsigned right shift by 31 yields zero or one according to bit 31.
- Complement and bitwise OR operate on word representations.
- A top-bit decision is not a general nonzero decision.
- The explicitly dereferenced signed integer occupies one 32-bit word, ordinarily four eight-bit bytes.

The working input, derived input, saved products, combined word, candidate result, and saved main-call result are represented as unsigned words. The first-input intermediate and dependency results are represented as signed integer words.

Global declarations are absent. Consequently, the signedness and conversions governing some intermediate expressions remain **documented_only**. Assignment into an unsigned destination does not establish that every preceding multiplication was unsigned or that signed overflow was avoided.

In particular, selector 13’s second OR operand forms a difference, multiplies that difference by an input-factor read, and only then multiplies by the signed dependency result. It must not be reassociated on the assumption that mathematical multiplication is commutative or associative.

Pointer width, exact alignment requirements, calling convention, and complete target integer semantics remain unresolved. The primary-factor right shift inside selector 13 may be signed or unsigned; it is not established to be logical top-bit extraction.

### Selector 11: capture

Capture updates state in this order:

1. Save the original context argument.
2. Form the saved second-input product by multiplying an input-factor read by the second signed input, then multiplying that intermediate by a primary-factor read.
3. Set the capture marker.
4. Form the signed first-input intermediate from a fresh input-factor read multiplied by the first signed input.
5. Form the saved first-input companion product from that intermediate multiplied by a companion-factor read.
6. Replace the combined word with the OR of the saved second-input product and a separately evaluated contribution. That contribution first multiplies a companion-factor read by an input-factor read, then multiplies the intermediate by the second signed input.
7. Save the original mode argument.
8. Read the capture continuation into the control word.
9. Save the original unsigned input as working input.
10. Save the original record pointer.

The factor accesses in the separate contributions are fresh accesses. Capture does not dereference the pointer or invoke a dependency.

Capture does **not** overwrite the candidate result. It also leaves the derived input, saved derived-input companion product, recorded conditions, and saved main-call result unchanged. Repeated capture reuses the original six arguments rather than previously transformed values.

These are conditional static criteria, not runtime results. Numeric examples assume defined arithmetic under the applicable word model and dispatch conditions that reach the stated operations.

2. **Capture preserves an earlier candidate.** Suppose the candidate is seven. 3. **Independent capture contributions.** With second signed input two and primary factor one, let the saved-product input-factor read be three. Let the later contribution read companion factor four and input factor five. The saved product is six; the combined word is six OR forty, or forty-six.

4. **Required preliminary grouping.** Let the primary-factor reads be signed value one, the input-factor reads be signed value three, and the preliminary dependency result be the signed value −1,000,000,000. Under a signed 32-bit interpretation of these intermediates, the difference is −1, its multiplication by three is −3, and the final multiplication produces 3,000,000,000, outside the signed range. 6. **Recorded conditions.** Selector 16 uses the pointer condition recorded by selector 17. Selector 10 uses the mode condition recorded by selector 4. Neither substitutes a fresh test.
