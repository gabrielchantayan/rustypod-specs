## Behavior

Invocations have between zero and four represented arguments. An invocation with no represented arguments is not established to be effect-free.

Arguments include:

- Primary results shifted left by eight positions.
- Identical transformed values in multiple argument positions.
- Aggregate high words, both unchanged and shifted.
- Saved results retained across intervening invocations.
- Explicit zeros and transformations of explicitly zero quantities.
- Secondary register outputs independent of primary-result notation.

Masking and shifting order is significant. Retaining the low 24 bits and logically shifting right by eight extracts original bits 8–23. Under 32-bit truncation, shifting left by eight and then logically right by eight instead retains the original low 24 bits.

No local conditional branch or loop is represented in this prefix. Unknown dependencies may mutate shared storage, invalidate memory, fail, or not return. Their effects cannot be replaced with assumptions of purity or global preservation.

The index is obtained by shifting a result left by eight, retaining 15 bits, then logically shifting right by six. Under 32-bit truncation, it is a multiple of four from 0 through 508; its doubled value ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight.

The final aggregate result supplies low and high words. The opening stores publish the saved 25-bit-shifted value at high-word offset 20, publish the high word’s low byte at shifted-base offset 9, and publish the \(2^{25}\)-scaled value at offset 84 from 256 times the aggregate low word.
