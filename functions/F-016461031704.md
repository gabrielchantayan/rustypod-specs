# F-016461031704

## Purpose

F-016461031704 executes an ordered sequence of dependency calls, byte-selector accesses, indexed word stores, linked memory reads, and shared-state updates. Its final represented invocation targets address zero.

No application-level purpose is established. Initialization, object construction, dispatch, and encoded-operation execution are possible interpretations, not contractual semantics.

This contract describes the supplied static representation. It does not establish that the represented function boundary, call targets, or calling convention accurately describe executable machine behavior.

## Behavior

### Ordering rules

The visible body is a forward sequence without an explicit branch or loop. Dependencies may branch, mutate memory, fail, or not return.

A recurring **selector write** first reads an unsigned byte, then writes one byte at address 0x11 plus the loaded value. Its destination lies between 0x11 and 0x110 inclusive. The selector read and dependent store are separate observable accesses.

A recurring **indexed store** writes a 32-bit value at a scaled index plus 0x14. Scales include four, sixteen, and 256.

Repeated expressions require new reads at their respective positions. Calls between accesses are observable ordering boundaries, not permission to cache memory or omit invocations. The phase descriptions below group call sequences without assigning those calls purity or interchangeable behavior.

### Entry and early selector clears

The first memory action reads a byte at the incoming implicit base plus one. The incoming implicit byte is then stored at the selected low-address destination. Only afterward does F-D001 receive the second explicit argument.

Following several calls, a selector clear reads at one plus the fourth argument logically shifted right by 19. That read address ranges from 1 through 0x2000.

Subsequent calls receive call-produced values shifted left by two, sometimes duplicated and sometimes accompanied by explicit zero arguments. Another selector clear rereads address 1.

A later byte read at a secondary-result base plus 0x0e is forwarded in four positions. After a paired result and a selector clear, another invocation receives the high half twice, zero, and the low half logically shifted right by 19. Further selector clears use secondary values shifted right by nine, plus one.

### Distinct scaled bases and initial word state

An earlier dependency result is logically shifted right by 18 and multiplied by 0x400. Call this the **early selector base**. Two selector clears read at this retained base plus one, with intervening calls.

Later, the represented contents of address 0x30 occupy four argument positions. Whether this corresponds to one physical read or multiple reads is unresolved. Two further clears reread a selector addressed by a retained secondary value shifted right by nine, plus one.

A different result is then obtained immediately before the invocation with four zero arguments. Shifting this newer result right by 18 and multiplying by 0x400 forms the **initial word-read base**. It is not the retained early selector base; their equality is not established.

After the four-zero-argument invocation and another call:

- A secondary word is stored at four times that same word plus 0x14.
- A word is read at the initial word-read base plus 0x14.
- The loaded word, logically shifted right by 19, is supplied alongside the secondary word to the next dependency.

Thus the indexed store precedes this word read. If their addresses alias, the read uses the updated memory.

Further calls and stores propagate retained values through four-times-index slots. A result scaled by four is stored at sixteen times its original index plus 0x14. A subsequent word read uses the four-times-index base plus 0x14, not the sixteen-times-index destination; its upper bits are forwarded.

### Packed fields and mixed-width reads

A later result supplies a left shift by two and three retained fields obtained after masking with 0x3fffffff and shifting right by 17, seven, or 20.

The low byte of the left-shifted value is written through a selector. After calls, the right-shifted-by-20 field supplies another selector-write byte.

One invocation has seven represented argument positions: four copies of a result, the retained right-shifted-by-17 and right-shifted-by-seven fields, and a control-address-like value. Their actual ABI interpretation is unresolved.

After a paired result, the selector at the retained right-shifted-by-seven field plus one is read and cleared, then read and cleared again after calls. The paired low half later supplies:

- Its top four bits as an argument.
- Selector-read addresses formed by shifting it right by nine and adding one.
- A byte-read address equal to twice that low half.

The loaded byte is forwarded four times. Later, its least significant bit is moved to bit 31 for an argument.

Another paired low half supplies both selector addressing and a word-read base. The word-read address adds either zero or 0x80000000, selected by bit 19 of that low half. The loaded word is forwarded four times.

A subsequent byte read at a secondary base plus 0x0e precedes another paired result. Later selector clears use the paired low half plus one, that low half multiplied by 0x1000 plus one, and a newer secondary base plus one.

One dependency receives the same stack address in four positions. The region’s extent, initialization, and permitted retention are unknown.

The next mixed-width sequence reads an unsigned byte at a retained low-half base plus a secondary offset. After calls and selector writes, a halfword is read at the sum of that byte and a newer secondary value logically shifted right by 19. The halfword is forwarded four times before another paired result.

### Transient byte fields, lookups, and linked state

A retained right-shifted value is stored into two indexed word slots, separated by calls.

The next byte field is at a retained paired low half plus 0x0d. It first receives the low eight bits of twice that low half. A word lookup at four times a newer result plus 0x44 and several calls occur before the byte field is overwritten with 4. The intermediate byte is therefore observable to dependencies.

A second lookup uses 0x44 plus the newer result masked with 0x3fffffff and shifted right by 20. This is a different address formula from the four-times-index lookup.

Further word stores include:

- Four times a retained result at 256 times a newer result plus 0x14.
- A field masked with 0x03ffffff and shifted right by 13 into a later indexed slot.
- Four-times-index values into sixteen-times-index slots and later four-times-index slots.

A retained sixteen-times-index region receives zero at +0x24. Its word at +0x74 is then loaded as a linked base.

After calls, a newer result shifted right by 20 is stored at the linked base plus 0x24. A word at that base plus 0x74 is read for a dependency. Two later selector writes reread the linked base plus one: first writing a truncated packed field, then clearing after calls.

The right-shifted-by-20 result is at most 0xfff. Its subsequent logical right shift by 21 is necessarily zero.

### Byte propagation and shared-state reset

Three separate secondary-result bases supply byte reads at +0x15, with intervening calls and selector clears. Their equality is not established. The third byte is widened unsigned and stored into an indexed word slot.

A retained field masked with 0x1fffffff and shifted right by 18 is later shifted left by 13 and stored. Additional selector writes publish truncated left shifts and masked upper fields; some reread address 1.

Later indexed stores propagate a call result, secondary results, scaled indices, and a field masked with 0x3fffffff and shifted right by 19. Another stored bit pattern originates from a secondary value shifted left by three, then by two, then by two again: a total left shift of seven under the 32-bit interpretation.

G-A is explicitly set to a 32-bit zero. Execution continues through calls, zero-valued indexed stores, and scaled-value propagation. No rollback or reset-completion check is visible.

A retained value is explicitly zero during a later sequence. Derived selector-read addresses consequently become address 1, and several forwarded fields become zero. This does not make memory at address 1 constant.

F-D003 receives the same retained word twice. Later, a byte at that retained word plus one is widened and used as the base of another byte read at plus one.

These two values have different widths: the retained word can have arbitrary upper bits; the widened byte cannot. Applying a 0x3fffff mask and shifting right by 12 necessarily yields zero for the widened byte, but not for the retained word.

Another byte read at address 1 is widened and stored in an indexed word slot.

### Late linked reads and G-A observations

Later operations clear a byte at a heavily scaled address plus nine, write a retained base’s low byte at that base plus nine, and read a word at that base plus 0x34.

After storing the retained base into an indexed slot, the function follows a dependent read chain:

- Read a word at a secondary base plus 0x14.
- Shift that captured word right by 22 and read another word at the resulting address plus 0x14.
- Use the newer word shifted right by one to select an indexed store.
- Separately read a word at the newer word itself plus 0x14.

Calls and stores remain interspersed in this chain. The addresses cannot be treated as independent lookups.

G-A is captured after many calls. Its earlier reset does not establish this capture as zero. The captured word is shifted right by 23; a later additional right shift by 23 necessarily produces zero. This deduction concerns the captured value, not current G-A storage.

Further indexed stores propagate linked-read upper fields and zeroes. A selector addressed by the twice-shifted G-A capture therefore reads address 1.

### First G-B publication and retained selector base

The relevant order is:

1. Capture the byte at address 1 as an unsigned base.
2. Clear the byte at that captured base plus 0x11.
3. Execute intervening dependency calls.
4. Obtain a newer 32-bit result and write it to G-B.
5. Execute subsequent dependency calls.
6. Read the word at the captured base plus 0x74.
7. Write that word’s low byte at the captured base plus one.

The captured base is retained; address 1 is not reread to choose the later word-read base. Conversely, the word at +0x74 is read only after the first G-B publication and subsequent calls. It must not be prefetched across them.

Later call-produced scaled bases undergo similar word-read-to-byte-write operations. One base’s +0x74 word is reread after intervening calls before another write to its +1 byte.

Another +0x74 word is retained and stored into both sixteen-times-index and later four-times-index slots. Further linked stores, a zero store, and paired-result propagation follow.

G-A is captured again. After intervening calls and another indexed store, a word is read at that captured G-A value plus 0x14. Its low byte and upper fields supply later selector writes.

### Final publication and terminal invocation

The final direct effects occur in this order:

1. Obtain a byte result from a dependency.
2. Store one zero byte through a pointer supplied as a secondary result.
3. Read the represented three-byte quantity beginning at address 9 and combine it as bits 8–31 with the dependency byte as bits 0–7.
4. Write the combined 32-bit value to G-B.
5. Store a 16-bit zero at an address formed from a retained paired low half masked with 0x3fffffff and shifted right by seven.
6. Invoke F-D004 at address zero with three explicit zero arguments.
7. Return without a value if F-D004 returns.

The pointer-zeroing store precedes the three-byte input read. Aliasing can change the published value. The final halfword-clear address is at most 0x007fffff; its validity and alignment are not established.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Entry ordering.** If the entry selector is 0xff and the incoming byte is 0xab, address 0x110 receives 0xab before F-D001.

2. **Argument boundary.** A fourth argument of 0xffffffff yields an early selector-read address of 0x2000. The loaded byte still determines the write destination.

3. **Independent scaled-base provenance.** If the earlier result is 0x00040000 and the newer result immediately preceding the four-zero-argument call is 0x00080000, the early selector base is 0x400 and the initial word-read base is 0x800. The later word read targets 0x814, not 0x414.

4. **Selector mutation.** Selector reads returning 2 and then 7 select destinations 0x13 and 0x18. Calls between them do not permit reuse of the first byte.

5. **Transient byte field.** If the retained paired low half ends in 0x81, its +0x0d field first receives 0x02 and later 0x04, with intervening dependencies able to observe the first write.

6. **High-bit address selection.** A relevant low half of 0x00080000 selects the additional 0x80000000 contribution, yielding word-read address 0x80080000.

7. **Captured base across G-B publication.** If address 1 initially contains 3, address 0x14 is cleared before calls producing the first G-B value. Even if those calls change address 1 to 9, the later word read remains at 0x77 and its low byte is written to address 4.

8. **Deferred word read.** In the preceding example, a dependency changing the word at 0x77 before the later read changes the byte written to address 4. Capturing the selector does not capture that word.

9. **G-A mutation.** A dependency changing G-A after its reset can affect a later capture. Substituting the earlier zero is invalid.

10. **Final publication.** If the final dependency byte is 0x5a and the three-byte quantity represents 0x123456 after the pointer-zeroing store, G-B receives 0x1234565a.

11. **Terminal failure.** If F-D004 traps or does not return, no normal return follows; preceding writes are not rolled back by any visible mechanism.
