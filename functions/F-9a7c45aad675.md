## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests. Continuations assume valid accesses and instructions, returning dependencies, and the arithmetic qualifications stated above.

1. **Initial effects precede the early boundary.**  
   For a zero selector and D = 0x60, the three B-relative stores and F-U001 occur before the boundary. Rejecting D before those effects would not match the displayed ordering.

2. **Bit 5 alone is insufficient.**  
   For D = 0x20, the early condition is false because shifting D right by six yields zero.

3. **Stack save precedes the word read.**  
   With D = 0x20 and continued execution, the slot at S − 0x0C receives 0xC8 before the transfer’s 32-bit read. The later frame saves do not erase this ordering requirement.

4. **The third prefix argument depends on a distinct address.**  
   If the represented unsigned address S + 0x1FC is 0x00600000, F-U003 receives 3 as its third argument. Its first two arguments remain the captured word. Redirecting the frame to S − 0x10 does not change that calculation.

5. **Bypass construction.**  
   With a nonzero selector and B = 0x00400001, the shared pair has low word 0x00002000 and high word 1. Prefix stores and frame redirection are bypassed.

6. **Unsigned flag is not unsigned comparison.**  
   High word zero produces 0xFFFFFFF9 after subtracting seven and therefore flag one. High word 0x80000007 also produces flag one.

7. **Bypass source base.**  
   Every bypass selector is at most 31. Subtracting 51 wraps, giving top byte 0xFF; the source base is therefore 0xFF.

8. **Bound failure preserves earlier effects.**  
   A captured global value of nine selects the boundary after the offset-0x68 store. The terminal stack stores are not reached through that continuation.

9. **Eight passes the bound.**  
   A captured value of eight permits continuation, produces a later byte value of 0x08, and makes the halfword destination 0x30. The halfword’s value still depends on T.
