# F-026a6f981cf6

## Behavior

### Execution model

The visible body contains no explicit branch or loop. The following phases describe direct effects in execution order.

Dependencies occur throughout the sequence, including calls with no explicit arguments and calls whose ordinary results are unused. Such calls remain observable. They may change memory, expose secondary-register results, fail, or not return. Saved scalar values must be distinguished from later reads of the storage from which they originated.

### Initial dispatch and linked word transfers

The initial call targets the incoming control-address value plus 3896 and receives the second explicit input. Subsequent calls obtain and forward a 32-bit result, then eventually supply an index.

The first direct store writes the incoming payload to Field(index). The routine next reads a word at address 20 plus the incoming unsigned address-forming value shifted right by 22. A dependency then receives four times the index.

A later returned value supplies a payload shifted left by two. The previously read word determines two different addresses:

- Its unsigned right shift by one selects the Field destination for that shifted payload.
- Its original, unshifted value selects a word read at that value plus 20.

The destination store precedes the second read. The following dependency receives the right-shifted index, not its four-times-scaled form.

After further calls, another index selects a Field destination for the same saved left-shifted payload. The routine then samples S for the first time, before calling a dependency with four times this new index.

### First shared-state sample and zero-producing transfers

Further calls expose a secondary-register index. The word obtained through the earlier unshifted address is shifted right by 22, producing a saved value designated **A**. The first S sample is shifted right by 23, producing a saved value designated **B**.

A is written to Field(the secondary-register index). The next dependency receives four arguments, in order: that index, the same index again, B, and A.

A subsequent result-forwarding sequence also derives B shifted right by 23. This derived value is necessarily zero under the established unsigned width.

A fresh index receives A at its Field destination, followed by a call with four times that index. Later calls expose another secondary-register index. Two distinct zero stores then occur in this order:

1. A **literal 32-bit zero** is written to Field(the secondary-register index). The following dependency receives that index itself.
2. After intervening calls and result forwarding, a fresh returned index receives **B shifted right by 23** at its Field destination.

The second store therefore writes zero because of the twice-shifted S sample. The preceding store writes zero independently of S.

Immediately after the second store, a word is read at address 20 plus the earlier secondary-register index masked to its low 29 bits and shifted right by 19. This address ranges from 20 through 1043 and need not be word-aligned. A dependency subsequently receives four times the latest returned index.

### Early byte updates

After more calls, the word just read is shifted right by 22. Designate this saved unsigned value **C**; it ranges from 0 through 1023.

The first byte update reads address 1 and writes C’s low byte to address 17 plus the byte read. Address 1 results from the earlier zero-producing double shift; it is not an unresolved pointer result.

A dependency then receives three identical secondary-register values followed by a fourth argument of zero. The following call sequence includes a call receiving an explicitly computed zero.

A later returned value does not replace C for the next byte update. That update reads a byte at address \(1 + 4C\), then clears the byte at address 17 plus the byte read. Its source-byte address ranges from 1 through 4093.

Subsequent dependencies receive returned values shifted left by two, including one call receiving two identical shifted arguments. Another returned word is forwarded. After a further call, address 1 is read again and the byte at 17 plus that new sample is cleared.

A newly exposed secondary-register value is saved across later calls; designate it **D**. The next dependency receives three copies of D.

### First write to T and byte refreshes

A newly obtained result is forwarded together with the previously forwarded word shifted right by 23. Additional calls supply another result.

The routine reads address 1 again, retains that byte as an unsigned base, and clears the byte at base plus 17. After further calls—including a call with two identical left-shifted arguments—a new 32-bit result is obtained.

This result is written to T **before** being forwarded to a dependency. After another call, the routine reads the word at the retained base plus 116 and writes its low byte to base plus 1. The base is the saved byte sample; address 1 is not reread to obtain it.

A dependency receives D shifted left by 11. The next represented 64-bit result is split into low and high words. Another dependency receives those words, in that order, followed by the result previously written to T shifted right by 23.

Further calls supply an index whose four-times-scaled value becomes a new base. At this base, the routine performs two separate refreshes:

- Read the word at base plus 116 and write its low byte to base plus 1.
- After a call receiving the base and several additional calls, read the same word address again and write its current low byte to the same byte destination.

The second refresh is not a reuse of the first read. Intervening dependencies may alter its source.

The later calls also supply another index. Designate its left-by-two result **E**. The following four-argument call receives D shifted left by 12, E, E again, and that later index shifted left by 18.

### Saved-word propagation

Following further calls and result forwarding, a fresh index supplies a four-times-scaled base. The routine reads the full word at base plus 116, saves it as **W**, and writes W’s low byte to base plus 1. A dependency then receives the base.

After more calls, W is written to WideField(a newly returned index). The following dependency receives E shifted right by two and then left by 16. These operations must retain that order; the earlier scaling that produced E does not establish recoverability of discarded bits.

Further calls and forwarding supply another index. W is written to its Field destination, followed by a call receiving four times that index.

A later returned value is shifted left by two. The shifted value is written to address 20 plus E masked with a low-two-bits-clearing mask, then passed twice to a dependency.

After more calls, forwarding, and a new index result, a literal 32-bit zero is written to Field(that index). The next dependency receives four times that index.

### Paired-result stores and the second sample of S

A subsequent dependency returns a represented 64-bit value. Its low word, interpreted for the represented signed left shift by two, supplies a saved payload. Its high word supplies an index.

The payload is written to Field(the high-word index). S is then read again. This is a fresh sample, not reuse of the first sample.

The following dependency receives the payload, the high-word index, and two more copies of the payload.

Further calls produce another index. The same payload is written to Field(that index). The routine then reads a word at address 20 plus the fresh S sample; designate this word **V**. Only after that read does a dependency receive four times the latest index.

### Final byte updates and terminal operations

Another dependency returns a represented 64-bit result. Designate its unsigned low word **L** and its high word **H**.

Before the following dependency call, the routine reads a byte at address 1 plus W shifted right by 23. It writes V’s low byte to address 17 plus the byte read. The source-byte address ranges from 1 through 512.

The next dependency receives H three times and L shifted left by two as its fourth argument.

After further calls, another byte update reads address 1 plus L masked to its low 30 bits and shifted right by 21. It writes the low byte of V shifted right by 22 to address 17 plus the byte read. This source-byte address also ranges from 1 through 512.

Additional dependencies receive shifted results, including one call with two identical shifted arguments. Later, a no-explicit-argument call supplies a word that is forwarded, and the final result-producing dependency supplies an 8-bit value.

The terminal direct effects occur in this order:

1. Clear **one byte** through a pointer exposed by the final secondary-register result.
2. Read the represented three-byte shared value at address 9 and write T with that value in bits 8–31 and the final dependency byte in bits 0–7.
3. Write a 16-bit zero at the address obtained by masking L to its low 30 bits and shifting right by seven.
4. Attempt a call to address zero with three zero arguments.

The halfword address ranges from 0 through 8,388,607 and need not be aligned. Normal return occurs only if the address-zero call returns.

## Side effects

### Memory and ordering

Direct effects include word stores, byte stores, a halfword store, two samples of S, and two writes to T. No visible bounds or mapping checks protect the accesses.

Widths are significant. Clearing a byte does not clear adjacent bytes. Reading a word and writing its low byte transfers the numerical low eight bits, not necessarily the byte stored at the word’s lowest address.

Computed addresses may alias each other, S, T, address 1, or the three-byte value at address 9. Consequently:

- Earlier stores may influence later reads.
- Separate reads cannot be merged solely because their address expressions match.
- Saved scalar samples remain distinct from subsequent memory contents.
- The terminal pointer-byte clear can affect the later three-byte sample if they overlap.
- The halfword clear occurs after the final T write and may overlap it.

The contract describes directly represented ordering, not atomicity or hardware memory-order guarantees.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not executed tests. Each assumes execution reaches the relevant operations and that the required accesses and arithmetic are valid.

1. **Independent zero operands, preserved order.**  
   If the first S sample is 4,294,967,295, its right shift by 23 produces B = 511, and B shifted right by 23 produces zero. The earlier secondary-index Field store writes a literal zero. After the intervening calls, the later fresh-index Field store writes the zero derived from B. Both write 32-bit zero, but only the latter store uses the twice-shifted sample.

2. **Full-word preservation versus byte extraction.**  
   If W is 305,419,896, its low byte is 120. The immediate byte refresh writes 120. The later WideField and Field stores write the entire word 305,419,896, not merely 120.

3. **Repeated reads can produce different refreshes.**  
   If the first repeated base-plus-116 read has low byte 18, and an intervening dependency changes that source to have low byte 52, the two destination refreshes write 18 and then 52.

4. **Fresh address-1 samples.**  
   If two address-1 reads associated with byte clears produce 7 and 9, those clears target addresses 24 and 26. Reusing the earlier sample would change the represented behavior.

5. **Saved base survives dependency mutation.**  
   If the base retained before the first T write is 7, the subsequent refresh reads a word at address 123 and writes its low byte at address 8. A dependency changing address 1 in between does not change those saved-base addresses.

6. **Terminal shared-word construction.**  
   If the three-byte sample taken after the terminal pointer-byte clear contributes 0x123456, and the final dependency byte is 0xAB, the final T write has bit pattern 0x123456AB. This specifies bit placement, not memory byte order.

7. **Terminal failure is not rollback.**  
   If the final address-zero call cannot execute, normal return is not promised. Assuming preceding accesses completed, the pointer-byte clear, final T write, and halfword clear have already occurred in that order.
