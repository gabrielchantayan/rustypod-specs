### Arithmetic interpretation

Established transformations include:

- Selecting bits 10–19 by masking to twenty bits and logically shifting right ten, then moving that field to higher positions.
- Selecting low 24-, 22-, 20-, 19-, and smaller bit fields before shifting.
- Logically shifting combined 64-bit results right by 34, 36, or 44 and retaining the displayed word result.
- Moving individual retained bits into high-order positions.
- Retaining zeros and subsequently shifting them.

These are bit transformations, not established application-level scaling.

**Conditional word-bitvector model:** when a displayed operation is interpreted as a 32-bit bitvector operation, each intermediate result truncates modulo 2³² before subsequent operations. Successive left shifts whose total reaches or exceeds 32 can therefore discard every original bit even though each individual count is below 32.

This conditional model does not resolve all signed-looking operations in the executable. Signed shifts, overflowing multiplication or subtraction, and address arithmetic remain subject to documented_only machine semantics.

### Descriptive retained quantities

The following names describe dataflow, not object identities:

- **Final pair:** the low and high words of the last combined prefix result.
- **High-position base:** an earlier primary result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **High-bit value:** a retained primary result shifted left 25.
- **Shifted byte value:** a later primary result shifted left eight.
- **Small index:** a late primary result shifted left eight, masked to fifteen bits, then logically shifted right six.
- **Doubled-index base:** twice the small index.

Under the conditional word-bitvector model, the small index ranges from 0 through 0x1FC in multiples of four.

### Opening memory effects

- The low byte of that high word is stored at high-bit value plus 0x09.
- The high-position base is stored at final-pair low word multiplied by 256, plus 0x54.
- The earlier byte destination is overwritten with the high-bit value’s low byte.
- Only after those stores is the word at high-position base plus 0x14 cleared.

A byte at high-position base plus 0x05 is then read and saved. Before its unsigned value is stored as a word at high-position base plus 0x34, the word at doubled-index base plus 0x14 is cleared.

The word at high-position base plus 0x14 is subsequently read and retained. The doubled-index word at displacement 0x14 is cleared again. Two separate byte stores then place the retained word’s low byte at doubled-index base plus 0x15.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

### Extraction and intermediate truncation

A word 0xFFFFFFFF at a site selecting bits 10–19 supplies field 0x3FF. Moving that field left six positions produces 0xFFC0.

Under 32-bit bitvector semantics, shifting 0x12345678 left eight produces 0x34567800. A subsequent logical right shift eight produces 0x00345678, not the original value. Two successive left shifts by 28 discard all original bits after intermediate truncation.

### Clear before observation

Assume ordinary storage and no mutation of high-position base plus 0x14 between its clear and subsequent read. The read yields zero.

A byte store completed before the clear does not invalidate that outcome, even if it aliases the word. Relevant exceptions concern intervening mutation, external changes, or nonordinary storage.

### Publication capture

If the publication-base global contains 0x1000 at its specified capture event, subsequent publication accesses use that retained base. A later write to 0x60 or a later write aliasing the global does not cause a reload.

A computed write that changes the global before capture can affect the captured value. Moving capture after the 0x60 write violates the established order.

### Intervening 0x3A write

Assume valid ordinary memory, no external mutation, and a computed word access whose byte span overlaps absolute address 0x3A. The final-region read at final working base plus 0x14, or the read at derived output base plus 0x04, must observe its overlapping byte after the specified byte write.

For adjusted-scalar low bits equal to one, the stored byte is 0x38. An overlapping subsequent word read must incorporate that byte according to the applicable byte order. This example does not establish that every base assignment permits either overlap.

The retained final-working word used as the third transfer argument must come from this post-write observation. Capturing both computed words before writing 0x3A is not acceptable.

### Separate observations of 0x67

If the initial assignment places 0xAB at 0x67 and no intervening effect changes it, the 0x21 assignment and both later 0x67-sourced derived-output stores receive 0xAB.

If the three later read events instead observe 0x11, 0x22, and 0x33, their respective consumers receive those values. Reusing the initial 0xAB would violate the established read relationships.

### Access trace and failure
