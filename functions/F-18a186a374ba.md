# F-18a186a374ba

## Purpose

The supplied static representation does not establish a higher-level purpose. In particular, the repeated stores, self-references, small absolute addresses, and overlapping fields do not justify describing this operation as initialization, serialization, rendering, or container management.

## Behavior

### Initial object and descriptor mutations

The initial phase establishes self-references and writes incoming state into several address-bearing regions.

Before reading the linked base, the operation writes, in order:

- The low byte of the primary base at original third input plus `0x15`.
- The low byte of the auxiliary base at primary base plus `0x0D`.
- The primary base itself as a word at primary base plus `0x14`.
- The link value as a word at original third input plus `0x54`.

The linked base is then read. Its byte at `0x0D` receives the low byte of the auxiliary base, followed by a word self-reference at `0x14`.

The descriptor base is read only after those linked-region writes. The secondary region then receives the auxiliary low byte at `0x09` and the fourth input as a word at `0x44`.

The byte at descriptor base plus `0x05` is read twice, at distinct points. The first value is zero-extended and written as a word at auxiliary base plus `0x44`. Before that word write, first input plus `0x09` receives the secondary-base low byte.

Between the two descriptor-byte reads:

- Original third input plus `0x14` receives the fourth input.
- Secondary base plus `0x15` receives the descriptor-base low byte.
- Secondary base plus `0x44` is overwritten with the original third input.

The second descriptor-byte read supplies the selector. After this read, original third input plus `0x15` receives the fourth-input low byte, and the index word is read from `selector + 0x74`.

The operation subsequently writes the descriptor-base low byte to original third input plus `0x15`, repeats the link-value word store at `0x54`, writes the double-index low byte at first input plus `0x09`, and repeats the fourth-input word store at original third input plus `0x14`.

These repeated and overlapping writes are part of the represented behavior. They are not interchangeable with a single final-state assignment.

### Constant stores and scaled-address state

The next phase uses the index word and adjusted input as address-forming values.

It writes numeric `0x3A`:

- As one byte at the quad-index address.
- As one halfword at eight times the index word.
- As one word at quad index plus `0x20`.

The third input is adjusted downward by `0x96` after the first of those stores and before the halfword store. Subsequent references to its adjusted or scaled forms do not mean the original third-input object has moved.

Scaled base plus `0x20` receives four successive word values:

1. `0x20`.
2. `0x2F`.
3. The zero-extended byte read from scaled base plus `0x04`.
4. `0x20`.

The byte read occurs after the `0x2F` store and before the final `0x20` store. Aliasing or externally changing memory may therefore affect its value.

The anchor base is then loaded from its fixed global slot. After that read, the operation stores the word `0x20` at absolute address `0x60`.

### Repeated relationship stores

The operation next establishes relationships among the anchor base, scaled base, doubled link value, and octuple base.

An initial group writes:

- Quad index as a word at doubled link value plus `0x54`.
- The scaled-base low byte at anchor base plus `0x19`.
- The anchor-base low byte at scaled base plus `0x11`.
- Doubled link value as a word at anchor base plus `0x34`.

A long finite sequence of repeated groups follows. Each group writes, in this order:

1. Quad index as a word at octuple base plus `0x54`.
2. The scaled-base low byte at anchor base plus `0x19`.
3. The anchor-base low byte at scaled base plus `0x11`.
4. Octuple base as a word at anchor base plus `0x34`.

These are repeated stores to fixed derived destinations, not evidence of traversal through an array. No index advancement or intervening memory read is shown in this sequence.

The exact group count is `documented_only` in this contract. Final-state examples below do not validate trace multiplicity. An access-trace implementation must preserve the supplied finite sequence rather than silently collapse it.

### Snapshot capture and overlapping updates

After the repeated groups, four word reads occur in this order:

1. Absolute address `0xA9`.
2. Quad index plus `0x74`.
3. Double index plus `0x14`.
4. Scaled base plus `0x04`.

Between the second and third reads, absolute address `0x3A` receives the low byte of octuple base plus `0x30`.

These reads capture values used later; intervening stores do not cause those particular snapshots to be reloaded.

The next mutations include:

- Scaled-base low byte at double index plus `0x0D`.
- Byte `0x6C` at scaled base plus `0x08`.
- Anchor-base low byte at scaled base plus `0x0D` and then `0x09`.
- Double index as a word at double index plus `0x54`.
- Low byte of the `0xA9` snapshot at absolute address `0x67`.

A word is then read from double index plus `0x44`; call its value the **shift payload**. The byte at absolute address `0x67` is read and copied to absolute address `0x21`. This is a memory read, not merely reuse of the earlier snapshot.

The shift-payload low byte is written at scaled base plus `0x10`, followed by the full shift payload at anchor base plus `0x54`. Double index plus `0x01` receives the low byte of a signed right-shift expression whose count is the low byte of scaled base.

Counts outside the ordinary 32-bit shift range, and the exact treatment of negative values, remain unresolved. This operation cannot be specified as an unconditional logical shift.

The anchor word at `0x54` is then overwritten with scaled base.

Next, a word is read from scaled base plus `0x40`. The earlier snapshot from scaled base plus `0x04` is written at anchor base plus `0x74`; the newly read word is written at scaled base plus `0x10`. That word store replaces the earlier byte write at the same starting address and may overlap other fields.

The anchor receives double index at `0x44`. Further updates copy a fresh read of absolute byte `0x67` to scaled base plus `0x09`, write the scaled-base low byte at double index plus `0x09`, and write the original `0xA9` word snapshot at scaled base plus `0x44`.

### Final linking and indirect invocation

A word read from scaled base plus `0x54` supplies the **destination base**. After that read, another fresh read of absolute byte `0x67` is written to scaled base plus `0x09`.

The low byte of the earlier snapshot from quad index plus `0x74` is written at destination base plus `0x0D`. The saved word from double index plus `0x14` is written at double index plus `0x54`.

A word is then read from scaled base plus `0x14`; its low byte is retained for a later destination-field write. Before that later write:

- Double index plus `0x11` receives the low byte of the quad-index snapshot.
- Scaled base plus `0x09` receives the destination-base low byte.
- Scaled base plus `0x44` receives the destination base as a word.

A word read from double index plus `0x04` supplies the **final base**. A halfword `0x0065` is written at twice that final base. Only afterward is the word at final base plus `0x64` read, supplying the **final link**.

Destination base plus `0x11` receives the retained low byte from scaled base plus `0x14`; destination base plus `0x54` receives word `100`, decimal.

The last snapshot and linking actions are:

- Read a word from final base plus `0x74`.
- Read a word from double index plus `0x64`, supplying the **final target**.
- Write the final-base low byte at double index plus `0x09`.
- Write the final link at final target plus `0x54`.
- Write the final target as a word at twice the final link.

The unresolved indirect dependency is then invoked with these four 32-bit arguments, in order:

1. `0x72`.
2. Final link.
3. The earlier word snapshot from double index plus `0x14`.
4. The last word snapshot from final base plus `0x74`.

## Side effects

The direct effects are memory reads and writes. No allocation, deallocation, ownership transfer, locking, validation, or error reporting is established.

Address-derived regions may overlap each other or the fixed-address globals. Consequently:

- A nominal field write may alter a later pointer read.
- Byte stores may modify words subsequently read or overwritten.
- Cached snapshots may differ from current memory.
- Repeated stores may be observable even when their final values are identical.

Byte order determines which byte of an overlapping word is affected by a store at offset `0x15` relative to a word at `0x14`. No byte-order assumption is imposed here.

The direct body does not establish ownership of any region. The indirect dependency may introduce further effects; none are constrained by this evidence.

There is no visible rollback. If an access faults and the execution environment permits observation of preceding writes, those earlier mutations may remain. Exception delivery and recovery are unknown.

## Acceptance examples

This does not establish their final contents if subsequent accesses alias those locations.

An implementation that reuses the first byte for the lookup does not match this behavior.

### Scaled addressing

- Adjusted input: `0x0F6A`.
- Scaled base: `0x3DA8`.
- Octuple base: `0x7B50`.

The four successive word writes described at scaled base plus `0x20` therefore target `0x3DC8`. The original object remains addressed through `0x1000` during the earlier phase.

### Width-sensitive constant stores

- Byte address `0x400`, width 8 bits.

### Restricted shift example

This example does not resolve negative operands or counts of 32 or greater.

### Terminal arguments retain earlier snapshots
