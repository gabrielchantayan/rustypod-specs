# F-80bf11d66331

## Purpose

F-80bf11d66331 is represented as a three-argument, non-returning sequence of external calls, processor-state transfers, and one final 16-bit memory store. The supplied representation ends at an instruction-decoding failure, not at a normal return.

**Established static evidence:** the visible sequence combines external results with fixed-width shifts and masks, forwards selected values to subsequent calls, performs several coprocessor-directed transfers, and finally attempts to write the value 205 to a computed address.

**Inferred intent:** the sequence may manipulate low-level machine state. No application-level purpose, device protocol, resource lifecycle, or useful interpretation of its arguments is established.

**Contract boundary:** this document describes the visible operations conditionally on execution reaching them. It does not establish that the supplied representation accurately describes an executable instruction stream. In particular, its terminal decoding failure and unexplained incoming processor state prevent an unconditional claim that the complete sequence executes.

### 2. Early processor transfers and wide-result processing

- Its low word is supplied to a call.
- Its upper bits determine the destination of the final 16-bit store.

After the low-word call, another call produces a word. The next call receives that word and zero. One more call occurs before the store.

Let **T** be bits 52–63 of the final wide result, interpreted as an unsigned 12-bit number. The store address is:

**T + floor(T / 4)**

This follows from an unsigned 64-bit right shift by 41, followed by a right shift by 11 and an additional unsigned right shift by 2. The intermediate signed interpretation cannot make the address negative: the value before the signed shift is at most 23 bits wide.

Consequently:

- T ranges from 0 through 4,095.
- The destination address ranges from 0 through 5,118.
- The address calculation does not overflow 32 bits.
- The address can be odd.
- No destination validity or alignment check appears.
- The attempted store writes the 16-bit value 205.

The displayed sequence then reaches its bad-instruction termination marker.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance examples, not executed tests. They assume the relevant calls return and execution reaches the described operation.

1. **Third-input truncation.** A third input of 1 contributes 2,048 to the early call’s third argument. A third input with only bit 21 set contributes zero.

2. **High-word truncation.** In the early cumulative 28-bit high-word shift, a high word of 1 contributes bit 28. A high word of 16 contributes zero.

3. **Eighteen-bit mask.** A low word of 262,143 produces 4,194,288 at the masked four-argument call. A low word of 262,144 produces zero there.

4. **Zero propagation.** Arguments derived solely from the explicitly assigned local zero remain zero despite intervening calls in the supplied representation. This says nothing about dependency effects on global state.

5. **Signed shift does not restore a word.** If the retained word before the left shift by 5 is 134,217,727, the shifted bit pattern is interpreted as −32. An arithmetic right shift by 5 produces −1, not the original value.

6. **Final address zero.** If bits 52–63 of the final wide result are zero, the store attempts to write 205 at address zero. No protective check is visible.

7. **Final address boundary.** If those bits are all ones, the computed address is 5,118. Changing only the lower 52 bits leaves this address unchanged.

8. **Potential misalignment.** A value of 1 in bits 52–63 produces address 1. The representation contains no alignment correction; success versus fault depends on the machine and mapping.

9. **Call-order prerequisite.** The final store occurs only after the intervening low-word consumer, word producer, two-argument consumer, and final call return. The store is not moved ahead of these calls by this contract.
