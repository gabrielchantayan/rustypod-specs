# F-8b216ed7ec32

## Purpose

F-8b216ed7ec32 performs an ordered series of indirect invocations, transforms values obtained from those invocations, and modifies memory through dynamically constructed addresses and fixed low addresses. It ends with an unresolved indirect transfer carrying four explicit arguments.

No application-level purpose is established. Character-like constants do not establish a formatter, string buffer, encoding, or output stream. Address-like values and reciprocal writes do not establish an object model or ownership graph.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** behavior explicitly represented in the supplied material, subject to its unresolved execution semantics.
- **[INFERENCE]:** possible interpretations, not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior left unresolved rather than assigned a guessed meaning.

No runtime tests have been performed.

## Behavior

### Execution and arithmetic model

The represented path is straight-line: no explicit conditional branch, retry, or data-dependent loop appears. A nonreturning invocation or failed access can prevent all later effects.

Access widths are:

- Byte: 8 bits.
- Halfword: 16 bits.
- Word: 32 bits.
- Paired invocation result: represented as 64 bits.

Byte stores retain the low eight bits. Numeric byte loads are zero-extended. Splitting a paired result selects bits 0–31 and 32–63. Whole-result right shifts by 34, 36, and 44 operate on the represented 64-bit quantity, not on a 32-bit operand.

Unsigned right shifts introduce zeros. Masks select fields of several widths, including 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, and one bit. Mask placement and intermediate width matter. A 32-bit left shift followed by a right shift does not restore discarded high bits.

Several shifts and multiplications have signed 32-bit operands. Executable overflow, address wrap, and signed-shift semantics are **documented_only**. Statements below about retained bits or modular scaling are conditional on a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, not a claim of portable signed arithmetic.

### Ordered invocation phase

F-U01 receives the first input and the fourth input shifted left by seven. Two following invocations have no explicit arguments. The second of those produces a paired result; the next invocation receives its low word shifted left by eight, its high word unchanged, and its low word shifted left by twelve.

The long remaining invocation phase combines:

- Calls with no explicit arguments.
- Primary results shifted before forwarding.
- Low and high components of paired results.
- Secondary result-channel values with unresolved producing interfaces.
- Repeated arguments from one captured value.
- Explicit zeros, masks, and unsigned field extraction.
- Values retained across intervening invocations.

These calls are ordered effects, including those whose results are not explicitly consumed. A call with no explicit arguments is not established to consume no implicit state. Similar target-construction patterns do not prove target identity.

The complete per-invocation argument ledger and exact invocation count are **documented_only** in this contract; the phase must not be interpreted as an interchangeable collection of transformations.

### Address roles for the memory suffix

The following descriptive roles identify captured values without asserting structure types:

| Role | Derivation or capture |
|---|---|
| **Shifted base** | A captured primary low word shifted left by 25. |
| **Scaled base** | A later primary result multiplied by 2²⁵. |
| **Small index** | Another primary result shifted left by eight, masked to fifteen bits, then logically shifted right by six. |
| **Doubled index** | Twice the small index. |

Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, the small index ranges from 0 through 508 in multiples of four. The doubled index ranges from 0 through 1016 in multiples of eight. These are potentially low memory addresses, not established heap pointers.

All subsequent descriptions preserve distinctions between captured numeric values and fresh memory reads. Different roles may alias, including through partial overlap.

### Opening stores and early fixed-address chain

The opening suffix writes the shifted base as a word at offset 0x14 of the last pair’s high-word base. It writes that high word’s low byte at offset 0x09 of the shifted base, writes the scaled base as a word at offset 0x54 of the last pair’s low word multiplied by 256, then overwrites the same shifted-base byte with the shifted base’s own low byte.

The scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared. Its offset-0x05 byte is captured and later promoted into its offset-0x34 word, with a zero word store at doubled-index offset 0x14 between the byte capture and promotion. The scaled base’s offset-0x14 word is then read; call this the **cleared-field capture**. Another zero word store occurs at doubled-index offset 0x14, followed by two separate byte stores of the cleared-field capture at doubled-index offset 0x15.

The fixed-address chain then has these established dependencies:

- The byte at fixed address **0x05 is read first**, zero-extended, and retained as the **byte-derived base**.
- The byte at fixed address **0x01 is then written** with the low byte of the doubled index.
- The captured byte-derived base selects a byte destination at offset 0x15, which receives the small index’s low byte.
- Around a byte write at scaled-base offset 0x11 and its later replacement with zero, a word at cleared-field-capture offset 0x54 receives the forwarded value.
- The word at byte-derived-base offset 0x54 is captured as the **old-link value**, then replaced with the cleared-field capture.

The fixed 0x05 read is therefore neither a word load nor a value reloaded after the 0x01 write. Its captured byte determines later addresses even if intervening stores alter address 0x05.

Further dynamic updates use the old-link value, the word read at scaled-base offset 0x74, and words read at doubled-index offset 0x74. They include self-relative word writes at offset 0x24, repeated byte writes at offset 0x09, and replacement of offset-0x54 words.

The later fixed **word store at 0x54** receives twice the old-link value. Its placement is significant: a doubled-index offset-0x14 word receives a scaled invocation-derived value before that fixed store, and receives the old-link value twice afterward.

Next, two separate byte stores at doubled-index offset 0x15 receive the low byte of four times the old-link value. Only after those stores does fixed address **0x15 receive a byte**, the low byte of the doubled index. Dynamic word writes then continue through bases derived from four times the old-link value.

These operations may overlap the fixed locations. Neither the cleared-field capture nor any later field read can be replaced unconditionally with an earlier stored value.

### Dynamic relationship updates and constant writes

The middle suffix follows loaded words and zero-extended bytes to obtain further bases. Its observable patterns include:

- Reading an old offset-0x54 word before replacing it and retaining the old word as a base.
- Using an offset-0x74 word as another write destination’s base.
- Storing a base into a word field relative to itself.
- Loading a byte at offset 0x05 and storing its unsigned numeric value into an offset-0x44 word.
- Reading that offset-0x05 byte again after intervening writes.
- Distinct repeated stores at offsets 0x14, 0x15, 0x24, and 0x54.

The fresh byte reads are not necessarily equal. Offset usage does not establish one shared structure layout.

For the remaining phases, define the **constant base** as the word obtained through the later of those byte-selected offset-0x74 reads. The **final working base** is twice that captured word. The **adjusted base** is four times the old-link value minus 0x96. The **repeated scaled base** is four times the adjusted base.

The constant-write region stores numeric 0x3A:

- As a byte at four times the constant base.
- As a halfword at eight times the constant base.
- As a word at four times the constant base plus 0x20.

At repeated-scaled-base offset 0x20, four separate word stores receive 0x20, 0x2F, a freshly read and zero-extended byte from repeated-scaled-base offset 0x04, and 0x20, in that order.

### External-base capture and repeated updates

After those four word stores, the function **captures the separately stored 32-bit external base before writing the word 0x20 to fixed address 0x60**.

The captured external base is reused throughout the repeated region. It is not loaded after the 0x60 store and is not reloaded between groups. An overlap affecting its storage does not retroactively change the captured value.

Before the first three-store group, a word at eight-times-old-link-value offset 0x54 receives four times the constant base. Each group then has this ordered relationship:

1. External-base offset 0x19 receives the repeated scaled base’s low byte.
2. Repeated-scaled-base offset 0x11 receives the captured external base’s low byte.
3. External-base offset 0x34 receives an eightfold-scaled value.

The first group’s word value is eight times the old-link value. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted base. Between groups, a word at eight-times-adjusted-base offset 0x54 receives four times the constant base. The final group has no such following store.

This is static repetition, not a count-controlled runtime loop. Exact repetition count is **documented_only** here. Replacing the region with final assignments would lose established access multiplicity and order.

### Final fixed-global chain and saved values

Immediately after the repeated region:

- A **32-bit word at fixed address 0xA9 is captured** as the **global-word capture**.
- A word at four-times-constant-base offset 0x74 is captured as the **saved link**.
- Fixed address **0x3A receives a byte** formed from eight times the adjusted base plus 0x30, retaining the low eight bits.
- Only afterward are the **saved working word**, from final-working-base offset 0x14, and the **saved scaled word**, from repeated-scaled-base offset 0x04, captured, in that order.

Consequently, the 0x3A write can affect those subsequent captures if it aliases their storage.

Dynamic byte writes follow at final-working-base offset 0x0D and repeated-scaled-base offsets 0x08, 0x0D, and 0x09. The offset-0x08 byte receives 0x6C; the other values derive from captures made before this phase. A word at final-working-base offset 0x54 receives the final working base.

The next fixed-global relationship is:

- Fixed **0x67 receives the low byte of the global-word capture**.
- A dynamic word at final-working-base offset **0x44 is read**.
- Fixed **0x67 is freshly read as a byte**, and that read value is written as a byte to fixed **0x21**.

The dynamic word read separates the 0x67 write from its first read. The 0x21 value is not specified as an unconditional copy of the original low byte from 0xA9.

The captured dynamic offset-0x44 word supplies a byte at repeated-scaled-base offset 0x10 and a word at external-base offset 0x54. A byte at final-working-base offset 0x01 derives from a signed right shift of that word; its count is the low eight bits of the repeated scaled base. External-base offset 0x54 is then overwritten with the repeated scaled base.

A word from repeated-scaled-base offset 0x40 is captured. External-base offset 0x74 receives the saved scaled word, repeated-scaled-base offset 0x10 receives the captured offset-0x40 word, and external-base offset 0x44 receives the final working base.

Two additional fresh reads of fixed **0x67** occur later:

- The first supplies a byte store at repeated-scaled-base offset 0x09.
- A dynamic byte store at final-working-base offset 0x09, a word store of the global-word capture at repeated-scaled-base offset 0x44, and a word read at repeated-scaled-base offset 0x54 intervene.
- The second supplies another byte store at repeated-scaled-base offset 0x09.

Thus this final region contains **three distinct byte reads of 0x67**. They cannot be replaced by the captured low byte from 0xA9 or by one another. Aliasing and external modification remain unresolved.

### Remaining final updates and terminal transfer

The captured repeated-scaled-base offset-0x54 word selects another base. The saved link’s low byte is written at that base’s offset 0x0D and at final-working-base offset 0x11. The saved working word replaces final-working-base offset 0x54.

A fresh word from repeated-scaled-base offset 0x14 supplies a later byte store. Further updates replace repeated-scaled-base offset 0x09 and its offset-0x44 word with values derived from the selected base.

A word loaded from final-working-base offset 0x04 selects the **terminal source base**. A halfword at twice that base receives 0x65. Its offset-0x64 word is captured as the terminal second argument. After intervening byte and word stores, its offset-0x74 word is captured as the fourth argument.

The function then reads final-working-base offset 0x64 to select a final destination. After another byte update, that destination’s offset-0x54 word receives the captured second argument. A word at twice the captured second argument receives the final destination value immediately before F-U03.

F-U03 receives 0x72, the captured second argument, the saved working word, and the captured fourth argument, in that order.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial forwarding:** If the fourth input is 1 and the represented shift executes normally, F-U01 receives 128 as its second explicit argument. Its first argument preserves the first input’s 32-bit pattern.

2. **Paired result:** An early paired result with low word 1 and high word 2 produces the next explicit arguments 256, 2, and 4096.

3. **Early fixed-byte dependency:** If the fixed 0x05 read yields 0xFE, the byte-derived base is 254. The subsequent 0x01 write does not change that capture. Later accesses use offsets from 254, provided execution reaches them.

4. **Fixed-store widths:** Reaching the early fixed writes requires a 32-bit store at 0x54 and, later, an 8-bit store at 0x15—not two interchangeable word assignments.

5. **External-base timing:** If the external-base capture yields one address and the following 0x60 word store changes its backing storage, repeated groups still use the captured address.

6. **Saved-value timing:** If the 0x3A byte write overlaps the later saved-working-word location, that saved word reflects memory after the byte write, according to the environment’s byte order.

7. **Fresh global reads:** If the three reads of 0x67 observe 0x12, 0x34, and 0x56, fixed 0x21 receives 0x12, and the two later dynamic byte stores receive 0x34 and 0x56 respectively. Equality with the original global-word capture is not required.

8. **Index boundary:** Under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, the small index cannot be 1 or 511; its maximum is 508.

9. **Failure prefix:** If a dependency does not return, no subsequent represented access is required. Earlier effects have no represented rollback.

10. **Terminal captures:** Later modification of the saved working word’s original location does not replace the terminal third argument with a fresh load.
