## Behavior

### Initial publication and input capture

The first displayed memory read obtains the context address from incoming-context offset `0x04`.

The incoming callable target is then invoked with the word read from incoming-context offset `0x00`. Its ordinary result is not explicitly consumed. A secondary 32-bit result is stored at the address formed from the context address plus the third argument.

After that store, the routine reads an unsigned 16-bit value at third-argument offset `0x3c`. The value is zero-extended and retained as a later base address. It is not subsequently added to the third argument to obtain that base.

This ordering makes aliasing observable: the initial result store can affect the later 16-bit read. No non-aliasing guarantee is present.

### Conditional pair adjustment and early stores

An opaque call produces an original pair of 32-bit result words. The low word determines whether an additional call occurs.

Separately, the routine tests bit 29 of the unsigned 32-bit difference between the context address and 6. This is a bit test, not an unrestricted ordering comparison.

If the original low word is zero:

- An additional opaque call produces a replacement pair.
- The replacement low word remains selected.
- The replacement high word is replaced by a 32-bit memory load.
- That load’s address is the replacement high word minus the context address, minus an adjustment, plus `0x74`.
- The adjustment is zero when the earlier bit test is set and one otherwise.

An opaque call then receives the selected low and high words as two arguments. Its computed target differs between the zero and nonzero paths.

Two stores follow, in order:

1. A 16-bit store at offset `0x32` from this last call’s result. **The context address is reduced by 6 before multiplication by 8.** The stored bit pattern is the low 16 bits of  
   \[
   8 \times (\text{context address} - 6).
   \]
   It is not the low 16 bits of \(8 \times \text{context address} - 6\). The represented intermediate narrowing does not change this low-16-bit relationship under the stated width interpretation.
2. An 8-bit store at offset `0x09` from the **original** pair’s low word. Its value comes from a 32-bit load at original-low-word offset `0x70`: retain that load’s low byte and clear every bit set in the context address’s low byte.

The second operation continues to use the original low word even when a replacement pair was obtained. On the zero path, it therefore reads a word at absolute address `0x70` and writes a byte at absolute address `0x09`.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional analytical cases, not executed tests. Each assumes execution reaches the operation, required memory accesses are permitted, and no unspecified effect invalidates the stated conditions.

1. **Subtraction precedes multiplication.**  
   A context address of 6 produces a zero halfword at the early result-relative offset `0x32`. It does not produce 42. A context address of 7 produces 8. Under the represented unsigned subtraction and low-16-bit storage, a context address of zero produces `0xffd0`.

2. **Initial aliasing affects the later input.**  
   If the first result store overlaps the 16-bit input at third-argument offset `0x3c`, that input is read after the store, subject to platform memory semantics.

3. **The zero path retains the original byte-operation base.**  
   An original paired-result low word of zero triggers the replacement-pair call. The later byte operation nevertheless reads a word at `0x70` and writes a byte at `0x09`.

4. **Context writes overlap.**  
   On a little-endian platform, a word of `0x11223344` written at context offset `0x0c`, followed by a byte of `0xaa` at offset `0x0d`, leaves `0x1122aa44`, absent another change.

5. **Masked extraction remains single-bit.**  
   At a bit-24 stage, `0x01000000` supplies 1, while `0x02000000` supplies 0.

6. **Byte selection changes the address.**  
   At stage 7, an extracted bit of 1 selects the byte at `0x11`. If that byte is `0xa5`, the supplied value is `0xa5`, regardless of the byte at `0x10`.

7. **Unsigned subtraction is not ordinary ordering.**  
   At stage 8, a high word of zero supplies 1. A high word of `0xffffffff` also supplies 1 because subtraction produces `0xfffffff8`.

8. **Late helper input is a nibble.**  
   A late result of `0xf1234567` causes F-2cc1255261f3 to receive 15.

9. **The final comparison is unsigned.**  
   Branch-control value 8 enters the tail and targets its 16-bit absolute store at `0x30`. Values 9 and `0xffffffff` skip the tail.

10. **Normal return is not established.**  
    Both displayed endpoints terminate at invalid-instruction markers. Silently returning after the final represented operation would not preserve that stated endpoint behavior.
