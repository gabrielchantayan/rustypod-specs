### Final output value

After normal dependency return, the wrapper derives a 16-bit quantity from the post-call working value and stores it at the original output address.

Under a 32-bit bit-vector interpretation, the result is the window consisting of working-value bits 1 through 16, inclusive. Bit numbering starts at zero for the least significant bit:

- Bit 0 has no effect.
- Bit 1 becomes result bit 0.
- Bit 16 becomes result bit 15.
- Bits 17 through 31 have no effect.

For an unsigned interpretation \(W\) of the post-call 32-bit pattern, this numerical relationship is:

\[
P=\left\lfloor\frac{W\bmod 131072}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

This describes a 16-bit extraction, not saturation or a range-checked conversion. It also differs from retaining an unrestricted 32-bit quotient.

That relationship requires an arithmetic qualification. The supplied expression performs a signed left shift before its unsigned operation. With 32-bit signed integers, the left shift is defined under ordinary signed-language rules only for working values from 0 through 65,535 inclusive. Within that range, the final numerical value is the integer quotient on division by two, from 0 through 32,767.

For negative values or values above 65,535, portable signed-language semantics do not establish the bit-window result. In particular, a working value of 65,536 already exceeds the defined signed-shift range. Results for such patterns remain conditional on a confirmed machine-level interpretation; they are not unconditional portable guarantees.

The narrowing step also uses a signed 16-bit representation before storage. Where the bit-vector result has its highest bit set, interpreting that conversion as preservation of the same 16 bits requires the corresponding target conversion behavior. The supplied fragment alone is insufficient to establish a portable rule for every such case.

The final store replaces the previous two-byte value. The wrapper does not visibly read or merge the old destination contents. The stored numerical quantity does not establish a portable byte order: the two bytes follow the target representation.
