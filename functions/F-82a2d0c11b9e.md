# F-82a2d0c11b9e

## Purpose

This contract describes an ordered interaction with external executable destinations and memory. Incoming state and dependency-produced information determine byte contents, numerical memory destinations, and arguments to later dependencies. Additional effects include two 16-bit reads, an assignment to distinguished global storage, and a 32-bit write through a retained address. The final interaction obtains an executable destination and invokes it.

No application-level purpose is established. In particular, the evidence does not establish initialization, decoding, allocation, release, or a reset operation.

The contract is conditional on reaching each described boundary with supported arithmetic, conversions, storage, and executable destinations. It does not promise successful completion. A right shift by 32 applied to a represented 32-bit quantity leaves complete portable execution unresolved.

## Interface

The represented interface has no declared parameters and no value-bearing return. Nevertheless, it consumes three kinds of incoming state:

| Input role | Represented width | Behavioral use |

## Behavior

### Observable ordering and content groups

The byte writes form six consecutive content groups:

| Group | Write count | Content source |
|---|---:|---|
| Entry content | 21 | Original incoming eight-bit content |
| Zero | 6 | Zero |
| Address-derived content | 1 | Character conversion of the numerical destination |
| Capture byte | 13 | A retained selection from the unsigned capture |
| Capture field | 1 | Character conversion of a separate capture selection |
| Three-bit content | 21 | A retained value from zero through seven |

Numbers identifying writes below are chronological positions within their group, not buffer indices.

Dependency invocations separate every pair of byte writes. Consequently, these groups are not uninterrupted fills or copies. Dependencies can observe or modify memory between writes. None of the byte replacements requires reading the previous byte.

The usual relationship between a byte write and its immediate consumer is:

- a newly obtained signed 32-bit observation determines a numerical destination eight times that observation;
- the next invocation receives two equal arguments, each 16,384 times the same observation.

“Usual relationship” refers only to these destination and argument relationships. It does not identify the dependency, constrain its effects, or eliminate intervening interactions elsewhere. Separate ordinary occurrences use independently obtained observations.

### Entry-content and zero groups

All 21 entry-content writes use the original eight-bit input. Later dependency results do not replace this retained content.

Four entry-content writes depart from the usual relationship:

- The first has the usual destination, but its immediate consumer receives only one argument, four times its controlling observation.
- The fifth uses a destination twice its controlling observation. Its immediate consumer receives two equal arguments, each 4,096 times that observation.
- The sixth has the usual destination. Its consumer receives, in order, the usual scaled argument twice, 1,024 times the incoming auxiliary information, and the usual scaled argument again.
- The twenty-first has the usual destination, but its consumer receives four times its controlling observation twice.

The sixth write’s four-argument consumer produces separately associated signed information. A later sole-argument consumer receives eight times that associated information. Neither the ordinary result nor the incoming auxiliary information substitutes for this associated value.

The usual scaled argument associated with entry-content write 17 is retained beyond that interaction. This contract calls it the **retained basis**. Its provenance remains that earlier observation, despite later acquisitions.

All six zero writes follow the usual relationship.

### Address-derived content

The address-derived write has the usual destination. Its content is the character conversion of that destination’s numerical value, not a transformation of the old memory content.

Its immediate consumer receives three arguments: the two usual equal arguments, followed by the numerical destination.

For a numerical value outside the character type’s representable range, the stored representation is **documented_only**. A universal rule of retaining the lowest eight bits is not established for this character conversion.

### Capture selections and retention

An unsigned 32-bit dependency result supplies two independent selections:

- The **capture byte** is eight times the value of numerical bits 0–4. Its possible values are the multiples of eight from zero through 248.
- The **capture field** is the value of numerical bits 19–28. Before character conversion, its range is zero through 1,023.

Bit zero is the least significant numerical bit. These selections do not depend on memory byte order.

All 13 capture-byte writes use the same retained byte. Their destination and consumer requirements vary:

- The first uses the capture scaled by 16 for its destination and by 32,768 for each of two equal consumer arguments. Both scalings are explicitly unsigned 32-bit operations, with modular reduction.
- The second uses the retained basis scaled by 65,536 for its destination and by 134,217,728 for each equal argument.
- The third, fourth, and fifth each use a fresh signed observation, scaled by 8,192 for the destination and by 16,777,216 for each equal argument.
- The sixth uses a **derived word** obtained from the retained basis scaled by 32,768. Its destination is 4,096 times that signed 32-bit word; its equal arguments are each 8,388,608 times the word.
- The seventh uses a fresh signed observation scaled by 8,192 for the destination and has the read-dependent consumer described below.
- The eighth through twelfth follow the usual relationship.
- The thirteenth has the usual destination, but its equal consumer arguments are each four times its controlling observation.

Only the first write’s stated scalings have established unsigned wraparound. The other relationships remain conditional on supported signed operations.

After the sixth write’s immediate consumer, the highest byte of the derived word’s unsigned 32-bit interpretation is retained. Its highest three bits supply the later three-bit content: equivalently, numerical bits 29–31 of that unsigned interpretation. This selection can represent every value from zero through seven.

The capture field is written after all 13 capture-byte writes. Its destination follows the usual relationship; its content undergoes character conversion. Its consumer receives four times that boundary’s signed observation twice. Conversion of field values outside the character range remains **documented_only**.

Capture bits 29–31 affect none of the specified capture-derived contents, numerical destinations, or explicit arguments. This does not constrain hidden dependency behavior. The first capture-byte destination depends only on capture bits 0–27, and its explicit arguments depend only on bits 0–16.

### Wider reads and global state

At capture-byte write seven, the byte write precedes a 16-bit read without an intervening invocation. The same signed observation controls both locations:

- the byte destination is 8,192 times the observation;
- the read location is 4,096 times the observation, plus 56.

The next invocation receives 4,096 times the observation first and the actual 16-bit memory observation second. The read location is not generally the byte destination plus 56.

After further dependency interactions, but before capture-byte write eight, distinguished global storage is assigned from the retained capture byte. The assignment width and conversion are unresolved. The immediately following invocation receives zero twice, independently of the assigned value.

The second 16-bit read follows three-bit write 15 without an intervening invocation. Its numerical location is 128 times the signed 32-bit conversion of the retained original address, plus 32. Its consumer receives only the actual read observation.

Both reads observe memory at their stated boundaries, not an earlier snapshot. Prior overlapping writes and dependency effects can change their observations. Interpretation or extension of the 16-bit information at invocation boundaries is **documented_only**.

### Three-bit content and the retained address

All 21 writes in the final byte group carry the same selected value from zero through seven. Re-retaining that selected value does not select another field or shift it down again.

Most follow the usual relationship. The exceptions are:

- Write two depends on the unresolved full-width shift described below.
- Write four uses separately associated address information rather than an ordinary signed result.
- Writes nine, sixteen, and twenty have the usual destinations, but their immediate consumers have no explicit arguments.
- Write eleven uses separately retained signed information.
- Write fifteen is followed by the second 16-bit read and its sole-argument consumer.
- Write seventeen is followed by the 32-bit write and a three-argument consumer.

For write two, signed 32-bit information undergoes fourfold left scaling and then a right shift by 32. The resulting quantity controls a destination twice its value and two equal arguments each 4,096 times its value. The represented operand width does not support a portable interpretation of that shift count. Widening the operand, masking the count, choosing zero, or predicting a fault would add unsupported behavior. A zero operand does not resolve this uncertainty.

For write four, separately associated address information undergoes signed 32-bit numerical conversion. The converted value determines a destination scaled by 256 and equal arguments scaled by 524,288. The **original address** is retained separately for later access. It is not replaced by the converted number or the scaled destination.

For write eleven, separately retained signed information determines a destination scaled by 2,048 and equal arguments scaled by 4,194,304.

At write seventeen, the **payload** is four times that boundary’s signed observation, where supported. The byte write is followed by a 32-bit payload write at the retained original address, with no invocation between them. The next invocation receives three equal copies of the payload. Four three-bit writes remain afterward.

The payload destination is neither write four’s scaled destination nor the second-read location. If the byte and payload writes overlap, the payload write is the later direct modification of the overlapping storage. Its constituent bytes remain dependent on multibyte representation.
