## Behavior

### Arithmetic obligations

Established masks select low fields of 24, 22, 21, 20, 19, 18, and 17 bits, together with smaller fields including 0x3FFF, 0x1FFF, 0xFFF, and 0x7FF.

Masking precedes repositioning where represented. Selecting the low 20 bits, shifting right by ten, then left by six selects original bits 10–19 and places them in positions 6–15. Bits outside that selected field do not contribute.

Explicit unsigned right shifts are logical. Unsigned 64-bit shifts by 34, 36, and 44 yield the high word shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 respectively when narrowed to a word.

**Conditional arithmetic model:** under 32-bit bit-vector semantics, left shifts discard bits above bit 31. Consequently, successive left shifts by eight and 24 produce zero; two successive shifts by 28 produce zero; and a word shifted left by 16 has a zero low halfword. These conclusions do not resolve signed-overflow or signed-left-shift semantics in the representation.

Address arithmetic, multiplication, and subtraction below retain their represented 32-bit provenance. They must not silently become unlimited-precision arithmetic. Wraparound behavior remains unresolved where target semantics are required.

### Initial direct mutation and fixed-storage capture

The following descriptive names identify captured values, not declared objects:

- **Retained seed:** a low paired-result word shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled data:** a later primary result shifted left by eight.
- **Earlier result:** another retained primary result.
- **Wide base:** the earlier result multiplied by 0x02000000.
- **Narrow base:** a later word shifted left by eight, masked with 0x7FFF, then logically shifted right by six.
- **Doubled base:** twice the narrow base.

Under the bit-vector interpretation, the narrow base is a multiple of four from zero through 508; the doubled base is a multiple of eight from zero through 1016.

Direct mutation begins after the final paired result. Its high word supplies an address for a +0x14 word store of the retained seed and supplies the byte first written at retained-seed +9. Between that byte write and its overwrite with the seed’s low byte, the function stores the wide base at +0x54 from the final low word multiplied by 256.

The wide-base +0x14 word is then cleared, and the wide-base +5 byte is captured. Next, doubled-base +0x14 is cleared, the captured byte is zero-extended into wide-base +0x34, and wide-base +0x14 is read back as the **captured link**. The doubled-base +0x14 word is cleared again. Two successive byte stores to doubled-base +0x15 receive the captured link’s low byte.

**Only after those two byte stores is the fixed byte at 0x05 read and zero-extended.** This becomes the **fixed-byte base** used in subsequent addressing. The fixed byte at 0x01 is then written with the doubled base’s low byte. Thus a dynamic store affecting 0x05 can influence the captured base, while the later 0x01 write cannot retroactively change it.

Next, fixed-byte-base +0x15 receives the narrow base’s low byte. Wide-base +0x11 receives the scaled data’s low byte; captured-link +0x54 receives the full scaled data; and wide-base +0x11 is cleared. The then-current word at fixed-byte-base +0x54 is captured as the **replacement target**, before that location is replaced with the captured link.

These read-backs and captures cannot be replaced with presumed values without establishing alias and memory semantics.

### Interlinked updates and the fixed 0x54 boundary

Doubled-base +0x34 receives the narrow base. A word read at wide-base +0x74 supplies a new base whose +0x54 word first receives the earlier result shifted left by 26.

Another nested operation reads doubled-base +0x74 separately for a destination base and for stored data. Both reads remain distinct; their relative evaluation within that single nested operation is not independently resolved.

The replacement target is written to its own +0x24 word, and its low byte is written at +9 of the newly loaded base. That base’s +0x54 word is then replaced with scaled data. The self-reference and byte publication are repeated.

The next alias-sensitive boundary is established precisely:

- Doubled-base +0x14 receives the earlier result shifted left by 26.
- The fixed 32-bit location 0x54 receives the replacement target shifted left by one.
- Doubled-base +0x14 receives the replacement target twice.

The fixed write therefore occurs between writes to the dynamic +0x14 location, not before or after the whole group.

Two byte stores to doubled-base +0x15 then receive the low byte of four times the replacement target. **After both stores**, the fixed byte at 0x15 receives the doubled base’s low byte. A scaled-data word store at four-times-replacement-target +0x54 follows. The fixed 0x15 write is therefore an ordered derived-value write, not an unrelated constant update.

The remaining interlinked region uses four and eight times the replacement target as bases or data. It includes self-referential +0x14 words, +0x0D byte stores, and +0x44 and +0x54 word replacements. Bytes read at +5 are zero-extended into further address calculations. The function writes numeric 0x3A as a byte at the publication payload, as a halfword at the related eightfold address, and as a word at publication-payload +0x20. These are separate accesses, not an established string.
