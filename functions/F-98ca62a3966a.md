# F-98ca62a3966a

## Purpose

F-98ca62a3966a performs an ordered sequence of indirect calls, transforms captured result quantities, mutates memory through calculated and fixed addresses, and finishes with a four-argument indirect transfer.

**Established static evidence:** the represented interface has no explicit arguments or result value. Calls, argument bit patterns, memory-access widths, capture points, and access order constitute its observable behavior. Repeated stores and intervening reads matter independently of final memory contents.

**Inferred intent:** some word stores resemble publication of relationships between records. Numeric constants also admit character interpretations. Neither observation establishes an application purpose, a record invariant, or text output.

This is a static behavioral contract, not an execution report. Unresolved details and portions not fully enumerated are marked **documented_only**. No runtime tests have been performed.

## Interface

### Inputs and outputs

No explicit parameters are represented. Implicit inputs include:

- Entry machine state used to form the opening indirect-call target.
- An incoming register-sized quantity used in an early argument shifted left by 24 bits.
- Primary, combined, and secondary result channels from indirect calls.

- The target of the final indirect transfer, whose origin is unresolved.

The absence of represented parameters does not establish that arbitrary entry state is valid.

No explicit result value is represented. Outputs consist of calls and their argument values, direct memory effects, effects performed by dependencies, and the final transfer. The function reaches its represented return only if that transfer returns.

## Behavior

### Indirect-call phase

The opening target is formed from incoming machine state plus displacement 0xFFE. Most subsequent targets use embedded anchors plus that displacement. Their resolved identities and possible equivalence are unknown.

The opening call produces a combined result. Its immediate consumer receives three represented arguments, in order:

- Zero.
- The high 32-bit word.
- The low 32-bit word shifted left by eight bits.

After an intervening call, another combined-result producer is followed by a consumer receiving the shifted low word, high word, zero, and zero.

The remaining phase contains many ordered call occurrences with zero through four displayed arguments. Observable argument relationships include equal arguments derived from one captured quantity, explicit zeros, primary and secondary results, masks, shifts, and captured values retained across intervening calls.

A call whose primary result is unused is not removable: a secondary result channel may supply later arguments. A call with no displayed arguments is not established to lack implicit inputs or side effects.

Representative field selections include bits 10–19 and bits 4–16. Unsigned combined-result shifts by 34, 36, and 44 bits select the high word shifted right by 2, 4, and 12 bits respectively; they do not shift the low word.

The complete occurrence-by-occurrence call schedule is **documented_only** in this contract. These descriptions do not authorize replacing the sequence with fewer generic calls.

### Address quantities entering direct mutation

Near the end of the call phase, captures supply:

- A **scaled base**, formed by multiplying a result by 2²⁵.
- A **shifted base**, formed from another result shifted left by 25 bits.
- A quantity shifted left by eight bits.
- A restricted unsigned index.
- A final combined result supplying further address components.

The restricted index is obtained by shifting a result left by eight bits, retaining fifteen low bits, then shifting right by six. Its possible values are multiples of four from 0 through 508. Its doubled value is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

The first direct effects preserve the following ordering:

- A word store at final-high-word plus 0x14 precedes a byte store at shifted-base plus 0x09.
- A word store at final-low-word times 256 plus 0x54 intervenes before another byte store at shifted-base plus 0x09.
- Those two byte stores use different captured sources.
- A zero word store at scaled-base plus 0x14 precedes a byte read at scaled-base plus 0x05.
- A zero word store at doubled-index plus 0x14 intervenes before the read byte is zero-extended into a word store at scaled-base plus 0x34.
- A word read at scaled-base plus 0x14 follows.

That last read cannot unconditionally be replaced with zero: intervening accesses may alias the location. Subsequent accesses include another zero store at doubled-index plus 0x14 and two byte stores at doubled-index plus 0x15.

### Constants and publication

Using the loaded link, three accesses store numeric value 0x3A:

- A byte at four times the loaded link.
- A halfword at eight times the loaded link.
- A word at four times the loaded link plus 0x20.

The later publication phase uses these descriptive quantities:

| Quantity | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Adjusted quantity | Four times captured root, minus 0x96 |
| Working base | Four times adjusted quantity |
| Publication value | Four times loaded link |
| Record base | Twice loaded link |

These relationships retain the arithmetic qualifications stated above.

At working-base plus 0x20, successive word stores supply 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from working-base plus 0x04, and 0x20 again. The byte is read after the second store and before the third.

After the fourth store, a word is captured from fixed publication-anchor storage. This **publication anchor** is captured before the fixed word store of 0x20 at address 0x60. Publication uses the captured anchor rather than an implied reload.

Each publication repetition has four ordered stores:

| Effect | First repetition | Later repetitions |
|---|---|---|
| Word containing publication value | Destination: eight times captured root plus 0x54 | Destination: eight times adjusted quantity plus 0x54 |
| Byte at publication-anchor plus 0x19 | Low byte of working base | Same captured byte |
| Byte at working-base plus 0x11 | Low byte of publication anchor | Same captured byte |
| Word at publication-anchor plus 0x34 | Eight times captured root | Eight times adjusted quantity |

The first repetition therefore differs in both a destination address and a stored word value.

No advancing index is represented in this group. It does not establish traversal of successive records. Repeated stores remain distinct effects even when addresses and values coincide. The exact repetition count is not enumerated here and remains **documented_only**; one repetition is not an equivalent replacement.

### Final-phase captures and fixed-byte observations

Immediately after publication, the function captures the word at fixed address 0xA9. Later uses refer to this captured value, even if subsequent stores alter that storage.

A word read at publication-value plus 0x74 follows. The byte at fixed address 0x3A then receives the low byte of eight times the adjusted quantity plus 0x30. This is not an unconditional constant.

Next, the function captures:

- A word at record-base plus 0x14, called the **saved record word**.
- A word at working-base plus 0x04.

The saved record word originates at offset 0x14, not offset 0x54.

Before publishing the low byte of the captured 0xA9 word to address 0x67, the function writes byte fields at record-base plus 0x0D and working-base plus 0x08, 0x0D, and 0x09. Working-base plus 0x08 receives 0x6C. A word at record-base plus 0x54 receives the record base itself.

There are three later, distinct observations of fixed byte address 0x67:

1. After the write to 0x67, a word is read at record-base plus 0x44. The current byte at 0x67 then supplies the byte written to fixed address 0x21.
2. After intervening accesses, including word stores at working-base plus 0x10 and publication-anchor plus 0x44, the current byte at 0x67 supplies a byte store at working-base plus 0x09.
3. After a byte store at record-base plus 0x09, a word store of the captured 0xA9 word at working-base plus 0x44, and a word read at working-base plus 0x54, address 0x67 is read again for another byte store at working-base plus 0x09.

Aliasing prevents treating these reads as unconditional copies of the byte originally written to 0x67. The two working-base-plus-0x09 stores are followed later by another store there using a different captured source.

The word stored at working-base plus 0x10 comes from a preceding word read at working-base plus 0x40. It follows an earlier byte store at offset 0x10 and can overwrite that byte and the adjacent offset-0x11 byte.

A byte store at record-base plus 0x01 uses a signed-looking right shift of the loaded offset-0x44 word. Its count is the low eight bits of the working base. Counts beyond 31 are possible; count handling and signed right-shift semantics are **documented_only**.

### Completion and saved-value transfer

After the third 0x67 observation, a byte derived from the earlier publication-value-plus-0x74 read is written at offset 0x0D of the base loaded from working-base plus 0x54.

The function then writes the **saved record word to record-base plus 0x54**. This is a 32-bit transfer of a value previously captured from **record-base plus 0x14**. The same captured record base supplies both relative addresses.

This is not established restoration of a previous offset-0x54 value. The earlier capture did not read offset 0x54, and the destination had meanwhile received the record base itself. Nor does this operation reread offset 0x14: later changes to the source storage do not change the saved value used here.

After this write, a word is read at working-base plus 0x14. A byte derived from the earlier publication-value-plus-0x74 read is then written at record-base plus 0x11. Further stores publish the captured working-base-plus-0x54 value at working-base plus 0x09 and plus 0x44.

Near completion:

- A word read at record-base plus 0x04 supplies another base and the destination component for a halfword store of 0x65 at twice that value.
- A word is read at that newly loaded base plus 0x64.
- A byte store at offset 0x11 of the base captured from working-base plus 0x54 precedes a word store of decimal 100 at offset 0x54 of that same base.
- A word is read at the offset-0x04-derived base plus 0x74.
- A separate word read at record-base plus 0x64 supplies a destination base for a later offset-0x54 word store.
- A byte at record-base plus 0x09 receives the low byte of the offset-0x04-derived base.
- The later offset-0x54 word store receives the earlier offset-0x64-loaded word.
- A final word store writes the separately loaded destination base at twice that earlier offset-0x64-loaded word.

The final indirect transfer receives, in order:

1. 0x72.
2. The word loaded at offset 0x64 from the offset-0x04-derived base.
3. The saved record word captured at record-base plus 0x14.
4. The late word loaded at offset 0x74 from the offset-0x04-derived base.

The third argument is the same captured quantity used by the offset-0x14-to-offset-0x54 write. Its application meaning is unknown.

## Side effects

Direct effects include calculated-address accesses and fixed-location accesses. Fixed writes include bytes at 0x01, 0x15, 0x21, 0x3A, and 0x67, and words at 0x54 and 0x60.

Earlier fixed effects include:

- Address 0x01 receives the low byte of the doubled restricted index after the fixed byte read at 0x05.
- Address 0x54 receives the captured root shifted left by one.
- Address 0x15 later receives the low byte of the doubled restricted index.
- Address 0x60 receives word value 0x20 after publication-anchor capture.

These locations are not automatically classified as invalid memory. Their mapping, device behavior, and correspondence to a concrete address space are unresolved.

No allocation, release, reference acquisition, or ownership transfer is established. Address-shaped stored values do not prove ownership or lifetime guarantees. Dependencies are not assumed to preserve globals, avoid allocation, retain arguments, or release memory.

A fault or nonreturning dependency may leave earlier effects in place. No atomicity, transaction boundary, idempotence, reentrancy, or thread-safety guarantee is established.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static-review examples, not executed tests. All assume execution reaches the relevant accesses and those accesses are valid.

1. **Opening split.** If F-opaque-001 supplies high word 0x12345678 and low word 0xAB, F-opaque-002 receives 0, 0x12345678, and 0xAB00.

2. **Restricted index.** A captured result of 0x7F produces index 508 and doubled value 1016. Index 511 does not match the represented extraction.

3. **Unsigned promotion.** A byte read of 0xE7 supplies word value 0x000000E7 to its directly dependent unsigned word store.

4. **First publication differs twice.** With captured root 0x40, the adjusted quantity is 0x6A. The first publication-value word store targets 0x254; later ones target 0x3A4. Corresponding publication-anchor-plus-0x34 values are 0x200 and 0x350.

5. **Captured publication anchor.** If the fixed 0x60 write changes publication-anchor storage through aliasing, publication still uses the anchor captured before that write.

6. **Dependent fixed byte.** With adjusted quantity 0x6A, the byte written at 0x3A is 0x80, the low byte of 0x350 plus 0x30.

7. **Distinct fixed-byte observations.** If an intervening aliased store changes address 0x67, a later working-base-plus-0x09 store uses the byte present at its own read point. The three observations need not agree.

8. **Overlapping widths.** If working-base plus 0x40 supplies word 0x11223344, that word is supplied to the later store at working-base plus 0x10. Preserving the earlier offset-0x10 byte instead would omit the word overwrite. Individual resulting bytes depend on byte order.

9. **Offset-0x14-to-offset-0x54 transfer.** Suppose record base is 0x2000 and the capture at 0x2014 yields 0x11223344, while 0x2054 initially contains 0x55667788. The later saved-value write supplies 0x11223344 to 0x2054, not its initial value. This does not establish restoration.

10. **Capture survives source mutation.** In the preceding example, if an intervening alias changes 0x2014 after capture, the later write to 0x2054 and third final-transfer argument still use 0x11223344. Neither use implies a fresh source read.

11. **Observable overwrite sequence.** Storage whose individual writes are observable must distinguish the successive working-base-plus-0x20 values 0x20, 0x2F, the read-derived byte value, and 0x20. Final contents alone are insufficient.

12. **Interrupted execution.** A nonreturning dependency prevents subsequent accesses and the final transfer. Earlier effects are not promised to be undone.
