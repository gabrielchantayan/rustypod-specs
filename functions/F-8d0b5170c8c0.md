# F-8d0b5170c8c0

## Purpose

F-8d0b5170c8c0 performs an ordered sequence of indirect calls, transforms selected call results and incoming machine values, modifies storage through fixed and derived addresses, and finishes with an unresolved indirect transfer.

No application-level purpose is established. Character-like constants do not establish text output. Repeated offsets do not establish a collection, object schema, or ownership relationship.

This contract distinguishes:

- **Established static evidence:** operations, widths, conversions, and ordering explicitly supported by the supplied representation.
- **[INFERENCE]:** interpretations that are not acceptance requirements.
- **documented_only:** behavior for which the available evidence does not support a complete executable rule.

The three explicit inputs are insufficient to determine execution. Incoming machine state, unknown dependencies, global storage, memory contents, and aliasing also matter.

### Early clear and reread

An early region clears the word at scaled base + 0x14, reads an unsigned byte at scaled base + 5, clears the word at small base + 0x14, stores the widened byte as a word at scaled base + 0x34, and then reads the word at scaled base + 0x14.

Under ordinary byte-addressed memory, valid accesses, the stated 32-bit address model, and no external interference, **that final read returns zero**:

- The intervening small-base clear writes only zero, even if it overlaps the cleared word.
- The four-byte store at scaled base + 0x34 is disjoint from the four-byte location at scaled base + 0x14, including under modulo-2³² addressing.
- The intervening byte read does not change ordinary memory.

Those intervening stores therefore cannot make the reread nonzero through ordinary aliasing alone.

The read remains part of the represented behavior. Device semantics, concurrent modification, nonordinary address mappings, or faults could change its significance; these are separately unresolved, not consequences of the ordinary overlaps above.

Another clear at small base + 0x14 follows. Two successive byte stores at small base + 0x15 use the reread’s low byte. Under the ordinary-memory premises, both assigned values are zero.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Logical shifts.** Incoming words 0x00018000 and 0x80000000 produce initial shifted arguments 3 and 0x00010000.

2. **Field extraction.** Masking 0xAB123456 to 24 bits and logically shifting right by eight produces 0x1234.

3. **Small-base bound.** Under the stated arithmetic model, a source word of 0xFFFFFFFF produces small base 1,016.

4. **Ordinary-memory clear.** With valid ordinary-memory accesses, no external interference, and the stated addressing model, the early reread at scaled base + 0x14 returns zero. Overlap by the intervening zero clear does not justify a nonzero value.

5. **Read preservation.** Replacing that reread with a constant is not an unconditional equivalence for devices, concurrent storage, or fault-observable execution. Those cases require additional environmental evidence.

6. **Global narrowing.** Assigning an eight-bit expression derived from 0x123456AB to the 0x67 global establishes an expression bit pattern of 0xAB. It does not establish a one-byte destination footprint or the value of a later global evaluation.

7. **Separate global evaluations.** If additional declarations establish unsigned-byte storage at 0x67 and 0x21, and intervening overlapping stores change 0x67 from 0xAB to 0xCD and then 0xEF at the relevant intervals, the three evaluations can supply those three distinct values. Without those declarations, the byte-load interpretation is not an acceptance rule.

8. **Global capture.** Retaining the 0xA9 global’s evaluated value in 32 bits does not justify implementing its source access as a four-byte load without declaration or instruction evidence.

9. **Partial progress.** If an intermediate dependency does not return, subsequent explicit stores are not required to occur.
