# F-660ff5331229

## Purpose

F-660ff5331229 performs an ordered series of memory reads and writes, then invokes an unresolved indirect dependency. Its effects depend on three explicit arguments, additional unexplained entry state, external storage, and addresses obtained from earlier reads.

**Established static evidence:** accesses have 8-, 16-, and 32-bit widths. The transformation includes carry-sensitive address calculations, repeated external reads, overlapping writes, pointer-like traversals, self-address stores, and reads from small fixed addresses.

**Inferred intent:** recurring offsets suggest interconnected records. They do not establish a container type, application purpose, ownership model, or graph invariant. Character-like numeric constants do not establish string processing.

This contract describes the supplied static representation. It does not claim that the apparent interface or final indirect invocation completely recovers the executable’s machine-level behavior.

## Behavior

### Ordering and notation

The represented body has no explicit loop, recovery path, or early return. Conditional expressions affect arithmetic and whether one external read is required.

External 32-bit storage locations are labeled E01–E21 in first-use order. These are document-local storage labels, not dependency IDs. Their distinct labels do not establish disjoint storage; the supplied representation explicitly warns of overlapping external symbols.

### Initial publication and external reads

The indirect target’s low halfword is captured before the first write. The full first argument is then stored through the entry-state pointer. Next, a halfword is written at the sum of the first argument, entry displacement, and additional unsigned value. Its contents are the low 16 bits of the second argument plus the captured target halfword.

E01 is read separately to obtain:

1. A retained full-word base.
2. A retained low byte.
3. A value whose double locates a byte store of that retained byte.

The third read need not equal the first capture.

The low byte of displacement plus additional unsigned value is written at third argument plus the retained E01 base. A character read at twice the numeric entry-state pointer is promoted and added to that base; the resulting address receives the base’s low halfword.

The third argument is doubled and augmented by the incoming carry. Call this the **first doubled component**. The following accesses occur in order:

1. Write the incoming byte at twice the retained E01 base.
2. Read E02 as a word.
3. Write E01’s retained byte at twice that base.
4. Load a word at retained E01 base plus `0x52`.
5. Write halfword `0x0052` at first doubled component plus that base.
6. Write the loaded word’s low halfword at loaded word plus `0x52`.
7. Write the target halfword at twice the retained E01 base.
8. Write the loaded word at twice a value read from E03.

E04 is captured. A separate E04 read contributes to a byte-store destination formed with the first doubled component. The stored byte combines the loaded word’s low byte, the additional unsigned value’s low byte, and the incoming character-sized value.

### Further arithmetic-derived writes

E05 is captured before the target’s low byte is written at twice captured E04. E06 is then captured, followed by a byte store of captured E04’s low byte to that same doubled address.

At captured E06 plus `0x2c`, byte `0x2c` is written. A character is then read at twice captured E06; its promoted value plus captured E04 locates a halfword store of captured E04’s low halfword.

An unsigned **address component** is formed by doubling the first doubled component and adding one exactly when captured E06 is unsigned-greater-than `0x4e`. Captured E05’s low byte is written at twice captured E04.

E07 is read once for a retained full-word base and again for a retained low halfword. That halfword is written at captured E04 plus `0x53`.

E08 is captured. A word is loaded at address component plus captured E07. E09 is read for a retained low byte. A subsequent byte store uses separate E08 references for its doubled destination and low-byte value, not the earlier E08 capture. Operand-evaluation order between those two references is not independently established.

The earlier word-load location receives E02’s low halfword. The target halfword is written at twice captured E08.

The loaded word, interpreted as signed 32-bit data, is shifted right by its own low eight bits. The shifted result is:

- Written as a word at original loaded value plus shifted result.
- Used in the destination of a halfword store at shifted result plus captured E08.

That halfword’s **contents are captured E08’s low halfword**, not the shift result.

The count ranges from 0 through 255. Count masking is not established. Oversized counts and right shift of negative signed values remain unresolved.

E10 is captured. In order, the function writes:

- E02’s low byte at shifted result plus captured E07.
- Byte `0x52` at the earlier word-load location.
- The address component as a word at address component plus captured E10.
- The target halfword at captured E10 plus captured E07.

E11 is captured, then read separately for the doubled destination of a byte store combining E09’s retained low byte and the target’s low byte. E12 supplies the base for byte `0x59` at offset `0x59`.

Captured E07’s low byte is written at address component plus captured E11. The address component’s low halfword is then written at the earlier word-load location.

### Carry-sensitive transition

E13 is captured, then read separately to locate a halfword store at offset `0x54`, containing E07’s retained halfword. E14 and E15 are captured afterward.

A **doubled external value** is formed from twice captured E13 plus one when the additional unsigned entry value is less than or equal to the address component. Its carry is true if either the doubling or addition of that comparison bit overflows 32 bits.

One E16 read plus this carry forms a **carry-adjusted external base**. Further E16 reads determine another carry: whether an unsigned value exceeds `0xffffffa6`, or otherwise whether adding the prior carry to a separately read value plus `0x59` overflows. The latter read is unnecessary when the first condition is true.

Captured E07, the doubled external value, and this second carry form a **constructed address**. A fresh E14 read supplies a retained comparison bit: additional unsigned entry value less than or equal to that fresh value.

Two halfword stores follow:

- At address component plus constructed address, store the constructed address’s low halfword.
- After augmenting the constructed address by the additional unsigned entry value, store its low halfword at address component plus that augmented address.

Captured E14’s low halfword is written at twice the doubled external value. Byte `0x45` is written at carry-adjusted external base plus `0x9e`.

E17 is captured, and E18 supplies a retained low halfword. The recently halfword-written location is read as a word-sized pointer; byte `0x45` is written at that pointer plus `0x45`. The location then receives the augmented constructed address’s low halfword again.

At captured E17 plus `0x45`, a halfword receives the truncated sum of captured E15, the E18 halfword, and the comparison bit for the indirect target being below address `0x46`.

The pointer-bearing location is read again. Through this second capture, word `0x00000045` is written at offset `0x45`. At captured E17 plus `0x45`, a byte then receives E02’s retained low byte plus `0x53` plus the fresh-E14 comparison bit.

These two pointer reads straddle writes and cannot be substituted for one another.

A character is read at another retained record value plus the parent address, then converted to unsigned 32 bits. Signed character interpretation can produce a large unsigned result.

The retained address-bearing value’s low halfword is written at E19 plus that value, then again at that value plus the parent address. Word `0x45` is written at character-derived unsigned value plus `0x45`.

In order, the function captures E20, captures E21, captures the parent’s `0x44` word, writes byte `0x44` at character-derived value plus `0x44`, stores captured E21 at twice captured E20, and stores E20’s low halfword at an earlier retained record value plus E20.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static deductions, not executed tests.

1. **Initial truncation:** second argument `−1` and target low halfword `2` produce initial halfword contents `0x0001`, provided address formation and access complete.

2. **Independent external reads:** E01 reads yielding base `0x1000`, retained byte `0x22`, and destination-forming value `0x2000` produce a byte store of `0x22` at `0x4000`. Later retained-base uses still use `0x1000`.

3. **Intervening read:** the incoming-byte store at twice retained E01 precedes the E02 read, which precedes the retained-byte store there.

4. **Comparison boundary:** captured E06 equal to `0x4e` contributes zero to its associated doubling calculation; `0x4f` contributes one.

5. **Shift-store contents:** loaded value `0x1000` has shift count zero. With captured E08 `0x2345`, the word result is stored at `0x2000`; the following halfword store is at `0x3345` and contains `0x2345`.

6. **Repeated-byte sequence:** for retained pointer `0x1000` and retained byte `0x5a`, the later sequence writes `0x5a` at `0x1009`, reads a word at `0x1050`, writes an earlier captured word’s low byte at `0x1011`, then writes `0x5a` again at `0x1009`. No disjointness from other records follows.

7. **Alias-sensitive capture:** if a loaded-pointer store overlaps a parent’s `0x64`, captures before and after it may differ. Their consumers retain the respective values.

8. **Final argument:** character-derived unsigned value `2` produces first indirect argument `0x46`; the operation is OR, not addition.

9. **Access failure:** an inaccessible intermediate destination has no specified checked error result, and earlier writes need not be undone.
