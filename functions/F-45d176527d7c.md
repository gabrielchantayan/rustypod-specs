## Behavior

### Initialization

The following ordering is explicit:

1. Capture the first incoming stack word.
2. Invoke F-000000000001 at the destination obtained by adding **3,806** to the incoming control-link value. Under the address interpretation above, this addition retains 32 bits.
3. Split its packed result into low-word and high-word addresses.
4. Read one unsigned byte from the high-word address plus 25.
5. Write the 16-bit value 39 at the low-word address plus 41.
6. Invoke F-000000000002 with the low-word address plus 13, the high-word address plus 50, and the captured stack word.
7. At its returned address plus 36, write the saved byte as a zero-extended 16-bit value.
8. Invoke F-000000000003 with no explicit arguments.
9. Invoke F-000000000004 to obtain a signed 32-bit scalar.
10. Evaluate the initial scalar condition.
11. Invoke F-000000000005 with:
    - The scalar.
    - Its arithmetic right shift by nine, plus 65.
    - The arithmetic right shift by twenty of −21; under the stated interpretation, this is −1.

F-000000000005 is invoked regardless of the initial condition’s outcome. Its packed result supplies the next low and high words.

The initial condition compares the unsigned interpretation of the scalar’s arithmetic right shift by nine against `0xFFFFFFBE`, using a strict greater-than comparison. Under the stated interpretation, the condition succeeds exactly for scalar values **−33,280 through −1**, inclusive.

If that condition fails, execution enters the common phase directly.

If it succeeds:

1. Write the saved byte as a zero-extended 16-bit value at the new low-word address plus 42.
2. Test signed overflow from subtracting 47 from the new high word.
3. If there is no overflow, enter the common phase directly.
4. If there is overflow, enter the leading phase.

The overflow condition succeeds for high-word signed values **−2,147,483,648 through −2,147,483,602**, inclusive. The offset-42 store occurs before this decision, including when execution proceeds directly to the common phase.

For direct initial entry to the leading phase:

- The stored working word is the first preserved incoming register value minus the saved unsigned byte.
- The first working argument is the packed low word logically shifted right by 25, yielding 0 through 127.
- The working address is the packed high word.

- Scalars −33,280 and −1 satisfy the initial condition.
- Scalars −33,281 and zero do not.
