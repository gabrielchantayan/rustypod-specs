# F-4101adf1d1b2

## Purpose

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation exposes field extraction, shifts, masks, represented 64-bit results, ordered memory accesses, repeated stores, and the terminal transfer. It does not establish an application-level operation such as allocation, parsing, text output, or container insertion.

**Inferred intent:** some constants resemble character values, and some stores publish address-like values. Neither observation establishes a string operation, record format, or linked data structure.

This is a conditional, source-facing behavioral contract. It describes the supplied static representation without assuming that its recovered interfaces and arithmetic are a complete executable machine specification.

## Behavior

### Dependency-driven transformations

The opening phase is a long, ordered succession of indirect invocations. Explicit arguments include zero, shifted primary results, secondary results, masked subfields, and repeated copies of a transformed value.

The third-input field contributes arguments scaled by 256, 32768, and 65536. Their respective maxima are 261888, 33521664, and 67043328; these particular calculations fit within unsigned 32 bits.

Later transformations use low 24-bit, 22-bit, 20-bit, 19-bit, and smaller fields. Represented 64-bit results are also split into low and high 32-bit portions. Right shifts of a represented 64-bit result by 34, 36, or 44 positions correspond to its upper word shifted right by 2, 4, or 12 positions. They are not shifts of the low word alone.

Explicitly initialized zero intermediates remain zero through the displayed shifts. This establishes zero arguments at the relevant invocation sites, not redundant invocations. Ignored results likewise do not establish absence of effects.

No explicit loop or conditional branch appears in the outer flow. Dependencies may nevertheless branch, fail, transfer control, or never return. The sequence cannot be replaced by a pure computation over the third input.

### Width and arithmetic interpretation

The representation distinguishes 8-bit bytes, 16-bit halfwords, 32-bit words and address-producing values, and represented 64-bit results.

Where a fixed-width interpretation is applicable:

- Byte and halfword stores retain the low eight and sixteen bits respectively.
- Explicit unsigned byte reads widen to values from 0 through 255.
- Unsigned right shifts introduce zero bits.
- Intermediate truncation affects subsequent shifts and scaling.
- Address arithmetic uses 32-bit quantities rather than unbounded mathematical integers.

Some arithmetic and shifts are represented on signed 32-bit values. Their overflow behavior, conversion behavior, and machine-level correspondence are **documented_only**. Modular 32-bit arithmetic is a conditional interpretation, not a portable signed-arithmetic guarantee.

A final-region right shift has a count taken from the low eight bits of a computed address-producing value. Counts above 31 are possible. Neither reduction modulo 32 nor any other oversized-count rule is established. The right-shift treatment of a negative signed operand is also unresolved.

### Opening memory mutations

The first direct-memory region uses the final dependency-produced low/high pair, earlier shifted results, a large scaled base, and a selector.

The selector is formed by shifting a dependency result left by eight positions, retaining fifteen bits, and shifting right by six positions. Under the fixed-width interpretation it is a multiple of four between 0 and 508. Its doubled address-producing value is a multiple of eight between 0 and 1016.

The opening observable constraints include:

- A word store at offset 0x14 through the final pair’s high word precedes a byte store at offset 9 through an earlier shifted base.
- A word store at offset 0x54 through the final pair’s low word scaled by 256 separates two byte writes to that offset-9 destination.
- A word at offset 0x14 through the large scaled base is cleared before a byte at offset 5 from that base is read.
- A selector-derived word at offset 0x14 is cleared after that byte read and before the widened byte is stored at offset 0x34 through the large scaled base.
- A later reload of the large base’s offset-0x14 word precedes another selector-derived clear and two identical byte stores at selector-derived offset 0x15.

These are access-order constraints, not merely final-value requirements. Aliasing can make the intervening stores affect later reads.

### Middle memory region and early absolute accesses

A value associated with absolute address **0x05** is accessed and converted to an unsigned 32-bit value for use as a base. **The access width is unresolved.** The conversion establishes the width of the resulting value, not the width of the underlying memory read; the global declaration is absent.

That access precedes an **8-bit write at absolute address 0x01**, containing the low byte of the doubled selector. Computed writes then include a byte at offset 0x15 through the converted base and writes through other dependency-derived bases.

A 32-bit word at offset 0x54 through the converted base is captured before replacement with a previously loaded word. Call the captured value the **exchanged word**. This descriptive role is not an ownership or structure designation.

Subsequent computed updates include self-referential word stores, repeated byte stores, and destinations scaled from loaded words. The early absolute effects have these additional positions:

| Absolute location | Access | Ordering and value |
|---|---|---|
| 0x54 | 32-bit write | Receives twice the exchanged word, after a selector-derived offset-0x14 store and before subsequent stores of the exchanged word to that computed field. |
| 0x15 | 8-bit write | Receives the low byte of the doubled selector, after two computed offset-0x15 byte stores derived from four times the exchanged word and before further scaled-base word updates. |

Thus the absolute access sequence in this region is: unresolved-width access at 0x05, byte write at 0x01, word write at 0x54, then byte write at 0x15, with computed accesses interspersed.

The computed region uses byte offsets including 1, 5, 9, 0x0D, 0x11, and 0x15, and word offsets including 4, 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. These locations may overlap.

One significant chain reads a byte at offset 5, uses that widened byte itself as an address base, and reads a word at offset 0x74 from it. No unstated table or index interpretation is permitted. Repeated offset-5 reads remain separate observations.

### Constants and publication region

For descriptive purposes, the following roles identify values in this region:

- **Fourfold base:** four times the exchanged word.
- **Adjusted scaling value:** the fourfold base minus 0x96.
- **Publication base:** four times the adjusted scaling value.
- **Publication payload:** four times a word obtained through the later byte-to-address chain.
- **Global base:** the captured global memory-base word.

These relationships retain the arithmetic qualifications above.

Numeric 0x3A is stored with three distinct widths: a byte at the publication payload address, a halfword at twice that address-producing value, and a word at offset 0x20 from the payload address.

At publication-base offset 0x20, successive word writes contain 0x20, 0x2F, a widened byte read from publication-base offset 4, and 0x20 again. The offset-4 byte read occurs after the first two writes.

**The global memory-base word is read before the 32-bit store of 0x20 at absolute address 0x60.** Publication uses the already captured global base. The 0x60 store is not evidence that the global read can be delayed until afterward.

The initial publication group writes, in order:

- The payload to offset 0x54 through eight times the exchanged word.
- The publication base’s low byte to global-base offset 0x19.
- The global base’s low byte to publication-base offset 0x11.
- Eight times the exchanged word to global-base offset 0x34.

The subsequent unrolled groups retain that four-access ordering but use eight times the adjusted scaling value for the first destination’s base and the final word’s value. Their bases do not visibly advance.

Every repeated write remains part of the access trace. The exact numeric repetition count is **documented_only** in this abstraction; no permission to reduce the sequence to one group follows.

### Final absolute/global observations

The final region preserves a distinction between a captured word and later fresh byte reads:

1. A **32-bit word at absolute address 0xA9 is captured**.
2. A computed word at payload-base offset 0x74 is captured.
3. An **8-bit write at absolute address 0x3A** stores the low-byte result corresponding to eight times the adjusted scaling value plus 0x30.
4. Further computed captures and mutations occur before an **8-bit write at absolute address 0x67** publishes the saved low byte of the 0xA9 word.

The 0x3A write therefore follows both the 0xA9 capture and the intervening computed offset-0x74 read.

After the 0x67 write, a computed word is read at offset 0x44 through twice the byte-chain-derived word. Only then is **0x67 read as a byte and copied to absolute address 0x21**.

Two further, distinct byte reads of 0x67 supply later writes to publication-base offset 9:

- The first follows several intervening computed writes, an offset-0x40 word read, and further word stores.
- A write of the full saved 0xA9 word to publication-base offset 0x44 and a word read at publication-base offset 0x54 separate that first use from the second 0x67 read.

The three reads of 0x67 cannot be replaced by one cached byte without an established memory model and alias restrictions. In contrast, the low byte initially written to 0x67 comes from the saved 0xA9 word, not a fresh 0xA9 read.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Input extraction.** Third inputs 0 and 1023 yield field zero; 1024 yields one; 0x000FFFFF yields 1023; 0x00100000 yields zero. This does not establish equivalent complete executions.

2. **Selector boundaries.** Under the fixed-width interpretation, a selector-source result of 127 yields selector 508 and doubled value 1016. A result of 128 yields zero. Selector 511 is inconsistent with the transformation.

3. **Unresolved global width.** If the 0x05-associated value converts to unsigned value 0x1234, subsequent address calculations use that converted value. Acceptance does not require a four-byte read at 0x05; that width remains unresolved.

4. **Early absolute effects.** On access-observable memory, an execution reaching the middle region includes the byte writes at 0x01 and 0x15 and the word write at 0x54 in their stated positions. Computed stores do not substitute for these events merely because final bytes happen to match.

5. **Global-base capture.** If the global-base read observes one value and the later 0x60 store aliases that global location, publication still uses the captured value. Moving the read after the store is not accepted without an equivalence proof.

6. **Saved absolute word.** If the 0xA9 capture observes 0x123456AB, the subsequent 0x67 write contains 0xAB, subject to completion of intervening operations. It does not refresh 0xA9 after the 0x3A write.

7. **Fresh 0x67 observations.** If an admitted access-sensitive memory model supplies different byte values on the three later reads of 0x67, the 0x21 copy and the two computed offset-9 stores use their respective observations. Reusing the saved 0xA9 low byte for all three is not accepted.

8. **Mixed-width constants.** The three 0x3A stores retain their byte, halfword, and word widths. Three byte stores are not equivalent under this contract.

9. **Aliasing after clearing.** A later word read from a cleared location is zero only if intervening accesses do not alter its bytes and the memory model supports ordinary read-after-write behavior.

10. **Terminal values and errors.** If the terminal target is reached, its first explicit argument is 0x72 and its third is the saved offset-0x14 word. An invalid earlier address has no established invented status-code result.
