# F-5bce1d390765

## Purpose

F-5bce1d390765 performs an ordered sequence of indirect calls, 32-bit memory accesses, and byte writes. It transforms incoming and dependency-produced values through shifts, masks, and scaled address calculations.

**Established static evidence:** the supplied representation shows extensive memory mutation, including writes to low numeric addresses, and ends at an invalid-instruction boundary rather than a normal return.

**Inferred intent:** the recurring offsets and scaling operations might represent encoded references or structured state. No application-level interpretation is established. In particular, this contract does not identify the operation as allocation, serialization, device control, or object initialization.

**Scope:** this is a source-facing contract for the visible sequence, not a claim that the representation accurately captures executable behavior beyond its identified limitations.

### Width and arithmetic conventions

All word values, address operands, and scaling calculations below are described as 32-bit quantities. A byte write retains the low eight bits. Unsigned right shifts insert zeros. For an eight-byte dependency result, “lower half” means bits 0–31 and “upper half” means bits 32–63.

For compact descriptions, scaling and left-shift results refer to their low 32 bits. This is a machine-width interpretation, not proof that the source-like signed operations have portable language semantics. Signed overflow, negative signed left shifts, address wrapping, and address validity remain uncertainties.

Word byte order is not supplied. The byte accesses do not establish it.

## Behavior

### Dependency notation and sequencing

Every unresolved indirect call is designated **F-unknown**. This is an opaque unresolved-dependency marker, not an assertion that the destinations are identical. No additional trustworthy function identities are available.

The sequence below preserves the order of visible memory accesses and operand-bearing calls. Intervening calls remain significant even where they have no explicit operands or their explicit result is unused: they may modify memory, registers, or external state.

All descriptions are conditional on the earlier accesses and calls completing.

### 1. Capture the initial words

First, read a 32-bit word at the fourth argument plus `0x04`. Call this the **input word**.

Next, read a 32-bit word at the implicit context base plus `0x74`. Call this the **context word**. Write its low byte to the implicit context base plus `0x01`.

Call F-unknown with four operands, in order:

1. Context word.
2. Second explicit argument.
3. Second explicit argument again.
4. First explicit argument.

A subsequent no-explicit-operand call supplies a word. Pass that word and the context word shifted right by two to another F-unknown call.

Independently retain the input word shifted right by 22. This **extracted field** has at most ten significant bits.

Three further no-explicit-operand calls precede a fourth such call whose result supplies the next address calculation.

### 2. Transfer a dependency-selected word

Multiply that last result by four to obtain the **first scaled base**. Read a word at this base plus `0x74`, then write its low byte at this base plus `0x01`.

Call F-unknown with the first scaled base. Four no-explicit-operand calls follow. After the fourth, a secondary register result supplies a destination index.

Write the previously read word to four times that secondary result plus `0x14`. Call F-unknown with the unscaled secondary result.

A no-explicit-operand call then supplies a word. Pass it to F-unknown together with two identical operands, each formed by masking the extracted field to nine bits and shifting left by 21.

Three no-explicit-operand calls follow; a fourth supplies another index. Write the extracted field shifted left by 23 to four times this index plus `0x14`, then call F-unknown with four times the index.

The two uses of the extracted field are not equivalent: the earlier call explicitly masks to nine bits; the later word write shifts the full ten-bit field within a 32-bit result.

### 3. Establish retained scaled values

Three no-explicit-operand calls precede a fourth that returns the **seed word**.

Write four times the seed word to sixteen times the seed word plus `0x14`. Retain:

- The **seed-times-four**, calculated before masking.
- The **masked seed**, the seed word masked with `0x3fffffff`.
- The **seed quarter**, the masked seed shifted right by two.

Call F-unknown with the masked seed. Three no-explicit-operand calls precede a fourth returning the **structure index**.

Write the masked seed to four times the structure index plus `0x14`, then call F-unknown with four times the structure index.

After another four no-explicit-operand calls, retain the final result as the **link word**.

Write the low byte of eight times the structure index to the context word interpreted as an address plus `0x01`. Then write the link word shifted left by two to eight times the structure index plus `0x34`.

Call F-unknown with the link word shifted left by two.

### 4. Replace the link field using a paired result

A no-explicit-operand call supplies a word that is passed to the next F-unknown call.

Retain the **link base**, formed by masking the link word with `0x3fffffff`. Three no-explicit-operand calls precede a fourth returning an eight-byte result.

Write the low byte of eight times the structure index to the link base plus `0x01`. Then overwrite the word at eight times the structure index plus `0x34` with the upper half of that paired result.

Call F-unknown with the lower half shifted left by two.

Thus, the two writes at offset `0x34` are separate ordered effects, with intervening calls. They cannot be collapsed into one final write.

### 5. Perform byte updates through scaled addresses

Three no-explicit-operand calls precede a fourth returning another word.

Retain sixteen times the structure index as the **wide structure base**. Write its low byte to:

- The link base shifted right by 20, plus `0x11`.

### 6. Establish a second scaled record

## Side effects

The direct accesses establish no ownership transfer, allocation, release, reference-count operation, or cleanup obligation. Dependencies may perform such actions, but that is unknown.

The routine directly writes:

- Bytes at offsets `0x01`, `0x07`, `0x0d`, and `0x11` relative to several differently derived bases.
- Words at offsets `0x14`, `0x24`, and `0x34`.
- Bytes directly at numeric addresses `0x0d` and `0x11`.

A byte-derived destination of the form index plus `0x11` lies between `0x11` and `0x110`, inclusive. These are literal numeric addresses in the supplied representation, not established offsets into an omitted global object.

No non-aliasing guarantee exists. A write may affect a later read, a dependency’s state, or another apparent field. Repeated accesses separated by calls must remain distinct.

No rollback, validation, explicit error branch, bounds check, or recovery path is visible. A failing access or dependency may prevent later effects; the failure mechanism is unspecified.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static acceptance criteria, not executed tests.

1. **Initial low-byte write.** If the context word is `0x123456ab`, the initial byte write stores `0xab` at the implicit context base plus `0x01`. The first dependency still receives the full `0x123456ab`.

2. **Distinct field transformations.** If the input word is `0x00400000`, the extracted field is one. The duplicated call operands are `0x00200000`, while the later word write contains `0x00800000`.

3. **Upper extracted bit.** If the input word is `0x80000000`, the extracted field is `0x200`. Its nine-bit-masked call operands are zero. Its left shift by 23 also has low 32 bits zero; this follows from width truncation, not from the earlier mask.

4. **Fresh byte lookup.** If the record-base byte initially reads as 5 but a dependency changes it to 9 before the second read, the first indexed write targets `0x16` and the later zero write targets `0x1a`.

5. **Paired-result overwrite.** If the paired result in phase 4 has upper half `0xaabbccdd`, the second word write at the eight-times-structure-index field stores `0xaabbccdd`, regardless of the earlier shifted link word.

6. **Visible low-address overwrite.** If execution reaches both direct writes to address `0x0d`, that address first receives the saved byte index and later receives `0xc0`. Intervening dependency observations remain possible.

7. **No normal completion guarantee.** Reaching the final visible boundary does not satisfy an acceptance criterion requiring an ordinary return or success status.
