# F-d2e2d03715f2

## Purpose

F-d2e2d03715f2 performs an ordered sequence of unresolved indirect invocations, then mutates fixed and computed memory locations, and finally transfers control indirectly with four represented arguments.

No application-level purpose is established. The evidence does not identify initialization, serialization, rendering, allocation, or collection management. Address-like values and self-referential stores suggest possible link manipulation, but that is **inferred intent**, not an object-model or ownership requirement.

This contract describes **established static evidence**, conditional on execution reaching each operation. Unavailable execution semantics and dependency behavior are marked **documented_only**. Successful completion is not guaranteed.

## Interface

### Inputs and outputs

The represented interface has no explicit parameters and no explicit return value. Its implicit inputs include:

- Incoming return-link state, used to derive the first indirect target.
- An incoming unsigned 32-bit word and a different incoming signed-represented 32-bit word.
- Primary and secondary words produced or exposed by dependencies.
- Memory contents used as payloads, byte selectors, and further addresses.
- Fixed-address storage and a late stored-base word.
- An unresolved final transfer target.

The first indirect target is the incoming return-link value plus 0xFFE. Most subsequent targets use fixed code-location anchors plus 0xFFE. Their callable interfaces are unresolved.

There is no established success code, returned buffer, or ownership result. Outputs are dependency effects, direct memory mutations, and the final handoff. Its represented arguments, in position order, are:

1. 0x72.
2. A late word read at offset 0x64 from a memory-derived base.
3. An earlier captured word read at offset 0x14 from another base.
4. A later word read at offset 0x74 from the base used for argument 2.

An apparent return follows the handoff. Whether the target returns, or the operation returns normally, is **documented_only**.

## Behavior

### Dependency ordering and retained values

The dependency phase is statically straight-line: no explicit loop, conditional selection, retry, or error return is shown. A dependency may nevertheless fault, alter state, or not return.

For the following relationships, “low word” and “high word” mean the respective 32-bit portions of a represented combined result.

The first invocation produces a combined result. The first subsequent four-argument invocation receives, in exact position order:

1. The first low word shifted left by eight.
2. The first high word, unmodified.
3. The same shifted low word as argument 1.
4. The incoming unsigned word logically shifted right by 22, then shifted left by six.

The next invocation receives the preceding invocation’s primary result shifted left by eight. Intervening invocations with no represented explicit arguments remain part of the call sequence; absence of displayed arguments does not establish absence of register inputs.

The other incoming word has two distinct early uses:

- A two-argument invocation receives a newly obtained primary result shifted left by eight, followed by that incoming word shifted left by 18.
- A subsequent three-argument invocation receives a field derived from the retained **first** low word, a then-available secondary word, and that incoming word shifted left by 18.
- Later, a fresh combined result supplies a four-argument invocation with its low word shifted left by eight, its unmodified high word, and two copies of the incoming word shifted left by 24.

The early retained field is formed from the first low word after its eight-bit left shift: retain the low 20 bits, logically shift right by ten, then shift left by five. Under fixed-width interpretation, this selects original low-word bits 2 through 11 and places them at bits 5 through 14.

A later extraction has a different origin. A **different combined result’s high word** is shifted left by six and retained across intervening invocations. Its low 20 bits are then shifted right by ten; the extracted field is subsequently supplied in argument positions 2 and 3 after left shifts of seven and 13. This selects bits 4 through 13 of that later high word. It is not a reuse of the first low-word field.

Other established relationships include:

- Repeated argument tuples containing a shifted low word, its companion high word, and another transformation of one of those same words.
- Four-argument invocations that repeat the same shifted low word in positions 1 and 3 or positions 1 and 4.
- Retained primary results reused after intervening calls, rather than replaced with the newest result.
- Retained high-word fields reused in masked 22-bit forms after later combined results have arrived.
- Explicit zero arguments, including values retained as zero through subsequent shifts.
- Extraction above bit 31 of represented 64-bit results. Logical shifts by 34, 36, and 44 yield at most 30, 28, and 20 bits respectively. Some extractions use an older retained combined result rather than the immediately preceding result.

The invocations consuming zero or repeated arguments are not established to be inert.

### Arithmetic boundaries

Unsigned 32-bit shifts retain only the resulting 32 bits. For example, a left shift by eight followed by a logical right shift by eight loses the original high byte.

Signed-represented left shifts and multiplications occur as well. Overflow behavior for those expressions is **documented_only**; this contract does not assign portable signed-overflow semantics.

Two late quantities are important for the memory phase:

- A retained result multiplied by 0x02000000 supplies a **wide base**. Under fixed-width machine interpretation, its low 25 bits are clear.
- Another result, shifted left by eight, masked to 0x7FFF, then logically shifted right by six, supplies a **bounded scalar** between 0 and 508 in multiples of four. Twice that scalar supplies a **bounded base** between 0 and 1016 in multiples of eight.

A separate retained low-word transformation shifted left by 25 supplies another address-like base. A further retained result shifted left by eight supplies an early stored payload. These distinct values must not be conflated.

### Entry into memory mutation

The final combined dependency result supplies both the first destination base and another scaled destination. Initially:

- Its high word, used as a base at offset 0x14, receives the retained 25-bit-shifted value.
- Offset 9 of the separate 25-bit-shifted base receives first the low byte of the final high word, then the low byte of the retained shifted value.
- Between those byte stores, a word at offset 0x54 from the final low word scaled by 256 receives the wide base.

The operation clears the wide base’s word at offset 0x14, reads its unsigned byte at offset 5, and later writes that byte as a zero-extended word at offset 0x34. A bounded-base word at offset 0x14 is cleared twice, with the widened-byte store and another read between the clears. The captured word from the wide base’s offset 0x14 supplies two consecutive byte stores at bounded-base offset 0x15.

These intermediate writes remain distinct. Final values alone do not describe their possible observability.

### Computed layouts and selector chains

The mutation region uses byte offsets including 1, 5, 9, 0x0D, 0x11, 0x15, and 0x19; word offsets include 4, 0x14, 0x24, 0x34, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74. These are access layouts, not validated record definitions.

The captured link supplies several scaled bases, notably four and eight times the link. Subsequent effects include:

- Repeated self-valued stores at offset 0x24 of the captured link.
- Replacement and repeated replacement of computed offset-0x14 and offset-0x54 words.
- Stores of truncated address-like values into neighboring bytes.
- Reads through words at offset 0x74, followed by writes through the resulting bases.
- Word reads at offset 0x74 from an **unsigned-byte-derived base**.

A word at offset 4 of the quadrupled captured link supplies another base. Its byte at offset 5 is read twice, with computed writes between the reads. The later byte selects a word at offset 0x74. Call that selected word the **selected value**; twice it is the **payload base**.

Repeated reads must not be merged without establishing memory equivalence. An intervening write can change a later selector through aliasing.

### Constants and repeated four-write region

The selected value supplies three constant destinations, written in order:

- A byte containing 0x3A at four times the selected value.
- A halfword containing 0x003A at eight times the selected value.
- A word containing 0x0000003A at offset 0x20 from four times the selected value.

Define the **adjusted coordinate** as four times the captured link minus 0x96, and the **working base** as four times that coordinate, subject to the arithmetic limitations above.

The working base’s offset-0x20 word receives four successive values: 0x20, 0x2F, a zero-extended byte read from working-base offset 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after the first two stores. The stored-base slot is then read, followed by the fixed word store of 0x20 at 0x60.

There are **64 statically present four-write groups**, totaling **256 stores: 128 word stores and 128 byte stores**. They are consecutive ordered effects, not a data-dependent loop.

Within every group, the order is:

1. A scaled-destination word store containing four times the selected value.
2. A byte store at stored-base offset 0x19 containing the low byte of the working base.
3. A byte store at working-base offset 0x11 containing the low byte of the stored base.
4. A word store at stored-base offset 0x34 containing a scaled link or coordinate.

The first group differs in **both** scaled relationships: its first destination is eight times the captured link plus 0x54, and its final value is eight times the captured link.

The remaining **63 groups** use eight times the adjusted coordinate plus 0x54 as their first destination and eight times the adjusted coordinate as their final value. Their two byte destinations and values remain as above.

The stored base and store payloads are retained values; no source reread is shown within these groups. Destination aliasing remains possible. Known static multiplicity does not establish how a particular runtime exposes repeated stores.

### Final snapshots, fixed bytes, and handoff

After all 64 groups, the final region begins with:

- A 32-bit read at fixed address **0xA9**.
- A word read at offset 0x74 from four times the selected value.
- A byte write at fixed address **0x3A**, containing the low eight bits of eight times the adjusted coordinate plus 0x30.
- Captures of the payload base’s offset-0x14 word and the working base’s offset-4 word, in that order.

Computed mutations then write the working base’s low byte at payload-base offset 0x0D, write 0x6C at working-base offset 8, place the stored base’s low byte at working-base offsets 0x0D and 9, and store the payload base itself at its offset 0x54.

Only then does fixed byte **0x67** receive the low byte of the previously captured 0xA9 word.

The next relevant snapshot is the payload base’s offset-0x44 word. After that read, fixed byte **0x21** receives a **fresh read of byte 0x67**. It is not specified as a copy directly from the earlier 0xA9 snapshot.

The offset-0x44 snapshot supplies subsequent computed stores, including a byte at working-base offset 0x10 and a word at stored-base offset 0x54. A shifted form is stored as a byte at payload-base offset 1. Its shift count is the working base’s low eight bits; oversized-count behavior and signed right-shift semantics are **documented_only**.

Further mutation copies a word read at working-base offset 0x40 into working-base offset 0x10. The captured working-base offset-4 word is stored at stored-base offset 0x74; other link writes occur around these operations.

There are then **two additional, separate reads of current byte 0x67**, each supplying a byte store at working-base offset 9. Computed writes intervene between initialization of 0x67 and these reads, and between relevant uses. Those writes may alias 0x67. Neither read may automatically substitute the originally stored byte or the value previously copied to 0x21.

The full captured 0xA9 word is also stored at working-base offset 0x44 before that word is later replaced with a different memory-derived base.

The last portion:

- Uses the earlier offset-0x74 snapshot’s low byte for two computed byte stores.
- Restores the captured payload-base offset-0x14 word at payload-base offset 0x54.
- Captures the current working-base offset-0x14 word for a later byte store.
- Reads a further base from payload-base offset 4 and writes halfword 0x0065 at twice that base.
- Reads the final second argument at offset 0x64 from that further base.
- Writes decimal 100 as a word at offset 0x54 from another memory-derived base.
- Reads the final fourth argument at offset 0x74 from the further base.

A later read at payload-base offset 0x64 supplies a destination base. Before handoff, its offset-0x54 word receives the captured second argument, and a word at twice that argument receives the destination base. The final arguments retain their captured values despite these intervening writes.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

- **First argument ordering:** Given first-result low word 1, high word 2, and incoming unsigned word 0xFFFFFFFF, the first four-argument invocation receives 0x100, 2, 0x100, and 0xFFC0, in that order.

- **Distinct early fields:** Given first low word 0x00000FFC, its retained extraction produces 0x7FE0. A later high-word extraction must instead use that later result’s bits 4–13; equal outputs are not required.

- **Fixed-width loss:** Shifting unsigned 0xAB123456 left by eight and logically right by eight produces 0x00123456.

- **Bounded addressing:** An all-ones input to the bounded transformation produces scalar 508 and bounded base 1016. Substitution of an allocation-relative pointer is unsupported.

- **Byte widening:** An offset-5 byte of 0xFE supplies word 0x000000FE, not 0xFFFFFFFE.

- **Repeated region:** If execution reaches the complete region without interruption, it contains 64 four-store groups. A single group or merely equivalent final ordinary-memory contents does not preserve the established store sequence. The first group uses the captured-link scaling; the other 63 use adjusted-coordinate scaling.

- **Current 0x67 storage:** If an intervening computed write changes byte 0x67 to 0xA5, a subsequent ordinary-memory read of that byte supplies 0xA5, even if initialization stored another byte.

- **Partial failure:** A dependency that does not return prevents later operations from being required. A fault after completed stores does not require rollback.

- **Handoff snapshots:** Later writes to a captured argument’s source do not refresh that argument. The first represented handoff argument remains 0x72.
