Masking before shifting, masking after shifting, narrowing before a later shift, and extracting a high word before transformation are distinct operations. They must not be substituted for one another.

- A word store at offset 0x14 through the high-word-derived base precedes a byte store at offset 9 through a retained shifted base.
- A word store at offset 0x54 through a base formed by multiplying the paired result’s low word by 0x100 precedes a second byte store to that same offset-9 destination.

Do not infer that a word loaded after a clear must be zero without examining intervening aliases. Likewise, duplicate byte stores remain separate represented events.

Through that byte-derived base, the function loads an offset-0x54 word before replacing the word. Call the captured old value the **original word**. Subsequent calculations use this captured value, not an assumed reread of the replaced field.

Later fixed writes include:

- After intervening dynamic stores, a byte store at 0x15 carrying the low byte of the small calculated base.
- Much later, a word store of 0x20 at 0x60.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static consequences, not executed tests.

### Paired-result separation

At an invocation forwarding the low word shifted left by eight, the high word unchanged, and the high word shifted left by six, low word 0x12 and high word 3 produce 0x1200, 3, and 0xC0, provided those operations are defined.

### Unsigned extraction and promotion

Selecting bits 10–19 from 0xFFFFFFFF yields 1023; repositioning left by six yields 0xFFC0. An unsigned byte load of 0xFE subsequently promoted to a word yields 254, not −2.

### First-group address distinction

Suppose the original word is 100 and the relevant arithmetic and accesses are valid. The adjusted value is 250. The first group’s offset-0x54 store targets address 884 and its offset-0x34 store carries 800. Subsequent groups’ corresponding destination is 2084 and corresponding stored value is 2000. The first group cannot be modeled merely as a different value written to an otherwise common destination.

### Ordered intermediate stores

Under the abstract access-event memory model, the offset-0x20 sequence retains separate stores of 0x20, 0x2F, the byte-derived word, and 0x20, with the byte load after the first two stores. Replacing this with one final store does not preserve that trace. No hardware observer is required to distinguish all four values.

### Fixed-address rereads

If an intervening dynamic store aliases 0x67 and changes its byte after the initial write but before a later load, that later load uses the changed byte under the sequential storage model. The write to 0x21 and the later offset-9 stores cannot unconditionally reuse the byte originally captured from 0xA9.
