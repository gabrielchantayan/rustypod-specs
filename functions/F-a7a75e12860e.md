## Behavior

### Ordering and captured values

The phases below occur in the stated order. A captured value retains the value obtained by its particular read, even if the memory from which it came is subsequently overwritten. A later read of the same address is a distinct observation and can produce a different value.

No disjointness is assumed between inputs, derived addresses, or fixed storage. Consequently, earlier writes can affect later reads. Descriptions of an “overwrite” identify repeated destination expressions; they do not assert that no other effects occur through aliasing.

### 1. Initial repeated stores

The function first writes Q as a word at B plus `0x34`.

It then performs three rounds of stores to the same four destination expressions. Within each round, the order is:

1. Store P as a word at twice A plus `0x54`.
2. Store A’s low byte at B plus `0x19`.
3. Store B’s low byte at A plus `0x11`.
4. Store twice A as a word at B plus `0x34`.

The byte derived from A and the byte derived from B are captured during the first round and reused in subsequent rounds. They do not come from memory reads.

Thus the initial write of Q is followed by three writes of twice A to the same word destination. All three writes of P also occur. These repetitions are observable on memory with write-sensitive behavior, and a fault may prevent later repetitions from occurring.

The multiplication of unsigned A by two has a 32-bit unsigned interpretation in the supplied representation. Machine address formation and wrapping remain target-dependent.

### 2. Initial snapshots and byte setup

After the repeated stores, the function captures Global W as a word, then captures the word at P plus `0x74`.

It next writes Global X with the byte obtained from twice A’s captured low byte plus `0x30`. Although the representation uses a character-valued intermediate, the stored low eight bits are independent of whether that intermediate character is interpreted as signed or unsigned.

The function then captures, in order:

- The word at C plus `0x14`.
- The word at A plus `0x04`.

It next writes A’s captured low byte at C plus `0x0D`, writes byte `0x6C` at A plus `0x08`, and writes B’s captured low byte at A plus `0x0D` and then A plus `0x09`.

A word containing C is then stored at C plus `0x54`. Global Y receives the low byte of the previously captured Global W word.

The snapshots in this phase are not interchangeable with reads made at another time. For example, the word from P plus `0x74` is read after all initial repeated stores, not on entry.

### 3. Shift-derived byte and replacement fields

The function reads a signed word from C plus `0x44`; call the captured value **R**.

It then reads Global Y and copies that byte to Global Z. This is a read of Global Y at that point, not an unconditional reuse of the earlier Global W snapshot.

Next, R’s low byte is written at A plus `0x10`, and R is written as a word at B plus `0x54`. A byte derived by right-shifting R is then written at C plus `0x01`. The represented shift count is A’s low byte.

The shift has two unresolved aspects:

- R is represented as signed, so negative-value right-shift behavior depends on the target semantics.
- The count can range from 0 through 255, while the shifted operand is 32 bits. Counts of 32 or greater have no portable result established by the supplied representation.

After the shift-derived byte write, A is stored as a word at B plus `0x54`, replacing the earlier R write to that destination expression.

The function then captures the word at A plus `0x40`. It writes the previously captured word from A plus `0x04` to B plus `0x74`, and writes the newly captured word from A plus `0x40` to A plus `0x10`.

That word write covers offsets `0x10` through `0x13`. It therefore overlaps earlier byte writes at A plus `0x10` and A plus `0x11`; their final byte-level relationship depends on byte order and subsequent effects.

C is next stored as a word at B plus `0x44`. A fresh read of Global Y supplies a byte write at A plus `0x09`. A’s captured low byte is then written at C plus `0x09`.

Finally in this phase, the original Global W word snapshot is stored at A plus `0x44`.

### 4. First loaded address and redirected writes

The function reads a word at A plus `0x54`; call this address-bearing snapshot **D**. This read occurs after the preceding writes and therefore can reflect any applicable aliasing.

Global Y is read again, and its current byte is written at A plus `0x09`.

The low byte of the word previously captured from P plus `0x74` is then written at D plus `0x0D`. The earlier snapshot from C plus `0x14` is stored as a word at C plus `0x54`, replacing the earlier C-valued write to that destination expression.

The function next captures a word from A plus `0x14`. It writes the same low byte derived from P plus `0x74` at C plus `0x11`, writes D’s low byte at A plus `0x09`, and stores D as a word at A plus `0x44`.

This last word write replaces the earlier Global W snapshot at that destination expression. The complete word captured from A plus `0x14` is retained temporarily, but only its low byte is subsequently used by this body.

### 5. Further loaded addresses and final invocation

The function reads a signed word at C plus `0x04`; call it **E**. It immediately uses twice E as an address and writes the halfword `0x0065` there.

It then reads a signed word from E plus `0x64`; call it **H**. This read follows the halfword write, so aliasing can make that write relevant to H.

Next, the low byte of the captured word from A plus `0x14` is written at D plus `0x11`, and the word value 100 is written at D plus `0x54`.

The function then captures, in order:

- A word from E plus `0x74`; call it **T**.
- A word from C plus `0x64`; call it **J**.

E’s low byte is written at C plus `0x09`. H is stored as a word at J plus `0x54`, followed by a word containing J stored at address twice H.

The multiplications involving E and H use values represented as signed. Their behavior outside the signed 32-bit representable range is not established as a portable arithmetic contract.

Finally, the unresolved indirect dependency is invoked with four represented arguments, in this order:

1. `0x72`, decimal 114.
2. H.
3. The word captured earlier from C plus `0x14`.
4. T.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional acceptance criteria derived from static evidence. They are not runtime test results.

### Repeated writes remain observable

Given valid, non-overlapping ordinary destinations and an observer capable of recording stores, the observer must see Q written once at B plus `0x34`, followed by three writes of twice A there. It must also see three P-valued word writes at twice A plus `0x54`.

An implementation that performs only the final store at each destination does not preserve this access behavior.

### Byte truncation uses the low eight bits

If A ends in hexadecimal `AB`, the initial byte written at B plus `0x19` is `0xAB`. The Global X byte is `0x86`, because twice `0xAB` plus `0x30` has low byte `0x86`.

This example requires valid accesses and no earlier aliasing that prevents execution from reaching those stores. It does not require a particular character signedness.

### Overwritten fields still have intermediate states

With disjoint relevant regions and no external interference, B plus `0x54` first receives R and later receives A. Similarly, A plus `0x44` first receives the captured Global W word and later receives D.

A final-memory-only comparison cannot prove preservation of those intermediate writes.

### Loaded addresses are snapshots

If D is read as an address value and the source word at A plus `0x54` subsequently changes, the later writes described at D plus `0x0D`, D plus `0x11`, and D plus `0x54` still use the captured D.

This criterion concerns the represented body’s local snapshot, not any behavior inside the indirect dependency.

### Invocation arguments preserve the specified reads

If the C-plus-`0x14` read captures `0x11223344`, the H-producing read captures `0x55667788`, and the E-plus-`0x74` read captures `0x99AABBCC`, then a reached indirect invocation receives, in order, `0x72`, `0x55667788`, `0x11223344`, and `0x99AABBCC`.
