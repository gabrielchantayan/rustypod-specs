# F-1188ca3bdf28

## Purpose

The supplied static evidence does not establish a domain-level purpose. Character-like constants do not establish text processing; repeated offsets do not establish a record schema; repeated stores do not establish synchronization, polling, or retries.

## Behavior

### 1. Ordered dependency phase

A long sequence of indirect calls precedes the direct-memory phase. The first destination depends on incoming return context; subsequent destinations are formed from embedded continuation-like values and a fixed displacement.

Call order is significant. Calls without displayed arguments still have unknown effects. They must not be treated as pure operations or assumed to preserve globals, reachable memory, or undocumented machine state.

Represented argument patterns include:

- Explicit zero pairs and other explicit zero arguments.
- Separate forwarding of a paired result’s lower and upper words.
- Distinct shifted forms of one result in different argument positions.
- Previously retained quantities reused across intervening calls.

Four-argument calls frequently combine a lower word shifted by 8, an upper word, a retained transformation, and another lower-word transformation. These remain separate arguments.

Important unsigned field extractions are:

### 2. Initial direct-memory publications

For readability, the following are descriptive value roles, not declarations of independent objects:

- **Shifted base:** a paired result’s lower word shifted left by 25.
- **Multiplied base:** a separate returned word multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Retained shifted word:** another returned word shifted left by 8.
- **Selector:** a returned word shifted left by 8, masked with 0x7FFF, then shifted right by 6.
- **Doubled-selector base:** twice that selector.

Under the bit-vector model, the selector is a multiple of four from 0 through 508. Its doubled base is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016.

The final paired dependency result supplies two further address components. The opening memory publications are ordered:

1. A word containing the shifted base is stored at the upper result word plus 0x14.
2. The upper result word’s low byte is stored at the shifted base plus 9.
3. The multiplied base is stored as a word at the lower result word multiplied by 256, plus 0x54.
4. The shifted base’s low byte is stored again at the shifted base plus 9.

The fourth publication is zero under the bit-vector model. The preceding byte publication remains part of the access history.

### 3. Clearing, reload, and early absolute writes

The multiplied base’s offset-0x14 word is cleared before a byte is read from its offset 5. A zero word is then stored at the doubled-selector base plus 0x14, followed by publication of the zero-extended byte at the multiplied base plus 0x34.

The subsequent word reload from the multiplied base plus 0x14 yields **zero under ordinary memory semantics**, assuming no external mutation or exceptional memory behavior. The intervening potentially nonzero word publication is at offset 0x34, not within the cleared word. The selector-based publication writes only zeros, so aliasing with it cannot make the cleared word nonzero.

Another selector-based zero word publication precedes two byte publications of the reloaded word’s low byte at the doubled-selector base plus 0x15. Both bytes are therefore zero under those assumptions. Offset 0x15 overlaps the word starting at offset 0x14.

Next, the operation reads the byte at absolute address 0x05 and retains its zero-extended value as a **byte-derived base**. This read precedes the byte publication at absolute address 0x01, which receives the doubled-selector base’s low byte.

The following effects occur before the later scaled-address region:

- The selector’s low byte is published at the byte-derived base plus 0x15.
- The retained shifted word’s low byte is published at the multiplied base plus 0x11.
- That retained shifted word is published as a word at the reloaded-zero-derived base plus 0x54.
- The multiplied base’s offset-0x11 byte is cleared.
- A word is read from the byte-derived base plus 0x54, then that source word is overwritten with the reloaded zero.

Call the word retained by this last read the **retained link word**. Under ordinary-memory assumptions, the earlier zero-derived word publication is an absolute-address-0x54 publication. Its value is the retained shifted word, distinct from the later absolute-0x54 update.

### 4. Link-based mutations and the later absolute publications

The doubled-selector base plus 0x34 receives the selector. An offset-0x74 word read from the multiplied base supplies another computed base. Its offset-0x54 word first receives a returned quantity shifted left by 26, and later receives the retained shifted word.

Other accesses include a nested offset-0x74 read from the doubled-selector base, a self-publication through the resulting address, and repeated self-publications at the retained link word plus 0x24. Byte publications at the other computed base plus 9 use the retained link word’s low byte.

The next ordered publication cluster is material:

- The doubled-selector base plus 0x14 receives the quantity shifted left by 26.
- The word at **absolute address 0x54 receives the retained link word shifted left by one**.
- The doubled-selector base plus 0x14 receives the unshifted retained link word twice.
- Two byte publications at the doubled-selector base plus 0x15 each receive the low byte of four times the retained link word.
- The byte at **absolute address 0x15 receives the low byte of the doubled-selector base**.
- Subsequent publications use four-times-scaled link destinations, including an offset-0x54 word publication and an offset-0x34 publication from the doubled-selector base.

These absolute writes cannot be moved outside the cluster on a presumed disjointness basis.

The remaining link-based region combines four-times and eight-times scaling, byte-derived addressing, and nested word reads. It includes:

- Word accesses at offsets 4, 0x14, 0x44, 0x54, 0x64, and 0x74.
- Byte accesses at offsets 5, 9, 0x0D, and 0x15.
- Self-address publications at offset 0x14.
- Successive different word values at the same offset-0x44 or offset-0x54 destination.
- Repeated byte reads whose values can change through intervening aliasing writes.

One branch is obtained by reading a byte, using that byte as a base for an offset-0x74 word read, and doubling the resulting word. Its final doubled value is called the **tail base** below. These descriptions do not establish independent records or disjoint storage.

### 5. Literals and repeated publication

A later nested word supplies a scaled base for three stores of 0x3A:

- An 8-bit store at four times that word.
- A 16-bit store at eight times that word.
- A 32-bit store at four times that word plus 0x20.

Separately, four times the retained link word is reduced by 0x96. Call this the **adjusted link**, and call four times the adjusted link the **publication base**.

The publication base plus 0x20 receives four ordered word values: 0x20, 0x2F, a freshly read zero-extended byte from the publication base plus 4, and 0x20 again. The byte read occurs after publication of 0x2F and before publication of its own value.

The operation then **loads and retains a global word before storing the word 0x20 at absolute address 0x60**. The retained word becomes the **global publication base**. There is no intervening reload establishing that the absolute store changed the retained base. Reversing these accesses is not justified by any disjointness guarantee.

A repeated publication region follows. Each group contains, in order:

1. A word publication at a scaled base plus 0x54.
2. A byte publication at the global publication base plus 0x19, containing the publication base’s low byte.
3. A byte publication at the publication base plus 0x11, containing the global publication base’s low byte.
4. A word publication at the global publication base plus 0x34.

The first group uses eight times the retained link word for its scaled destination base and final word value. Subsequent groups use eight times the adjusted link. Their retained published word is four times the nested word that also supplied the 0x3A destinations.

The region contains a finite, statically repeated sequence, not an established polling loop. Its exact cardinality is documented_only here. Repeated stores must not be collapsed into final-state equality where access history is observable.

### 6. Tail publications and final transfer

After repetition, the operation reads the word beginning at absolute address 0xA9 and reads an offset-0x74 word through the retained repeated-publication value. It then writes a byte at absolute address 0x3A, derived from eight times the adjusted link plus 0x30 with byte truncation. No decimal-digit interpretation is established.

Before later tail mutations, it retains:

- The word at the tail base plus 0x14.
- The word at the publication base plus 4.

Early tail publications place the publication base’s low byte at the tail base plus 0x0D; 0x6C at the publication base plus 8; and the global publication base’s low byte at publication-base offsets 0x0D and 9. The tail base is also stored at its own offset 0x54.

The low byte of the absolute-0xA9 word is stored at absolute address 0x67. After an offset-0x44 word read from the tail base, the operation reads that global byte for publication at absolute address 0x21. Later accesses read it again for computed byte publications; these reads need not agree if intervening storage aliases it.

The tail also includes:

- A byte at publication-base offset 0x10 followed later by a word at the same offset.
- Two different word publications at the global publication base plus 0x54.
- A variable right-shift result truncated to a byte at the tail base plus 1; the count is the publication base’s low byte.
- Publication of the retained offset-4 word at the global publication base plus 0x74.
- Publication of a word read from publication-base offset 0x40 at publication-base offset 0x10.
- Word publications at offsets 0x44 and 0x54, interleaved with byte publications at offsets 9, 0x0D, and 0x11.

The variable shift is not a rotation. Its native signedness and oversized-count behavior remain unresolved.

For the final stage, a word read from publication-base offset 0x54 becomes an **intermediate destination base**. It receives byte publications and, later, the word decimal 100 at offset 0x54. The previously retained tail-base offset-0x14 word is restored at tail-base offset 0x54.

A word read from tail-base offset 4 becomes the **final field base**. A halfword containing 0x65 is stored at twice that base. The operation then reads the **earlier offset-0x64 word** from the final field base plus 0x64. Before the remaining argument loads, the intermediate destination base receives a byte at offset 0x11 and the word decimal 100 at offset 0x54.

The final retained argument is then read from the final field base plus 0x74. Another word is read from the tail base plus 0x64, becoming the **newly loaded base**.

Three publications occur after these reads and before the final dependency transfer:

1. A byte at the tail base plus 9 receives the final field base’s low byte.
2. A word at the newly loaded base plus 0x54 receives the earlier offset-0x64 word.
3. A word at twice the earlier offset-0x64 word receives the newly loaded base.

These mutations are visible to the final dependency if it observes the affected storage.

The final transfer receives four represented arguments, in order: 0x72, the earlier offset-0x64 word, the retained tail-base offset-0x14 word, and the final retained offset-0x74 word. A represented return follows, conditional on the destination returning control.

## Side effects

The operation mutates computed storage and absolute low-address storage with overlapping byte, halfword, and word accesses.

Explicit absolute destinations include 0x01, 0x15, 0x21, 0x3A, 0x54, 0x60, and 0x67. Explicit absolute sources include the byte at 0x05 and the word beginning at 0xA9. Computed accesses can reach additional absolute locations.

No disjointness, atomicity, volatility, synchronization, or transaction boundary is established. Ordinary aliasing affects later loads, but zero-only aliasing does not invalidate the cleared-word conclusion in Behavior section 3.

No allocation, release, reference-count change, or ownership transfer is explicitly established. Passing or publishing address-like words does not establish ownership transfer. Dependencies may have additional effects; none are assumed absent.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static examples, not executed tests.

1. **Field extraction:** An all-ones input masked with 0x7FFFF and shifted right by 2 produces 0x1FFFF.

2. **Selector boundary:** Under bit-vector arithmetic, a selector source with its low seven bits set produces 508; the doubled-selector base is 1016.

3. **Cleared reload:** With ordinary stable memory and completed accesses, the multiplied-base offset-0x14 reload is zero. Overlapping selector-based zero stores do not permit a nonzero result.

4. **Absolute publication order:** If the retained link word is 0x12345678, the later absolute-0x54 word publication contains 0x2468ACF0. It follows the shifted-by-26 selector-field publication and precedes both unshifted link publications.

5. **Absolute byte publication:** If the doubled-selector base is 0x108, absolute address 0x15 receives 0x08 after the two computed offset-0x15 byte publications.

6. **Global capture:** If the absolute-0x60 store changes storage underlying the global-word source, the repeated region still uses the word captured before that store, absent another established mechanism changing the retained value.

7. **Mixed width:** The 0x3A halfword publication changes two bytes. Replacing it with a byte store is not equivalent.

8. **Final mutations:** If the earlier offset-0x64 word is 0x100 and the newly loaded base is 0x300, the final word publications place 0x100 at 0x354 and 0x300 at 0x200, assuming valid, noninterfering accesses. Both precede the final transfer.

9. **Failure:** A zero dependency result is not visibly rejected. Invalid subsequent addressing does not promise a clean return or rollback.
