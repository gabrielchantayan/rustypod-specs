## Behavior

### Arithmetic model

The following describes the represented behavior under 32-bit unsigned address arithmetic.

Let **selector** denote the fourth argument. Define:

- **Word base:** selector shifted right by 4 bits, multiplied by `0x2000`, with the result reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).
- **Reduced selector:** selector shifted right by 8 bits.
- **Halfword address:** reduced selector plus 10.
- **Halfword value:** the low 16 bits of reduced selector shifted left by 12.
- **Call value:** reduced selector shifted left by 24, reduced modulo \(2^{32}\).

Both right shifts are logical because the fourth argument is represented as unsigned.

These width rules are material. The word-base multiplication must not be interpreted as an unbounded address calculation. In particular:

- Word base is a multiple of `0x2000`.
- Only selector bits 4 through 22 affect word base.
- Selector bits 23 through 31 are discarded by the 32-bit multiplication result.
- Halfword value is equivalently the low four bits of reduced selector placed in bits 12 through 15 of a 16-bit value.
- Call value is equivalently the low eight bits of reduced selector placed in bits 24 through 31 of a 32-bit value.

Reduced selector ranges from zero through `0x00FFFFFF`. Consequently, halfword address ranges from `0x0000000A` through `0x01000009`; this addition does not overflow 32 bits.

### Nonzero first argument

### Overlap and alignment

The three word destinations are consecutive and 4-byte aligned under the stated arithmetic model. Their starting addresses do not wrap when adding 4 or 8 to word base.

The halfword destination is calculated independently and can overlap the word-write region. If it overlaps and the writes behave as ordinary memory accesses, the later halfword write replaces the overlapping bytes. Endianness is not established, so the resulting numerical value of an affected word cannot generally be specified.

Halfword address can be odd. No evidence establishes whether such an access succeeds, faults, or has target-specific behavior.

A calculated address of zero is possible. The function does not reject it. Address zero must not be presumed either valid or invalid without execution-environment information.
