# F-16ccc0212b5f

## Purpose

F-16ccc0212b5f adjusts four components of a compact calendar-like record, writes five primary fields to a destination, invokes a destination-processing dependency, and then copies three metadata fields from the then-current source.

**Established static behavior:** positive clock excess carries from seconds to minutes, minutes to hours, and hours to days. Negative signed clock totals do not borrow. Day adjustment performs at most one year transition.

Initial component accumulation uses unsigned 32-bit arithmetic, followed by conversion to signed 32-bit values. Destination writes involve additional conversions: year and day are converted to signed 16-bit values; clock fields and one metadata field are converted to plain character values. Out-of-range conversions do not unconditionally establish low-bit-preserving storage.

**Inferred intent:** the fields describe a date and time, and F-7264181a6e68 supplies an adjustment to a base year length of 365. The calendar and the dependency’s actual contract are not established.

## Behavior

### Initial accumulation and signed conversion

Each initial component total begins with a source value widened to unsigned 32 bits. Its additions therefore use unsigned 32-bit arithmetic:

- Seconds combine the extracted source second value and the second adjustment.
- Minutes combine the source minute byte, minute adjustment, and second-derived carry.
- Hours combine the extracted source hour value, hour adjustment, and minute-derived carry.
- Days combine the source day value, day adjustment, and hour-derived carry.

Signed adjustments and carries are converted to unsigned 32 bits for these additions. Every addition wraps modulo 2³², including cases whose corresponding mathematical signed sum would exceed signed 32-bit range.

Each resulting unsigned total is then converted to signed 32 bits:

- Values from 0 through 2,147,483,647 convert exactly.
- For values from 2,147,483,648 through 4,294,967,295, the target’s result or implementation-defined signal is **documented_only**.

Subsequent clock comparisons and normalization operate on the signed results. A negative result inferred from an upper-half unsigned residue therefore requires a target-conversion assumption.

### Clock normalization

The first component access reads the 16-bit source value at offset 10 and extracts its low eight numeric bits as seconds.

After accumulation and signed conversion, seconds are repeatedly reduced by 60 while their signed value exceeds 59. Each reduction contributes one minute carry.

Only after this stage does the function read source byte 9. It accumulates minutes, converts the total to signed 32 bits, and repeatedly reduces it by 60 while it exceeds 59. Each reduction contributes one hour carry.

Only after minute normalization does the function read the 16-bit source value at offset 8 and extract its low eight numeric bits as hours. It accumulates hours, converts the total to signed 32 bits, and repeatedly reduces it by 24 while it exceeds 23. Each reduction contributes one day carry.

For each clock stage:

- A nonnegative signed starting value is reduced to the conventional component range.
- A value exactly equal to the stage divisor becomes zero with one carry.
- A negative signed starting value remains unchanged and produces no carry.
- There is no borrowing from the next component.

Source clock values outside conventional ranges are not rejected. Extracted seconds and hours may initially range from 0 through 255, and the minute byte may also range from 0 through 255.

The clock-loop subtractions and carry increments remain within signed 32-bit range for signed 32-bit starting values.

### At most one year transition

- F-7264181a6e68 is called again, with that decremented year.
- The second result is added to 365, and that sum is added to the negative day total, using signed 32-bit arithmetic.

The first query result is unused on this path. Its side effects are not excluded. Its addition to 365 is not evaluated on this path.

No further backward transition occurs, even if the adjusted day remains negative.

For a nonnegative signed day total:

- The day is compared against that threshold using a signed comparison.
- If the day is at least the threshold, the unsigned year is incremented once and the threshold is subtracted once.
- Otherwise, year and day are unchanged.

The incremented year is not queried. No further forward transition occurs.
