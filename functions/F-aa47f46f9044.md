## Behavior

### Indirect-call prefix

Visible interactions include calls with no explicit arguments, calls receiving shifted primary results, and calls receiving separate portions of combined results. Some sites additionally forward duplicated arguments, explicit zeros, masked fields, or secondary machine-state words.

A common transformation shifts the low result word left by eight positions and forwards the high word unchanged. Other sites shift different portions, retain masked fields, or use earlier saved values after intervening calls. These differences are observable argument requirements; they are not interchangeable encodings.

There are no visible returned-status checks. In particular, zero results are transformed or forwarded rather than uniformly interpreted as failures.

### Arithmetic requirements

Under a confirmed 32-bit word model, unsigned left shifts discard bits beyond bit 31, and narrowing stores retain only the destination width. Unsigned right shifts introduce zeros.

Important distinctions include:

- Retaining 24 bits before shifting left by six produces at most 30 significant bits.
- A combined result shifted right by 34, 36, or 44 positions yields the original high word logically shifted right by two, four, or twelve positions, respectively.
- A word shifted left by 28 and then left by another 28 has no surviving bits under the 32-bit interpretation.
- Multiplication, subtraction, and signed-looking shifts are not independently proven to have modular machine semantics merely by their displayed types.

Signed overflow, signed right-shift behavior, and arithmetic outside a validated address model remain **documented_only**. Algebraic simplification must not erase a narrowing, mask, or intermediate-width boundary.

### Entry into direct mutation

The following descriptive roles identify retained values without asserting object identities:

- **Encoded base:** an earlier primary result shifted left by 25.
- **Scaled base:** another retained primary result multiplied by 2²⁵.
- **Small index:** a later result shifted left by eight, masked to 15 bits, then logically shifted right by six.
- **Doubled index:** twice the small index.
- **Retained shifted word:** an earlier result shifted left by eight.
- **Final low word** and **final high word:** portions of the last combined result before direct mutation.

The opening mutation order is significant. A word containing the encoded base is stored at final-high-word plus 0x14. The low byte of the final high word is written at encoded-base plus 9. A word containing the scaled base is then stored at 256 times the final low word plus 0x54. Only afterward is the byte at encoded-base plus 9 overwritten with the low byte of the encoded base.

The scaled-base offset-0x14 word is set to zero. A byte is read at scaled-base plus 5. A zero word is stored at doubled-index plus 0x14 before the byte’s zero-extended value is stored at scaled-base plus 0x34. The scaled-base offset-0x14 word is then read, followed by another zero word store at doubled-index plus 0x14.

That read is a memory read at its represented position, not a substitution of the earlier zero. Intervening accesses can alias.

The low byte of the loaded word is written twice at doubled-index plus 0x15. Both writes are represented occurrences.

## Acceptance examples

These are conditional static criteria, not execution reports.

### Word transformation

Under a confirmed 32-bit model, a common forwarding site receiving low word 0x12345678 and high word 0x89ABCDEF supplies transformed low word 0x34567800 and unchanged high word 0x89ABCDEF.

At the 24-bit-mask-then-six-bit-shift site, input 0xFFFFFFFF produces 0x3FFFFFC0.

### Small-index boundary

The small index can be a multiple of four from 0 through 508, not an arbitrary nine-bit number. The doubled index is a multiple of eight from 0 through 1016. These bounds do not establish address validity.

### Intermediate writes and fixed-global ordering

If encoded-base plus 9 is observable storage, both initial byte writes must occur with the intervening low-word-derived word store.

If the fixed-address 0x54 store aliases the doubled-index offset-0x14 destination, later reads and final memory must reflect its position between the surrounding derived writes. Moving it before or after all such writes is not equivalent.

### Retained global-base snapshot

If a preceding derived-base offset-0x20 store changes the pointer-sized global, the retained base captures the changed value. If a subsequent group overwrites that global storage, later groups still use the already saved base unless another represented operation changes the retained value. Reloading the global for every group fails this requirement.

### Fresh byte reads versus saved words

If an intervening derived write changes byte 0x67, a later read used for derived-base plus 9 sees the value present at that read. It need not match the earlier value copied to 0x21.

Changing memory at 0xA9 after its word was saved does not replace that saved word in subsequent uses.

### Explicit repetition

For observable memory, acceptance requires the initial distinct group and every subsequent explicit group, preserving their order and multiplicity. Matching final bytes alone is insufficient. No unresolved loop bound is needed to establish this represented sequence.

### Faulting destination

If a loaded destination is inaccessible, the evidence establishes no explicit error result or rollback. Successful completion is not required beyond the failing access.
