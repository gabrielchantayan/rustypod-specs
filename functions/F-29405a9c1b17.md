### Integer transformations

**Additions.** F-U04 and F-U10 each receive a preceding 32-bit result increased by 255. There is no visible clamp, range check, saturation, or error branch.

For results whose addition remains within the represented signed range, the arithmetic is unambiguous. For overflow cases, the evidence does not independently prove whether the intended contract is wrapping machine arithmetic or some other interpretation of the recovered expression.

**Left shifts.** F-U07 consumes three transformations of the same low 32-bit half, shifted left by 12, 16, and 24 bits. These are not rotations. No independent masking operation is visible.

Under a 32-bit machine-word interpretation, high bits shifted out are discarded and low vacated bits become zero. The supplied representation alone is insufficient to establish a language-level signed-shift contract for every input.

**Repeated shifted argument.** The left-shift-by-16 value is computed before F-U07 and supplied again to F-U08. F-U08 does not visibly obtain this second argument by rereading the original 64-bit result from external storage.

**64-bit splitting.** F-U07 and F-U23 receive halves of two separate 64-bit results. No relationship between those two results is established.

**Final right shifts.** F-U30 receives the same unsigned 32-bit value shifted right by 16 in both its first and second argument positions. These are logical right shifts, with results in the range 0 through 65535.

The fourth argument to F-U30 is the 32-bit pattern `0xFFFFFF3B`. If interpreted as a signed two’s-complement 32-bit integer, it denotes −197; if interpreted as unsigned, it denotes 4,294,967,099. The receiving dependency’s interpretation is unknown.
